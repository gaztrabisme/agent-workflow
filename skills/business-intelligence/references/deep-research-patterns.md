# Deep Research Patterns

Start from the client decision, then collect the smallest set of current evidence that can change it. A source list is not a finding: record identity, date, relevance, limits, and what the source actually establishes.

## Source quality and confidence

- Do not use an unsourced point until it has a source. [book:articulating-design-decisions], # Chapter 7. Choose a Message
- For conflicting findings, locate a primary source to resolve the conflict. [book:think-like-ux-researcher], ## Sharpening Your Thinking Tools
- For unverified single-source claims, triangulate with additional sources. [book:intelligence-driven-incident-response], ### Collection
- Grade the source and information separately for each intelligence item and record collection date; this supports the readiness gate but does not prescribe its specific workflow. [book:intelligence-driven-incident-response], ### Confidence Levels
- Lead a report with a concise summary of its issue, solution, and reason; the guide does not specify the separate source, conflict, and ranking checks. [book:hbr-guide-business-writing], # **Memos and Reports**
- Research indicators of customer challenges and issues you could resolve; the book does not require analyst concerns, stated challenges, and public admissions as a fixed source list. [book:selling-to-big-companies], ## DIG UP THE DIRT
- Score source reliability separately from confidence in the information content, combine both on each intelligence item, and record its collection date. [book:intelligence-driven-incident-response], ### Confidence Levels
- Test that the proposed capability is formulated to solve the stated business problem, alongside the evidence, fit, and gap. [book:data-science-for-business], Business and Data Understanding
- The book grades each source and information item separately, supporting contextual confidence assessment instead of treating all social sources as uniformly low reliability. [book:intelligence-driven-incident-response], ### Confidence Levels
- The book grades evidence strength in three tiers, rather than separating findings into only high and low confidence. [book:think-like-ux-researcher], ## UX Research and Strength of Evidence
- The book grades source reliability and the credibility of each information item on separate scales instead of ranking source types in one table. [book:intelligence-driven-incident-response], ### Confidence Levels
- The book recommends analyst trend sources and quadrants when scoping vendor technology, rather than specifically resolving vague competitive research. [book:mastering-api-architecture], ## Radars, Quadrants, and Trend Reports
- The book records collection dates and judges whether information is perishable without prescribing source-specific refresh searches. [book:intelligence-driven-incident-response], ### Date of Collection
- The book requires date and perishability checks but does not set the executive-role or source-age thresholds in this rule. [book:intelligence-driven-incident-response], ### Date of Collection
- The book treats SEC filings as primary for financial fundamentals, not as the first source for every strategy or target claim. [book:machinelearningforalgorithmictradingsecondedition], ## Financial statement data
- The book uses SEC filings for company financials but does not establish annual reports as a comprehensive source for all strategy and metrics. [book:machinelearningforalgorithmictradingsecondedition], ## Financial statement data
- Use separate source-reliability and information-confidence scores on each claim, combining the A–E and 1–5 dimensions into one item label. [book:intelligence-driven-incident-response], ### Confidence Levels
- Verify the person’s responsibilities rather than assuming their title means the same thing across organizations; the passage does not set a recency check. [book:meddicc], ## DISCOVERY
- When a subsidiary or division lacks a public annual report, look for internal reports and newsletters as substitutes and note any remaining evidence gap. [book:key-account-management], ## 7.2 Key account analysis
