# Value Engineering

Separate measured current conditions from the desired outcome. Show assumptions and scenario ranges so an estimate does not read as a promised result; use customer data where available and label other inputs.

## Compact business-case outline

- **Decision:** problem, proposed response, and recommendation.
- **Cost of inaction:** current baseline and measured impact, with source.
- **Target value:** baseline-to-target measures, expected benefit, and timing.
- **Investment:** implementation and operating costs, plus total cost over the period assessed.
- **Analysis:** return range, key assumptions and their sources, scenario sensitivity, risks, and mitigations.
- **Measurement:** success measure, owner, and plan to check realized impact.

## Build a defensible business case

- Anchor the financial analysis on the customer's own required ROI ratio, which you should ask for directly. [book:meddicc], DECISION CRITERIA
- Do not include a business-case claim without both a confidence grade and a source. [book:software-architect-elevator], Chapter 20. Writing for Busy People
- Keep one up-to-date full business case and derive a short summary proposal for senior executives instead of selecting a different artifact by deal size. [book:meddicc], METRICS
- Research the customer's business state before every call rather than only when the client is unfamiliar or pricing is expected. [book:meddicc], Discovery
- Test each competitor’s access to the asset and ability to obtain comparable value before stating a limitation. [book:data-science-for-business], ## Sustaining Competitive Advantage with Data Science
- The book grounds a competitor-specific limitation in an inability to match the asset or its value, rather than a bare “cannot do” claim. [book:data-science-for-business], Sustaining Competitive Advantage with Data Science
- The book requires evidence to support research claims and decisions, but does not prescribe a separate workflow for forming value claims. [book:think-like-ux-researcher], UX Research and Strength of Evidence
- The book separates data-derived probabilities from business values that often come from outside sources, such as subject-matter experts. [book:data-science-for-business], A Key Analytical Framework: Expected Value
- The book sequences issue listing, evidence, impact, and constraints before solution work, rather than prescribing this exact five-step value-engineering chain. [book:think-like-ux-researcher], Conducting an Effective Stakeholder Interview
- The book uses pre-call research to avoid questions answerable from outside sources, without prescribing it specifically for SPIN Implication questions or financial-impact evidence. [book:meddicc], ## DISCOVERY
- The book's advantage test asks why competitors cannot possess the asset or get the same value; a one-sentence entry for each named competitor is the skill's format. [book:data-science-for-business], Sustaining Competitive Advantage with Data Science
- The example quantifies the client's current problem and the value of solving it; sales-discovery use is the skill's framing. [book:think-like-ux-researcher], ## Conducting an Effective Stakeholder Interview
- Trace every number in the business case to customer data, a benchmark, or an estimate.
- Treat hard savings as budget-line reductions, soft savings as convertible only when tied to spending, and strategic value as quantified risk exposure when possible.

## Model value, cost, and uncertainty

- Build a capability model from desired outcomes and map specific gaps against it, rather than treating a product-fit map as the whole assessment. [book:fundamentals-enterprise-architecture], ## Map Capability Gaps
- Build a tailored capability model from intended outcomes and assess its gaps; the book supports this analysis, not a named Capability Map for every presales engagement. [book:fundamentals-enterprise-architecture], ## Map Capability Gaps
- Calibrate value numbers conservatively — take the low end of any range and halve it — rather than quoting the benchmark figure as what the client will get. [book:value-based-fees], Quantitative and Qualitative Measures and Criteria
- Compare all options, including status quo, across like-for-like three-to-five-year periods and all mandatory cost categories.
- Compare costs in a multi-year TCO and include operating costs; the status-quo option and three-to-five-year boundaries are skill extensions. [book:modernnetworkobservability], ## Cost analysis
- Define the audience, desired outcome, available evidence, and risks before structuring the meeting communication. [book:storytelling-with-data], ## Consulting for context: questions to ask
- Define the organizational capabilities needed for target outcomes and assess actual gaps or weak solutions; four-way labels alone do not show these causes. [book:fundamentals-enterprise-architecture], ## Map Capability Gaps
- During scoping, clarify the outcome the user wants. [book:storytelling-with-data], ## Consulting for context: questions to ask
- First agree with the buyer on business outcomes, measures of progress, and what counts as value; the book does not prescribe a process-to-KPI sequence. [book:value-based-fees], CHAPTER 1 The Origins of Value: *What People Want Is Not as Important as What They Need*
- Include a plan to evaluate business costs and benefits in the skeleton, not only the deal trigger and outline. [book:data-science-for-business], Evaluation and Deployment
- Include quantified problem, solution, investment, return, recommendation, cost of inaction, future-state KPIs, TCO, financial analysis, sensitivity, risks, and an assumption log in the business case.
- Keep seller-sourced business-case numbers in the assumption category until customer conversations confirm them, and grade confidence accordingly. [book:meddicc], ## MEDDICC SCORING
- Rank pains first by customer priorities and measurable outcomes, then assess how well the offering fits those needs. [book:meddicc], ## METRICS
- The book asks stakeholders to consider the do-nothing case and quantify the value gap over time, without naming a cost-of-inaction calculation. [book:think-like-ux-researcher], Conducting an Effective Stakeholder Interview
- The book defines TCO through direct and indirect costs and separately highlights opportunity cost, which should be checked in the formula's coverage. [book:fundamentals-data-engineering], Total Cost of Ownership
- The book explains NPV calculation; it does not prescribe when to use each metric or an IRR policy. [book:excel-for-accountants], ## NPV Function
- The book grades the strength of problem evidence; it does not prescribe building a business case from discovery data or assigning confidence to every assumption. [book:think-like-ux-researcher], ## Conducting an Effective Stakeholder Interview
- The book models probability-weighted outcomes and values rather than prescribing a base-case ROI range or a fixed number of drivers. [book:data-science-for-business], A Key Analytical Framework: Expected Value
- The book tests input sensitivity with base, best, and worst cases; it does not prescribe 3–7 drivers or downside and severe labels. [book:excel-for-accountants], Chapter 6
- The interview method probes the no-action case and quantifies value over time; the book does not set the skill's cost categories or projection horizon. [book:think-like-ux-researcher], ## Conducting an Effective Stakeholder Interview
- Treat reference customers as a scarce selection criterion — a few, financially viable ones — rather than building one per vertical regardless of cost. [book:key-account-management], 2.2 Selection criteria
- Use a foot-in-the-door strategy: land a toe-hold project or order in one area, deliver impressive results, and then expand; the book sets no $10K–$50K range and no 30–90-day proof window. [book:selling-to-big-companies], REMEMBER WHAT'S IMPORTANT
- Use base, best, and worst-case scenarios and test input sensitivity; the ROI range and three-to-seven driver count are skill-specific. [book:excel-for-accountants], ## Scenarios and Multiple Inputs
- Use scenario sensitivity to identify decision-critical inputs; the book does not map customer data, benchmarks, and estimates onto High/Medium/Low provenance labels. [book:excel-for-accountants], ## Scenarios and Multiple Inputs
- When exact figures are impossible, name the business impact qualitatively — lag time, lost windows, downtime cost — instead of forcing a number, so a soft saving is never dressed as hard. [book:selling-to-big-companies], What If You Don't Have Metrics?

- Compare proposed results against an objective baseline tied to the actual problem. [book:data-science-for-business], Evaluation and Deployment
- Make quantitative claims testable with an explicit baseline and a plan to measure deployed impact. [book:data-science-for-business], ## Evaluation and Deployment
- Pause before pricing to verify customer consensus on value. [book:meddicc], MEDDICC CHECKLIST
- Resolve disputed value or scope points with the client by negotiating on objective criteria selected in advance. [book:getting-to-yes], 5 Insist on Using Objective Criteria
- Review a proposed analytics project against business fit, measurement, cost, and deployment criteria. [book:data-science-for-business], Evaluation and Deployment
- Review analytics proposals for task-appropriate metrics, holdout evaluation, baselines, and business costs and benefits. [book:data-science-for-business], ## Evaluation and Deployment
- Role-play a new call plan, value story, or Q&A response with a colleague before using it with a client. [book:selling-to-big-companies], MAKE SURE YOU'RE SET UP FOR SUCCESS
