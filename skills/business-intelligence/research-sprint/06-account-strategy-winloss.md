# Account Strategy, Win/Loss Analysis, and Multi-Year Intelligence

## 1. Strategic Account Management (SAM/KAM)

SAM treats key accounts as markets of one. Unlike transactional selling, it's a company-wide discipline of protecting and growing key relationships over 1-3 years.

**Miller Heiman LAMP (Large Account Management Process):** The Gold Sheet covers: account selection criteria, Buy-Sell Hierarchy (where you sit on the vendor-partner spectrum), strategic players mapping, strengths/vulnerabilities, Charter Statement (mutual goals), revenue targets, 90-day review cycles.

---

## 2. Land and Expand

**Beachhead:** Land a small, low-risk engagement ($10K-$50K) a mid-level manager can approve. Prove value in 30-90 days. Expand.

**Expansion vectors:**
- Same department, bigger scope (POC to production)
- Adjacent departments (IT to business units)
- New geographies/subsidiaries
- New capability areas (dev to cloud to AI)
- Higher value chain (staff aug to managed services to consulting)

**North star metric:** Net Dollar Retention (NDR). Best-in-class B2B: 120-150%.

## 3. Account Plan Template (IT Services)

```
ACCOUNT PLAN: [Client Name]
Updated: [Date] | Owner: [Account Lead] | Review: Quarterly

1. ACCOUNT SNAPSHOT
   Industry, revenue, employees, HQ, key subsidiaries
   Strategic priorities (annual report / earnings calls)
   IT budget trends, digital transformation status
   Recent news / leadership changes

2. RELATIONSHIP MAP
   Org chart overlay: champion / supporter / neutral / blocker
   Relationship strength: green / yellow / red per contact
   Coverage gaps: divisions/levels with no relationship

3. ENGAGEMENT HISTORY
   All past engagements: scope, outcome, satisfaction, revenue
   Key wins and delivery issues
   Lessons learned per phase

4. WHITE SPACE ANALYSIS
   Matrix: rows = business units, columns = your capabilities
   Cells: current penetration | opportunity size | competitive presence
   Priority: opportunity size x relationship access

5. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE
   Who else is in this account? What do they provide?
   Differentiation strengths
   Incumbent vulnerabilities and trigger events

6. GROWTH STRATEGY (12 months)
   3 specific expansion opportunities with estimated value
   Required stakeholder access to unlock each
   Sequencing: what must happen first

7. RISK ASSESSMENT
   Single-threading risk
   Delivery risk from current engagements
   Competitive threats
   Client organizational changes

8. 90-DAY ACTION PLAN
   Specific actions, owners, deadlines
   Meetings to schedule, introductions to make
```

## 4. Relationship Mapping

**Two views:**
- **Stakeholder Matrix:** Rows = people, columns = influence / support / relationship strength
- **Org Chart Overlay:** Boxes with reporting lines + informal influence, color-coded by relationship quality

**Track per stakeholder:** Role in buying process, influence/interest quadrant, relationship strength (R/Y/G), last meaningful interaction, who on YOUR team owns this relationship.

**Gap analysis:** Where no contact above director? Which BUs have zero coverage? Is champion the only thread?

## 5. Win/Loss Analysis -- Building from Zero

**Phase 1 (Months 1-3):**
1. Pick 5 recent deals (mix wins/losses)
2. Internal debriefs (30 min each with sales lead)
3. Attempt 2-3 external client interviews
4. Document in simple template
5. Present patterns to leadership

**Phase 2 (Months 4-6):**
1. Standardize: every deal above $X gets debrief
2. Train neutral interviewer (NOT the deal rep)
3. Build 10-15 question bank
4. Quarterly pattern reports

**Phase 3 (Months 7-12):**
1. Target 20+ interviews/year
2. Segment by deal type, region, competitor
3. Feed into training, positioning, pricing
4. Consider third-party interviewer

**Timing:** Within 2 weeks of decision. For wins, before implementation pain overwrites sales experience.

**Core questions for IT services:**
1. What triggered the evaluation?
2. Who else did you consider?
3. Top 3 decision criteria?
4. How did you perceive our team vs alternatives?
5. What almost made you choose differently?
6. How did price factor in?
7. Did references/case studies influence you?
8. What could we have done better?
9. How did cultural fit factor in?
10. Would you recommend us?

## 6. Why IT Services Deals Get Won/Lost

**Won:** Demonstrated capability in specific tech/domain, reference customers in same industry, team quality (actual people, not brand), cultural fit, credible delivery plan, relationship trust from prior engagements.

**Lost:** Capability gaps (more than price), broken promises from sales, governance failures (40-70% of outsourcing fails on governance), high personnel attrition, communication breakdowns, incumbent advantage, misalignment between sales and procurement.

**McKinsey insight:** "Greater innovation was a desired goal on both sides but often was lacking." The innovation gap is where challengers win.

## 7. Deal Reviews

**Red flag indicators:**
1. Single-threaded
2. Stalled beyond average cycle time
3. Champion goes silent
4. No clear decision process or timeline
5. Budget uncertainty
6. No access to economic buyer
7. Competitor entering late (price leverage)

---

## 8. Competitive Displacement

**Four-part framework:**
1. **Change Trigger** -- What changed that makes status quo insufficient?
2. **Gap Evidence** -- Proof incumbent can't meet new requirements
3. **Risk of Inaction** -- Cost of staying with current vendor
4. **Decision Architecture** -- Make switching feel structured and low-risk

**Never attack incumbent directly.** Shift from "who is better" to "what has changed."

**As a challenger:** Position as logical "number two" first. Find what incumbent isn't addressing. Use pilots to reduce switching risk.

## 9. Multi-Year Arc

**Year 1 -- Establish Credibility:**
- Land beachhead, deliver flawlessly
- Build 3-5 stakeholder relationships
- Intel need: client business model, org structure, decision process

**Year 2 -- Expand Scope:**
- Cross-sell adjacent departments/capabilities
- Provide unsolicited insights
- Get invited to planning discussions
- Intel need: 3-year strategy, internal politics, budget cycles

**Year 3 -- Strategic Partnership:**
- Joint innovation, embedded in planning cycles
- Extension of client's team
- Intel need: industry disruption, M&A implications, technology alignment

## 10. Customer Success and Retention

**Health scoring for IT services:**
- Delivery satisfaction (CSAT per milestone)
- Stakeholder engagement (meeting attendance)
- Scope stability (change request trends)
- Team stability (attrition on either side)
- Communication quality (response times, escalation frequency)
- Strategic alignment

**Churn risk indicators:**
- Champion leaves or reassigned
- Budget cuts or priority shifts
- New CIO/CTO with different vendor preferences
- Client stops inviting you to planning meetings
- Procurement benchmarking your rates

**Critical:** Presale intelligence (stakeholder map, business drivers, competitive landscape) should transfer to delivery/CS team. This almost never happens.

## 11. Reference Selling

- 84% of B2B buyers start with a referral (HBR)
- 83% trust peer experiences more than vendor statements
- 73% say case studies significantly influence purchasing

**Applied:** land one flagship reference per vertical — a utility for energy, an oilfield-services firm for O&G — and build the reference ecosystem around verticals rather than around individual logos.

## IT Services Common Mistakes

1. Treating every deal as one-shot -- no institutional memory
2. Single-threading -- one relationship per account
3. Selling capabilities not outcomes
4. Promising the moon to close
5. Cost-first positioning -- trains clients to treat you as commodity
6. No governance framework
7. Using accounts as training grounds (high attrition)
8. Ignoring cultural fit (critical for offshore delivery into European/US/Gulf buyers)
9. No win/loss practice
10. Static account plans
11. Misalignment between sales and delivery
12. Failing to transition presale intelligence to delivery
