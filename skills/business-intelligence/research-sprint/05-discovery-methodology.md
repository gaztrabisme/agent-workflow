# Presale Discovery & Live Meeting Methodology

## 1. Discovery Call Structure

**T-Shaped Model:** Go broad first (horizontal bar), then deep on 2-3 areas (vertical).

**Time allocation (45 minutes):**

| Phase | Minutes | Activity |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Opening/Rapport | 2-3 | Upfront contract: agenda, time, expected outcome |
| Broad Mapping | 5-8 | Context questions -- current state, stakeholders, initiatives |
| Deep Dive | 20-25 | Pain funnel on 2-3 critical areas |
| Summary/Confirm | 5 | Paraphrase back, test understanding |
| Next Steps | 3-5 | Specific actions, owners, dates |

**The 80/20 rule:** Prospect talks 80%. If you're talking more than 40%, you're pitching, not discovering.

---

## 2. SPIN in Practice

**Situation Questions (3-5 max):**
- "Walk me through how your team currently handles [process]."
- Every situation question you ask is one you could have answered with research.

**Problem Questions:**
- "Where does that process break down?"
- "What happens when [system] doesn't perform as expected?"
- Let the buyer describe their own frustrations.

**Implication Questions (where deals are won):**
- Top performers ask 4x more implication questions than average.
- The "implication ladder" -- each answer feeds the next:
  - "How does that 10-week ramp affect quarterly targets?"
  - "When you miss targets, how does that affect the board's willingness to fund next hiring?"
  - "If hiring stalls, what happens to committed projects?"
- Why most fail here: They hear a problem and jump to solution. Never built cost of inaction.

**Need-Payoff Questions:**
- "If you could cut that time in half, what would that mean for Q3?"
- Pivot signal: When prospect says "Yeah, this is really costing us" -- stop expanding pain, start asking what solving it would mean.

## 3. Sandler Pain Funnel -- Deep

**Level 1 -- Surface Pain:**
- "Tell me more about that."
- "Can you give me an example?"
- "How long has that been a problem?"

**Level 2 -- Business/Financial Pain:**
- "What have you tried to fix it?"
- "And did that work?"
- "How does that affect [revenue/throughput/satisfaction]?"

**Level 3 -- Personal/Emotional Pain:**
- "How does that make you feel?"
- "Have you given up trying to solve this?"
- This is the decision driver. Business cases close on paper; personal pain closes in reality.

**Pain-o-Mometer:** "On a scale of 1-10, how severe?" Then: "What would make it a 10?"

**Reversing:** When prospect asks you a question, respond with a question to understand intent before answering.

---

## 4. Active Listening for Presale

**Three things to listen for:**
- **Buying signals:** Pricing, timelines, specific features, "Can we do a POC?" 2.4x more likely to hit quota when caught.
- **Objection signals:** "That might be difficult" = invitation to dig deeper.
- **Political signals:** "That might be challenging internally" = organizational power map.

**What they're NOT saying:** Tone changes, hesitation, vague phrasing, topic avoidance, inconsistencies between stakeholders.

**Techniques:**
- Paraphrase: "So what I'm hearing is..."
- Summarize: "Let me make sure I have this right..."
- Label: "It sounds like that's really frustrating" (Chris Voss)
- Soft hypothesis: "I'm getting the sense that [X] might be the bigger issue -- am I reading that right?"

## 5. Technical Discovery

**Start with user journey, not architecture.** "Walk me through what happens when a customer places an order" reveals systems, data flows, integration points without feeling like an audit.

**Sequence:**
1. User/process flow: "Who touches this, and what do they do?"
2. Data flow: "Where does data come from? Where does it go?"
3. Integration points: "What systems need to talk to each other?"
4. Constraints: "SLAs? Data volumes? Compliance requirements?"
5. Change management: "How does your team handle deployments?"

**Critical distinction:** Separate business objectives from solution preferences. "We need microservices" is a preference. Ask: "What's driving the move?"

**Do not traffic in secondhand information.** If IT says "we're building an app for marketing," talk to marketing directly.

---

## 6. Needs Analysis vs Needs Creation

**Needs Analysis (SPIN/Sandler):** Understand stated problems and deepen them.

**Needs Creation (Challenger):** Teach them something they didn't know about their own problem.

**Challenger six-step choreography:**
1. Warmer: Show you understand their world (specific insight, not generic rapport)
2. Reframe: "Most companies assume X. The data shows Y."
3. Rational Drowning: Data proving the cost
4. Emotional Impact: Story of a similar company that paid the price
5. A New Way: The approach that solves the reframed problem
6. Your Solution: How you deliver it

**When it backfires:** When you teach what they already know (arrogant), when your insight is generic (like every vendor), when they've already defined requirements.

## 7. Meeting Types

| Type | Your Role | Question Style | Prep Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Initial Discovery** | Curious diagnostician | Broad then deep, SPIN | Research, 5-7 core questions |
| **Technical Deep-Dive** | Peer architect | Architecture probing | Know their stack, integration scenarios |
| **Demo + Q&A** | Storyteller | "Does this match?" | Great Demo!: Situation Slide, Do It, Peel Back |
| **Executive Briefing** | Strategic advisor | ROI-focused, Challenger | 6-min chunks, business outcomes |
| **POC Scoping** | Collaborative designer | "What would prove this?" | Success criteria, evaluation timeline |
| **Negotiation** | Problem-solver | Sandler reversing, LAER | BATNA, walk-away points |

## 8. Handling Difficult Moments

**Don't know the answer:** "Great question -- I want to give you an accurate answer. Let me get back to you by [time]." Never bluff.

**Demo breaks:** Turn it into discovery. "While I pull this up, tell me more about how your team handles this." Have backup resources.

**Room goes hostile:** Identify what detractor wants. Ask champion how that person is measured. Find ways to help their objectives.

**Trap question:** Don't answer directly. "Interesting angle -- what's driving that question?"

**Conversation off-track:** "Let's park that for follow-up and come back to [topic]."

**Prep was wrong:** "I came in thinking your main challenge was X, but Y sounds like the priority. Tell me more about Y." Builds more trust than pretending.

## 9. Note-Taking and Follow-Up

**Capture during meeting:**
1. Pain points (in their exact words)
2. Business impact (quantified where possible)
3. Decision process (who, when, how)
4. Technical constraints
5. Political dynamics (who championed, resisted, was silent)

**Post-meeting (within 1 hour):** Structure notes. Tag each insight: confirmed fact, assumption to verify, signal to investigate.

**Follow-up (within 24 hours):** Summary email with pain points paraphrased, proposed next steps, specific actions with owners. "Based on our conversation, here's what I heard as your top priorities: [1, 2, 3]. Please let me know if I missed anything."

The follow-up email IS your first deliverable. It proves you listened.

## 10. Objection Handling

**LAER (most versatile):**
- **Listen** -- Let them finish
- **Acknowledge** -- "I appreciate you raising that"
- **Explore** -- "What specifically concerns you about [X]?"
- **Respond** -- Address with evidence, then confirm

**Reframing:** Price objection -- don't defend price. "The question isn't whether you can afford the solution -- it's whether you can afford another year of [quantified problem]."

**Real vs smokescreen test:** "If we could resolve [objection], would you be ready to move forward?" Real objection = "yes." Smokescreen = another objection.

---

## 11. Pre-Call Planning

**30-minute checklist (with your AE):**
1. Objective: "As a result of this call, the buyer will [specific action]"
2. Minimum acceptable outcome
3. Top 3 questions we NEED answered
4. Top 2 likely objections with prepared responses
5. Role assignment: who leads, who handles technical, who takes notes
6. Relevant evidence: which case study matches
7. Landmines: what could go wrong

---

## Engineers-Turned-Presale Common Mistakes

1. **Solutioning too early** -- hear problem, immediately architect fix
2. **Jargon overload** -- technical language to business audience
3. **Feature-walking** -- demo every feature instead of 3 that map to pain
4. **Free consultancy** -- detailed architecture advice for months without commercial commitment
5. **Secondhand discovery** -- accepting IT's version of what marketing needs
6. **Treating discovery as interrogation** -- rapid-fire without context or reciprocity
7. **Ignoring non-technical stakeholders**
8. **No upfront contract** -- diving into questions without setting expectations
9. **Slipping into support mode** -- erodes pipeline capacity
