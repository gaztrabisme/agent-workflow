# Stakeholder Mapping & Buying Process Intelligence

## 1. The Buying Committee / Decision-Making Unit

CEB/Gartner (2015): 5.4 decision-makers. Gartner (2023): 8-13. Forrester (2024): 13 stakeholders spanning multiple departments.

**The roles that matter (Miller Heiman + Challenger synthesis):**

| Role | What they do | How to spot them |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic Buyer** | Final budget authority, signs the check | "Who can approve spend at this level without escalation?" |
| **Technical Evaluator** | Gates on feasibility, security, compliance | Sends you the technical questionnaire |
| **User Buyer** | Lives with the solution daily | Asks workflow/integration questions |
| **Champion/Mobilizer** | Actively sells internally on your behalf | Volunteers next steps, does homework between meetings |
| **Coach** | Gives you inside intelligence | Tells you things about org politics, competing priorities |
| **Blocker** | Actively opposes | Goes silent, raises new objections after each resolved |
| **Gatekeeper** | Controls access to Economic Buyer | EA, Chief of Staff, procurement lead |

**Invisible stakeholders:** Legal/compliance (appears late, kills deals), Security/InfoSec (veto power, never in early meetings), CFO's office (involved in 79% of purchases), predecessor's preferred vendor champion.

## 2. Stakeholder Mapping Frameworks

| Framework | Practical for presale? | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Power/Interest Grid** (Mendelow) | YES -- primary tool | Every deal |
| **Miller Heiman Blue Sheet** | YES -- gold standard for complex deals | 6+ stakeholders, $100K+ |
| **Influence/Impact Matrix** | Useful supplement | Need to distinguish who influences from who decides |
| **RACI** | NO for presale | Internal project governance only |

**Key insight:** Power/Interest grid for fast classification. Blue Sheet for deep strategy. Use both.

**Critical rule:** A stakeholder map is a living document. Update after every interaction.

## 3. Champion Development

**Champion vs Supporter:** A supporter likes you. A champion spends political capital pushing your deal forward internally. Test: will they do homework between meetings?

**Identifying champions (Challenger Customer research):** Look for **Mobilizers** -- Go-Getters, Teachers, Skeptics. Avoid **Talkers** -- Guides, Friends, Climbers who are pleasant but won't drive action.

**How to test:** Assign a task -- call a reference, share internal data, draft a problem statement. If they follow through, they're real.

**Enabling champions:**
- Give them a customized business case in *their* language
- Provide role-specific collateral (exec summary for boss, tech spec for IT, ROI for finance)
- Build the internal memo *for* them
- 3x more likely to close when champions armed with helpful information (Gartner)

**Multi-champion strategy:**
- Executive Champion (air cover)
- Technical Champion (feasibility validation)
- End User Champion (workflow proof)

## 4. Buying Process Intelligence

**Standard enterprise procurement flow:**
```
Need identification -> Market research (RFI) -> Requirements definition ->
RFP issuance -> Vendor evaluation -> Shortlist -> Negotiation -> Contract
```

**How to gather intelligence without asking "how do you buy?":**
- "What would need to happen between now and a decision?"
- "Who else would need to weigh in?"
- "Have you done something like this before? What worked/didn't?"
- "Is there a budget cycle we should be aware of?"
- "What does your evaluation process typically look like at this investment level?"

**Pre-RFP influence:** 41% of buyers have a preferred vendor before formal evaluation. The game is won or lost before the RFP drops.

**RFP scoring reality:** Weighted matrices with 15-25 criteria. Weights locked before RFP goes out. If you didn't influence criteria, you're playing someone else's game.

## 5. Consensus Building & "No Decision"

**The biggest competitor is the status quo.** 40-60% of deals end in "no decision" despite buyer intent.

**Two types (critical distinction -- HBR research):**
1. **Status quo (44%):** Buyer underestimates cost of doing nothing. Fix: quantify cost of inaction.
2. **Indecision (56%):** Analysis paralysis. Fix: simplify choices, reduce fear of purchasing.

Using the status quo playbook on an indecisive buyer makes them *more* indecisive.

**Practical tools:**
- Map each stakeholder's personal "win" (Results = organizational, Wins = personal/career)
- Build mutual action plan with time-bound steps
- Quantify cost of inaction in financial terms
- Create shared vision all stakeholders can see themselves in

## 6. Political Mapping

- Job titles do not reflect actual power
- Map the decision architecture: which committee reviews? who has veto at each stage?
- Track faction dynamics: who aligned with whom? competing budget priorities?
- Score each stakeholder on -5 to +5 attitude scale

**Red flags:** Reorgs mid-deal, new executive hires (bring preferred vendors), sudden silence from previously engaged contacts.

---

## 7. Multi-Threading

**Single-threaded deals: 5% win rate. Multi-threaded (5+ contacts): 30%.** 6x improvement. Yet 78% of reps are single-threaded.

**Target by deal size:**
- $50K+ ACV: 5-7 contacts across 3+ roles
- $200K+ ACV: 7-10 contacts across functions

**How to expand:**
- Ask champion for introductions
- Offer unique value to each new contact
- Use events/workshops as natural multi-threading vehicles
- Reduces deal risk by 60-70%

## 8. Procurement Dynamics

**What procurement actually cares about (not features):**

| Priority | Why |
|---|---|
| Risk mitigation | Financial stability, cybersecurity, supply chain |
| Compliance | SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, industry-specific |
| Total Cost of Ownership | Not unit price -- lifetime costs |
| Vendor viability | Will you exist in 3 years? |
| ESG/Sustainability | Increasingly weighted in scoring |

## 9. The Dark Funnel

73% of B2B buying journey happens anonymously before vendor contact. Buyers consume 20-30 content pieces across review sites, peer communities, podcasts, analyst reports.

81% have already decided on their vendor by the time they talk to you.

**Implication:** Presale intelligence must include understanding what they found during anonymous research -- reviews, analyst ratings, peer sentiment.
