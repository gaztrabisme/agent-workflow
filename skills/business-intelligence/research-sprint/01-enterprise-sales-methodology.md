# Enterprise Sales Methodology Research for Presale Intelligence Skill

## 1. Deal Qualification Frameworks

### MEDDPICC (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Implicate the Pain, Champion, Competition)

The gold standard for enterprise B2B deals above $50K ACV. Created at PTC in 1996 where it helped grow revenue from $300M to $1B in four years. 73% of SaaS companies selling above $100K ARR use some variant. Teams adopting it report 18% higher win rates, 24% larger deals, and forecast accuracy within 10% of actual vs. 25-40% variance without it. The critical evolution from MEDDIC to MEDDPICC added Paper Process (procurement/legal/security review kills deals as often as feature gaps) and Competition (including "do nothing" -- the most dangerous competitor because it requires zero budget and zero risk). The key distinction: "Implicate the Pain" vs. merely "Identify the Pain" -- sellers must quantify cost of inaction, not just surface problems. Research from Ebsta/Pavilion shows early decision-maker involvement boosts win rates by 55%.

**Presale intelligence application:** Score every deal against all 8 elements. Missing elements = deal risk. Unknown Economic Buyer or weak Champion = immediate red flags. Paper Process intelligence (procurement timelines, security requirements, legal preferences) prevents late-stage death.

### BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline)

The simplest framework. Works as an early-stage filter for deal fundamentals before deeper qualification. Weakness: budget-first approach is backwards for modern enterprise buying where budget follows problem identification. Best used as a quick screen, then layer MEDDPICC for serious deals.

### SCOTSMAN (Solution, Competition, Originality, Timescales, Size, Money, Authority, Need)

Eight components specifically designed for complex enterprise B2B. Unique "Originality" component forces reps to articulate genuine differentiation per-deal, not generic pitch-deck claims. Follows "win fast, lose fast" principle. Best for deals above 50K EUR ACV. Downside: complex to execute in live conversations.

### CHAMP (Challenges, Authority, Money, Prioritization)

BANT flipped: leads with challenges (buyer's pain) instead of budget. More customer-centric, qualifies leads IN rather than OUT. Good for mid-market consultative sales. Lacks competitive analysis component.

**Go/No-Go decision matrix across frameworks:** No identified pain = walk away. No access to Economic Buyer = high risk. No champion with internal influence = stall likely. Active competitor with incumbent advantage + no differentiation = column fodder risk. Unknown decision process = cannot forecast.

## 2. Strategic Selling (Miller Heiman)

Founded on win-win principles. Published 1985, trained 1M+ sales professionals. Core artifact is the **Blue Sheet** -- a living one-page deal map capturing stakeholders, roles, response modes, win-results, red flags, competitive position, and action plan. Worth 3-5 hours per opportunity; apply to top 10-20 active deals, not everything.

**Four Buying Influences** (roles, not titles):
- **Economic Buyer:** Can say yes when everyone says no. Controls budget. Typically VP/C-level. Finding them early is the single highest-leverage activity.
- **User Buyer:** Daily user of the solution. May be multiple across teams. Care about workflow impact.
- **Technical Buyer:** Evaluates security, integration, compliance. Cannot say yes but CAN say no. IT/security/procurement.
- **Coach:** Internal advocate with organizational influence who actively supports YOUR solution. Single most important buying influence for deal success. Distinct from a "friendly contact" who lacks real power.

**Win-Results:** Each buying influence needs a personally meaningful win. Example: CFO wins on faster financial close; analyst wins on eliminated manual work; IT Director wins on compliance achievement.

**Red Flags that kill deals:** No access to Economic Buyer; Coach leaves the company; phantom decision-maker surfaces late; budget reallocated mid-cycle; you are "column fodder" (invited to validate a pre-made decision). Gartner data: 6-10 stakeholders average in B2B buying -- miss even one and performance suffers.

**Presale intelligence application:** Map every stakeholder to a buying influence role. Prepare win-results per person. Identify who your Coach is and whether they have real power. Flag any missing roles as deal risks.

## 3. SPIN Selling

Based on 12 years of research, 35,000 sales calls across 23 countries (Neil Rackham, 1988). The core finding: successful reps in complex deals follow a consistent questioning pattern that moves from context to quantified impact.

**Four question types:**
- **Situation:** Facts about current state. Use SPARINGLY (3-5 max). Every situation question you ask is one you could have answered with 10 minutes of research. Excessive situation questions signal inexperience.
- **Problem:** Surface difficulties, dissatisfaction. Lead the buyer to identify their own problems without naming your product.
- **Implication:** The multiplier. Explore ripple effects -- cost of inaction, impact on other departments, strategic consequences. Top performers ask 4x as many implication questions as average reps. This is where urgency is created.
- **Need-Payoff:** Get the buyer to articulate the value of solving the problem in their own words. When they state the benefit, they become their own best advocate.

**What separates great from mediocre discovery:** Great discovery is conversational, not checklist-driven. It spends 60%+ of time on Problem and Implication questions. Mediocre discovery is dominated by Situation questions and races to pitch. In a 30-minute call, 10-15 well-chosen questions is typical.

**Presale intelligence application:** Pre-research eliminates Situation questions, making the rep look prepared and the buyer feel respected. Prepare Implication questions in advance using industry/company data. Arm the seller with financial impact data to fuel Need-Payoff conversations.

## 4. Challenger Sale

CEB/Gartner research across 6,000+ reps. Finding: Challengers make up 40% of top performers overall and 54% in complex B2B. The Relationship Builder profile -- the one most sales managers try to hire -- is the worst performer in complex sales.

**Three pillars:**
- **Teach:** Bring a perspective the buyer hasn't considered. Teach for differentiation, not product education.
- **Tailor:** Adapt the core insight to each stakeholder's role and priorities.
- **Take Control:** Discuss money, push for commitments, challenge the buyer's assumptions.

**Six-Step Commercial Teaching Choreography:** Warmer (credibility through research) -> Reframe (new perspective on a known problem) -> Rational Drowning (data showing magnitude) -> Emotional Impact (stories of similar companies' costs) -> A New Way -> Your Solution. The solution comes LAST, not first. The reframe is the weapon -- it introduces a problem framing that only your solution addresses, marginalizing competitors who are all solving the same stated problem.

Key stat: 53% of customer loyalty depends on the sales experience itself, not brand/product/price.

**Presale intelligence application:** Build "commercial insights" per industry/account -- reframes that challenge the buyer's status quo thinking. The intelligence function's job is to find the angle that makes the client rethink their problem definition, not just confirm what they already believe.

## 5. Solution Selling

Core formula: **Pain x Power x Vision x Value x Control = Sale.** Any zero on the left = zero on the right (no sale).

**Three levels of buyer need:**
- Level 1 -- Latent Pain: Buyer doesn't know they have the problem. Seller must create awareness.
- Level 2 -- Admitted Pain: Buyer acknowledges the problem but isn't acting. Seller must create urgency.
- Level 3 -- Vision of a Solution: Buyer sees a path forward. If you created the vision, support it. If a competitor created it, reengineer it.

**Key principle:** Connect pain to solution without being product-pushy by making the buyer co-author the solution vision. When they help design it, they own it.

**Presale intelligence application:** Map stakeholder pain chains -- who feels what pain, quantified how. Prepare vision statements per stakeholder. Build value justification with the buyer's own numbers from discovery.

## 6. Sales Lifecycle Stages & Intelligence Needs

| Stage | Key Intelligence | What Changes |
|-------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Prospecting** | ICP fit, intent signals, technographics, org structure. Average 62 touches across 3+ channels needed. | Intelligence is broad -- industry trends, company financials, tech stack. Goal: identify fit. |
| **Qualification** | MEDDPICC scoring, budget signals, decision-maker identification. Qualify out 50% of leads in first discovery to protect SE capacity. | Intelligence narrows to deal viability. Economic Buyer identified? Real pain confirmed? |
| **Discovery** | Current workflow, quantified pain, cost of inaction, stakeholder map, decision process. Shallow discovery = generic demo = weak proposal = lost deal. | Intelligence becomes deep and specific. Dollarize impacts. Map buying committee. |
| **Proposal/Demo** | Custom demos per stakeholder group (IT = security, users = features, exec = business value). ROI calculator with buyer's own numbers. Same-day proposal delivery wins. | Intelligence is applied -- pre-built templates customized 20% per deal. |
| **Negotiation** | Procurement timelines, legal preferences, security questionnaire readiness, competitive positioning, stakeholder engagement tracking. | Intelligence shifts to process -- who needs to sign what, what redlines are standard, what concessions competitors offer. |
| **Close** | Contract logistics, onboarding requirements, success criteria. Deal doesn't end at signature -- failed onboarding kills renewal. | Intelligence transitions to delivery -- implementation risk, customer success handoff. |

Median B2B SaaS cycle: 84 days. Enterprise $100K+ ACV: 170 days. $500K+: 270+ days. Buying committees average 6.8 stakeholders (up from 5.4 in 2022).

## 7. Forecasting & Pipeline Management

**Pipeline coverage ratios:** 3-5x for standard B2B. 4-5x for enterprise. 10x for complex enterprise with <10% win rates. If quarterly quota is $500K, keep $1.5-2.5M in pipeline.

**Forecast categories:** Commit (90%+ confidence, verifiable buyer actions completed), Best Case (60-80%, most elements in place but risk remains), Upside (30-50%, early stage or with known blockers). Weighted pipeline = deal value x stage probability.

**Signals a deal is progressing:** Champion actively selling internally, new stakeholders engaging, decision criteria formalized, timeline confirmed, proof of concept scheduled, legal/procurement engaged.

**Signals a deal is stalling:** Declining buyer engagement, meetings rescheduled/cancelled, champion unresponsive, no clear next steps after calls, "do nothing" gaining traction, competitor evaluation expanding, budget review mentioned.

**Pipeline velocity** = (number of deals x avg deal size x win rate) / avg cycle length. Sustained drop in velocity is the earliest warning of a revenue shortfall.

## Cross-Framework Synthesis

**Frameworks that complement each other:**
- **SPIN + MEDDPICC:** SPIN structures discovery conversations; MEDDPICC qualifies the deal that emerges. Most high-performing orgs use both.
- **Challenger + SPIN:** Challenger provides the strategic reframe; SPIN provides the questioning technique to deliver it conversationally.
- **Miller Heiman + MEDDPICC:** Miller Heiman maps stakeholders and buying influences; MEDDPICC validates deal mechanics. Blue Sheet + MEDDPICC scoring = comprehensive deal view.
- **Solution Selling + Challenger:** Solution Selling diagnoses and co-creates the vision; Challenger ensures the vision is insight-led rather than buyer-led.

**Frameworks that compete:**
- BANT vs. MEDDPICC: BANT is a subset. Once you adopt MEDDPICC, BANT becomes redundant except as a quick initial screen.
- Challenger vs. pure Relationship Selling: Directly opposed philosophies. Challenger's research explicitly shows relationship-first approaches underperform in complex sales.

## Junior Presale Person Learning Prioritization

1. **SPIN Selling** -- Learn discovery first. Most deals die because discovery was shallow. Immediately applicable, no tools needed.
2. **MEDDPICC** -- Learn qualification second. Stops you from wasting months on deals you cannot win.
3. **Miller Heiman (Buying Influences)** -- Learn stakeholder mapping third. Enterprise deals have 6-10 stakeholders; missing one kills the deal.
4. **Challenger Sale** -- Learn commercial insight fourth. Requires deep industry knowledge that takes time to build; premature without strong discovery skills.
5. **Solution Selling / Forecasting** -- Learn the pipeline discipline last. These synthesize everything above into deal management.

## Common Mistakes Junior Sellers Make (That Frameworks Prevent)

1. **Over-optimism on pipeline** -- Creating opportunities from vague interest. MEDDPICC prevents this by requiring verifiable evidence at each element.
2. **Shallow discovery dominated by Situation questions** -- SPIN prevents this by structuring the conversation toward Implication and Need-Payoff.
3. **Selling too low / single-threaded** -- Miller Heiman prevents this by requiring all four buying influences to be mapped and engaged.
4. **Product pitching instead of insight-leading** -- Challenger prevents this by teaching reps to reframe the problem before introducing the solution.
5. **Misidentifying the Economic Buyer** -- Miller Heiman's "95% rule": ask "What percentage of similar deals have you signed off on?" If less than 95%, they are not the Economic Buyer.
6. **Ignoring procurement/legal** -- MEDDPICC's Paper Process element forces early engagement with the process that actually kills most late-stage deals.
7. **No clear next steps after calls** -- Every framework emphasizes mutual action plans; the absence of agreed next steps is the clearest signal a deal is stalling.
8. **Failing to quantify pain in dollars** -- Solution Selling's Value element and SPIN's Implication questions both force dollarization. Without it, the champion cannot justify spend internally.
