# Competitive Intelligence (CI) as a Professional Discipline

## 1. CI as a Profession

**SCIP** (Strategic Consortium of Intelligence Professionals) is the world's largest CI association -- 25,000+ members across 120 countries, 35 years old. They offer accredited SIC certification via their SMART training platform.

**What CI professionals actually do:** They don't just gather information -- they transform it into actionable, forward-looking insight that drives decisions. Ben Gilad's core distinction: market research, business intelligence, and market intelligence deliver *information*; competitive intelligence delivers *intelligence* -- a perspective on developments aimed at yielding competitive edge.

**CI vs. Market Research:**

| Dimension | Market Research | Competitive Intelligence |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Customer demand, market trends | Competitor strategies, market positioning |
| Orientation | Reactive, historical | Proactive, predictive |
| Method | Statistics-driven | Triangulation of diverse sources |
| Output | What is happening | What will happen next |

**The CI Cycle** (5 phases, continuous):
1. **Planning/Direction** -- Define the decisions that need support. "Tell me everything about Competitor X" is a bad brief; "What will Competitor X do if we cut price 15%?" is a good one.
2. **Collection** -- Gather from OSINT and HUMINT sources.
3. **Analysis** -- Apply frameworks to turn data into insight.
4. **Dissemination** -- Route intelligence to decision-makers in usable form.
5. **Feedback/Review** -- Evaluate what worked, refine the cycle.

**Two types:** Strategic CI (long-term: market entry, pricing, product roadmaps) and Tactical CI (short-term: sales calls, campaigns, positioning).

**For presale:** Frame every research effort as a Key Intelligence Topic (KIT) -- a specific question tied to a deal decision, not open-ended browsing.

## 2. Collection Methodologies

**OSINT (Open Source Intelligence):** Publicly available information -- SEC filings, job postings, patents, press releases, social media, review sites, conference presentations. Foundation of all CI. Challenge: information overload and credibility. Must cross-reference and verify.

**HUMINT (Human Intelligence):** Information from people -- trade show conversations, customer interviews, industry expert calls, supplier relationships. In CI, HUMINT is done *overtly* (unlike government intelligence). Primary information from human sources creates distinctive advantage because secondary sources are available to everyone.

**The Admiralty Code / NATO System (Source Reliability Framework):**

**Source Reliability (A-F):** A = Completely Reliable ... E = Unreliable, F = Cannot Be Judged

**Information Credibility (1-6):** 1 = Confirmed by independent sources ... 5 = Improbable, 6 = Cannot Be Judged

Example: **B2** = Usually reliable source, probably true information. **F6** = Unknown source, unverifiable info. Key principle: the two axes are assessed independently so source reputation doesn't bias content evaluation.

**Practical application for presale:** Tag every piece of competitor intelligence with a confidence rating. "Their sales VP told a customer they're launching Q3" (B2) is very different from "A Reddit commenter said they heard a rumor" (E4).

## 3. Ethical Boundaries

**SCIP Code of Ethics:**
- Always disclose your identity and organization before interviews
- Respect all confidentiality requests
- Comply with all applicable laws
- Promote the code within your company and with contractors

**Legal/Ethical CI:** Analyzing public filings, monitoring websites, attending public conferences, interviewing willing participants with disclosed identity, reverse-engineering publicly available products.

**Corporate espionage (illegal):** Theft of trade secrets (Economic Espionage Act), unauthorized computer access (CFAA), misrepresentation to obtain confidential info, bribing employees, accessing competitor property without permission.

**The Front Page Test:** Would you be comfortable if your collection method were reported on the front page of a major newspaper? If not, don't do it.

## 4. Analysis Frameworks Beyond Porter

**The Fleisher-Bensoussan toolkit:** 24-48 documented techniques, each evaluated using the FAROUT system (Future orientation, Accuracy, Resource efficiency, Objectivity, Usefulness, Timeliness). The meta-framework that tells you which analytical tool to use for which situation.

Key frameworks beyond Porter:
- **Blind Spot Analysis** (Gilad) -- What are you assuming that competitors won't do?
- **Scenario Planning** -- 3-4 plausible futures with strategies for each. Used when uncertainty is high.
- **Perceptual Mapping** -- How buyers perceive competitors across key attributes. Reveals positioning gaps.
- **3C Analysis** (Company, Competitors, Customers) -- Forces integration of all three lenses.
- **SWOT done properly** -- CI-grade SWOT is validated with evidence, weighted by impact, and cross-referenced against competitor SWOTs to identify asymmetries.

## 5. War Gaming

A structured exercise where cross-functional teams role-play your company, key competitors, and external stakeholders through realistic market "rounds." Goal: anticipate rival responses before committing resources.

**Two types:**
- **Landscape games** -- unmasking ambiguous market changes early
- **Test games** -- testing a developed strategy against likely competitor responses

**How to run one:**
1. Create team briefs -- objectives, constraints, historic response patterns, red lines
2. Run 2-4 rounds, each representing a time step
3. Prepare 3-6 injects (surprise events)
4. Adjudicate and debrief

**Outputs:** If-then playbooks, triggers and signposts, cross-functional alignment.

**War gaming for presale specifically:** Simulate what the competitor will say about you in the client's evaluation. Assign someone to play the competitor's sales team -- what claims will they make? What FUD will they spread? What demo will they show? Then prepare counter-positioning and proof points. This is the single most underused presale technique.

## 6. Win/Loss Analysis

60% of sellers are partially or completely wrong about why they lost a deal (Anova data).

**How to conduct interviews:**
1. Use a neutral third party (50-60% response rate vs. much lower for internal)
2. Interview decision-makers within 1 month of the decision
3. Start with broad, open-ended questions
4. Structure around: solution requirements, pricing, competitive landscape, sales experience
5. Be adaptive and conversational, not survey-like
6. Target 30-45 minutes per interview
7. Need minimum 10 similar deals to identify reliable patterns

**Key questions:** How the deal originated, what triggered the evaluation, who was involved, what criteria mattered most, how vendors were compared, what the competitor did well, what the sales experience was like, what would have changed the outcome.

**ROI:** Organizations running consistent win/loss programs for 2+ years report an average 10-20% lift in win rate.

## 7. Competitive Battle Cards

One-page cheat sheets that arm sales reps with positioning, objections handling, and differentiation against specific competitors. 57% of all B2B sales deals are competitive; 44% of buyers arrive having already identified possible solutions.

**Types:**
- **Discovery/Early-Stage** -- probing questions to surface pain points, frame "us vs. them" early
- **Late-Stage/Depositioning** -- specific talk tracks to counter competitor strengths
- **Product/Feature Comparison** -- technical deep-dive with roadmap context
- **Competitor Overview** -- high-level snapshot for executive conversations

**Content that matters:** Ready-to-go differentiators with proof points, specific objection responses (not generic), landmines to plant early, questions that expose competitor weaknesses, customer quotes/stories.

**Maintenance:** Must be living documents. Refresh after competitor launches, pricing changes, positioning shifts, or meaningful losses. Track usage as a KPI.

## 8. Signal Intelligence / Early Warning

**Five key signal categories:** Hiring signals, funding/investment, technology/patents, customer sentiment, market structure changes.

**Patent filings** are among the most reliable leading indicators -- they reflect actual R&D investment. Patents publish 18 months after priority date, revealing decisions made ~2 years prior.

**Job postings** signal strategy shifts before they're announced. A competitor suddenly hiring ML engineers or enterprise sales reps reveals their roadmap.

**Intent data vs. signals:** Intent data reflects research behavior. Signals reflect real-world changes. Signal-based triggers see 4x higher conversion than generic outreach. But: if everyone uses the same intent data vendor, no competitive advantage.

**For presale:** Monitor the target client's signals too -- executive changes, new funding, earnings call language shifts, job postings suggesting technology evaluation. These are triggers for timely, relevant outreach.

## The Core Distinction: Research vs. Intelligence

| | Research | Intelligence |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Find a specific answer | Predict what happens next |
| **Scope** | Narrow, one-time | Broad, continuous, systematic |
| **Sources** | One or two | Triangulated across many |
| **Analysis** | Surface-level | Pattern recognition, framework-driven |
| **Output** | A fact | An actionable recommendation |
| **Orientation** | Backward-looking | Forward-looking |

The litmus test: if it doesn't change a decision, it wasn't intelligence -- it was just information.
