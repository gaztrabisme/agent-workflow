## Naturalistic color: the solar-system route "UPV in Space"

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L42 · 00:38–01:46

The teachers begin with the denotative function of color, with naturalistic color. For this project, designing educational, informational and promotional materials for the event, they laid out a route through explanatory panels on the characteristics of each planet in the solar system. The route starts with Neptune, at the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao, and ends with the Sun, at the Vizcaya Aretoa in Bilbao; the panels are spaced at real scale relative to the solar system's actual measurements, so visitors can physically experience its true dimension.

They understood immediately that color here had to be denotative — fully descriptive — because the intention was a realistic treatment of color, describing the planets' appearance as faithfully as possible, as they truly are. They asked the university's own solar-system experts for high-resolution photographs of every planet, then vectorised them in Illustrator and rendered them in the most naturalistic color possible.

## Expressionist color: the door metaphor on "Book Cover Design"

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L42 · 02:35–03:41

Expressionist color appears on the cover of the book *Diseño de cubiertas de libros: recursos de retórica visual* (*Book Cover Design: Visual Rhetoric Resources*), which gathers a rigorous compilation of more than 650 book covers selected from the international professional editorial-design scene, classified by the rhetorical devices they use.

The cover aims to be significant of the content it wraps, so the designers drew on the metaphor of a cover as a door opening. The brown color of the text is the iconic color of a wooden door. They also wanted to create a somewhat enigmatic atmosphere through the color treatment: the dark, black silhouette of a person is meant, on one hand, to represent a genderless figure, and at the same time to express curiosity, intrigue and suspense about what one hopes to find behind that door — which they hope readers are willing to cross.

## Fanciful color: "UPV at Sea" and a 19th-century dictionary

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L42 · 03:41–05:08

To close the denotative function, the teachers show a project using color in its fanciful (*fantasiosa*) dimension. Like the UPV-in-Space project, this one also produces educational and promotional materials, here for the event "UPV at Sea." The materials advertise a series of activities — exhibitions, a scavenger hunt (*gincana*), guided visits, talks — meant to spark discovery of marine life (captions: *descubrimiento de la vida de la persona*): an interactive, visual introduction to marine biology, the consequences of pollution on ocean life, marine renewable energy, and navigation.

As a graphic device for evoking interest in this knowledge and the need to spread it publicly, the project drew on scientific illustrations from the book *Diccionario de palabras y de cosas* (*Dictionary of Words and Things*) by Larive and Fleury, published in 1895. The colors were used in a completely fanciful way, independent of the real colors these figures might have. Against a dark-blue background standing for the sea, the various marine motifs are laid out in saturated, autonomous, schematic colors that depart entirely from natural ones. The intention was a very colorful graphic system that draws a lot of attention against that dark-blue base.

## Psychological color: the UPV/EHU summer-courses sun poster

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L42 · 05:08–06:31

Whatever the intent or communicative aim behind a color choice, colors always generate some psychological meaning — as in this poster designed to announce the UPV/EHU summer courses held in Donostia–San Sebastián. In this poster and its adaptations to other supports (MUPI street furniture, banners, activity programmes), the colors used are iconic and noticeably exalted, aiming to convey psychological meanings of quality and vitality; in this communicative situation, color psychologically generates sensations.

The poster is built around an expressive rendering of the sun as an allegory of summer, and of the warm, vital atmosphere it creates by illuminating and giving energy — the sun as an expression of creative capacity, growth and enthusiasm. It is rendered through a graphic device of shape and color that tries to provoke optical illusions of irradiation, of attraction toward a central meeting point, and of movement: illusions that make people perceive reality differently, part of a graphic strategy of fascination meant to capture the viewer's eye and surprise the gaze with its optical and connotative effects.

## Symbolic color: the "Ciencia Astea" shell-spiral identity

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L42 · 06:31–08:26

At the symbolic level, the teachers highlight the visual identity for the International Science and Technology Week, called "Zientzia Astea" in the Basque Country — one of the ways the university brings the reality of science, technology and innovation closer to the public. They proposed that recognising how nature behaves is the best scientific lesson one can teach in order to bring innovation to human needs: science and technology are often inspired by nature itself, by looking for what is common within an ecosystem and adapting it to a new context. So it seemed fitting to draw on nature for the Ciencia Astea identity — nature as the guiding principle of science and technology.

To express this, they focused on the spiral typology, drawing specifically on the spiral shape of a seashell, which embodies a principle of vital development and growth in nature. The shell is rendered through straight lines forming square shapes, producing a fully geometric sign; this geometric configuration also connotes technology, rationality and industry, evoking the artificiality and precision of objects mechanically moulded by people. At the same time, the color is meant to express something else: the different colors used allude directly to the science whose central figure was Isaac Newton, who in the 17th century discovered the spectral nature of light.

## Schematic color: the traveling exhibition of science quotes

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L42 · 08:26–09:40

Within the sign function, the teachers have used schematic color in many projects; this is one of them — a traveling exhibition of typographic design. Through 50 typographic pieces, it lays out a path of visual puzzles that challenge visitors to discover a selection of famous quotes about science: ideas from scientific figures meant to make readers reflect and identify with them, particular theories built from personal experience that can, at times, help solve problems from a different point of view. It is a landscape of word-images about science across panels and vinyls, meant to catch the eye through the aesthetic treatment of the letters and their colors. Color is applied sometimes for contrast, sometimes for harmony, or to make some elements stand out against others; whatever the aim in each piece, in all of them the color is autonomous, abstract, sign color.

## Signal color: the smoke-free campus sign

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L42 · 09:40–10:47

For signal color, the teachers highlight a small sign carrying the slogan "piensa sin humo" (think smoke-free). The aim was an informational sign to help students, teaching staff, and administration and services personnel give up smoking on campus for good. From the start they wanted a motivating image that also referred to the academic context, so they replaced a cigarette with a pencil held between two victory-sign fingers — a metaphor for a learning tool, a pencil, standing in for smoke. The victory symbol represents a positive outcome achievable with success: a smoke-free environment. Because color here could not speak of prohibition, the sign's frame uses the blue chromatic code of a standardised information, notice or recommendation sign — the same code used by urban traffic-management signage.

### Key points (derived)

- Denotative function: naturalistic color (solar-system panels, faithful planet photography), exalted color (Lego Serious Play posters, iconic brick colors), expressionist color (book-cover door metaphor, brown door / black silhouette), fanciful color (UPV-at-Sea posters, saturated colors detached from real ones).
- Connotative function: psychological color (summer-courses sun poster, vitality and optical fascination), symbolic color (Ciencia Astea shell spiral, nature and Newton's spectrum).
- Sign (sígnica) function: schematic color (typography exhibition, autonomous sign color), signal color (smoke-free sign, blue informational code instead of red prohibition), emblematic color (Japan book cover, red as national color).
