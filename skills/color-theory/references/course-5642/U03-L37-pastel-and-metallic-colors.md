## Pastel colors: a definition

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L37 · 00:08–01:15

In this lesson, to finish with the connotative function of color, the teachers reflect on some psychological and symbolic aspects attributed to pastel, gold and silver colors.

Pastel colors are a family of colors that have a low-to-medium saturation. They generally imply a soft matte finish, as well as pale, lighter, less saturated colors. Vivid colors could be considered a kind of opposite to pastel colors. Pastel colors are considered those that promote a sensation of being lighter; they are achieved by adding some white to a hue, and in a color-ordering system they can be located in the part of the different color ranges closest to white. The most common pastel colors have names such as lilac, light pink, sky blue, aquamarine, sea green, salmon, mint green, mauve, peach, off-white.

## A pictorial technique, and a look for interiors

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L37 · 01:15–01:40

Pastel as a pictorial technique, based on dry chalk applied directly to the working surface without using any kind of solvent, is mentioned for the first time by Leonardo da Vinci in 1895. But it reaches its greatest splendor in the hands of Impressionist masters such as Degas, Cassatt and Puyart, whose pastel-colored works you can see here.

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L37 · 01:40–02:13

## Kopi Luwak: pink for the world's most unusual coffee

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L37 · 07:12–08:24

And to finish with this range of colors: the use of pastel colors can be an extraordinary resource for considering bold design options. This is the case of this project by Pati Núñez for Kopi Luwak, probably the most unusual coffee in the world. This coffee's beans are extracted from the animal's excrement; the enzymes in its stomach give it a unique, rich and intense aroma, with notes of caramel and chocolate. ## Silver as a cold color

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L37 · 16:09–17:01

And to finish: the color silver is close to white, blue and grey, which are cold colors — which makes it itself a cold color. Snow has silvery reflections, which is another reason we experience the color silver as associated with cold. Frozen foods are generally packaged in aluminium containers, because the color silver reflects heat and helps preserve them. That is why it appears associated with products that must be drunk very cold, such as vodka.

### Key points (derived)

- Pastel colors (low-to-medium saturation, made by adding white) read as light, soft and calming, which is why they dominate interiors and are traditionally coded feminine, childlike and maternal — and why they turn up in feminine hygiene (Perl Fischer), baby products (Lavernia en Cienfuegos) and dermo-cosmetics (Beatific).
- The same softness reads as elegant and sophisticated when applied to leisure, culture and hospitality brands (Liseberg, La Menorquina, Opéra de Saint-Étienne, Helsinki Food Company), and as youthful warmth for a dating app (Shakn).
- A brand consultancy (Saffron) can use pastel deliberately as a foil that makes black read as bold, and a bakery or pastry brand can lean into the literal pun between "pastel" (pastry) and "pastel" (the color family).
- Pastel can also be a deliberate shock tactic for a genuinely unconventional, high-priced product (Kopi Luwak coffee), trading on pink's 19th-century history as the color of exotic imported tea and coffee.
- Gold reads as eternal, divine and triumphant: it is the color of trophies and awards, of high-end goods across unrelated categories (cars, chocolate, watches, beer, perfume, champagne, cosmetics), and — in the Dubai Expo 2020 and Emirates identities — of a whole culture's historical relationship with the metal itself.
- Gold is also simply a device for identifying "this is the premium/expensive one" — the Australian beer-conference poster, Mauritshuis and Chivas all use gold as much for its label-legibility as for its symbolism.
- Silver is gold's permanent runner-up (second medal, 25th anniversary vs. 50th), but it carries its own distinct connotations: materiality (it identifies transaction media like bank cards), and — via its association with metal — hardness, reliability, precision and industry, which is why car brands use it for both trustworthiness (Outokumpu, generic reliability) and desirability (Volvo, Jaguar, and other gleaming redesigns).
- Silver is also read as a cold color, close to white, blue and grey, reinforced by its resemblance to snow and its real thermal reflectivity in packaging — hence its use for products served ice-cold, like vodka.
