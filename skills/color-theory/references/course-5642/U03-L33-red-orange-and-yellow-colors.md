### Fire, food and fuel

"Red" covers a whole family of similar colorings — reddish tones that vary in luminosity and saturation and carry many different names. Red has more terms for its shades than any other color: crimson, vermilion, fire-red, garnet (*granate*), sanguine (*sanguino*), colorado, scarlet, purple-red (*púrpura*) and tawny (*leonado*), among others. These different shades of red can mean different things.

![5642 · U3 · L33 · 00:53](frames/U03L33-F0053.jpg)

On screen: "CAVIMEX"; "Stevenson"; "Sweet"; "telepizza"; "Pizza Hut" [rest omitted].  
Shows: Preview collage of red examples to come — five red swatches, sweets, a Texaco star and a red-diamond logo, a red-tinted wedding photograph, Bram Stoker's "Drácula" (Penguin Classics), a wine bottle, an energy-shot can, perfume bottles, Stevenson's "El diablo de la botella," and the Telepizza/Pizza Hut logos.

Red, together with orange and yellow, is found in fire, which is why all three are considered warm colors. Red is the color of fire, which we associate with warmth or energy — for example in graphic brands linked to food, with a possible evocation of the comforting warmth of a meal.

Like fire, like the sun, like the spark that ignites an engine, it is the color of energy — fuel corporations, gas stations and oil companies use it in their identity brands.

### Attraction, love and passion

Red is a color barely present in nature, and when it does appear it usually serves an attraction function. Frigate birds develop huge red throat pouches during mating season — a warning signal to other males, a kind of red traffic light, and at the same time a call to attract females.

![5642 · U3 · L33 · 04:32](frames/U03L33-F0432.jpg)

On screen: [rest omitted].  
Shows: A male frigate bird displaying its inflated red throat pouch during courtship.

In Renaissance painting, red was used to draw the viewer's attention, often as the color of clothing for the figures meant to stand out.

![5642 · U3 · L33 · 04:41](frames/U03L33-F0441.jpg)

On screen: [rest omitted].  
Shows: Three Renaissance paintings (Botticelli's "Primavera" and two Raphael Madonnas) in which the central figures wear red garments.

In our cultural context, red can be an attractive, tempting, dangerous color, evocative of the forbidden. Its symbolic roots associate it with prohibition and sin, but also with desire and temptation — red can be said to describe the two sides of love: the divine, and the sin of the flesh.

Red therefore can carry different connotations, and its use in perfume packaging is no exception. Generally, the meaning attributed to red depends on the shade used and the color combinations: the purest, brightest reds can connote love, sexiness, sweetness, vitality, energy, attraction and stimulation, while the darker reds can connote a more forbidden, risky, dangerous, powerful, violent, energetic or ambitious passion — in short, a more fatal attraction.

### Optimism and friendliness

Psychologically, orange is associated with fun, celebration, happiness, sociability and cordiality — an eminently optimistic, cheerful and welcoming color. It is the complementary, opposite color to blue: if blue is spiritual, calm and reflective, orange is its polar opposite.

## Yellow — psychological and symbolic meanings

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L33 · 11:31–18:00

### Happiness and the smiley

![5642 · U3 · L33 · 11:31](frames/U03L33-F1131.jpg)

On screen: "_COLOR AMARILLO" ("_YELLOW COLOR"); KAS Limón, pg Personal Group, "hate crime," Nikon, Nuts.com, Post-it, GOODYEAR, LA·MOVIDA [rest omitted].  
Shows: The yellow section's title card over a preview collage of yellow products, brands and book covers.

Yellow is another primary color that cannot be created by mixing. Regardless of its tone, it is universally considered a cheerful color representing happiness, sunlight, optimism and creativity. Like red and orange it is a warm, "burning" color — but in this case more luminous and expansive.

A project that shows this suggestion of yellow's cheerfulness is this visual identity for Personal Group, a financial company that manages employee benefits, created by the London consultancy Song Wan. Smile and joy are the essential factor of its identity; the iconic smile is the recurring graphic element, carrying over into the theme of its photographs. As for color, they use a cheerful, lively yellow — a color that is light, that is joy, and which in this corporate context comes across as relaxed and casual.

![5642 · U3 · L33 · 12:30](frames/U03L33-F1230.jpg)

On screen: "pg Personal Group" (with a smiley formed by the lowercase "g"); "Work happy"; "dunk" / "hapi" (on two mugs) [rest omitted].  
Shows: Personal Group's branding — logo, brochure, tote bag, and yellow product photos (stapler, USB cable, smoke detector, headphones) each arranged or printed to suggest a smiling face.

In this next project, yellow is used with a clear communicative intention of optimism, as the signal color itself. It is the color selected by Landor to keep updating and signaling the number of lives protected by the French NGO Yantoli, which tries to detect diseases such as malaria or diarrhea early, before people die. The updated count of lives saved always appears in the logo in intense yellow, which also shows part of the digit that follows the final number, emphasizing the idea of a counter and an optimistic sense of growth.

The London agency Rugged Edge built a graphic concept around treating scooter use as a superpower for moving through cities: getting around cities today is frustrating and slow, whereas riding a scooter is stimulating and fun. The project aims to turn users into superheroes and daily commutes into pure happiness.

Observing Americans' exclusive pursuit of happiness, its author argues for melancholy as an essential factor for a prosperous culture, for driving creative thought and as an engine of innovation. ### Nobility, packaging and warning

However, in other cultural contexts the view of yellow is very different. In China, for example, it has always been the color of greatest nobility; yellow was the color of the imperial family, and its use was forbidden to others except in Buddhist places, where it represented nobility and elevation. For the Chinese, yellow symbolizes earth, harvest, gold, wealth and warmth, and evokes clarity, hope, nobility, joy, prosperity and brilliance.

### Key points (derived)

- Red, orange and yellow are the three warm colors, all linked psychologically to fire, warmth and energy.
- Red: food and fast-food branding (appetite/warmth), fuel and energy-company branding, evokes blood/hell/the devil when paired with black, has an attraction function in nature (frigate birds) and Renaissance painting, connotes love/sex/temptation, and signals vitality, speed, action, struggle and revolution.
- Orange: fire/energy branding, danger/risk association, but also optimism, fun, sociability and warmth (the opposite of blue); it is the color of Buddhist monastic robes, and is used as a warning color for its high visibility.
- Yellow: happiness, sunlight, optimism and creativity (the smiley face); but also a color of historical distrust and exclusion (Judas, Cain, the Nazi-era star badge, swindlers/spies); in China it is the color of nobility; in nature and signage, yellow-and-black is a universal warning combination.
- The meaning attached to a color shade often depends on its exact tone and the colors it is combined with (e.g. bright vs. dark reds; red+black for hell/blood; yellow+black for danger).
