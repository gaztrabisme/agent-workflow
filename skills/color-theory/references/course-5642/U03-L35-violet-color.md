## A mixed color: femininity, elegance and beauty

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L35 · 00:07–01:08

Violet is a mixed color, made by mixing red and blue. It is this mixed quality that gives violet different connotations, such as sensuality and spirituality, and it is the union of these connotations that determines the symbolism of the color violet and everything it represents.

Violet is generally an uncommon color in nature. When it does occur, it is usually in the form of a flower, which is why in the West it is traditionally a color associated with the feminine, the sensual, the elegant. That is why designers turn to it for presenting perfumes and fragrances, or for representing beauty-related products and services in general.

## The color of gender equality

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L35 · 01:08–01:26

Violet is the color that symbolizes the fight for gender equality. The reason this became the color chosen as the emblem of the feminist struggle can be explained by the idea that purple is the result of uniting two colors, blue and pink, which symbolize the masculine and the feminine respectively.

## Rare in nature: magic, fantasy and the cosmos

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L35 · 01:26–02:19

In a way it is the color of the different, of the uncommon, of the extraordinary — a color normally used to communicate or represent the cosmos, which has always carried a certain magical aura. ### Key points (derived)

- Violet is a mixed color (red + blue), which is why it carries mixed connotations: sensuality and spirituality.
- Rare in nature (mostly seen in flowers), it is traditionally linked in the West to the feminine, the sensual and the elegant — hence its use for perfumes and beauty products (e.g. the Bernian Cienfuegos perfume for Francis Montesinos).
- It is the emblematic color of the fight for gender equality, explained through the idea of blue (masculine) plus pink (feminine) making purple.
- Its rarity in nature also makes it the color of the extraordinary, the magical and the cosmic — used for fantasy, tarot, wizard (Merlin) and witchcraft themes, and for the Geniova "magic aligner" brand.
- It is used to make a product or brand stand out from its sector by sheer contrast, independent of any other meaning (Hawaiian Airlines' exotic identity; the many unrelated logos that share the color).
- Milka has used lilac since 1890 specifically to differ from other chocolate brands, and it has since become linked with exclusivity, sophistication and luxury; the Willy Wonka Candy Company brand draws on the same associations via its film.
- It carries long-standing spiritual and religious connotations — clergy and penitents' vestments, and possibly the deliberate violet tone of Notre-Dame's south rose window — and is widely used by yoga and meditation brands for the same reason.
- Historically, the high cost of producing violet dye made it the color of power (as in Byzantine imperial dress); this legacy persists today in violet's association with luxury.
