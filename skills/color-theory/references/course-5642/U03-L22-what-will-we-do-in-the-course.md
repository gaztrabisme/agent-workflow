## Two project options

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L22 · 00:00–00:46

In this lesson the teachers explain the steps to follow for the final project. You are offered a choice between two options. As a first option, you can work with color in a graphic project of your own: a poster, a book cover, packaging, a graphic identity mark, or whatever interests you most.

As a second option, you can present a color alternative to a graphic project — yours or another designer's — as long as you justify why the color change or new chromatic alternative is needed.

## Presentation criteria

Anchor: 5642 · U3 · L22 · 00:46–01:03

Whichever option you choose, you must explain in detail the color application criteria used: what function the color plays, which chromatic variant was selected, and what meaning you intend to communicate.

### Key points (derived)

- The final project has two tracks: build a color scheme for your own graphic project, or propose and justify a color alternative for an existing one (yours or another designer's).
- Either way, present the criteria behind your choice: the function color performs, the chromatic variant used, and the meaning it is meant to communicate.
- The teachers frame their own case studies (identity, posters, editorial graphics) using a denotative (*denotativa*) / connotative (*connotativa*) / sign-based (*sígnica*) classification. In the next lesson, Eduardo opens with some preliminary reflections on color in graphic design.
