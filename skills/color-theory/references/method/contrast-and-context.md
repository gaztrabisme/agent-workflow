# Contrast and context: every color is judged against its neighbours

Reader: an agent deciding how strong a color will look, or planning a sequence of images. Sources: [L05](../course/U02-L05-create-stories-with-color-and-contrasts.md), [L17](../course/U04-L17-the-secret-ingredients-temperature-and-saturation.md).

## Perception is relative

- **Contrast** is the difference between one thing and another. For values, it is a tone light enough to stand out against a dark ground. (1140 · U2 · L05 · 00:28–01:03)
- **Lightness depends on context.** A lavender-field photo looks light on its own, then dull after a brighter tulip image, then very bright again after a night street. The image never changed. (1140 · U2 · L05 · 01:03–03:13, 1140 · U2 · L05 · 03:13–03:37)
- **Colorfulness works the same way.** A colorful sunset looks washed out after neon streets and color-powder crowds, and colorful again after near-grey images. (1140 · U2 · L05 · 03:37–05:09)
- **Temperature and saturation are relative too.** The same teal swatch looks warmer, sadder or colder depending on the landscape around it. "Place it and adjust it until it gives the sensation you want." (1140 · U4 · L17 · 09:13–10:19)

## Making one color stronger without changing it

Remove that color from its surroundings. A sunset's red looks redder on a grey ground than on a red one. (1140 · U2 · L05 · 05:09–05:39)

This is the mechanism behind most focal-color decisions in [palettes.md](palettes.md) and [temperature-saturation.md](temperature-saturation.md).

## The eye supplies the missing counterpart

- A grey square inside a large teal field looks pinkish. Inside a large red field it looks blue-violet. The eye fills up with one color and invents its opposite. The teacher calls this **retinal persistence**. (1140 · U4 · L17 · 08:00–09:13)
- **Practical consequence:** grey or desaturated colors placed inside a strongly warm or saturated context read as cool. This creates temperature contrast without cool pigment. (1140 · U4 · L17 · 12:11–13:10)

## Color script: contrast across a sequence

- A **color script** plans light and color across a sequence of images (a film, a comic, a campaign). Each image is set up by the ones before and after it. (1140 · U2 · L05 · 06:26–07:51)
- Build light, colorful passages so that a dark or desaturated one lands as a surprise. The saturated reds of Minions' villain sequence, in Clement Griselain's art, work because duller frames precede them. (1140 · U2 · L05 · 06:26–07:51)
- **Local color can drive a sequence instead of light.** Wes Anderson's The Grand Budapest Hotel gets its pink-and-purple palette, and its figure-ground contrast, from the colors of objects and costumes, such as purple against ochre. (1140 · U2 · L05 · 07:51–09:18)

## Uses in the method

- **Stage 1 (intent):** for a series or a brand system, plan a color script before planning individual images.
- **Stage 4 (color plan):** decide the focal color by what surrounds it, not by the swatch alone.
- **Review:** judge colors in place. Never approve a palette from isolated swatches.

Practice the teacher sets:
- Write a short scene with an evolution of light.
- Build a 5-image color script from film stills.
- Reorder the same stills to tell two other stories.
