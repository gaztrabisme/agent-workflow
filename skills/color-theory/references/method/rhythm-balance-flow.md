# Rhythm, balance and flow: color as a compositional force

Reader: an agent placing and distributing colors across an image once the palette exists. Sources: [L11](../course/U03-L11-rhythm-and-balance.md), [L13](../course/U03-L13-history-the-perfect-pretext-to-add-color.md), [L08](../course/U03-L08-success-and-failure-of-an-image.md).

## Rhythm: sound and silence

- **Visual rhythm has two parts:**
  - **sound**: shapes, which are positive space;
  - **silence**: voids, which are negative space.

  (1140 · U3 · L11 · 01:01–02:40)
- **Even spacing is dull.** A steady base beat with varied notes on top is more interesting. (1140 · U3 · L11 · 01:01–02:40)
- **In the working sketches**, a main rhythm of large elements carries micro-rhythms of small glowing balls on top. (1140 · U3 · L11 · 02:40–03:45)
- **In the forest example**, the trunks sit at uneven distances, with a big silence at the center. (1140 · U3 · L11 · 07:09–07:30)
- **Applied to the course image**:
  - trunks are added in clusters and gaps, "like an encephalogram";
  - the main elements follow big note, silence, smaller note, silence.

  (1140 · U3 · L11 · 07:30–08:29)
- **Color follows the same rhythm.** "Don't forget that color must follow the same rhythm." (1140 · U3 · L11 · 13:55–15:01)

## Balance: counterweights

- The eye expects balance. An excess on one side needs a counterpart, or the image feels like it will tip over, like a bus with everyone on one side. (1140 · U3 · L11 · 03:45–04:32)
- **Color carries balance, not only shape:**

| Example | What holds the image | What happens when the color is removed | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Klaus hill | A cyan triangle against diagonal forces | Desaturating and darkening the cyans makes the image "seep through" | 1140 · U3 · L11 · 04:32–06:00 |
| Snowy forest | A red-orange glow as the gravity point where forces converge | Desaturating it toward purple collapses the balance | 1140 · U3 · L11 · 06:00–07:09 |

This is rarely taught: "we generally only talk about shape, but color has this function too." (1140 · U3 · L11 · 06:00–07:09)

## Flow: color families as paths

- **One color, used nowhere else, can form a path.** All the flowers take one purple that appears nowhere else, so the eye travels between them. (1140 · U3 · L08 · 06:31–09:58)
- **Direction elements can add movement without adding contrast.** Circles that shrink and bunch together push the eye to the right. They are painted in the area's own yellow, so they add rhythm without new contrast. (1140 · U3 · L11 · 08:29–09:37)
- **A color family can form a curve.** In the course image, near-grey pinks, fuchsias and a slightly redder tone read as "one family" and lead to the unique pink of the focal flower. (1140 · U3 · L11 · 09:37–10:39)
- **A reference image uses two families of lines.** An illustrated shopfront has two lines, one of oranges and one of blues, running through bicycles, crates, a T-shirt, a window and the roof. The oranges reappear redder and darker, the blues greener or desaturated. The image is globally grey and very desaturated overall, so these lines carry the eye. (1140 · U3 · L13 · 09:09–11:54)
- **Story objects spread color.**
  - An ice cream cone on a truck roof repeats the pinks below, making a triangle of color. (1140 · U3 · L13 · 07:48–08:34)
  - A small blue bird next to Ross Tran's orange-faced character spreads the image's blues and adds shape and color contrast. (1140 · U3 · L13 · 08:34–09:09)
  - A green dinosaur and a plant are chosen for a child's room because green bridges its blue wall and ochres. "We are literally designing the color." (1140 · U3 · L13 · 11:54–13:17)

## Checks

1. Mark the sound and silence rhythm on the image. If the spacing is even everywhere, vary it.
2. Remove, or desaturate, the most contrasting color patch. If the image loses its footing, that patch is a counterweight, so keep it.
3. Trace each color family with a line. Every line should lead toward the focal point, or frame it.

Practice the teacher sets:
- find 25 images that are excellent in balance, rhythm and harmony, and copy them with 3 values;
- add story elements to 2 existing illustrations.
