# Harmony: interaction with clarity, built with bridges

Reader: an agent making a contrasted palette feel unified without losing its structure. Sources: [L09](../course/U03-L09-looking-for-harmony.md), [L10](../course/U03-L10-looking-for-harmony-ii.md), [L18](../course/U04-L18-bonus-solving-common-color-problems.md).

## Definition

**Harmony is interaction with clarity.**
- **Clarity:** it is always clear which area belongs to which color.
- **Interaction:** each area holds a little of the other.

(1140 · U3 · L09 · 01:17–01:59)

The model is the yin-yang: a blue field with a white dot, and a white field with a blue dot.

The same structure shows in values. A Japanese garden reduced to two greys is light below and dark above, with a patch of each inside the other. (1140 · U3 · L09 · 01:59–03:02)

Like two people in a relationship, colors keep their identities, sit in identifiable fields, and still "give each other a little of their colors". (1140 · U3 · L09 · 03:02–03:57)

## Step 1: set up the maximum contrast

- Two opposite hues on the wheel are the color equivalent of black against white. Examples are green against purple and cyan against red. (1140 · U3 · L09 · 03:57–05:00)
- Color is richer than value here. Black and white have only one maximum pair, but any opposite pair around the wheel is a maximum contrast.
- Lay the two colors out as large blocks, one dominating each area, with spots of the other inside it. (1140 · U3 · L10 · 05:33–06:40)

## Step 2: build bridges

A **bridge color** gives two contrasting colors points of contact. There are two ways to make one. (1140 · U3 · L09 · 05:00–06:14, 1140 · U3 · L09 · 06:14–07:00)

| Method | How | Value analogy |
|---|---|---|
| Third hue | Any hue between the two opposites is necessarily less distant from each; for example cyan between blue and yellow | A middle grey between black and white |
| Lower saturation | Desaturated versions of the two colors sit inside the wheel, closer together | The same |

In the course's landscape, purple plus yellow alone is "boring, very literal". Adding a teal, or a muted green, gives dimension and depth. (1140 · U3 · L09 · 07:00–08:37)

**Whites and greys as bridges.** In a portrait, very desaturated whites (yellowish, pinkish, bluish) bridge the skin pink and the yellows. (1140 · U3 · L10 · 01:51–02:46)

## Step 3: spread colors between blocks

- Put a little of each block's color inside its neighbours. (1140 · U3 · L10 · 00:00–01:51)
- **Justify the spread with light:** a red floor tints the fur above it, and a warm ground lights characters' lower halves. See [light.md](light.md).
- **Repeat colors elsewhere.** A green or blue in the focal flower repeats a green or blue from elsewhere in the image. (1140 · U3 · L10 · 08:31–10:21)
- **A worked study of Nathan Fowkes:**
  1. two groups, ochre-green against cyan-blue;
  2. grey bridges;
  3. a little of each group put into the other;
  4. saturated blue birds carried across the green;
  5. a pinkish-red bridge.

  "The same principles at a masterly level." (1140 · U3 · L10 · 03:19–05:33)

## Step 4: add colors without betraying the structure

- **Add bridge colors in shapes that follow the flow toward the focal point.** Keep the original contrast, such as a large yellow area against purple shapes, fully readable. (1140 · U3 · L10 · 06:40–08:31)
- **Give the focal element a unique color, used nowhere else.**
  - Build its bridge on "the other side" of the wheel.
  - Take that route once only.
  - Concentrate the most color variety inside the focal element.

  (1140 · U3 · L10 · 08:31–10:21)
- **Check in grayscale** after every change. The focal flower must stay lighter than the ground, as planned. (1140 · U3 · L10 · 08:31–10:21)

## The rule of thumb

"The closer two colors are, the greater the harmony between them and the smaller the contrast they will generate." (1140 · U3 · L10 · 10:21–10:43)

- **Contrast is distance and harmony is closeness.** Every palette decision trades one against the other. See [palettes.md](palettes.md).
- **Inside a warm-cold contrast, harmony comes from the choice of family members.** Choose each family's member nearest the border, for example a cooler red against greens. (1140 · U4 · L17 · 02:37–04:32)

## Failure: harmonizing until the areas dissolve

This is Problem 1.
- **The failure:** blue flecks scattered everywhere through a yellow field "to harmonize" make the image look dirty and break the layout. (1140 · U4 · L18 · 00:30–01:47)
- **The fix:** keep each area coherent, and let only chosen elements interrupt it.
