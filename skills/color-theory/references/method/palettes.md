# Palettes: the theory, taught last on purpose

Reader: an agent choosing a palette type for a target amount of contrast and colorfulness. Sources: [L15](../course/U04-L15-color-theory-finally.md), [L16](../course/U04-L16-color-theory-finally-ii.md), [L08](../course/U03-L08-success-and-failure-of-an-image.md).

The course teaches palette theory at the end, after perception, structure and harmony. The reason given is that the terms are "only words put on concepts you have already seen". (1140 · U4 · L15 · 00:00–01:20)

The skill keeps that stance. Choose a palette type **to deliver the contrast the value plan needs**, never as the starting point.

## The wheel and the three ingredients

- **The color wheel**:
  - hue runs around the rim;
  - toward the center, saturation drops and the color moves toward white;
  - on other wheels it moves toward black instead.

  It is to color what the grayscale is to value. (1140 · U4 · L15 · 01:20–02:52)
- **The three ingredients of color** (1140 · U4 · L15 · 02:52–03:38):

| Ingredient | Also called | Maximum contrast |
|---|---|---|
| Value | luminosity | black against white |
| Color | hue | two opposite hues on the wheel |
| Saturation | intensity, purity | a pure hue against white, black or grey |

(1140 · U4 · L15 · 03:38–04:20)

## Palette types as amounts of color contrast

| Type | Structure | Color contrast | Colorfulness | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Complementary | A color and its opposite; the pair can sit anywhere on the wheel | Maximum, the equivalent of black and white | Low | 1140 · U4 · L15 · 04:20–05:08, 1140 · U4 · L15 · 05:53–07:02 |
| Monochromatic | One hue in several values | Near zero; value contrast must replace it | Lowest | 1140 · U4 · L15 · 05:08–05:53, 1140 · U4 · L15 · 05:53–07:02 |
| Composite | A color and a color near its opposite; cyan to magenta is the course example | Not stated | Not stated | 1140 · U4 · L15 · 07:41–08:57 |
| Split complementary | A color against two colors near its opposite, often as families | Not stated | Not stated | 1140 · U4 · L15 · 08:57–10:23 |
| Triadic | Three families at roughly equal distance | Lower | High | 1140 · U4 · L15 · 10:23–11:40 |

**The governing rule:** every color added between two opposites reduces the distance between colors. Contrast drops and colorfulness rises. (1140 · U4 · L15 · 05:53–07:02)

**Complementary images almost always have a bridge.** In the example, blue face light against green background light, a thin red is the bridge. (1140 · U4 · L15 · 07:02–07:41) Composite palettes usually have one too: desaturated gold as "a breather". (1140 · U4 · L15 · 07:41–08:57)

## Colors support the values

- In every palette type, "the colors always, always come to support the original value plan". (1140 · U4 · L15 · 11:40–12:36)
- **Worked example:** a yellow-against-green forest painting, checked with a Levels adjustment in black and white. It shows a white center against a black surround, the same structure as its colors. (1140 · U4 · L15 · 11:40–12:36)
- **How the course image's palette was chosen:**
  1. All the flowers take one color, opposite to the background.
  2. The background takes two close colors.

  (1140 · U3 · L08 · 11:48–12:59)

## Fewer colors, better decisions

- The course moves from "4 million colors" to about 100, then to about 10. Understanding structure cuts the number of decisions, and the reduced image still works as illustration, branding or character design. (1140 · U3 · L08 · 09:58–11:48)
- Limiting colors "forces us to make the right decisions". (1140 · U4 · L14 · 00:27–01:14)

## Arrangement changes the feeling

- One palette can read as gentle or aggressive.
  - **Gentle:** keep neighbouring tones close.
  - **Aggressive:** place complements directly against each other.

  (1140 · U4 · L16 · 04:24–05:37)
- The palette type fixes the materials. The layout decides the result.

## Tools

- **Adobe Color** generates monochromatic, complementary, compound (split complementary) and triad palettes to experiment with. (1140 · U4 · L15 · 12:36–13:51)
- Hue-angle rules are a starting sketch. For why equal angles are not equal perceptual steps, and for generators that correct for it, see [science-crosswalk.md](science-crosswalk.md).

Practice the teacher sets:
- build analog, complementary and multicolor palettes on the PSD;
- copy 3 excellent images in at most 10 flat colors;
- guess 5 picked colors by eye and check them with the picker.
