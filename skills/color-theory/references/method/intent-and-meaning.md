## Start from a feeling, stated as keywords

- Viewers read mood from color by instinct. The teacher asked viewers to choose among four words—happy, sad, terrifying or mysterious—and showed three sets of photos. (1140 · U2 · L04 · 00:00–01:41)
- Color (values plus colors) gives the **emotional tonality** of an image. The first question is always "how should this image make people feel?" (1140 · U2 · L04 · 06:54–07:47)
- Write the target as keywords. Studios describe a film's palette with words such as "fun, colorful, aquatic", and the whole palette is planned to deliver them. (1140 · U4 · L14 · 06:47–08:37)

## The three ingredients as mood scales

Each ingredient is a scale, and each scale moves the feeling. (1140 · U4 · L16 · 00:00–01:56)

| Scale | One end | Other end |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Light: happy | Dark: drama, terror |
| Color (hue count) | Monochromatic | Colorful |
| Saturation | Saturated: active, intense, happy | Desaturated: sad, mysterious, weak response |

The same mapping shows up in the opening game. Light values and saturated colors read as happy. Dark values read as terrifying. Desaturated colors read as sad or mysterious. (1140 · U2 · L04 · 01:41–02:57, 1140 · U2 · L04 · 02:57–03:30)

Darkness frightens because it creates ambiguity: what cannot be seen is unknown. (1140 · U4 · L14 · 05:14–05:49)

## Combining two feelings (derived)

Briefs often pair feelings, such as "warm but lonely". The course demonstrates single targets only, so this rule is the skill's derivation from the course's parts:
1. Give each feeling its own ingredient or area rather than averaging them. Loneliness can take the value and the space, with a mostly dark or cool field and wide silences (see rhythm in [rhythm-balance-flow.md](rhythm-balance-flow.md)). Warmth can take temperature on the protagonist.
2. Use a temperature composition to hold both: a cold image with a warm protagonist (1140 · U4 · L17 · 07:09–08:00).
3. Check each feeling separately against its own mood-board references (T4).

## No color has a fixed meaning

- Charts of color meanings, such as StudioBinder's film cheat sheet, list common associations, and viewers do expect them. (1140 · U2 · L04 · 03:30–04:17)
- But a red Elmo is not violent, and a tender scene can be mostly blue. Meaning "depends on how we make it" and "on what we associate it with". (1140 · U2 · L04 · 04:17–05:30)
- Treat a chart as a default to override deliberately, not as a rule.

## Identity: form plus color

- Form plus color creates **identity**. Flags, NASA, Warhol and McDonald's each fix a color code and repeat it until the colors alone are recognised. (1140 · U4 · L14 · 01:14–02:03, 1140 · U4 · L14 · 02:03–03:27, 1140 · U4 · L14 · 03:27–04:03)
- Color can outweigh form. A harmless shape, such as a person or a peacock, reads as frightening in the right palette. (1140 · U4 · L14 · 04:03–05:14, 1140 · U4 · L14 · 05:49–06:47)
- There is no formula for "elegant". Reach it by trial and error, judging each version by eye. (1140 · U4 · L14 · 08:37–10:12)

## Story gives the focal point a payoff

- Ask "what is this image about?" at the start, and again near the end. (1140 · U3 · L13 · 00:29–01:43)
- A couple of story details are enough to reward the attention the composition sends to the focal point. In the course image, a nest and an egg inside the flower do this. The film term is a **payoff**. (1140 · U3 · L13 · 01:43–03:03)
- Every story element must also do color work. See [texture-and-variety.md](texture-and-variety.md) and [rhythm-balance-flow.md](rhythm-balance-flow.md).

## Check against references

This check is for Problem 4, "the color does not fit the story":
1. State the feeling.
2. Build a **mood board** of reference images with that feeling.
3. Place versions of the image beside it and pick the one that fits.

(1140 · U4 · L18 · 07:27–08:56)

Practice the teacher sets: collect references that share a mood and write down how lighting, contrast and color make it. Change one PSD's mood to terror, happiness, suspense and sterility using only colors and values. See [U02-unit.md](../course/U02-unit.md).
