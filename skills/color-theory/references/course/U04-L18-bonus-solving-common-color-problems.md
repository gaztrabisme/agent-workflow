## Problem 1: harmonizing until the areas of the image dissolve

Anchor: 1140 · U4 · L18 · 00:30–01:47

One of the things we have seen is the idea of harmony (*armonía*), and above all the idea that we had to lay out (*diagramar*) the different parts of our image very clearly. If this layout, this division of the image into areas, stops existing because we start mixing everything together to create harmony, the image is already corrupted.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 00:36](frames/U04L18-F0036.jpg)

On screen: "LOS ELEMENTOS DE UN ÉXITO" (The elements of a success) / "ARMONÍA" (Harmony)  
Shows: Recap slide from an earlier lesson: a black-and-white landscape, the same landscape in dark blue and pale yellow, and a third version with a teal accent, each with its swatches and a color wheel; the cursor circles the teal accent.

That is what happens here. Wanting to harmonize my yellows with my blues, I have started putting those blues everywhere. The problem is that it gives a dirty feeling. Instead of making the image feel more colorful, it actually breaks the image's original layout, and so we lose the structure and the strength of the original image.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 00:56](frames/U04L18-F0056.jpg)

On screen: "RESOLVER LOS PRINCIPALES PROBLEMAS DEL COLOR" (Solving the main color problems) / "FALTA DE UNA DIAGRAMACIÓN CLARA DE FAMILIAS Y BLOQUES DE COLORES CONTRASTANTES" (Lack of a clear layout of families and blocks of contrasting colors)  
Shows: Problem 1, the wrong version: a fantasy meadow with a large pink flower-house holding an egg, a purple trunk on the left, blue flecks scattered through the yellow grass.

That was the case in the original version. Here the areas are clear. There are some interruptions, but not everything is interruption. Inside this whole large yellow area I try to keep continuity and coherence, and keep most of the tones yellowish, except the ones I really want to stand out (captions: *descarten*, "discard"). This is extremely important: try to keep control over your areas, and once you start harmonizing like mad, basically, do not overdo it.

On screen: "RESOLVER LOS PRINCIPALES PROBLEMAS DEL COLOR" (Solving the main color problems) / "FALTA DE UNA DIAGRAMACIÓN CLARA DE FAMILIAS Y BLOQUES DE COLORES CONTRASTANTES" (Lack of a clear layout of families and blocks of contrasting colors)

## Problem 2: color contrast in the wrong place

Anchor: 1140 · U4 · L18 · 01:47–03:19

The other thing we have seen is the idea of structure: building the structure of the image in black and white, and having color come in to strengthen that structure and never to damage it.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 01:56](frames/U04L18-F0156.jpg)

On screen: "LOS ELEMENTOS DE UN ÉXITO" (The elements of a success) / "FORMA" (Shape) "VALOR" (Value) "COLOR" / "CONTRASTE DE TAMAÑO Y FORMA" (Contrast of size and shape) + "CONTRASTE DE VALOR" (Value contrast) + "CONTRASTE DE COLOR" (Color contrast) / "PARTE 1" (Part 1)  
Shows: Recap slide: a gray circle and bar, a black-and-white landscape, and the same landscape in dark purple and yellow, joined by plus signs.

This matters a lot because, when we arrive at our image, we do not want the whole layout of the image inviting us to look here while, because I have placed my contrasts badly (in this case the saturation and color contrasts), my eye ends up over there. Let's do a small test.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 02:07](frames/U04L18-F0207.jpg)

On screen: "RESOLVER LOS PRINCIPALES PROBLEMAS DEL COLOR" (Solving the main color problems) / "EL COLOR NO APOYA LA COMPOSICIÓN DE VALORES" (The color does not support the value composition) / "DOMESTIKA / UN CURSO DE COLOR PARA TODOS LOS QUE SIEMPRE QUISIERON ENTENDER ESTE COMPLICADÍSIMO TEMA Y VERLO DE MANERA FACIL" (Domestika / A color course for everyone who always wanted to understand this very complicated subject and see it the easy way)  
Shows: Problem 2, the wrong version: a poster with a dark navy diagonal on the left, a muted orange flower with an egg, a desaturated sky and a blue butterfly on the dark shape.

This is the image as it is, where the color and temperature contrast sits here: orange against blue. Here this blue is continuous with this purple, and the purple, being a warm color, does not contrast much against these yellows.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 02:20](frames/U04L18-F0220.jpg)

On screen: "DEL COLOR" (of color) / "UN CURSO DE COLOR PARA TODOS LOS QUE SIEMPRE QUISIERON ENTENDER ESTE" [rest omitted] (A color course for everyone who always wanted to understand this…)  
Shows: Problem 2, the original version, zoomed in: saturated orange flower, blue egg in the hole, teal sky patches and a purple butterfly on the navy shape.

In the other image, by contrast, there is no longer much color contrast there, because this area and this one were heavily desaturated, so the desaturation joined them. The color contrast was also lost in the egg, which is now more or less the same color as the plant. Meanwhile, a lot of contrast has been added over here.

That is one of the mistakes we must not make. We have to be aware of what our image's layout is, what its skeleton is, which focal point it favors, and make sure our color decisions strengthen that decision and do not corrupt it, as happens here. This is very, very important. This is the problem: my contrast is not in the right place. This is the solution: my color works with, goes hand in hand with, the structure of my image's composition.

## Problem 3: lack of color variety

Anchor: 1140 · U4 · L18 · 03:19–05:09

We have also seen that one of the things we were after with color (in the case of this lesson, with textures) was creating variety. Inside one single big flat patch of green we could have many kinds of green, like these greens that turn into these, which are almost yellow, and then into these, and these, and these. Lots of variation. See how it never stops flickering here: the cursor never, ever stays fixed, because there is variety inside the area.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 03:32](frames/U04L18-F0332.jpg)

On screen: "BUSCANDO (AHORA) TEXTURAS !" (Looking for textures (now)!)  
Shows: Recap slide: two figures on a steep green hillside above the sea, with rocks and birds; the cursor sits in the green grass.

This is the opposite of a varied image: a completely monochromatic image with no changes of color, only changes of value. Let's see how to solve this problem.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 04:18](frames/U04L18-F0418.jpg)

On screen: "RESOLVER LOS PRINCIPALES PROBLEMAS DEL COLOR" (Solving the main color problems) / "FALTA VARIACIÓN DE COLORES, SATURACIÓN Y TEMPERATURA" (Lack of variation in colors, saturation and temperature)  
Shows: Problem 3: the flower poster rendered entirely in olive and pale yellow, varying only in value.

At the beginning we often have one of two problems with color: either we lack color variety, or we exaggerate the color contrasts. We saw the exaggerated color contrast in the first of these solved problems. This is the second: we lack color. How do we add colors? By creating groups. Here I am only using two colors, but you can see it already creates a more pleasant visual feeling. At least there is variation. It is still not very varied or very interesting, though, because this pink is the same everywhere.

I can create even more variation. Here I start to have saturation variation, from desaturated to saturated, and color variation, because this yellow is no longer the same as this one, and this one is a bit more orange than this one. I'll show you: here it is pinker, this orange leans a little more toward the oranges, and this one leans even more toward the yellows. Variation begins to appear and the image becomes much more interesting, but we can take it further still.

## The Hue/Saturation test: is the image colorful enough?

Anchor: 1140 · U4 · L18 · 05:09–07:27

However, I want to show you a trick I use often to find out whether an image is colorful enough. Careful: this image could work if the palette you want to create is fairly limited and subtle, if you want a pastel-like feeling that is not very aggressive. That is fine; it is allowed. But the image is still within a fairly monochromatic range. To prove it to you, we open an adjustment layer by clicking here and choose Hue/Saturation (*tono/saturación*).

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 05:36](frames/U04L18-F0536.jpg)

Shows: Photoshop with a new Hue/Saturation adjustment layer open over the poster; the cursor is on the adjustment-layer button.

In Hue/Saturation I can go to one color range. In this case the color that dominates the image is yellow. Let's run another test first. What this tool does is take certain hues and shift them. For example, red: if we look at my text up here, when I pick reds it moves only the reds.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 05:54](frames/U04L18-F0554.jpg)

In our image, if we pick yellows, it modifies almost the whole image. That is not normal; we will see later what happens when the image is more colorful. It means Photoshop identifies that almost every color in the image contains yellow. The problem with this kind of image is that it will be perceived as quite monochromatic.

## Problem 5: saturation out of control

Anchor: 1140 · U4 · L18 · 08:56–10:26

Fifth topic: saturation. We will see that saturation can cause us many problems if it is not handled well.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 09:02](frames/U04L18-F0902.jpg)

On screen: "DEGRADADO DE SATURACIÓN" (Saturation gradient)  
Shows: Recap slide: a fox in a forest clearing with orange flowers, with saturated warm swatches on the left and desaturated dark swatches on the right.

An image like this will not work. It is causing several things. First, it looks garish; it is aggressive to the eye. Second, since saturation is a contrast tool, it is generating a whole lot of unwanted contrasts. For example, here between the sky and the clouds, saturated against desaturated. Here in this part of the plant, saturated against desaturated. And here, because these colors are so saturated, they separate a lot. Remember that desaturation has the advantage of bringing colors closer together. Here it is the reverse: we have saturated fuchsia, saturated yellow, saturated orange, saturated blue. All of this will draw a lot of attention, and it will be very hard to get rid of.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 09:33](frames/U04L18-F0933.jpg)

Shows: Problem 5, the wrong version: the meadow scene with every color highly saturated (fuchsia flower, acid yellow grass, orange butterfly, strong blue shadow).

So it is wiser to be more prudent and go back to a version where saturation is laid out a little more clearly: desaturating the ground, and returning the sky to more human saturation levels that our eye can look at without hurting. And if you look, what I have done here is saturate the plant, because it is my focal point, so I can saturate it more.

## Problem 6: color that is not coherent with the light

Anchor: 1140 · U4 · L18 · 10:26–12:20

Last element. We have seen how important light was, because it affects color in a very general way.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 10:33](frames/U04L18-F1033.jpg)

On screen: "LUZ + COLOR" (Light + color) / "LUZ AMARILLA" (Yellow light) / "COLOR LOCAL ROJO" (Local color red) / "COLOR LOCAL AZUL" (Local color blue) / "- CAMBIO DE COLOR LOCAL -" (Change of local color)  
Shows: Recap slide: a sphere lit by yellow light from the upper left, orange on the lit side and plum in shadow, on a teal ground ellipse.

One of the things that most damages our image is establishing light information and then not following it, not being coherent. For example, here we are saying: OK, a very warm light comes in, you can see it is yellowish; here it hits the ground, and it is yellowish. But on this object we get it wrong. We say "ah, OK, it is yellow light," but in fact the lit part looks bluer and colder than the part in shadow. That is not coherent, because the light appears to be warm and the shadow, the ambient light, cold. Same thing here: the lit part seems colder than the part in shadow. Same thing here: it seems to be receiving a light that turns the blue into purple, which means it is receiving a warm light.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 11:04](frames/U04L18-F1104.jpg)

On screen: "RESOLVER LOS PRINCIPALES PROBLEMAS DEL COLOR" (Solving the main color problems) / "EL COLOR NO ES COHERENTE CON LA LUZ" (The color is not coherent with the light)  
Shows: Problem 6, the wrong version: a dim forest with warm yellow light from the right, a violet flower-house whose lit side is cool and bluish.

What we have to do instead, once we have set up our lights, is be very, very faithful to them. What does that mean? In this image it is very clear; I did it on purpose. The main lighting is warm, which means the lit part of my object will be warm.

![1140 · U4 · L18 · 11:33](frames/U04L18-F1133.jpg)

On screen: "RESOLVER LOS PRINCIPALES PROBLEMAS DEL COLOR" (Solving the main color problems) / "EL COLOR NO ES COHERENTE CON LA LUZ" (The color is not coherent with the light)  
Shows: The corrected version: the flower-house's lit side is now warm pinkish-violet, turning cooler toward the shadow side.

We can see it here: these tones will be warmer, closer to red, and these closer to blue. This evolution from warm to cold is very important, because it is what tells us: "ah, OK, it is a cold purple object, but it is lit by a warm light."

### Key points (derived)

- Harmonizing by scattering a color everywhere destroys the image's layout; keep each area coherent and let only chosen elements break it.
- Build the structure in black and white first; place color and saturation contrast where the value composition already sends the eye.
- A monochromatic image gains life through color groups, then variation in saturation and hue, then temperature contrast.
- Hue/Saturation test: if selecting Yellows moves almost the whole image, or Greens moves nothing, the colors are not distinct enough.
- Check the palette against a mood board of the intended feeling, compare versions, and choose the one that fits.
- Lay out saturation deliberately: desaturate secondary areas, saturate the focal point, then raise overall saturation if the image looks gray.
- Once the light is defined, keep lit sides in the light's temperature and shadows in the ambient temperature.
