### Key points (derived)

- The teacher files his color references in three families, arranged as a gradient: colorful (many hues at once), vivid (few, strong colors) and muted (gray or desaturated overall).
- Colorful images (Muyang Xu, Floriane Marchix, Nathan Fowkes, Theo Prins) combine many hues yet stay harmonious and feel natural.
- Vivid images (Jeff Turley, Gop Gap, Joon Ahn, Tobi Trebeljahr, Ryan Lang) make one or two colors read stronger than they really are.
- Color can also mean an almost black image with color only where it serves the story (Alex Mandradjiev, Nickolai Lockertsen).
- The muted family (Peleng, Michal Misowski, Joseph Zbukvic, Richard Schmid) follows one formula: gray or desaturated overall, with a small fraction of saturated color.
- Harmony, saturation, temperature contrast and value are named as topics the course will explain; Richard Schmid's *Alla Prima* is recommended reading.
