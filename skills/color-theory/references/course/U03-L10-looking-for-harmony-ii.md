Same thing here. If you look at this whole part, these tones that feel warm here, almost yellow, are possible because the light hits here and bounces directly over there. Same thing with the characters, whose lower part is very orange because it is receiving the bounce that comes from the floor.

## Whites as a bridge in a portrait

Anchor: 1140 · U3 · L10 · 01:51–02:46

This image is very beautiful, very nice. We have this pink tone of the skin. We have a complementary that in this case is not very far away, but it is a complementary all the same in this composition (captions: *de dos mods*). When there are no other colors, either of the two colors can be complementary in some way.

And here these whites actually work as a bridge, because they are very desaturated, and they also have a lot of variety. We have whites that are literally pure white, or perhaps still have a little yellowish tone. We have whites that are pinkish. And we have these whites that are very gray, almost blue; here we even have the odd blue. This whole range of desaturated colors in between is great: it creates a bridge between these two relatively close colors. So this skin tone here feels a little more desaturated than the rest of the skin. Very beautiful.

## The key to harmony

Anchor: 1140 · U3 · L10 · 10:21–10:43

The key to what I have just explained in this lesson is this: the closer two colors are, the greater the harmony between them and the smaller the contrast they will generate. In the next lesson I will talk to you about balance and rhythm.

### Key points (derived)

- A bridge color sits between two contrasting colors and gives them points of contact; it can be a desaturated gray or white range, or an intermediate hue.
- Bounced light justifies placing a color from one block inside a neighboring block.
- Build blocks first, then spread a little of each color into the others.
- New colors should follow the composition's flow towards the focal point and keep the original value layout.
- The closer two colors are, the greater the harmony and the smaller the contrast.
