## Painting reflections on skin and hair

Anchor: 1560 · U4 · L15 · 00:37–02:55

As with the sunny scene, a new layer is added on top of everything, with the eyedropper set to all layers so color can be picked from anything already painted. The white shirt is used to sample the color of the light itself, made brighter and more saturated. One of the light sources is imagined as a large TV casting a soft green light, so that TV is suggested as a reflection. The cheeks and nose can be slightly reflective, but this has to be handled carefully, or they start reading as some other material; the fingers take only a very slight touch. This step is the hardest part of the whole process, because it demands real subtlety. Reflections are also needed from the light source itself: on the side of the ear, and on the part of the head close to the skull, which is often quite reflective. Hair gets some reflection too, because it's actually very smooth, and because each strand reflects the light slightly differently, which gives the reflection texture as well as color. That texture helps sell the idea that there is a real light source, because the brain reads a reflection as a sign of one. A small red reflection goes on the side of the eye and the side of the nose; the difference it makes is clear when comparing with and without it, even though the change itself is almost nothing.

## Reflections on hard surfaces: the cap and a metal object

Anchor: 1560 · U4 · L15 · 02:56–04:15

The cheeks are genuinely difficult to get right, because it's easy to overdo how reflective they look. The edge of the character's cap can also be a little reflective. This pass over the painting is also a good moment to fix any small mistakes, now that every element of the scene is in place and colors can simply be picked and painted in. One object — probably metallic — is noticed as needing a reflection of the green light source; following its shape carefully with that green reflection helps sell the idea that the green light source is really there, since the reflection is how it's being seen.

## Reviewing the finished sunny scene

Anchor: 1560 · U4 · L15 · 04:16–05:07

With the reflections pass finished, the lesson moves on to review the completed images. The first is where the sunny scene stood at the end of the base colors and lighting stage; on top of that, details were added — some reflections and some blades of grass. At this stage, extra touches can be added freely, because the underlying structure is already solid, so nothing is at risk. The reflections around the character's face are quite important for helping the skin feel like skin, and scattered, randomly placed blades of grass make the scene feel less systematic and more organic. Even the character's hat has a little reflectiveness added to it along its edges.

## Reviewing the finished night scene

Anchor: 1560 · U4 · L15 · 05:08–06:03

At night, reflections are also added to the eyes, and a glow effect is layered on using a Linear Dodge layer — a layer type that, if switched back to Normal blending, turns out to be a fairly dark color underneath, which is what makes it well suited to glow effects. The green light source can be seen reflecting into a metal object, and again on the wooden table nearby, since wood can be somewhat reflective too. The eyes pick up the green reflection, while the side of the character's face carries a red reflection from the lamp. A number of small tweaks follow: a reflectance on the window, and some further detail on the character's face, all aimed at bringing out the volume of the tears in the wall.

## Wrap-up

Anchor: 1560 · U4 · L15 · 06:04–06:28

With that, all of the images for this exercise are complete. The lesson closes by hoping the process helped make sense of how lighting works, with the final project video still to come, reviewing everything covered so far.

### Key points (derived)

- The same "new layer, eyedropper on all layers" workflow used for the sunny scene is reused here for reflections in an interior lit by two colored light sources.
- Reflections should be sampled from the color of the actual light sources (a green TV-like source and a red lamp) and kept subtle, especially on the cheeks, nose and fingers.
- Hair reflects with visible texture because each strand catches the light slightly differently, which helps sell the presence of a light source.
- A Linear Dodge layer is a practical way to add a glow effect, since its pixels can be surprisingly dark once viewed in Normal blending mode.
- Reviewing finished work with the "details" layer toggled on and off is a way to see exactly what a reflections-and-translucency pass adds over the base colors and lighting.
