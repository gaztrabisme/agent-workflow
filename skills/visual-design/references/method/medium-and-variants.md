## Design for the support

- Include the support in the system decision. A design can fail when moved to a material or context it was not made for. (5642 · U6 · L86 · 03:03–04:39)
- Create the mark for its hardest application and check the reduced result at that size. A publisher's spine and a small online avatar are explicit examples. (5642 · U7 · L96 · 01:48–02:32; 5642 · U7 · L103 · 02:24–03:14)
- Plan horizontal, square and vertical formats early. Responsive identity adapts the amount of information to the space; it is not a separate fixed logo rule. (5642 · U7 · L103 · 03:14–04:24; 5642 · U7 · L103 · 04:24–05:43)
- Use supplied photography consistently. The course unifies images through grayscale, reframing or cut-outs, and warns that mismatched photographic treatment and pictograms can create competing visual languages. (5642 · U7 · L94 · 06:49–08:10; 5642 · U7 · L98 · 05:02–05:49; 5642 · U7 · L102 · 00:00–01:31)

## Test variants and presentation

- Apply the base system to front and back, then to further supports such as a bag, shirt or poster series. The point is to test how the identity flexes and what stays recognizable. (5642 · U6 · L90 · 00:23–01:47, 04:55–07:31)
- A system can use a symbol, wordmark or both according to the application. A complementary graphic element can extend the identity where the mark does not fit. (5642 · U7 · L96 · 06:19–07:26; 5642 · U7 · L103 · 04:24–05:43)
- Flexibility can mean letting a mark change style to join each production's visual language. Decide whether the brief calls for that changing identity or a repeated mark. (5642 · U7 · L99 · 07:23–09:08; 5642 · U7 · L99 · 09:08–10:23)
- Build presentation pages in the project's visual identity, lead with its concept and reuse process material as evidence. Review a page sequence as a sequence, not as isolated images. (5642 · U6 · L91 · 05:51–06:53; 5642 · U6 · L91 · 06:53–07:37; 5642 · U6 · L91 · 07:37–08:14; 5642 · U4 · L47 · 06:29–07:15)
- Mock the artifact at realistic scale and context before presenting it. The course uses a rendered package and its other applications as checks on the system. (5642 · U6 · L90 · 02:43–04:28)

Treat project-specific requirements and conventions as controlling constraints. A course example can suggest an option; it cannot override the actual audience, domain or production specification. (Owner decision 6.)

## Check before moving on

- The weakest application has been rendered and checked at actual minimum size.
- Required supports and variants are shown; meaningful differences are not confused with system drift.
- The presentation explains the concept and gives the next person reusable examples or templates.
- Run T12, plus any render-kit checks required for the delivery format. If the system fails at a support, return to stage 5; if it fails at size, return to stage 4 or 5.
