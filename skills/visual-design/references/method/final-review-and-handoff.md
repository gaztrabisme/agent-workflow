## Review the whole artifact

Use clarity, balance, contrast, unity and salience as prompts, interpreting them for the actual medium. (5642 · U4 · L46 · 01:40–02:56; 5642 · U4 · L46 · 02:57–04:35; 5642 · U4 · L46 · 04:36–06:14; 5642 · U4 · L46 · 06:15–07:07; 5642 · U4 · L46 · 07:08–07:39; 5642 · U4 · L47 · 00:09–01:37)

Hide construction guides and view the finished series at thumbnail scale. Check its rhythm, hierarchy and variation as a whole. (5642 · U2 · L21 · 14:00–17:46; 5642 · U4 · L56 · 08:05–10:31)

Check that the mark reads well and does not say something unintended. (5642 · U7 · L104 · 00:00–00:31) Check its minimum-size applications, including the spine and avatar. (5642 · U7 · L96 · 01:48–02:32; 5642 · U7 · L103 · 02:24–03:14; 5642 · U7 · L103 · 03:14–04:24) Make it easy for another person to apply, and give colleagues templates and examples rather than complicated guidelines. (5642 · U7 · L104 · 01:01–01:17; 5642 · U7 · L104 · 01:17–01:30)

## Run the checklist and return to the right stage

Run T1–T12 in order. A test identifies what failed and the stage to revisit; it is not permission to patch symptoms at the end. After changing the form or hierarchy, rerun the code measures and the visual questions that depend on that change.

The test-to-stage map is:

| Tests | Main return stage |
|---|---|
| T1–T2 | 1–2: brief, message and concept |
| T3 | 3: form and unity |
| T4–T6, T8–T9 | 4: composition and movement |
| T7, T10–T11 | 5: type and system |
| T12 | 6: medium and variants |

## Report and hand off

For each failure, state the test, what was seen, the return stage and one concrete change. Preserve the working parts when recommending a fix. Include the evidence status for every check: measured, judged by vision, not judged or skipped, with a reason for the last two.

Give the next user templates and worked examples that demonstrate correct use of the system. (5642 · U7 · L104 · 01:17–01:30)
