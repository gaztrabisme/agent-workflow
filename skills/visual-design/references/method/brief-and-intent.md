## Read the brief before choosing a style

- Graphic design gives form to an idea; the brief decides whether the solution should use familiar cues or take a more subjective route. Neither route is inherently better. (5642 · U2 · L07 · 00:00–02:04, 03:57–05:07)
- Start with the message's core, or “DNA”, and let the content determine the visual treatment. (5642 · U4 · L44 · 00:00–00:48; 5642 · U4 · L46 · 00:00–01:20)
- For an identity, define its use as a whole graphic language across applications, not just a logo. The course includes labels, stationery, networks and even nonvisual touchpoints in that view. (5642 · U7 · L92 · 01:12–01:28; 5642 · U7 · L92 · 01:29–01:36)
- Record the audience, required message, deliverables, supports, fixed content, viewing distance and production constraints. This checklist is derived from the brief-reading exercises and the lessons that design for a specific support. (derived from 5642 · U6 · L82 · 00:37–00:49; 5642 · U6 · L82 · 00:50–01:02; 5642 · U6 · L82 · 01:03–01:25; 5642 · U6 · L82 · 01:26–01:33; 5642 · U7 · L95 · 05:29–06:22; 5642 · U7 · L103 · 02:24–03:14; 5642 · U7 · L103 · 03:14–04:24; 5642 · U7 · L103 · 04:24–05:43)

## Derived content verification before concept and layout

- Before concept or layout, source every factual claim to its originating record; label it actual, plan or look-ahead; retain its units and dates; and keep chart semantics outside this skill's method. (derived: run-2 dogfood, finding row 1's source-record mismatch and adjacent-log look-ahead)

## Name the hardest application early

- Design for the application's narrowest constraint first: a publishing mark must work on a spine; a business card may carry the complete identity; an online identity must survive in an avatar. (5642 · U7 · L95 · 00:09–00:29; 5642 · U7 · L96 · 00:00–00:36, 01:48–02:32; 5642 · U7 · L103 · 02:24–03:14)
- Set a real output size and format before placing content. The nine-post project starts from 1080 × 1080 artboards. (5642 · U2 · L10 · 00:00–01:30, 06:57–08:18)
- In a visual-identity brief, distinguish facts about the product and audience, emotional values and the voice that should attract attention. The course associates these three lenses with a square, circle and triangle. (5642 · U6 · L81 · 07:07–07:43; 5642 · U6 · L82 · 00:37–00:49; 5642 · U6 · L83 · 00:45–01:47)

## Define a readable target

Write one sentence for the message, one for the intended audience and one for the desired first reading. A sentence-long explanation is also a useful final check in the course's “explain it by phone” test. (derived from 5642 · U6 · L83 · 01:47–02:42, 04:29–05:20; 5642 · U7 · L95 · 10:59–11:46)

Record whether the design is a single artifact or part of a sequence/system. An editorial sequence uses rhythm across pages; a system needs related pieces with individual functions and a shared order. (5642 · U4 · L47 · 06:29–07:15; 5642 · U6 · L86 · 10:23–11:46)

## Check before moving on

- The brief names the audience, message, real supports and hardest application.
- The visual target can be stated in plain language without relying on a colour choice.
- Run T1 for message fit. If the brief is incomplete, state the missing decision instead of guessing.

Practice the teacher sets: design nine 1080 × 1080 social posts as one consistent set, carrying the brief's concept into the forms, type and composition. See [U02-unit.md](../course/U02-unit.md).
