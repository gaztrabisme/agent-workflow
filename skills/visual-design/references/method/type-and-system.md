# Type and system: make hierarchy repeatable

Reader: an agent choosing typography, grid rules and recurring parts for one artifact or a family of applications.

## Make typography part of the composition

- Typography is nearly as central to communicating an idea as the image itself; choose it as part of the message's visual language. (5642 · U2 · L11 · 00:00–02:18)
- Choose typefaces for their role and character. If pairing two faces, make the difference legible while looking for compatible proportions. (5642 · U2 · L13 · 00:00–02:18; 5642 · U4 · L51 · 06:24–07:56)
- Set hierarchy before choosing faces. For identity work, the course demonstrates brand name, category and technical information at distinct levels. (5642 · U6 · L89 · 00:07–01:09)
- Adjust size, tracking and leading at the actual output size. Do not stretch a typeface to fit; use an appropriate family width or a different placement. (5642 · U2 · L12 · 03:02–04:44; 5642 · U2 · L14 · 02:56–04:58; 5642 · U2 · L14 · 04:58–06:57)
- Counters, letter weight and spacing affect one another. Small counters make a face look heavier and call for tighter spacing; typographic colour changes as letter, word and line spacing change. (5642 · U4 · L51 · 00:00–00:56; 5642 · U4 · L51 · 01:37–02:27)
- Keep running text distinct from the display face where small-size readability requires it. The logotype's typeface does not have to serve every brand application. (5642 · U7 · L97 · 09:58–11:10; 5642 · U7 · L100 · 11:49–12:46)
- Correct the actual text and font before polishing: confirm script coverage, genuine punctuation and small caps, and check for collisions at the final size. (5642 · U7 · L95 · 01:48–02:14; 5642 · U7 · L95 · 02:15–03:07; 5642 · U7 · L96 · 04:03–05:43; 5642 · U7 · L97 · 08:12–09:58; 5642 · U7 · L102 · 02:08–03:13)
- Wood-type letters were made large for reading at a distance and set close together; the course shows tightened leading, sometimes nearly to the spacing between words. Match a row of secondary marks by optical weight, not common width. (5642 · U7 · L101 · 03:59–07:48; 5642 · U7 · L101 · 08:57–10:26)

## Fix the system's structure

- Keep a consistent construction axis and stable margins across a set. A page that mixes centred and flush-left alignments without purpose looks inconsistent. (5642 · U7 · L93 · 06:29–07:22, 07:55–10:09; 5642 · U7 · L100 · 02:07–02:40)
- Use proportional scales as options for constructing type sizes and margins. Course examples use ratios including 1.25 and the golden ratio; neither is a universal requirement. (5642 · U4 · L60 · 07:58–09:46; 5642 · U4 · L60 · 09:46–13:38; 5642 · U4 · L61 · 07:08–07:58; 5642 · U4 · L61 · 07:58–09:47; 5642 · U4 · L62 · 02:12–04:56; 5642 · U4 · L62 · 04:56–05:49)
- Choose columns and baseline increments around the content. The course derives leading from the number of lines that should fit a column, then sets the baseline grid to that leading. (5642 · U4 · L63 · 04:04–06:37; 5642 · U4 · L64 · 01:20–02:55; 5642 · U4 · L64 · 02:55–04:48; 5642 · U4 · L64 · 04:48–06:48; 5642 · U4 · L64 · 06:48–07:19; 5642 · U4 · L64 · 07:19–09:29)
- Reusable modules and styles let one adjustment update related instances. Repetition can work at macro, module and micro levels, but an overused module can become monotonous. (5642 · U4 · L57 · 00:00–02:32, 05:19–07:31, 12:43–14:26)

## Separate constants and variables

- A system contains parts and the relationships between them. Identify its logo, typography, colour, image, concept, composition, tone and support. (5642 · U6 · L86 · 00:26–01:50; 5642 · U6 · L86 · 01:50–03:03; 5642 · U6 · L86 · 03:03–04:39)
- Decide the system's complexity, strategy and flexibility. Duplicate a base design and vary one zone at a time to see which elements carry the constant idea and which can change. (5642 · U6 · L86 · 04:39–06:41; 5642 · U6 · L86 · 06:41–07:26; 5642 · U6 · L86 · 07:26–08:33; 5642 · U6 · L86 · 08:33–09:08; 5642 · U6 · L86 · 09:08–10:23; 5642 · U6 · L87 · 00:21–00:51; 5642 · U6 · L87 · 00:51–01:16; 5642 · U6 · L87 · 01:16–01:48; 5642 · U6 · L87 · 01:48–02:16)
- A system should have related parts and an order; each piece should be specific to its use while contributing to the whole. Avoid flat repetition unless a closed system is intended. (5642 · U6 · L86 · 10:23–11:46)
- The system check is **derived** from this principle: name the fixed elements and permitted variables before creating the variants, then verify each variant against them. (derived from 5642 · U6 · L86 · 10:23–11:46; 5642 · U6 · L87 · 00:21–00:51; 5642 · U6 · L87 · 00:51–01:16; 5642 · U6 · L87 · 01:16–01:48; 5642 · U6 · L87 · 01:48–02:16)

## Check before moving on

- The type hierarchy can be stated and every face has a reason.
- Alignment rules, grid and type scale are declared where they govern more than one item.
- Repeated modules remain consistent without flattening useful variation.
- Named constants survive all versions; each variable has a defined range.
- Run T7, T10 and T11. If the structure works but the small text does not, return to type selection and scale; if variants drift, return to the constants and variables.
