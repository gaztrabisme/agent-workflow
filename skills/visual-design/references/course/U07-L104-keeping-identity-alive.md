## Make the mark legible, and don't say more than you mean

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L104 · 00:00–00:31

This closing lesson gives a few tips for keeping a brand alive and lasting.

![5642 · U7 · L104 · 00:08](frames/U07L104-F0008.jpg)

On screen: "Mantener la identidad viva" (Keeping identity alive)  
Shows: Title card for the lesson.

To start, a mark has to read well — it sounds obvious, but plenty of illegible logotypes exist. A mark also shouldn't say something that isn't meant to be said: use symbolism that's inspiring, not simply odd.

## Development matters more than the mark itself: the Ikea example

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L104 · 00:31–00:45

In some environments the graphic mark matters, but developing the brand around it matters more. Take a brand like Ikea: it's a fairly weak logotype, but it develops its graphic language very well.

## Design to last — except when the job is meant to be seasonal

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L104 · 00:45–01:01

It's good to stay current, but don't fall in love with particular typefaces or colours: use things so they last. Although if the job at hand is something seasonal, something that isn't meant to last, go ahead — that's exactly what it's for.

## Don't make your mark invasive, and make it easy to apply

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L104 · 01:01–01:17

Make sure a mark doesn't use an invasive system: a brand doesn't need to dominate over others, and besides, its designer won't always be there to see it applied correctly. The easier a mark is to apply, the more it will be applied, and the better.

## Skip complicated rules — give templates and examples instead

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L104 · 01:17–01:30

Don't write complicated brand guidelines; make things easy for colleagues — the better the groundwork, the better they'll do with it. Seen this way, making templates and giving examples matters more than writing rules.

## Closing: move your own project forward

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L104 · 01:30–01:53

Following these tips, now is the moment to move your own project forward — whether that means carrying on with what this course has covered, or applying it to a project already under way. Either way, go ahead. That brings this Domestika course to its end, though one lesson remains to talk about your own project.

### Key points (derived)

- Legibility comes first: an illegible logotype, or one carrying an unintended meaning, undermines everything built on top of it.
- A strong graphic system can carry an identity further than the logotype's own drawing does — Ikea kept a visually weak logotype through decades of redesigns while developing its graphic language very well.
- Build brand colours and typefaces to last; treat only genuinely seasonal or time-limited work as disposable by design.
- A brand should support what sits alongside it rather than dominate it, and should be simple enough that others can apply it correctly without the original designer present.
- Prioritise templates and worked examples over long rulebooks — they get followed more reliably than written normative rules.
