## Identity beyond the logo

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L92 · 00:08–00:29

Of all the messages an entity puts out, the graphic image is only one part. Designers normally call this part identity (*identidad*). That's not strictly correct, but it's how we understand each other. An identity can represent a company, a service, a product, a rock band, even a person.

## Heraldry: the historical precedent

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L92 · 00:30–00:44

This already existed in the past, under the name heraldry (*heráldica*). Heraldry is the discipline that studies shields, flags and the like, and, in turn, studies how these signs function together.

## Modern identity manuals

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L92 · 00:45–01:11

In the mid-20th century the modern corporate image appears. For this, systems are set up using identity manuals (*manuales de identidad*). These manuals fix precisely what used to be, say, a yellowish colour — now it's a specific Pantone number — and what used to be a plain sans-serif typeface (*tipografía de palo*) is now Helvetica Bold (spoken: *hervética*).

## Identity as a graphic language

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L92 · 01:12–01:28

Identity, as we understand it today, is not just an imagotype or a logotype, but all these elements together that create the graphic language. This graphic language extends across labels, networks, and a whole series of elements we're going to work on in this course.

## Identity beyond the visual

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L92 · 01:29–01:36

An identity can also be much more than this. It can be, for example, the way you're attended to on the phone, or the music playing in a shop.

## This course's method

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L92 · 01:37–01:54

To get there, in this course we're going to work in a very particular way. We'll start from elements that don't work — aesthetically as much as conceptually — and transform them into things that do work. This is what will help you learn to think like an identity designer.

## Screen communication: where we start

Anchor: 5642 · U7 · L92 · 01:55–02:13

For the world of brands and business, the heart of it all is still the imagotype and the logotype, and that's what we're going to study in this course. To start, we'll work with something designers find very difficult and that is very effective: screen communication.

### Key points (derived)

- Identity is only one part of everything an entity communicates, though designers commonly use the word to cover the whole graphic image.
- Heraldry — the study of shields, flags and how such signs work together — already existed in the past under that name.
- Mid-20th-century identity manuals turned loose descriptions ("a yellowish colour", "a sans-serif typeface") into fixed specifications (a Pantone number, Helvetica Bold).
- Today identity is the whole graphic language — beyond the logo, across labels, networks and other applications.
- Identity can extend past the visual: phone manner and in-store music are part of it too.
- The course's method is to take examples that don't work, aesthetically or conceptually, and transform them into ones that do.
- The course starts with screen communication, an element designers often find difficult despite its effectiveness.
