## Choose a color mode for paper or screen

Anchor: 5642 · U2 · L16 · 00:00–02:17

Color setup depends on whether a design is for paper or a screen. Printed photographs, projects, and magazines use pigments; screen posts and websites use light. This difference explains why a color that looks vivid on screen can look much duller in print.

For print, the lesson introduces CMYK (*modo CMYK*). The letters are C for cyan, M for magenta, Y for yellow, and K for black. On a screen, color comes from tiny lights rather than ink. The red, green, and blue light mode is RGB (*modo RGB*). The more intense these lights are, the brighter the color; when red, green, and blue are all at their maximum, they form white.

Many colors can be represented in both modes, but some very bright screen colors cannot be reproduced by a printer’s standard inks, so they look desaturated on paper. Set CMYK when printing and RGB when designing for screen [she repeats “for printing” in the second instruction].

## Special inks and numeric color values

Anchor: 5642 · U2 · L16 · 02:17–05:08

If a design needs a very bright printed color outside the standard CMYK range, a printer can use a specific ink such as Pantone. Open a Pantone color chart, choose the desired color, and the printer can add that ink to the print. Colors also have numeric values in both modes. CMYK values run from 0 to 100. A C value of 100 with M, Y, and K at 0 is a pure cyan: there is a high amount of cyan and none of the other pigments.

The next example gives C0, M0, Y0, K0. With none of the inks present, she says, “That would be black” (*negro*). She then gives a more complex combination—C96, M48, Y57, K3—and identifies it as a greenish blue. Cyan and yellow are relatively high and black is low, so she describes it as fairly bright.

RGB values run from 0 to 255. In this mode, higher values move the color toward white. Red at 255 with green and blue at 0 makes pure red. A list of color codes is provided in the course ideas for viewers to guess. The next lesson turns to palettes.

### Key points (derived)

- Print uses pigment values in CMYK; screens use additive light in RGB.
- Standard inks may not reproduce the brightest screen colors; Pantone inks can supply specific print colors.
- Read each color code in the scale for its mode: 0–100 for CMYK and 0–255 for RGB.
