## Composition techniques and the ground rule

Anchor: 5642 · U6 · L85 · 00:00–00:59

This lesson looks at different composition techniques, bringing geometry into play to build dynamic design structures. Balance, force, rhythm and gravity are the tools for generating unique compositions. To start composing what will become the label, more elements were developed, all coming out of exploring the mood board: the more that mood board and its details get explored, the more resources there are to work with. For now everything is worked in black and white — colour can be added along the way if wanted, but the point of this first part is to talk about composition.

## Playing with the shapes: not too literal

Anchor: 5642 · U6 · L85 · 01:23–02:18

The intention is for this to turn into a kind of traveller in time and space — as discussed before — but without being too literal about it. So the plan is to start playing with shapes that give a sense of a helmet, something a little solemn, and see how far that exploration goes. Maybe it becomes a double container, just to see what happens, imagining a possible label.

## Composing on the plane: symmetry

Anchor: 5642 · U6 · L85 · 02:18–02:57

The important part is working out how to compose within this plane. Here the design leans entirely on symmetry, though less symmetrical forms could just as well be explored. The idea is to see which pieces can hold others and start combining them — maybe one piece can serve as a container for other miscellaneous elements — and to see how everything generated so far can be integrated together.

## Closing: colour comes later

Anchor: 5642 · U6 · L85 · 04:44–05:02

For now it all stays in black and white, since colour will get its own attention a couple of lessons further on. Now that the tuning, rehearsing and the whole path behind it are done: ready to rock.

### Key points (derived)

- The label design is built directly from the geometric shapes extracted from the mood board in the previous lesson.
- The can (the support) is first treated only as a rough guide box — final size is not decided yet.
- The core shape (helmet/basket, mirrored into a symmetrical face-like form) is meant to evoke "a traveller in time and space" without being too literal, and relies entirely on symmetry for now, though asymmetric composition is also possible.
- Typography (the "Kashmir" wordmark, alcohol content, placeholder circular text) is placed early to test where information will sit, even before the final copy is set.
- The whole exercise stays black and white; colour is deferred to a later lesson.
