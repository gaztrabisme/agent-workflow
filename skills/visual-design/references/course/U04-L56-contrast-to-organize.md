## Contrast as an essential ingredient

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L56 · 00:00–02:00

Contrast is another of the essential ingredients of any composition. You'll learn to give your project precision, hierarchy and dosage. Let's look at the factors at play.

Without noticeable differences, order isn't possible in good design. Contrast is a good antidote to ambiguity: only by opposing things can we underline what matters and appreciate beauty. It's a mechanism of comparison between one or several elements. Sometimes it isn't a question of adding more attributes to what you want to highlight, but of taking attributes away from the other elements so the effect appears.

## Hierarchy is contrast of degree

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L56 · 02:00–02:40

At a more particular level, contrast helps modulate gradations of information. Think of a dictionary: how does it move from this entry to that one? Subtleties help you identify and categorize the information you're taking in. The difference between shapes and sizes in this example suggests a system of categories for the text.

![5642 · U4 · L56 · 02:03](frames/U04L56-F0203.jpg)

On screen: recurring course title and author sidebar [rest omitted] · "composición Del lat. compositio, -ōnis. 1. f. Acción y efecto de componer. 2. f. Obra musical o literaria. 3. f. Poema, texto de sentido unitario, normalmente en verso." (composition. From Lat. compositio, -ōnis. 1. f. The action and effect of composing. 2. f. A musical or literary work. 3. f. A poem, a text of unified meaning, usually in verse.)  
Shows: Dictionary-style definition slide, its three numbered senses set as a plain example of graded information.

Hierarchy, then, is a contrast of degree. There are extreme degrees and also intermediate nuances; each interval is a portion of content. These nuances differ progressively from the value before and the value after, so they can be recognized within the whole sequence.

### Key points (derived)

- Contrast is a comparison mechanism: it can come from adding attributes to what should stand out, or from removing attributes from everything around it.
- The visual center of interest relies on contrast at the level of the whole format; visual disparity sets a spatial reading order — first, then, last.
- Composition has opposites beyond the standard visual attributes: style (gestural/pictorial vs. photographic), full vs. empty, horizontal vs. vertical vs. diagonal, and image vs. text.
- Editorial design uses contrast across a page sequence, not just within a single spread.
- Hierarchy is contrast of degree: extreme values plus intermediate nuances, each differing progressively from its neighbours so the whole sequence stays legible.
- In the live edit, contrast was sharpened by: simplifying a competing map, resizing an oversized headline, setting a technically-driven 8mm top margin, fixing bilingual text alignment, giving a photo credit its own size and weight, removing a first-paragraph indent, rebalancing visual weight across a page sequence, and adding one muted colour accent.
