## Why the presentation matters

Anchor: 5642 · U6 · L91 · 00:07–01:05

In this lesson I'll work out how to put together the final presentation. I'll teach you how to generate a high-impact document that raises the quality of the project. We'll finish the project by telling a solid, one-of-a-kind story.

Having reached the end of the project, one of the most important parts is understanding the best way to present it. It often happens that a project reaches the end and we don't quite understand how to present all the work we did, which is sometimes very long, very tiring, very tedious — and the work loses its shine in that presentation, where we don't manage to get the client to fully understand our vision for the project and the intention we want it to create.

For a while now I've understood that the presentation was one of the most interesting parts to work on, which is why I focus a lot on developing it, on explaining how the project was carried out from its conception to the final versions and the final printed pieces.

## Closing thoughts: valuing the process

Anchor: 5642 · U6 · L91 · 12:59–13:48

This is how we'd be finishing the final visual identity project: published, thought through, and above all giving a lot of importance to the journey, the path from beginning to end, beyond the final result of the proposal. It's interesting to be conscious of each of the processes and stages our mind and body go through in this project and in this creative process, which takes us from an initial idea to a project and a final product. With this lesson we close out the whole theoretical part of the course — next, we'll see each other in the final project.

### Key points (derived)

- A presentation deck should carry the same visual identity as the project itself, not just list the final pieces.
- Opening with the concept, rather than a plain summary, hooks the viewer and sets up everything that follows.
- Nothing developed during the project is wasted: mood boards, color references and process sketches can be reused and re-curated as presentation content.
- Export finished artboards as a single PDF, or as PNG at 150 dpi (named for search, using product, category and discipline keywords) for uploading to a platform like Behance.
- On Behance, image order and the spacing between images matter as much as the images themselves for how the project reads.
- The cover image is the single most important image in a Behance post; choose one strong, final mockup shot to represent the whole project.
