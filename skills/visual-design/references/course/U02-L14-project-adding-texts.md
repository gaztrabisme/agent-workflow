## Choose the typefaces for the project

Anchor: 5642 · U2 · L14 · 00:00–02:56

The lesson puts the typography advice into practice on the Instagram posts. The aim is to choose typefaces that work together and build a typographic scale (*escala tipográfica*) so text sizes and weights remain consistent. A new Figma page keeps the work organized; she names it “Tipografías.”

She tests GT Flexa, which feels sober but has playful details in its endings and counters. She also wants to use Circa, but it has not yet been installed. Installing a font while Figma is open may not work, so she closes the program, double-clicks the font file, installs it, then reopens Figma and checks that the new face appears in the catalog.

Circa has a strong personality and geometric letter details, including square dots on the i. The two selected faces differ, so they do not repeat the same visual idea, but their letterforms share proportions: the A crossbars and heights are similar, as are features of the D. She plans to use Circa for sophisticated, characterful headlines and GT Flexa in smaller sizes and body copy. The Figma canvas shows “Patio” in both faces for comparison.

## Set the headline size and spacing

Anchor: 5642 · U2 · L14 · 02:56–04:58

To judge sizes, she creates an artboard in the proportions of an Instagram post. Circa will be used for the headline, so she enters a temporary title and adjusts its size, letter spacing, and line spacing. Figma’s default size of 200 points looks suitable: it is large, but it leaves room for a couple of words on each line. At this scale, the letters appear too far apart, so she reduces the letter spacing to about −4.5.

She then adds another line to see how the line spacing feels. The default is too open for her preference, so she brings the lines closer, to about 190. From a distance and close up, the words still have room to breathe rather than merge. The first values are provisional and can be adjusted later.

## Set a smaller body-text style

Anchor: 5642 · U2 · L14 · 04:58–06:57

She tests GT Flexa at a smaller size for body copy. A longer sample is more useful than a single line, so she writes a possible portfolio-submission message for people who may want to work at the studio. The headline and body need a stronger size difference. Although the text looks large on the canvas, it will be smaller on a phone, so legibility matters. She settles on 50 points for the body.

At that size, the default line spacing works well, at 60. The letters do not look as widely spaced as they do in the huge headline, but she tightens them slightly for consistency, to about −2. The resulting type scale is: Circa headlines near 200 points, about 190 line spacing and −4.5 letter spacing; GT Flexa body text at 50 points, 60 line spacing and −2 letter spacing. Keeping these values in mind will make it easier to build the posts consistently.

## Test copy in the post templates

Anchor: 5642 · U2 · L14 · 06:57–08:03

The content is not the priority yet because these are templates, but she tests phrases that could appear in future posts. One likely post will show photographs of recent projects. One project was an exhibition full of erasers, so she tries a headline about the exhibition and a short description, or a “New Project” label. She is pleased with the result and considers the typography choices clear enough to continue. The next lesson turns to color.

### Key points (derived)

- Use Circa for distinctive headlines and GT Flexa for smaller body text.
- Test type choices at the target post size, then review them at a phone-sized scale.
- Keep the project’s headline and body values consistent across templates.
