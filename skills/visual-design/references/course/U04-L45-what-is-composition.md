## Composing means bringing many things into a single whole

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L45 · 00:00–00:53

In this lesson we will see what visual composition is. I will explain the concepts of unity and set, and how to apply this creative strategy to the different disciplines of design and the visual arts. Let's look at it.

When we talk about composition, we are looking to make several things into one. Composing is organizing forms in space. This can be done by bringing them together, relating them, placing them in a certain way and order. Composition as a methodology lets us manipulate different parts of a given language to form or build a whole.

## Written composition: semantics and syntax

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L45 · 01:08–01:47

When we write, we communicate ideas through the use of visual signs, through letters. Choosing the right words correctly helps what we want to say be understood. This is the domain of semantics (*semántica*). But the meaning of words is not enough if we do not order them into sentences that can be understood. This is syntax (*sintaxis*). Just as in written composition, in graphic design we make use of a visual syntax to secure the sense of the messages we want to communicate.

## Musical composition: harmony, melody, rhythm

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L45 · 01:48–02:35

Composition is also talked about in music: the organization of sounds and silences in time to produce aesthetic effects in whoever listens to it. This has its own rules. Harmony (*armonía*) links chords and relates the different parts of a work. Melody (*melodía*) assigns meaning and its own identity to certain sounds of a composition; it hierarchizes them. Rhythm (*ritmo*), on the other hand, is the movement marked by the succession of musical elements. But rhythm is not the exclusive property of music, since we also find it in dance, in poetry, or in visual media — though in these it shows itself in space.

## Composition in the visual arts: the vocabulary graphic design borrows

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L45 · 02:36–03:22

The composition process is the most important step in resolving a visual problem. Just like music, the visual arts have flourished throughout history and built up a considerable body of work around their composition. Composition in graphic design is based almost entirely on the visual arts. Unity, weight, rhythm, movement, balance, contrast (*unidad, peso, ritmo, movimiento, balance, contraste*) are some of the concepts we know in graphic design that come from this pictorial heritage, and that we will develop in this course.

## A complex discipline: writing, music, and the visual arts combined

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L45 · 03:23–04:22

It's a complex and fabulous discipline that combines its composition criteria from writing, music, and the visual arts. Narrative structure first chooses the significant part of a story, to then arrange it in a logical and expressive way. For an audiovisual story to hold up over time, it has to pay special attention to the rhythmic and harmonic sequence of its scenes. Every shot or take is composed by placing its elements according to intentions of plastic beauty and narrative. The type of shot, the lighting, the contrast, or the depth of field are some of the visual criteria considered to achieve balance, movement, and variety — and they are the obsessions of the director of photography.

## Unity: what holds a composition's parts together

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L45 · 04:23–05:49

The search for a set is what subjects each of its parts. In composition, it's important to integrate, to harmonically relate all the plastic elements. The elements that make up a set share characteristics. They can have color or shape in common, they can be placed very close together, or perhaps different elements can come together by sharing the same direction. The options are infinite, but what's essential is that however varied a design's elements may be, they must be perceived as portions of the same set. Achieving the unity of a graphic piece is a natural goal of every composition. Every visual element has to support a main idea, which is the unifying axis of the work. It creates the way in which elements relate to one another and to space. It creates the perception of set and unity in the design. Visual unity is synonymous with coherence. What's coherent is perceived as logical, without contradictions that confuse us or generate distrust in us. Designing the whole is also designing the system of relationships that articulates the visual message. That syntax is what we will try to explain in this course.

### Key points (derived)

- Composition means organizing parts of a language into a whole; the course's language is the visual one.
- The same idea recurs across disciplines: semantics/syntax in writing, harmony/melody/rhythm in music, unity/weight/rhythm/movement/balance/contrast in the visual arts, and framing/lighting/rhythm in film.
- A composition achieves unity when its varied elements are still perceived as parts of one coherent set.
