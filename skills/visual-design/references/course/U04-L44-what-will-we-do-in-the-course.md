## Composition (*composición*) follows the content

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L44 · 00:00–00:48

For me, composition is a tool that lets you honor the content. In the end, it is the content — the message you want to communicate — that dictates how your design proposal should look. A well-achieved composition can capture the attention of its intended audience, but it can also generate enjoyment, an aesthetic pleasure, in the person experiencing it. We designers generally tend to separate what is the container from what is the content, when in reality they are two sides of the same coin of communication.

## Choose a composition for the intended effect

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L44 · 00:49–01:35

For example, if you wanted to communicate a message of dynamism, energy, or innovation, resources that increase contrast and hierarchy, and that emphasize diagonal lines and movement, might be the right ones to achieve that goal. However, if your interest is to stop the reader or viewer so they reflect, so they take their time to contemplate a piece, there are other resources, such as symmetry (*simetría*), such as a tighter balance, that will let you get through to the message you need to convey to the other person.

## The course moves from principles to a project

Anchor: 5642 · U4 · L44 · 01:36–02:32

When it comes down to it, design is a work of translation: we put an abstract idea or a text into visual codes. In this Domestika course I am going to explain the key principles, elements, and tools for carrying out an effective composition. You will also discover some elements of visual language and how design can articulate them. You will learn the basics of composition, and how to use rules and proportions to articulate visual space. You will understand how grids (*retículas*) work, to structure the information of a simple or complex project. These tools are fundamental to achieving an effective design that has clarity, that has salience (*pregnancia*) and force, while at the same time stimulating your creative capacity.

### Key points (derived)

- Let the message determine the composition: the form honors the content.
- Use contrast, hierarchy, and movement for a dynamic effect; use symmetry and tighter balance for a reflective effect.
- Design a piece together using the course ideas; optionally redesign earlier work.
