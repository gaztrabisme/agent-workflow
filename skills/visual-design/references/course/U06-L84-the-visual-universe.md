## Rule: don't reference already-designed work

Anchor: 5642 · U6 · L84 · 04:59–05:59

The same approach continues with the other available images, adding them one by one to the mood board to keep enriching the gaze. An important rule: don't add images that are already graphic design — material already processed by another designer's eye. The goal is references with a lower degree of existing graphic design: architecture, photography, clothing and so on. The reason is to avoid getting hooked on references someone else has already designed. What matters when developing a creative process is building your own search criteria rather than letting the process be swayed by it — not getting too influenced by trends, avant-garde flourishes or fashionable winks currently circulating on design platforms.

## Three ways of looking: close, distant, complementary

Anchor: 5642 · U6 · L84 · 10:58–12:07

Working from this finished mood board, the plan is to keep using the rule of geometry — the geometric data — to generate three types of elements that will later complement one another. In the square column, the most rational one, that's the close gaze (*mirada cercana*): any image, design or figure that becomes the most primary, principal image of the proposal. In the circle column, the distant gaze (*mirada lejana*): textures, patterns and other details that complement the primary image in a more interesting way. In the third column, the triangle, the complementary gaze (*mirada complementaria*): any miscellaneous element that accompanies the design.

### Key points (derived)

- The mood board is built column by column: square = rational references, circle = emotional references, triangle = attention/personal references — and columns can be combined (e.g., Sahara/Morocco covers both emotional and attention).
- Rule for image search: avoid material that is already graphic design (posters, already-processed photos) as a design reference; look instead at architecture, photography and clothing, to keep the search criteria personal rather than trend-driven. Exception: a poster can still go in, as with the Led Zeppelin concert poster, when it's used only to understand a band's or era's aesthetic rather than as a current design reference.
- References gathered for this project: the *Physical Graffiti* album cover, a Led Zeppelin concert poster, the band's four member symbols, 1970s London street/clothing photos, a Moroccan mosaic fountain, desert dune textures, *El Eternauta* (comic), *The Time Machine* (1960 film), the Mellotron (instrument + internal mechanism diagram), and a Celtic, boat-shaped instrument.
- Once the mood board is finished, it is mined for three types of visual elements: close gaze (primary images, square), distant gaze (textures/patterns, circle) and complementary gaze (miscellaneous elements, triangle).
- Geometric shapes are then extracted directly from the reference images (e.g., the album cover's windows and doorways) to build a personal shape vocabulary for the next stage, composition.
