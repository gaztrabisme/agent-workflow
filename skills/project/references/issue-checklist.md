# Issue checklist

Run before a workbook or plan goes to anyone. Each line is a check; the rule and its reason live where the link points. Run it with the evidence at hand: the owner's file, the last issued version and, where a price was set, the price target; lines 1, 2, 12, 13 and 20 cannot be checked without them, and line 21 needs the covering text, and a line that only reads labels can be passed by labelling. A line that does not apply to this project is marked "n/a" with a word of why, not skipped (for example lines 1 and 2 on a first draft with no owner's total); the reviewer checks that reason too. How each line is itself tested: [checklist test](checklist-uat.md).

1. The owner's effort total (their method) and, before any new pricing, their cost total (their cost rules) are recomputed in a visible cell beside their stated figure; a mismatch is listed as a question to them, not corrected. → [Summarize only from detail](wbs-workbook.md#summarize-only-from-detail)
2. The bridge runs from the owner's total (or, after a first issue, the previous issued total) to ours, its lines sum exactly to the difference, and each line names the rows it moved and a reason specific to them; a price-driven cut is its own line (line 12). → [Summarize only from detail](wbs-workbook.md#summarize-only-from-detail)
3. Every task row's basis is actuals or analogy (each naming the past project), a parts breakdown shown in the row, or expert judgement; expert-judgement rows name the lead who must confirm them. → [Every row states its basis](estimating.md#every-row-states-its-basis)
4. Each row's confidence follows the stated test (high: scope traced to the client's words and no input missing; medium: rests on assumptions, missing inputs or another party's work; low: not traced, or a test of the approach failed), and no low-confidence row is quoted as a price. → [Where contingency lives](estimating.md#where-contingency-lives)
5. Each risk-budget line names its cause, evidence, effect, owner and what would remove it; risks not yet identified are their own line; no missing input is covered by a general buffer; tolerance, risk budget and change budget are kept apart, and a single template buffer says which one it stands for. → [Where contingency lives](estimating.md#where-contingency-lives)
6. A contingency row exists for test, review or rework cycles, or the decision to have none is recorded with its reason. → [Structure and staff the WBS](wbs-workbook.md#structure-and-staff-the-wbs)
7. The size threshold (the team's, or 30 days) is stated in the workbook, the rows above it are listed, and each shows parts with their own figures or waits on an investigation row. → [Every row states its basis](estimating.md#every-row-states-its-basis)
8. Each derived percentage cites the team's past project it was measured on; rows derived from coding are set on their own basis after any coding adjustment; an AI adjustment is one column with a reason per task type, and no other factor, rate or summary formula applies it again. → [Activities derived as a percentage](estimating.md#activities-derived-as-a-percentage), [AI-assisted work](estimating.md#ai-assisted-work)
9. Rows drafted for another discipline show the drafter, the lead who confirms, and whether that lead has confirmed; an unconfirmed row is visibly marked as a draft. → [Drafting into a team's WBS](wbs-workbook.md#drafting-into-a-teams-wbs)
10. Every total on every sheet is a formula whose range covers all its detail rows; no subtotal is typed. → [Summarize only from detail](wbs-workbook.md#summarize-only-from-detail)
11. Duration is shown as at least two options, each with team size, end date, slack and cost, with a recommendation that keeps slack. → [Schedule with capacity and slack](planning-method.md#schedule-with-capacity-and-slack)
12. Each concession (free work, a lower rate, a removed cost, an allocation below the formula) is its own line with its amount and the price-setter who approved it; a reduction made to meet a price is a separate bridge line, never a change to a productivity factor or rate. → [Concessions and closing a price gap](business-case-and-tolerances.md#concessions-and-closing-a-price-gap), [Cutting to reach a price](business-case-and-tolerances.md#cutting-to-reach-a-price)
13. No testing, documentation or design effort was lowered to meet a date or a price; compare those rows with the previous version, and a reduction there needs a reason other than the price. A cut to reach a price removes features or is shown as an accepted margin or risk (line 12). → [Fixed price, fixed scope](business-case-and-tolerances.md#fixed-price-fixed-scope), [Cutting to reach a price](business-case-and-tolerances.md#cutting-to-reach-a-price)
14. The documents the client will see are listed; in each, no text offers what the exclusions exclude, and each cut has been removed from acceptance criteria, demo lists, appendices and slides. → [Fixed price, fixed scope](business-case-and-tolerances.md#fixed-price-fixed-scope)
15. The assumptions that explain how the price is built (PM percentage, contingency policy, margin) are on an internal sheet, and no file the client receives contains them in any sheet (hidden or visible), formula, comment, defined name or label. A rework or contingency row of effort in the WBS is not a pricing assumption. → [Two business cases](business-case-and-tolerances.md#two-business-cases)
16. Another company's work is registered as a dependency naming its owner, with no effort against it anywhere in the workbook. → [Plan from products, then activities](estimating.md#plan-from-products-then-activities)
17. Each dependency has a named provider, a deadline as a week or WBS milestone, the WBS IDs it blocks (all of which exist), and the effect if it is late. → [Register dependencies](wbs-workbook.md#register-dependencies)
18. The workbook has one legend; every fill used, including conditional formatting, appears in it with one meaning. → [Hand over a workbook the planner can edit](wbs-workbook.md#hand-over-a-workbook-the-planner-can-edit)
19. A reviewer who has not seen the drafting, a fresh one each round, has checked this version; open findings after the third round have gone to the PM. → [Hand over a workbook the planner can edit](wbs-workbook.md#hand-over-a-workbook-the-planner-can-edit), [Independent reviewer](review-and-audience.md#independent-reviewer)
20. The version number and date are in the file name and on the first sheet; every difference from the issued copy that we did not make has been carried into this version or raised with its author; a workbook the recipient now edits by hand is marked final and no longer regenerated. → [Hand over a workbook the planner can edit](wbs-workbook.md#hand-over-a-workbook-the-planner-can-edit)
21. Every figure quoted in the covering email, note or slide that sends or cites this file matches the file it cites (same value, same period, same scope); a figure taken from another file names that file. → [Reviews before issue](lifecycle.md#reviews-before-issue)
22. Every WBS row has an entry in the skill-and-book map: the routed skill's reference files and verified book chapters, or "none found" with the searches run, or "not applicable" with a reason; each brief sent for a row carries that entry in its Load section. → [Planning flow](lifecycle.md#planning-flow), [Lane plan](run/lane-plan.md)
