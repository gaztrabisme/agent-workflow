## What Adobe After Effects is for

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L03 · 00:00–01:06

In this lesson, we will look at what Adobe After Effects is for and the uses I have made of it over my career. We will consider its pros and cons compared with other animation and video software.

Here we are inside After Effects, and I will briefly explain what it is and what I have used it for. This is a project in After Effects. First, After Effects is a tool for digital video compositing (*composición digital de vídeo*) based, as you can see here, on layers. As in Photoshop, this is a great advantage: layers, including their blending modes, layer styles, and other features, behave exactly as they do in Photoshop. So if you are comfortable using Photoshop, this should initially be a fairly easy step.

After Effects can serve many purposes in audiovisual work. That is why I usually call it “the Swiss Army knife of audiovisual work”—After, for friends.

## Three uses in audiovisual work

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L03 · 01:06–02:52

The characters were drawn in Illustrator and imported into After Effects, but they could just as well have been made from scratch and animated in After Effects. Now let us go back and look at another piece: a social-media campaign. For this piece I used After Effects as a compositing (*composición*) tool. By compositing, I mean assembling different parts that come from different media. For example, this section of bricks in the background is 3D; the rotating shoe is stop motion that I made by photographing it one frame at a time; the smoke is a frame-by-frame animation made in Adobe Animate; and the characters are also 3D. Compositing is the process of bringing everything together and adding a post-production finish. That is the second use for After Effects.
