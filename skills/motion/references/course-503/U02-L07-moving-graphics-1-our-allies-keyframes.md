I have already made a shape layer with a circle. A keyframe is the basic building block of an animation. An animation can contain many keyframes that record movement, scale changes, or another property change.

## Create position keyframes

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L07 · 01:20–02:51

The basic layer properties are expanded. Beside each property is a small stopwatch. Move the playhead (*cabeza lectora*) to the point where the first keyframe should go; for this example, I move to exactly two seconds and click the stopwatch for Position. That creates the first keyframe there.

Next I move the playhead to another point, such as four seconds, and change Position. After Effects creates a second keyframe. The change in value between those two recorded states makes a position animation. There is no animation before the first keyframe because no earlier position keyframe has been set. Another way to create a position keyframe is to move to another time, select the cursor tool with V, and drag the shape layer (the captions mis-transcribe “shape layer” as “same layer”). Moving the circle changes Position, so it creates a keyframe too.

## Edit the motion path and its corners

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L07 · 02:51–04:36

The preview shows a line representing the circle’s movement. Clicking a point on this path reveals Bezier handles (*tiradores Bézier*), which let me change how the circle travels. I can also change a keyframe’s Position while the playhead is elsewhere; the path updates to show the movement.

The Pen tool shortcut is G. When I move the Pen tool over a point, its icon changes. Clicking can make the movement linear; I can change it back to a Bezier movement with softened corners and handles to create curves. I can edit the trajectory in the preview. To clear the example, click the stopwatch to remove all keyframes, or select one or more keyframes and delete them. I reset Position to put the circle back in the middle.

## Animate scale with either the property or the preview

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L07 · 04:36–05:34

Now I animate Scale. Move to the start of the timeline and click its stopwatch to make the first keyframe. To make the circle twice its original size, enter 200, giving an animation from 100% to 200%. Delete the second keyframe to clear that version.

The second method is more freehand: change the scale directly in the preview by manipulating the object. Hold Shift so the shape does not become distorted. These are the two ways to create the scale keyframes.

## Treat keyframes as movable animation material

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L07 · 05:34–07:08

The main idea I want you to take from this lesson is that keyframes are the clay of animation. You do not have to place them precisely on the first try, because you can move them later. You can move one or several keyframes on the timeline. Clicking a property value selects all the keyframes for that value so they can be moved together.

For example, I make a Scale animation in which the circle gets smaller and then grows again. Select the three keyframes and, while holding Alt, drag one of them. This keeps their relative timing while stretching or compressing the spacing. Less time between keyframes makes the animation faster; more time makes it slower. Practice adjusting them, even frame by frame, until the animation has the timing and speed you want.

## Preview the animation at its playback speed

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L07 · 07:08–10:16

The preview area is a time range on the timeline that limits what plays. Its lighter region has a beginning and an end. Move its blue handles to change the range, then press Spacebar to preview. The green portion shows what has been loaded and is playing in real time. Move a keyframe and preview again to judge the timing and speed.

To set the preview range without dragging its beginning and end, move the playhead to the desired start and press B; move it to the desired end and press N. The whole range can also be moved while keeping its length, or lengthened at either end. In the Preview panel, Spacebar is the assigned playback shortcut in my setup. You can choose another shortcut there. Remember that keyframes are clay in your hands for making animations; have fun with them. Now that we have seen what keyframes are, we are ready for animation curves.

### Key points (derived)

- A keyframe records a property value at a time; After Effects animates the change between keyframes.
- Move or retime keyframes after creating them: closer spacing speeds the motion, wider spacing slows it.
- Use the preview range to judge the animation at playback speed.
