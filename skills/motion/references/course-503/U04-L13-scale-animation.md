## Let the character determine the scale movement

Anchor: 503 · U4 · L13 · 00:00–01:38

This is our first animation: a scale animation (*animación de escala*). Base it on the character you chose and on that character’s attributes. I have imported a short scene from Dirty Harry so we can watch Clint Eastwood in action. Watching the film again gives us many clues. In this scene, a bank robbery is taking place with all the violence imaginable, while Harry is eating a hamburger. He seems not to care much about what is happening; he is reluctant to do his job, although in the end he gets out. Notice that he is even finishing the hamburger. He heads straight toward the man he has already shot and hit. This is the poor man bleeding. After watching your reference, draw conclusions about your character and write them down, whether the character is someone well known or not.

For this animation, I want him to be reluctant to get moving at first. The animation curve (*curva de animación*) at the beginning will be quite pronounced (captions: *culpa de animación*). Then he will move decisively, with a little drag, and when he arrives there will be very little easing because he will go straight into action. Moving your arms or acting out the movement yourself can help you work out how the animation should feel.

## Bring in the visual explorations and build a square scene

Anchor: 503 · U4 · L13 · 01:38–03:18

Keep the library of visual explorations from the earlier work close by. For this scale exercise, the element can simply be a circle or a square, but the earlier explorations will be a reference when we make more complex animations. First import the color palette created earlier. The simplest way is to double-click in After Effects’ Project window, go to the folder containing the file, and place it in the Footage folder created earlier so the project stays organized. ## Set the starting and ending scale, then ease the movement

Anchor: 503 · U4 · L13 · 03:18–04:20

Now we can animate. Press S to reveal Scale. Set a keyframe for the final position, or final state, of the animation; go to the beginning and set the scale to zero. We now have the basic animation, and need to decide how it should move.

## Refine the curve and extend the slow start

Anchor: 503 · U4 · L13 · 04:20–05:42

The first preview may feel too slow, so move the handle closer to the keyframe and preview again. Limit the preview area with N, or move its boundary manually. Return to the Graph Editor and preview once more. Adjust the handle again, and use the fit-curves button to bring the graph into view. At some point the Bezier handle cannot smooth the curve any further, but I want Mr. Eastwood to be extremely reluctant at the beginning. To extend that slow start, choose the Pen tool with G and add another keyframe. Switch back to the Selection tool with V, select the new point, and lower it carefully. Preview the change. Move it to the right so the beginning takes even longer, adjust the other point, and bring this keyframe a little closer. This is fine-tuning.
