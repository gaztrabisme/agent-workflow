## Tavo and the start of my motion-graphics work

Anchor: 503 · U1 · L02 · 00:36–01:39

503 · U01 · L02 · 00:36–01:39

The first influence, and a fundamental one in getting me started in motion graphics, is Tavo Studio (captions: *Tab* at 00:38 and *Tau* at 00:43). When I discovered Tavo’s work, I went crazy for it; I loved it. It was one of the things that really pushed me to throw myself into learning After Effects and everything to do with motion graphics. Why? Because he was a guy from Seville doing incredible work. When someone is relatively close to you, even geographically, you think, “Hey, maybe there’s a way to work with him or reach his level.” I was lucky enough to get a job at the studio in 2008.

Tavo has been doing this work for many years. If you look at his portfolio at Tavo.es, you’ll see a motion-graphics style with a lot of weight, based heavily on 3D technique, but with very polished, tactile art direction (*dirección de arte*). It’s spectacular work; he reinvents himself a little with each project, and it always feels current. So, respect to Tavo, and thank you for giving me the chance to work with you at the beginning.

## Collaborating with Podenco Studio

Anchor: 503 · U1 · L02 · 01:39–02:47

503 · U01 · L02 · 01:39–02:47

Next we move from Tavo to Podenco Studio, a studio in Valencia directed by Antoni Sendra. He’s a phenomenon, a force in motion-graphics direction and production in general, especially for music videos and cool pieces. I’ve been very lucky to collaborate with Antoni on a few projects. One example is the campaign for Giovanni Rana, for which my role was compositing (*composición*) and animation. It was a campaign for the Rana brand for German-speaking countries, and being involved in it was an amazing experience. The captions render the brand as “Yonani Rana” and the campaign phrase as “para llevar en ranas”; the product shown is Rana pasta.

Another project we did together—this one here—was the official lyric video for Green Day’s single “Revolution Radio.” It was another real pleasure, and a brief bout of madness that I was lucky to take part in with him. In general, these are people I’d like to work with again. They motivate me to raise my level so I can work with them: they’re great people and very talented. Take a look at all their work. I hope you enjoy it as much as I have.

## Valistika’s illustration-led work

Anchor: 503 · U1 · L02 · 02:47–04:00

503 · U01 · L02 · 02:47–04:00

Continuing with people I know whose work is exceptional, I don’t know how to describe Valistika’s work. I’d use a swear word, but let’s just say it’s fucking amazing. Miguel and Guadalupe make the work. I’m lucky to be friends with Miguel, who is from Santander and works mainly in illustration. At first glance it’s more straightforward graphic design, with a few touches of animation, but it’s extremely high quality, strongly based on illustration, and beautifully finished. Whatever work you look at, it’ll amaze you.

Look at their work on Valistika.com and on their Behance profile. You can see what they make; I could start browsing and never stop. You can enter at any point and find something inspiring. Some pieces are static, but you can imagine them in motion and immediately think, “If I could animate that, it would be a hit.”

There are many people and studios I’ve worked with who are references for me. They do very good work, and they’re also kind, professional people who make collaboration a pleasure. I’ll quickly go over a few more. Those I don’t mention, forgive me; but you all know who we’re talking about.

## Valistika’s original illustrations

Anchor: 503 · U1 · L02 · 05:24–07:12

503 · U01 · L02 · 05:24–07:12

We’ve seen the work of these amazing people. As the icing on the cake, I’ll show you the work of Valistika Studio (captions: *Ballistica Studio*). Here are some samples of their work and some originals. In this case, for example, we can see the cover of Yorokobu. What I like about Valistika Studio is the level of virtuosity they have in illustration. All their graphic design has a very handmade look, based on illustration. On top of that, they’re incredibly humble; they make these amazing pieces and such varied work. Here is another example, more based on typography.

They’re people I know who do work that I think is exceptional technically and always has a lot of style. This one isn’t suitable for minors. Their work is very illustration-based but also extremely eclectic, and I think their level is impressive. For example, let’s look at this piece here, made with a ballpoint pen (*boli*). It’s astonishing. This is an original they made with a ballpoint pen. Believe it or not, every one of these little hairs was drawn with a pen, and it was for a T-shirt. As they told me, perhaps the detail wouldn’t show, but I’m amazed by their level of perfection and how they keep pushing themselves. If they can do it, I probably can’t—but maybe you can reach this level.

I’ve shown you the work of people I know who inspire me and have given me a lot. I’ll leave two additional links in the resources to motion graphics with geometric animation very similar to what we’re going to make: Stephen Keleher and Sander Van Dyck. In the next lesson we’ll see what Adobe After Effects is and what it’s for.
