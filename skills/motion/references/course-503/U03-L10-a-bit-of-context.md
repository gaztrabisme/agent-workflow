## Material Design as a movement reference

Anchor: 503 · U3 · L10 · 00:00–01:21

In this lesson I’ll explain what we mean by designing movement (*diseñar el movimiento*) through examples available online, including Google’s Material Design and Audi’s Brand Portal. Let’s look.

Today the clearest reference is Google’s Material Design manifesto. Type material.io/design into your browser (captions: *material. Io. barra design*), or search Google for Material Design and you will land on this portal. Google introduced Material Design to the world in 2014. The idea was for all its systems, browsers, and applications to be based on the same principles, both visually and in how they behaved, including every element.

The part that interests us most is movement, which is covered here. If you have never looked through this system, it is like a brand manual: a guide to how everything in Google’s world should work. Look at it; it is genuinely interesting for us as designers.

We’ll go straight to the part we like and care about: Motion. The menu on the left is extensive, like a Pandora’s box that reveals a huge number of topics. We’ll click Motion and go to the first section, Understanding Motion.

## Motion principles and easing

Anchor: 503 · U3 · L10 · 01:21–02:42

In Understanding Motion, Google sets out the principles that should form the basis of movement across its systems. They are Informative, Focused, and Expressive: movement should inform, focus attention, and express.

We have now moved to Speed in Motion, and the subsection that interests us is Easing, or motion smoothing (*suavizado del movimiento*). It explains acceleration and deceleration curves (*curvas de aceleración y desaceleración*), which will be our fundamental tool in this course, apart from our brains. It also talks about timing (*timing*): when things start, when they stop, and how they behave in motion. We will work with curves in a more logical than technical way.

## Audi’s movement system

Anchor: 503 · U3 · L10 · 02:42–04:08

Now we’ll look at a second case: movement systems applied to a brand. Search Google for Audi Brand Portal; the first result will take you to Audi’s brand portal. I’ll provide the link as well. It is interesting for how it sets out movement and animation.

In the menu on the left, go down through Basics to Animation. This section presents how the brand’s elements move. Movement systems have become especially important with user interfaces and the digital world. Here you can see the animation curve: how elements start or accelerate, then decelerate or stop, and how an animation ends.

The portal also has more specific user-interface examples and a small library that sets out principles for how elements should move, including their direction and scale. We can see how the curve behaves in terms of timing, duration, and smoothness. This is very close to the kind of work we will do in our own movement system or language.
