## Set After Effects to English

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L04 · 00:00–01:10

In this lesson, we will install After Effects in English, and I will explain why. We will look at the interface and customize it so that the tools we need are in the same place.

My first recommendation is to install Adobe After Effects in English. I will have it installed in English, so you will be able to follow my instructions more easily. Before installing the software, open the Creative Cloud application, which is where you download and install it. At the upper right, open Preferences and choose Creative Cloud. There are two options here. The language selected here is the language that the apps you download will use. If it is set to Spanish, for example, the app will download in Spanish. This is a recommendation, not a requirement, but you will generally find much more material about After Effects in English, so it is a good idea.

## Create and name a composition

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L04 · 01:10–02:59

Now we have opened After Effects and are inside the program. First, create a new composition (*composición*), which is a kind of container. There are three ways to do this: use the large button in the middle; choose Composition, then New Composition, from the menus above; or use Control on a PC or Command on a Mac.

![503 · U2 · L04 · 01:23.00](frames/U02L04-F0123.00.jpg)

On screen: "New Composition" / "New Composition From Footage" / [rest omitted]  
Shows: After Effects start area with two composition-creation buttons above an empty timeline.

This dialog lets us adjust the settings, talk about specifications, and consider formats. The course project is to create a library of twelve animations, with Instagram as the main distribution channel. We will therefore work in a square format. You can see the width and height here. We will discuss the most suitable Instagram dimensions at the end, but we will use a generous 1080 by 1080 pixels for now. Set the pixel aspect ratio to Square Pixels every time. The frame rate (*frecuencia de fotogramas*) is usually 25 frames per second; we will also discuss that at the end.

After creating the composition, which will be our workspace, we can still change any setting, such as its size, frame rate, duration, or something else. To change the settings later, go to Composition, then Composition Settings. The shortcut is Control or Command-K. For example, this duration is very long, so I will change it to ten seconds. That gives us a ten-second timeline.

Next, change the composition name. The easiest way is to click its name in the Project area, press Enter, and type a new name—for example, “Animation.” The more complicated way is to open Composition Settings. Remember that the simplest way is to select the name, press Enter, and change it there.

## Read the interface

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L04 · 02:59–04:41

Now let us look at the After Effects interface step by step. At the upper left are the dropdown menus, through which we can access most operations and commands. Below them is the Project window, where the compositions we create will appear. The videos, audio, and other external files we need for our animations will also appear or be stored there.

In the middle is the Preview window, where we can see the work in progress. The most important part for this course may be the timeline. We move its playhead backward or forward, and this is where we will create keyframes (*fotogramas clave*). The layers are on the left. We do not have any yet, but, as in Photoshop, we will put them one above another and move them up or down.

I want us all to be roughly aligned before we go further. On the right, the many panels and buttons may start to look intimidating. At heart, most are familiar design tools, similar to Photoshop, apart from features such as Preview.

## Choose a workspace and arrange panels

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L04 · 04:41–05:59

At the top you can see options such as Standard and Small Screen. These are preset layouts, or workspaces, for different kinds of work. The menu is under the two small arrows where I am pointing. After Effects includes several presets. It is useful for everyone to work with the same layout. Here we will choose Animation. The layout is less cluttered and gives us roughly what we need for motion-graphics work (the captions render this as “Modesto Graphics”).

If a panel or controller we need is not open, the Window menu lists the panels that are currently hidden without a checkmark. For instance, if I need Tracker, I can select it and it will appear somewhere in the interface. You will also see the familiar hamburger menu—the three lines at the right of a panel. From there, I can close the panel or unlock it so I can move it to another part of the interface.

## Choose the layer types for this course

Anchor: 503 · U2 · L04 · 05:59–07:15

Now let us focus on one of the central topics: layers. If we open the Layer menu and choose New, we can see all the kinds of layers available in After Effects. I want you to see how extensive this menu is, because it can help make the program feel less intimidating. For this course, though, we will use at most three kinds. We will work with shape layers (*capas de formas*; the captions render this as “6 layers”), perhaps use Null Objects (*objetos nulos*) to help with animations, and possibly use text. We will not use the rest. We will not cover lights or cameras.

It is useful to understand that this course is partly about enjoying the work: we will not study 70–80 percent of the features available in After Effects. This software is like a Swiss Army knife. In this case, we will use the screwdriver and the knife. That is all. We have taken our first steps in After Effects; in the next lesson, we will move into the world of layers.
