## Why planning comes before production

Anchor: 433 · U2 · L04 · 00:00–01:03

In this lesson, I will introduce the three fundamental pillars to identify before starting any kind of storytelling project. Before we dive into the story we want to develop, we need to emphasize a stage that is essential to the success of our creative process and therefore of the final piece: planning (*planificación*).

It is not unusual for both amateurs and professionals to make the mistake of starting a project without planning first. By planning, I do not just mean being ready to work and gathering the materials. I mean being able to picture the whole project, to take an overall scan (*radiografía*) that helps us discover where we will work and what rules we will use to make the project.

## The individual pillar

Anchor: 433 · U2 · L04 · 01:03–01:56

Personally, I work with what I call the tandem pillars (*pilares tándem*). There are three: the individual (*individuo*), time, and the objective. I call them tandem because each has two sides, two heads, or two perspectives that help define it. Let us start with the individual.

On one side, there is the sending individual, or sender (*emisor*): who commissioned the project? Who is speaking behind it? This could be a client, a company, or a brand. It is important to be clear about who they are, to understand them and know them, so we can also know what they expect from us.

On the other side is the receiver (*receptor*): who is this video for? I need to know the person, individual, or group who will receive the message. In other words, one individual is the receiver and the other is the sender (the captions repeat *receptor* in the second reference before giving *emisor*). Being clear about both lets me define the tone and language of my piece.

## The time pillar

Anchor: 433 · U2 · L04 · 01:56–02:46

The time pillar helps us understand the more abstract dimension of our process. One side is the duration of the message I want to communicate, or approximately how long that message will be. It is different to work with a five-second message, when I have five seconds to tell a story, and to work with an hour-and-a-half message. It may sound obvious, but knowing this immediately sets boundaries for the work.

The other side is how much time I have available. If I have a week to solve a project or a year to solve it, I can use different personal capacities to work on it. Managing this time dimension lets us project and ground the reality of production.

## The objective pillar

Anchor: 433 · U2 · L04 · 02:46–04:12

Finally, there is the objective (*objetivo*). One side is what I want from the viewer. Once my receiver has experienced the narrative I have presented, what reaction do I want them to have? (The captions read *chiquito* immediately before this question.) I might want them to like the video or laugh. Do I want them to cry? Do I want them to stand up and vote for an initiative I am encouraging them to support? What do I want?

Being clear about this objective is the only way we can later measure the real success of the narrative we made. The other side is my personal objective: what do I want for myself? What do I want to achieve with this piece? This is not what I want from the viewer; it is what I want for myself. I might want to explore a new narrative language or new visual ways to complement the story and narrative I am building.

When we take on and prioritize this level of challenge as part of the piece’s objective, stories start to become more interesting and projects begin to have their own unique value. Once these objectives are clear, we can really open up the narrative and visual possibilities of the final piece.

So the three pillars are: the individual—who and for whom; the objective—what I want from the viewer and what I want for myself; and time—how long it lasts and how much time I have available.

## From the script to production

Anchor: 433 · U2 · L04 · 04:12–04:42

To plan, I obviously need an input. This is usually called the script or voice-over (*guion* or *voice over*). In this particular course, we will not work on developing the script or voice-over, because that development would be a course in itself. But we will work with a script that I will provide as the base material for the exercises in the following lessons. Once we have that script and have put the plan together, we can move on to producing the story.
