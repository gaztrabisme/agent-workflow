## What counts as a character

Anchor: 433 · U4 · L10 · 00:00–00:49

In this lesson we are going to talk about characters (*personajes*) and their role in telling a good story. Character handling is another important tool you should know. Every story has characters, whether humanoid or abstract. Can a motion-graphics video have characters? Of course: they can be typography, geometric shapes, and so on. A character is a mental construction made through language and image. A character is any actor in your story who has a defined role so the story can be told properly. Characters should make sense in relation to the story, of course, but the most important thing is for the narrator to see them as real actors who need direction to bring out their best.

## Character roles and development

Anchor: 433 · U4 · L10 · 00:49–01:45

Characters may be protagonists (*protagonistas*), who integrate the organization of events and are therefore a structural part of the action. The actions related to them, or carried out by them, form the axis around which the story is structured.

Secondary characters (*personajes secundarios*) are those who do not have a very important role in the development of events but provide greater coherence, understanding and consistency to the narrative. Finally, extras (*extras*) are characters who are not permanently present in events, but whose participation can help order, explain, relate, coordinate or even delay their development.

All these characters share the same DNA in keeping with the overall narrative, but they do not necessarily share the same degree of narrative and visual development. How fully developed they are depends on their role in the story.

## The pillars of character construction

Anchor: 433 · U4 · L10 · 01:45–02:28

To build a character, whether a living being or a graphic figure, we need to define certain pillars that bring it to life, always in service of the story we want to tell. First, we need a visual distinction (*distintivo visual*). How do we recognize the character? By its shape? By a detail in its anatomy? By its colour? By an accessory? And so on.

The second pillar is the character’s personality (*personalidad*): how it moves, how it speaks, and what makes it unique and recognizable to others. Finally, we need to establish the laws (*leyes*) that govern its behaviour. Does it walk on two feet or roll? Is it soft or fragile? And so on.

As a side note, remember that none of these tools are very rigid laws. In fact, sometimes a character is created first, and a story is built around it.

## Character, action, conflict and story

Anchor: 433 · U4 · L10 · 02:28–02:57

A good character is fundamental to a good story. Without a character, we simply have no action. Without action, we have no conflict. As we saw at the beginning of the course, without conflict we simply have no story. You should devote the time needed to create a good character so that later you can trust its role as part of your story.

![433 · U4 · L10 · 02:34](frames/U04L10-F0234.00.jpg)

## Knowing even the simplest character

Anchor: 433 · U4 · L10 · 02:57–03:09

No matter how simple the character you are going to work with, take the time to know it inside and out, even if it is just a simple square. Now that we have reviewed a character’s role in a story, we can move on to the next tool: transitions.
