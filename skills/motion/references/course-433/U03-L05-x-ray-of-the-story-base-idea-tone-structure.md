## The story X-ray and the equation

Anchor: 433 · U3 · L05 · 00:00–00:50

Next, we will look at what I call the story X-ray (*radiografía de la historia*), which gives us an overall view of what we are going to tell. To do that, we first look at the equation that makes it up. In this unit we will work with the story X-ray; as its name suggests, we take an X-ray of the process. The process in between what we have and the shape of a memorable story is the core DNA of what we know as storytelling. In this first lesson, I will introduce the equation in broad terms.

## Input and output

Anchor: 433 · U3 · L05 · 00:50–01:41

To start working on a story, we first need a brief: some base material to work with, the thing I am going to tell. It can be very basic: a joke, an anecdote, an explanation, or a horror story. It makes no difference. What matters is that we have base material, raw material that will be transformed into our story.

This is what I am going to tell, which is quite different from how I am going to tell it. That is our final output. We work on base material related to the content that will be presented in the narration, but how I tell it is something completely different. That is where an intermediate process takes place and the magic happens. This process transforms raw material, which may even be bland, into something memorable. That intermediate process, that middle link, is what we call the story X-ray.

## The three links in the story X-ray

Anchor: 433 · U3 · L05 · 01:41–02:24

Regardless of what the story will look like, this equation supports it and allows us to build worlds, characters, and more. How is this story X-ray composed (captions: *bibliografía*)? It consists of three main links. The first is the base idea (*idea base*), our general concept: the idea that lets us create this world. It is also the premise (*premisa*) of the story. The second link is tone (*tono*), the tone in which we tell it. Tone is fundamental because it gives the story personality, and that personality distinguishes one story from another. A clear personality makes our storytelling stand out from other stories. The third link (captions: *aldabón*; later captions: *enlabones*) is structure (*estructura*), which gives the story order and support: a beginning, a development, and an ending, so the story works properly.

## Experimenting with the chain

Anchor: 433 · U3 · L05 · 02:24–03:12

These three links form part of an experimental chain: they are a series of elements we can play with in a laboratory, where the order of the factors does not affect the result. This is the moment when we can experiment and make magic in the lab, creating the DNA of the storytelling for our project. Now that we understand the equation, we can look at each of its links in the following lessons.
