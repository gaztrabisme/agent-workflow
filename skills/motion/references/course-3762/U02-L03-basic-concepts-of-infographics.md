## Infographics make information accessible

Anchor: 3762 · U2 · L03 · 00:08–00:44

In this lesson I will talk about infographics, a tool for telling stories and visualizing information or data. Infographics can be used across different media: digital, print, or interactive. What is an infographic for? It visualizes data and tells stories. Its main objective is to turn information into images that anyone can access. It helps connect with a shared imagination (*immaginario collettivo*) that is easy to understand, so information becomes clearer and more immediate for the audience. It should be clear and understandable to everyone.

## Imagining a future train

Anchor: 3762 · U2 · L03 · 01:13–02:00

Another example of telling scientific and specific material in an editorial context is Hyperloop. Here we imagined something that does not exist: a future way to travel between cities. We described how the technology developed and how trains evolved over the years, leading to the features of something that has not yet been built.

We showed the interior and pointed out its strengths and what makes it different from traditional trains. We imagined possible routes and the cities that might adopt this technology. We also compared it with other available technologies, a comparison that helps show how the technology could reduce distances between cities.

## Color as a visual code

Anchor: 3762 · U2 · L03 · 02:15–03:08

An infographic can do more than convey information; it can also establish an identity system, as in Change, a festival connected with architecture and sustainability. We used shapes and colors to create a clear system and code. The elements here address the disposal and recycling of plastic waste through 2015. The color code is key to interpreting the information: blue represents recycled plastic, which is only 19.5%, while green represents discarded plastic. In this graphic, color becomes the code for reading the data.
