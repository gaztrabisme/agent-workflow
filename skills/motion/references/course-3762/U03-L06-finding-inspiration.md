## Finding sources and giving a project context

Anchor: 3762 · U3 · L06 · 00:00–01:50

In this lesson, we will talk about visual research (*ricerca visiva*) and how to find inspiration for a project.

Visual research (*ricerca visiva*) is essential for our work and for finding inspiration for a project. It is often one of the best parts of starting a project because it lets us look at the work of artists and designers. Inspiration can also come from things that are not strictly visual or graphic. It may come from architecture, a walk, or nature.

The goal of visual research is to create a context for a project and place it in its surroundings. This process helps us develop new ideas and connect our work with the outside world. It also helps us experience and understand, through other people's work, how a project has been developed and perceived. Exploring leaves room for experimentation and innovation.

Visual research is not just searching for images online or on Google. It is important to look beyond online searches. In the studio, we often do this through books, and it is essential to build a personal library. It lets us explore new ideas and deepen our knowledge. Over time, we add to that library everything we love and everything that continually inspires us. For this project, too, we researched through books. Here are some examples.

## Pierre Charpin: from sketches to objects

Anchor: 3762 · U3 · L06 · 01:50–03:00

The first book is a monograph on the French designer Pierre Charpin (captions: *Pierre Charpentre*). He always begins with a very important sketching phase. His visual research starts in the imagination: he first creates images and interactions, and often color marks that later become concrete objects.

In one example, Charpin creates an illustration from lines and a particular behavior. These develop into a series of baskets and fruit bowls, showing his innovative vision.

## Curva: color and transparency

Anchor: 3762 · U3 · L06 · 03:00–03:40

A Pierre Charpin project that strongly inspired our final project is his collection of vases called Curva. As in his other work, this collection began with a series of illustrations. Charpin explores transparency and color, both highly relevant to our project about cocktails.

## Max Bill: movement and stillness

Anchor: 3762 · U3 · L06 · 03:40–05:31

Another book that greatly inspired this project is by Max Bill, a Swiss designer whose work ranges across architecture, painting, graphic design, and industrial design. In some of his works, Bill explores movement and stillness through shapes and colors. That is useful for our final project and its cocktails. These works inspired our choices of color and form, and especially helped us think about the movements to give the cocktail ingredients.

Visual research is essential before moving on to the mood board and sketch phases. It is important to collect images, words, and free thoughts. In the next lesson, we will make the mood board for our final project together, using the inspirations we have gathered to capture the essence of our project.

### Key points (derived)

- Build a visual context from many sources, including architecture, nature, artists' work, and books.
- Use the references to explore form, color, transparency, and movement before sketching the project.
