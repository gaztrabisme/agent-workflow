## Why sketch on paper

Anchor: 3762 · U3 · L08 · 00:00–00:44

With the mood board complete, it is time to begin sketching (*schizzo*). You can work on paper, an iPad, or a whiteboard. In our studio, we often use a sketchbook. Our relationship with paper is important because it lets us sketch freely and write ideas on a page. This preparation phase is essential: it helps us develop and visualize our thoughts, transforming the visual references into something intriguing and useful for the final project.

## Start with cocktail glasses and their contents

Anchor: 3762 · U3 · L08 · 00:44–02:17

Now we begin sketching. The first thing that comes to mind when we think of cocktails is the glasses. Glasses have simple shapes, but what matters most is what they contain.

Two cocktails can be represented simply. A triangle and a line are enough to evoke a Martini. A black circle and a line suggest an olive. A cocktail glass with ice and a lemon wedge can also be imagined easily. I have drawn the contents in layers because the data, and therefore the cocktail's contents, are central to this project.

## Represent each drink as layered data

Anchor: 3762 · U3 · L08 · 02:17–04:31

Now we focus on the contents. We chose to use histograms (*istogrammi*) and concentrate on layering the ingredients in each cocktail so that its parts become visible.

Cocktails contain different ingredients: alcoholic bases such as gin or tequila, liqueurs such as Campari or Vermouth, and soft drinks. This creates significant variation in the contents and ingredients shown in our visuals, reflecting the diversity and complexity of each cocktail. We can imagine these shapes as the ingredients that make up the drinks.

## From static diagrams to movement and transparency

Anchor: 3762 · U3 · L08 · 04:31–06:00

This way of visualizing the drinks may seem a little static, so we can try to give the shapes movement. I will explore shapes and data together with movement and transparency, in keeping with our mood board, to create a dynamic visual experience.

## Three visual behaviors and cocktail decorations

Anchor: 3762 · U3 · L08 · 06:00–08:23

At this point, I have identified three kinds of behavior. The first concerns the forms and how they move. It evokes the fluidity of liquid in a glass and suggests waves moving slowly.

The second is the use of different shapes. As you can see, I have used shapes besides rectangles to show that a cocktail can contain different ingredients, such as gin and tonic water, which combine to create a varied experience.

The final concept is transparency, which is characteristic of liquids. When different elements overlap, they create a new color. We should also remember the cocktail decorations, so we decided to add more elements to the sketch. I wanted to include details such as an orange slice, along with ingredients such as salt and sugar, which are commonly used in cocktails to enrich the taste.

To recap the sketch process: we began with the glasses, which represent the idea of the cocktail. Then we analyzed the data, the essential ingredients of the cocktails. Finally, we animated the contents by introducing movement into the forms, along with transparency and, at the end, the decorations. Now that the sketch phase is finished, we will move on to the storyboard in the next lesson.

### Key points (derived)

- Begin with recognizable glasses, then make the contents and ingredient data visible through layers.
- Add movement, varied shapes, transparency, and decorative details to make the diagrams feel more like cocktails.
