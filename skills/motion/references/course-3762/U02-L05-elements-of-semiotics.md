## What semiotics adds

Anchor: 3762 · U2 · L05 · 00:08–00:22

Semiotics (*semiotica*) is the study of signs (*segni*) and their meaning. In infographic work, it helps us represent concepts clearly and simply.

## Designing a clear sign

Anchor: 3762 · U2 · L05 · 00:22–00:58

This matters when we make icons (*icone*). We needed to represent a trash bin, so we studied which elements were essential. The lid is open and the waste is going into the bin.

Semiotics also helped us represent when vegetables expire. With simple shapes, we made figures that evoke vegetables. With other simple shapes, we represented a magnifying glass and the idea of a calendar.

## Giving familiar objects a new visual cue

Anchor: 3762 · U2 · L05 · 01:37–02:25

Now we move to an animated example. The client asked us to tell the story of an iconic design object in an ironic way. We chose the Atollo lamp by Vico Magistretti (the Italian captions say *Eclisse*) because its shape resembles a mushroom. We decorated the lamp's shade to look like a mushroom cap and added leaves and flowers so that a small forest appears beneath its base.

In the same series, we worked with Bruno Munari's Falkland lamp. Its shape resembles a beehive. We added small colored dots that, when moving, evoke bees.

## Using basic design to combine ideas

Anchor: 3762 · U2 · L05 · 02:25–03:08

Basic design (*basic design*) is another useful tool. We had to make a cover about Bologna and Easter. We chose two iconic elements: Bologna's porticoes and an Easter egg. The egg gave form and structure to the vaulted arcades. We added small capitals to make the porticoes clearer and to emphasize the importance of those elements. Basic design helped make the structure of the portico easier to understand.

## Using geometry to carry data

Anchor: 3762 · U2 · L05 · 03:08–03:42

Basic design helped again when we had to tell the history of cosmetics and present the related data effectively. We chose geometric shapes because they recall cosmetic packaging, then arranged them with specific colors. The result is a pattern that brings us into the world of cosmetics while also suggesting data. Differences in size, shape, and color emphasize the transformation of data and create a visual link between cosmetics and data analysis.

## Hiding one form inside another

Anchor: 3762 · U2 · L05 · 04:22–05:12

In another project, we were asked to explore the idea of form. Basic design was essential: we hid shapes inside other shapes, so that some of them can only be distinguished by looking closely. This creates a game of discovery and surprise.
