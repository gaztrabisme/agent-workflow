## Setting scene timings and marking their endpoints

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L11 · 00:02–01:56

Now we are going to organize and name the layers (*capas*) and arrange the timing of each precomposition (*precomposición*). We will make simple animations, without much detail, to see how the video develops. I have all my precompositions separated over time. In practice, yours will all take up the full frame like this; use Control + Shift + D to trim and delete the excess. I separated mine by estimating.

As I said, our animation was going to be 40 seconds. I gave the title sections no more than two or three seconds. They are things we read, and then they pass quickly. I gave panels (*placas*) with a lot of information about ten seconds so people have time to read everything. Keep in mind that not everything will appear at the same time. Usually one text appears, then another. We have to guide the person watching the video across the screen so they can take in all the information; the animations are an extra detail that decorates the infographic. Again, give titles two or three seconds and information screens with a lot more time so they can be read. That is all.

When everything is divided, you can go to the end of each scene, double-click, and drag this to create a marker (*marcador*). The marker gives you an idea of where each scene ends, so we can try to animate everything over a few seconds for the viewer to see it. Do not animate anything for this part, or it will be cut off and look wrong.

## Animating the title and subtitles

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L11 · 01:56–04:43

What details can we animate at first? We can go step by step and select simple things, for example the title. When we try to click something and this whole panel appears, we look at what is selected. For example, I do not want to select the glows here, so I lock them. Then I can select other small things like this. We also have these two subtitles. Obviously, if everything is organized and named, it will be much easier to find.

I am going to try to have two styles of text animation. The first will simply use scale (*escala*), position (*posición*), and transparency (*transparencia*). Go to the end, where we want the text to be visible, and click Position, Scale, and Opacity (*opacidad*) under Transform. We will not need Position, just Scale and Opacity. Now go to the beginning, hold Control, make the scale a little smaller, and set Opacity to zero.

Select the keyframes (*fotogramas clave*) and go here to the graphs, which give the animation an extra touch. If we view it as it is, the text appears simply. Adding this gives it a little acceleration and deceleration. When we select the keyframes and go to this icon, you may not see this graph. Choose Edit Speed Graph; it will show the speed of our animation. It starts slowly, goes fast, and finishes slowly. Select this, use these handles to edit it, and keep trying out the animation.

We can do the same with the two subtitles. Maybe here we can use some position. Go to the end point where we want the text to reveal, click Position and Opacity, and press U to see the keyframes we have created. Hold Control to make the movement more subtle. Without holding it, the movement is faster, and if you press Shift it goes off the screen. This text can come in from this side and the other one from the other side. Set the opacity to zero, add the easing again, and now the text is animated.

## Converting the dotted ring to vectors and animating its rotation

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L11 · 04:43–05:56

Another small detail we can animate is this circle. For many things we bring in from Illustrator, we will convert them to vectors in After Effects because they have some properties that are not available here, and those let us animate certain things. For this ring, go to Create. In some other versions of After Effects this may be somewhere else; in this version you have to open Create. It converts the object to a vector, and now you can see many more options to animate.

Within the dotted stroke, we are going to animate Offset (*desplazamiento*), which makes the dotted line rotate. Since this will last three seconds, go here and add a little movement. Then the line will keep rotating like this. At the beginning of our animation, where we can see everything, we see the text come in and the dotted lines rotating. That is all; we will add more details to the remaining items later.

## Moving between information panels

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L11 · 05:56–07:20

The idea is to animate everything simply and smoothly, giving viewers time to read each piece of information. We will do the same with PayPal, with this panel. Each one will have a different design. In my case, here, where it cuts from one panel to another, I will add a Position keyframe at the beginning to the whole precomposition and another for Opacity. Move forward a little and shift it to one side. Leave the opacity at zero. Do the same with the panel above, but start it from the end. Set its opacity here, and this panel will come in from this side with its opacity at zero.

Press U to see the keyframes. Apply Easy Ease, which gives our animation more sense, and there we have a small transition from one panel to another. We can review the timing, the reading time, and the flow of the animation. We have now seen in detail how we will begin animating the panels at this stage.
