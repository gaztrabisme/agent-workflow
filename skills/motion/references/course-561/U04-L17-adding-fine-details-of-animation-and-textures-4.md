## Finishing the rocket scene and staggering the employees

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L17 · 00:00–02:13

561 · U04 · L17 · 00:00–02:13

This is the last composition. The text is animated: I brought it in from Illustrator and applied the effect that makes one character appear at a time, so it looks as if it is being written. Now I need to arrange everything and animate a couple more things. I have to animate the glow (*brillo*) of this text so it appears together, then lock it.

This precomposition (*precomposición*) lasts a while, so I will make the rocket leave from here and travel through the whole animation in the precomposition. I will animate these employees so they all scale (*escala*) from zero. I select them all, create a Scale keyframe (*fotograma clave*), go to the beginning, set it to zero, and select them again. Then I adjust the graph. The tedious part is moving each layer one frame, then the next, one by one. I use a plugin to save time and make it a little faster. Now they all enter. I will put one frame, or less, between them so they come in faster. I close what I have already animated to clear some room, then continue with simple animations.

## Linking and rotating the planets

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L17 · 02:13–05:38

561 · U04 · L17 · 02:13–05:38

I continue with the other text. The plugin applies the same effect I would apply to the shape paths, but with one click, so it is a little faster. I stagger the layers so they enter one after another. I do the same thing here: the glow follows the Moon, the Moon follows the Earth, and the Earth rotates. This animation is long, more than ten seconds, so I will give it two turns.

This layer has three elements. I cannot separate them because it is one Photoshop layer, so I duplicate it three times and make three copies. In the first copy, I mask (*enmascarar*) it and leave only the Sun. The mask does not have to be perfect; the point is for the Sun to show. I do the same thing with another copy but set its mask to Subtract. I select an orbit, then set this one to Subtract. Now we have the three layers.

We can see the Earth turning and the Moon orbiting the Earth. I use the same principle, create another Sun, and link Mars to this Sun. I use the same keyframes and give it more rotation. Much of my project has unnamed layers, which is chaotic, although I can find them by clicking. I recommend naming them as you go, or it becomes a mess. Here is the other Moon. I do the same thing: the glow follows it, the Moon follows the Earth, and the Earth rotates. I use the same thing again, with two turns.

## Revealing the remaining text and spheres

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L17 · 05:38–09:00

561 · U04 · L17 · 05:38–09:00

Continue making everything appear consecutively. Once the text up here appears, in my animation I will make this other text appear. I use the principle we used for the loading bars. Once this text appears, I make the line draw from top to bottom. I will animate all these spheres so they come from above.

I continue applying the same effects; keep everything simple. With so many elements on the screen, we do not need to do many different things—simple effects are enough. I have lost a layer here. No problem: I bring the text in from Illustrator, paste it here, and line it up roughly where it was. I apply the same effect, starting it roughly there. To apply it, I keep the layer selected and either double-click the effect or drag it onto the layer. It builds very slowly, so I adjust the keyframes to make it faster.

## Reviewing the animation and preparing to render

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L17 · 09:01–10:18

561 · U04 · L17 · 09:01–10:18

You can more or less see what we have done. Your designs and animations will all be different, but the idea is to use simple things: rotation (*rotación*), opacity (*opacidad*), position (*posición*), and a little scale. Always check the keyframes, render (*renderizar*), and see how everything turns out and how the transitions work. That is all. We are almost ready. Now we will render our project and look at the publishing possibilities.
