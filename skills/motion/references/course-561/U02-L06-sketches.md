## Organizing the collected information

Anchor: 561 · U2 · L06 · 00:02–00:44

Now we will make the sketches. You can make them in Illustrator or Photoshop; I am going to use Illustrator. We already have the information we collected, and now we are going to distribute it a little better. The idea is to curate all that information and decide what will be useful and what will not. We should not go overboard with the scenes and make a very long video. We create each block and put the data in it. You can make precompositions (*precomposiciones*) and canvases. The point is simply to put the information you have onto the page.

## Keeping the information legible

Anchor: 561 · U2 · L06 · 01:49–03:07

Although I am going to opt for a simple, abstract design, the important thing is for the information to be legible. Typography is very important: it has to be simple, and you should not use script fonts. You can do this in Illustrator, Photoshop, or even a notebook. Do not worry if you do not know how to illustrate; just make icons and add information. Keep things simple: circles and lines.

## Designing the three infographic subjects

Anchor: 561 · U2 · L06 · 08:50–10:45

To recap the design of the panels, since I am covering three topics, my idea is to put in a title and its explanation, then another introductory title and its explanation, and continue that way. Elon will cover PayPal, which is about money; Tesla, which is about technology and electric cars; and SpaceX, which is about space. I added all those small facts here on the first screen, so to speak: a small spacecraft, planets, dollar signs, and random little elements floating in this character's universe. I did the same with the titles, adding a little information and random elements.

For the information panels, I added details to some of them. For example, since Tesla is about cars and the technology of electric cars that do not use fuel, I tried to put all the facts into boxed screens, like the display in a car, with the information we see in a car. For SpaceX, I tried to use space, ships, and planets. The main thing is to arrange the information and leave space for it to be read. You can add details or leave them out. You can remove some things to leave more room for reading and keep each bit of information small. Keep the information in roughly the same places so that it can be read.

## Sequencing the information

Anchor: 561 · U2 · L06 · 10:45–12:04

Center things and add some large information too, to attract attention. Here, because there is a lot of data, I tried to put each fact into its own little square. Sometimes small bits of information that cannot be read are only there as decoration. These sliders will look much better later in the animation. When there is a lot of information, introduce it in parts: first this appears, then this, then this, in sequence. Make all the planets, lines, and circles cause that movement.

If you can use employees, people, or things like that, always use simple icons and concrete facts. I used quite a lot of text in my example, but I will give it enough time to be read. Again, we will introduce the information sequentially: first the title enters, then this text, and so on for everything. Now that our sketches are ready, we will move on to designing the panels.
