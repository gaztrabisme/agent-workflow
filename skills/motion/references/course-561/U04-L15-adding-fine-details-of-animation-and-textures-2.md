## Setting up the PayPal scene

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L15 · 00:00–02:19

This scene is much more complex, but little by little we add the details. In this case I am locking the text so I can animate everything else: the glows and the elements underneath. Nothing very complex; we simply animate opacity (*opacidad*). We see when the circles enter and synchronize the glows' opacity with them.

For the background miscellaneous elements we apply the same method we have been using in the previous scenes. This creates a system for the whole video, so the animations are not all wildly different. The scenes are longer, and we should always play them back and try to read the information we put in. Give it the right amount of time, sometimes a little more so people can appreciate the animation and read the information.

To animate the glows, I brought the glow from the precomposition outside this scene into this one. Now we move the layers so they appear one after another.

## Drawing paths and sequencing items

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L15 · 02:19–05:55

Now we will apply Trim Paths (*recorte de trazados*) to these lines, so that after the location icons scale up, the lines appear. Apply Easy Ease. The keyboard shortcut is usually F9 on Mac. We keep checking how everything is going. The first part looks quite good. The items at the bottom and all the miscellaneous elements in the middle are still missing.

Using the same principles as before, we can avoid applying Trim Paths to every line by moving the anchor point (*punto de anclaje*); you have to do this manually. Sometimes plugins can solve the problem in a single click. For example, the one I use, Motion 2, lets you move all the anchor points at once. It is a paid plugin, but I recommend it because it saves us a lot of time and the sometimes tedious work of moving each item one by one. Here we do it with one click. I unlink the Scale dimensions so the lines scale horizontally without scaling vertically. That makes it look as if the lines are being drawn. We adjust the speed graph to make the animation more interesting and less monotonous.

Here is another plugin I use: it moves the layers sequentially so we do not have to move them one by one. With one click it moves them all. We scale each item and apply a sequence. Sometimes two keyframes are enough. In this case we will animate the check marks, and we will use Trim Paths because the shape is more complex and cannot be scaled. Create the final keyframe; at the beginning everything is contracted. Adjust the speed. This sequence plugin works by selecting the last layer first and going through to the first layer; it creates the sequence for you. We finish this part. It all looks good sequentially, and we arrange it in order from top to bottom, left to right. After everything appears, we make the glow appear at the end.

We apply the same principles: scale from zero to one hundred, use Easy Ease and adjust the graphs. Make a sequence, arrange it, and that is done. As you practice, you will get faster at all of this. With this kind of animation I sometimes used to take hours, and then I was surprised when I could do it in ten or fifteen minutes. Experience gives you a lot of speed. Again, we arrange things sequentially and turn on the glow. We had been locking layers so we could select others. With many items, textures and glows, the scene gets more complex. Sometimes we want to select one thing and select another instead, and we cannot select it unless we lock layers or search for them by name. If we are organized, we can find them; if not, it is a mess. Always keep your work organized.

## Finishing the infographic scene

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L15 · 05:55–08:46

We will scale the planet and give it a rotation that goes past the marker. Remember always to animate a little farther, just in case. We may have to extend a scene; the animation may have ended earlier without our noticing, leaving it cut off. What you do here depends on each person: you might want one text to appear first, give people time to read it and then show the next, or have everything appear quickly in sequence and leave the animations as decoration while people read all the information on screen. It depends on your taste, the design and the amount of information.

Here I scale from zero to one hundred and add another keyframe in the middle, at a slightly larger scale, so it looks as if it bounces: it scales up too far and returns to its original place. It is always better to work on everything together, keep checking it, and add the speed and sequence of the animations at the end. We position the items so they appear at the right moment, and that is done. All that remains is to animate the glow. We find Opacity; in this case I lowered it a little because it was too strong.

As we go along, we will notice things in the design that we can retouch in After Effects, recreate, or retouch in Illustrator or Photoshop. It depends on what you have, but everything can be fixed or improved. Sometimes effects or layers cannot be converted to vector or do not work very well. We create them in After Effects and solve the problem.

## Adjusting the bounce and completing the sequence

Anchor: 561 · U4 · L15 · 08:46–11:45

Since I applied that bounce effect, I will apply it to these other circles too, to keep the same system. I create the keyframe in the middle, select everything, apply Easy Ease and go to the graph to adjust the speed. The final keyframes are too fast, so the effect does not come across well. We will apply it again: Control+Z, stretch the final keyframe a little and apply Easy Ease. That looks a little better. Make a sequence and check how it looks.

We will animate this item, always keeping the same style. We will animate the position of this transparent layer underneath. We move the text so it appears at the end, and the sequence is practically finished.

As we build the animation, the project becomes heavier. It is a very full project: we have vectors, textures from Photoshop and Illustrator, and we keep adding animations. Even when they are simple, sometimes they load the file and the computer too much, and the project gets heavy. If some of you have a smaller computer, it can sometimes be better to render the project. We will look at the rendered, finished video later, and if you see anything you can go back and adjust it, because when After Effects is very heavily loaded, it does not play at real speed. We have finished these first scenes. We are watching them in real time, and we move on to the next scene.
