# Visual direction: make the information readable and repeatable

## Reader and scope

**Reader:** a planner developing the visual world and assets for a motion sequence. **Scope:** collect references, choose a visual direction and adapt information into forms that remain true to their source. Static chart selection belongs to `information-design`; overall composition belongs to `visual-design`.

## First idea, stated as the rule

- Gather references from more than one source and organize the ones that connect to the subject, proportion, behavior, color or transparency. The cocktail project moves from a recognizable drink toward abstraction. (3762 · U3 · L06 · 00:00–01:50; 3762 · U3 · L07 · 00:56–02:43; 3762 · U3 · L07 · 05:00–06:46)
- Analyze what is strong in references instead of copying an image; three or four attributes can anchor a direction. (503 · U3 · L12 · 03:18–04:19; 503 · U3 · L12 · 07:29–08:33)
- For information graphics, choose an encoding that suits the information and makes the visual code understandable. (3762 · U2 · L03 · 02:15–03:08; 3762 · U2 · L03 · 03:08–03:22; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 00:52–01:34; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 03:06–03:34; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 03:34–04:20)
- Preserve data while adapting its appearance. Keep editable source data before separating graph elements; resizing can preserve proportions, while editing component points can distort them. (3762 · U4 · L12 · 08:41–11:07; 3762 · U4 · L13 · 00:00–01:11; 3762 · U4 · L13 · 01:12–02:49)
- A repeatable information-plate system can use a consistent color, type, size, stroke and grid language, with titles and room for reading. (561 · U2 · L06 · 00:44–01:49; 561 · U2 · L06 · 01:49–03:07; 561 · U3 · L07 · 01:43–02:44; 561 · U3 · L07 · 02:45–06:02)
- Texture can be limited to elements that benefit from emphasis; additional layers bring extra animation work. (561 · U3 · L09 · 00:02–00:40; 561 · U3 · L09 · 02:49–04:01)

## Failures

**Problem 1: the mood board becomes a copying brief.**
- **The failure:** copied colors or shapes stand in for an analysis of why a reference works. (503 · U3 · L12 · 03:18–04:19)
- **The fix:** name the reference's useful attribute, then make a new design that serves this subject and audience.

**Problem 2: adaptation changes the information.**
- **The failure:** a visual edit distorts the source data's relative proportions. (3762 · U4 · L13 · 01:12–02:49)
- **The fix:** keep source values and an editable source version; compare every transformed value or area with its source.

## Check before moving on

1. **T5 — Visual system and encoding:** pass when the reference attributes and information mappings are named, all required values still match their sources, and repeated elements follow the declared system.
