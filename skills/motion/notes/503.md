## Method steps

1. **Study movement systems.** Look at references such as Google Material Design and Audi’s brand portal; decide what movement should communicate, where timing and easing matter, and how a system keeps motion consistent. 503 · U3 · L10 · 00:00–01:21; 503 · U3 · L10 · 01:21–02:42; 503 · U3 · L10 · 02:42–04:08
2. **Choose a character as the movement guide.** Pick someone with distinctive body language; describe their movement with attributes that can guide every exercise. 503 · U3 · L11 · 00:00–01:21; 503 · U3 · L11 · 01:21–02:53; 503 · U3 · L11 · 04:31–06:02
3. **Collect visual references and analyze them.** Search until the references approach the intended visual world, follow related images, save them to a board, and analyze design choices rather than copying a source image. 503 · U3 · L12 · 00:34–01:48; 503 · U3 · L12 · 01:48–02:29; 503 · U3 · L12 · 03:18–04:19
4. **Choose visual attributes and build a design direction.** Compare opposites such as simple/complex, organic/synthetic, order/disorder, and monochrome/polychrome; record at least three or four attributes, explore compositions through iterations, and keep a palette for later use. 503 · U3 · L12 · 04:19–05:17; 503 · U3 · L12 · 05:17–05:54; 503 · U3 · L12 · 05:54–06:39; 503 · U3 · L12 · 06:39–07:29; 503 · U3 · L12 · 07:29–08:33; 503 · U3 · L12 · 10:12–11:50; 503 · U3 · L12 · 11:50–12:41
5. **Read or act out the character before designing a specific motion.** Revisit the reference performance, note what the character does and how it feels, and use your own body to help decide the movement. 503 · U4 · L13 · 00:00–01:38
6. **Plan complex actions before animating.** Sketch the elements, their starting states, their actions, their cause-and-effect order, and how the sequence will resolve or loop; paper and pencil are recommended, while the teacher also demonstrates planning in Photoshop. 503 · U5 · L18 · 01:23–03:25; 503 · U5 · L19 · 00:00–01:50; 503 · U5 · L20 · 00:00–01:25; 503 · U5 · L21 · 00:00–03:16
7. **Build a small movement library from individual exercises.** Make scale, position, position-with-stretch, rotation, and a two-element swipe/drag; keep the chosen character’s attributes present in each motion. 503 · U4 · L13 · 00:00–01:38; 503 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–00:56; 503 · U4 · L15 · 00:00–01:32; 503 · U4 · L16 · 02:29–03:46; 503 · U4 · L17 · 00:00–01:58
8. **Set the important states, then shape timing and travel.** Establish initial and final states, preview, and refine keyframe timing and curves until the start, acceleration, travel, and arrival express the intended character. 503 · U4 · L13 · 03:18–04:20; 503 · U4 · L13 · 04:20–05:42; 503 · U4 · L14 · 02:28–03:48; 503 · U4 · L14 · 04:25–05:48; 503 · U4 · L16 · 02:29–03:46; 503 · U4 · L16 · 03:46–05:22
9. **Choreograph several elements by assigning roles and timing.** Identify a leader, followers, triggers, and responses; stage the elements in sequence, adjust delays and curves, and hide other elements when they obscure the timing judgment. 503 · U4 · L17 · 01:58–04:15; 503 · U4 · L17 · 04:55–06:27; 503 · U4 · L17 · 06:27–08:20; 503 · U5 · L18 · 03:25–06:34; 503 · U5 · L18 · 06:34–08:31; 503 · U5 · L18 · 08:31–10:01; 503 · U5 · L20 · 04:33–06:31; 503 · U5 · L21 · 10:30–13:46
10. **Review the whole sequence and choose its ending.** Preview a bounded range, tune event timing and holds, then decide whether the join should be a seamless loop or an intentional jump. 503 · U5 · L18 · 16:07–18:46; 503 · U5 · L19 · 07:40–10:45; 503 · U5 · L19 · 10:45–11:28; 503 · U5 · L20 · 10:46–12:26; 503 · U5 · L21 · 13:46–16:37; 503 · U5 · L21 · 16:37–17:26

## Principles and claims

- Google Material Design names three movement goals: informative, focused, and expressive. 503 · U3 · L10 · 01:21–02:42
- Easing lets elements accelerate and decelerate; transitions without easing look stiff and mechanical. 503 · U3 · L10 · 01:21–02:42
- When elements should move together, synchronize their acceleration; speeding up one while another should be slowing down makes the impression disorganized. 503 · U3 · L10 · 01:21–02:42
- A movement system can specify direction, scale, curve, duration, and smoothness; when several elements or stages are animated, define a clear sequence and animate as few elements simultaneously as possible. 503 · U3 · L10 · 02:42–04:08
- A distinctive character and its body-language attributes can act as the consistent basis for a series of animations. 503 · U3 · L11 · 00:00–01:21; 503 · U3 · L11 · 04:31–06:02
- Use references to understand what is strong in a design and find a direction; the teacher says not to reproduce one reference image exactly. 503 · U3 · L12 · 03:18–04:19; 503 · U3 · L12 · 07:29–08:33
- Three or four visual attributes can anchor a design direction; the teacher’s palette uses three or four colors, five at most. 503 · U3 · L12 · 07:29–08:33
- Iterating by duplicating a composition and testing added complexity or alternatives is a useful way to explore; a visual library should guide how simple exercise shapes look. 503 · U3 · L12 · 08:33–10:12; 503 · U3 · L12 · 10:12–11:50
- Keyframes can be moved and retimed after first placement; closer keyframe spacing makes the animation faster, wider spacing makes it slower. 503 · U2 · L07 · 05:34–07:08
- In the Value Graph, time runs horizontally and property value vertically; steep portions mean faster animation and flatter portions mean slower animation. 503 · U2 · L08 · 06:22–09:12
- A Bezier handle pulled vertically can change the value and cause an overshoot or bounce; avoid changing the value when the intended ending should not overshoot. 503 · U2 · L08 · 06:22–09:12
- Stretching a form at the fastest part of its travel is used to suggest speed; the teacher calls stretch an animation principle. 503 · U4 · L15 · 00:00–01:32
- A swipe needs at least two elements: one drags and one is dragged; a slight follower delay can make the interaction feel reluctant. 503 · U4 · L17 · 00:00–01:58
- In a cause-and-effect choreography, a visible trigger can start a second movement; the trigger and response should be synchronized so the relationship reads visually. 503 · U5 · L20 · 04:33–06:31; 503 · U5 · L20 · 10:46–12:26
- A planned sequence can leave room for improvisation in personal work; for client work, the teacher says to follow the presented plan more closely and explain storyboard changes. 503 · U5 · L19 · 00:00–01:50; 503 · U5 · L19 · 07:40–10:45; 503 · U5 · L21 · 08:56–10:30
- A loop may be an exact seamless join or an intentionally imperfect jump, depending on the effect desired. 503 · U5 · L18 · 12:39–14:40; 503 · U5 · L19 · 07:40–10:45; 503 · U5 · L19 · 10:45–11:28; 503 · U5 · L20 · 10:46–12:26
- A simple bounce need not be extremely physical or realistic, and the teacher cautions against adding too many bounces. 503 · U5 · L21 · 10:30–13:46
- For a reel, cut on movement so adjacent actions connect; select the strongest pieces, though a simpler piece can provide a quieter moment. 503 · U6 · L22 · 01:26–02:49; 503 · U6 · L22 · 02:49–03:19
- Variants can help neighboring tiles contrast and work together; offset timing can make a mosaic more dynamic. 503 · U6 · L22 · 05:05–06:22; 503 · U6 · L22 · 09:13–11:10
- The teacher recommends that a reel also work silently; music is optional and can provide practice synchronizing image and sound. 503 · U6 · L22 · 11:10–12:42
- At the course’s 2019 recording, the teacher recommends a 20–40 second reel and says to check current platform limits when publishing. 503 · U6 · L22 · 14:53–16:23

## Checks the teacher uses

- **Does the motion express the chosen character?** The teacher previews and adjusts until the movement reads as the attributes chosen for the character; this needs judgement by eye. 503 · U3 · L11 · 02:53–04:31; 503 · U4 · L13 · 05:42–06:14; 503 · U4 · L14 · 04:25–05:48
- **Does the animation start, travel, and arrive at the intended pace?** The teacher previews repeatedly while changing curve handles and keyframe spacing; this needs judgement by eye. 503 · U4 · L13 · 03:18–04:20; 503 · U4 · L13 · 04:20–05:42; 503 · U4 · L16 · 02:29–03:46; 503 · U4 · L16 · 03:46–05:22
- **Does the path or curve overshoot and create an unwanted bounce?** The teacher checks the curve against the intended value and previews the result; render-measurable in principle by measuring the property or object position against its intended endpoint over frames (proposed measure; see Derived). 503 · U2 · L08 · 06:22–09:12; 503 · U5 · L18 · 14:40–16:07
- **Does the shape stretch at the fast part and gather back at the intended point?** Preview the whole trip and refine timing; the intended sense of speed needs judgement by eye, while stretch length and timing could be measured frame by frame in a render (proposed measure; see Derived). 503 · U4 · L15 · 01:32–02:42; 503 · U4 · L15 · 02:42–03:31; 503 · U4 · L15 · 03:31–04:30
- **Does a follower respond after the leader, and does the trigger appear to cause the next action?** The teacher adjusts keyframe timing and previews until the relationship reads correctly; needs judgement by eye, with event delay measurable from frame timing (proposed measure; see Derived). 503 · U4 · L17 · 04:15–04:55; 503 · U4 · L17 · 04:55–06:27; 503 · U4 · L17 · 06:27–08:20; 503 · U5 · L20 · 04:33–06:31
- **Do geometric parts align where intended?** The teacher uses a guide and snapping to line up the shape with other edges; render-measurable by comparing the rendered shape edges and positions (proposed measure; see Derived). 503 · U4 · L16 · 01:13–02:29; 503 · U5 · L20 · 02:22–04:33
- **Does the loop join as intended?** The teacher previews the bounded work area, looking for either a seamless restart or a chosen jump; judgement by eye, and render-measurable in principle by comparing the first and last frames for image differences (proposed measure; see Derived). 503 · U5 · L18 · 16:07–18:46; 503 · U5 · L19 · 10:45–11:28; 503 · U5 · L20 · 10:46–12:26
- **Can the viewer read the sequence before the next event?** The teacher adds time for the eye/brain to register a designed moment and checks the full animation; needs judgement by eye. 503 · U5 · L21 · 10:30–13:46
- **Does the edit flow across the cut?** The teacher previews two movements cut together and judges whether one connects to the next; needs judgement by eye. 503 · U6 · L22 · 01:26–02:49
- **Do music and image synchronize?** The teacher listens, inspects the waveform and places markers, then adjusts cuts and animation to the beat; beat positions and event offsets can be measured in rendered audio/video, while whether the synchronization feels right needs judgement by ear and eye. 503 · U6 · L22 · 12:42–14:53
- **Do tile colors and motion work together?** The teacher duplicates and recolors a composition when it clashes with neighboring tiles, then previews the mosaic; needs judgement by eye. 503 · U6 · L22 · 05:05–06:22; 503 · U6 · L22 · 07:46–09:13; 503 · U6 · L22 · 09:13–11:10

- Set up a square composition at 1080 × 1080, Square Pixels, 25 fps; the course exercises use a ten-second composition. 503 · U2 · L04 · 01:10–02:59; 503 · U2 · L05 · 00:00–01:00
