# Course 3762 — working notes

Course: *Animated Infographics with Illustrator and After Effects*. Notes below use the lesson prose; figure-only observations are marked **(frame)**. U04 L13 and U05 L18 are marked “Captions only” in `BLOCKED.md`, so no figure-caption claims from those lessons are used.

## 1. Method steps
1. **Choose a subject and establish trustworthy information.** Check links and data against their sources; choose a topic of interest that can be shared with others. 3762 · U2 · L03 · 04:58–05:50
2. **Choose visual encodings that fit the information.** Select a chart or map suited to the data; define the visual code and use labels, shapes, size and color so the data and subject remain readable. 3762 · U2 · L04 · 00:08–00:30; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 03:06–03:34; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 03:34–04:20; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 06:14–06:53; 3762 · U2 · L03 · 02:15–03:08; 3762 · U2 · L03 · 03:08–03:22
3. **Research the visual context.** Look beyond web searches to books, architecture, nature, walks, artists and designers; collect references, words and free thoughts relevant to the subject and to possible forms, colors and movement. 3762 · U3 · L06 · 00:00–01:50; 3762 · U3 · L06 · 03:40–05:31
4. **Select and organize references in a mood board.** Keep references that made an impression and set aside irrelevant ones; group images, names, shapes, words and colors so their relationships can be seen. For the cocktail project, move from figurative cues toward abstraction and connect references to information/data, proportion, transparency, behavior and color. 3762 · U3 · L07 · 00:00–00:56; 3762 · U3 · L07 · 00:56–02:43; 3762 · U3 · L07 · 05:00–06:46
5. **Sketch the subject, its data and possible behaviors.** Start with a recognizable glass, identify the ingredients as layered data, then simplify them into shapes. Explore liquid-like movement, different shapes, transparency and garnish. 3762 · U3 · L08 · 00:44–02:17; 3762 · U3 · L08 · 02:17–04:31; 3762 · U3 · L08 · 06:00–08:23
6. **Plan the sequence in a storyboard.** Decide what movement says; use visual metaphors such as floating and passing between forms. Retain a shared element between successive cocktails so it can guide the transition and let the other ingredients transform around it. 3762 · U3 · L09 · 00:00–00:32; 3762 · U3 · L09 · 00:32–00:48; 3762 · U3 · L09 · 00:48–01:22
7. **Translate the sourced data into a repeatable shape system.** Assign forms to ingredient roles, encode relevant amounts and alcohol content, and keep a working copy of editable graph data before separating or adapting the graphs. 3762 · U4 · L12 · 00:54–03:03; 3762 · U4 · L12 · 07:22–08:41; 3762 · U4 · L12 · 08:41–11:07; 3762 · U4 · L13 · 00:00–01:11
8. **Fit the data to a consistent layout without changing its values.** Align bars and artboards to the grid, retain proportions, keep ingredient order consistent, and convert mixer rectangles to area-equivalent outlined squares. 3762 · U4 · L13 · 01:12–02:49; 3762 · U4 · L13 · 02:50–04:34; 3762 · U4 · L13 · 07:11–08:07; 3762 · U4 · L13 · 08:08–10:30
9. **Develop each artwork through interaction and explanation.** Arrange forms to meet and overlap without distorting data; add gradients, background, garnish patterns and restrained labels; repeat the visual system across the set. 3762 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–02:56; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 04:02–06:50; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 06:51–08:01; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 08:02–10:37; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 10:38–12:40; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 12:41–14:28; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 14:29–16:23; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 16:24–18:22
10. **Build the After Effects sequence around main and secondary movement.** Use the shared ingredients and main movement to change between cocktails, with secondary movement making the shapes float; in this 25-second example, one-second animations are followed by two-second pauses. 3762 · U5 · L15 · 00:00–03:19; 3762 · U5 · L15 · 08:33–10:05; 3762 · U5 · L15 · 10:05–13:21; 3762 · U5 · L16 · 09:24–12:15; 3762 · U5 · L16 · 16:09–19:45
11. **Animate changes in shape, color, decoration and text in step with the sequence.** Add shape-property changes and masked gradients; make garnish enter, leave or morph at the relevant transitions; time labels to the cocktail they explain; complete the cycle back to the opening. 3762 · U5 · L15 · 13:21–17:13; 3762 · U5 · L15 · 17:13–20:29; 3762 · U5 · L17 · 00:00–03:38; 3762 · U5 · L18 · 03:27–05:50; 3762 · U5 · L18 · 05:50–07:25; 3762 · U5 · L18 · 07:25–09:27; 3762 · U5 · L19 · 05:59–08:19; 3762 · U5 · L19 · 08:19–11:45
12. **Preview, refine and prepare the output.** Review the motion in the composition, keep the overall sequence within the planned work area, render with the chosen output settings, then consider adaptations to other media and formats. 3762 · U5 · L15 · 10:05–13:21; 3762 · U5 · L16 · 09:24–12:15; 3762 · U5 · L16 · 12:15–14:49; 3762 · U5 · L19 · 11:45–12:38; 3762 · U5 · L20 · 00:24–01:26; 3762 · U5 · L20 · 01:26–02:51; 3762 · U5 · L20 · 02:51–03:24
## 2. Principles and claims

- An infographic visualizes information or data and tells a story; it should make the information clear and understandable to everyone. 3762 · U2 · L03 · 00:08–00:44
- Check data and links against their sources before using them. 3762 · U2 · L03 · 04:58–05:50
- The content should guide the choice of visualization; animation can show data changing over time while visual language maintains identity and continuity. 3762 · U2 · L03 · 03:22–03:55; 3762 · U2 · L03 · 03:55–04:16
- Histograms can show categories and time series; a pie chart is for a finite set of parts of a whole, and a treemap also divides a finite area into proportional parts. 3762 · U2 · L04 · 00:52–01:34; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 03:34–04:20
- Proportional areas make relative magnitudes easy to compare; shapes other than circles can carry the same proportional data. 3762 · U2 · L04 · 03:06–03:34; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 04:20–05:03
- A heat map uses color to show geographic values, while a speculative map can encode numerical information in fictional regions. 3762 · U2 · L04 · 06:14–06:53; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 06:53–07:48
- Shape, size and color can function as a visual code; typography and shapes together can form a coherent communication system. 3762 · U2 · L03 · 02:15–03:08; 3762 · U2 · L03 · 03:08–03:22
- Signs work when their selected visual features make a concept readily recognizable; basic shapes can combine familiar ideas or carry data. 3762 · U2 · L05 · 00:22–00:58; 3762 · U2 · L05 · 00:58–01:12; 3762 · U2 · L05 · 02:25–03:08; 3762 · U2 · L05 · 03:08–03:42
- Research can draw from non-graphic subjects and physical books as well as online images; it gives the project context and supports experimentation. 3762 · U3 · L06 · 00:00–01:50
- Proportion expresses relationships between forms, and those relationships matter when different-sized shapes represent data. 3762 · U3 · L07 · 03:25–04:20
- For this project, the cocktail is not drawn conventionally: its behavior and movement lead from a figurative idea toward abstraction. 3762 · U3 · L07 · 00:56–02:43
- In the sketch, the glass signals the cocktail while layered contents expose its data; movement, transparency and varied forms make the data feel less static. 3762 · U3 · L08 · 00:44–02:17; 3762 · U3 · L08 · 02:17–04:31; 3762 · U3 · L08 · 04:31–06:00; 3762 · U3 · L08 · 06:00–08:23
- A storyboard should make both the intended message and movement clear; a shared ingredient can guide the transformation from one drink to another. 3762 · U3 · L09 · 00:32–00:48; 3762 · U3 · L09 · 00:48–01:22
- **(frame)** The storyboard example places ingredient-labeled drink sketches beside successive abstract shape arrangements. 3762 · U3 · L09 · 00:48–01:22
- **(frame)** The completed mood board groups references around data/information, proportion, behavior, color and transparency, with drawn arrows between some references. 3762 · U3 · L07 · 05:00–06:46
- In the cocktail encoding, base spirits and liqueurs share rectangular structure; liqueur corner rounding varies with alcohol percentage; colorants are squares and decorations use patterns. 3762 · U4 · L12 · 00:54–03:03
- The alcoholic base is placed first in the ingredient order; for the final artworks it is kept at the bottom, or the highest-alcohol ingredient is used there when there is no distilled base. 3762 · U4 · L13 · 07:11–08:07; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 16:24–18:22
- Keep the original graph data editable: once Illustrator separates graph elements, its data and style cannot be changed as a graph. 3762 · U4 · L12 · 08:41–11:07; 3762 · U4 · L13 · 00:00–01:11
- Resizing a graph with the Selection tool preserves the data proportions; editing its nodes can distort those proportions. 3762 · U4 · L13 · 01:12–02:49
- The teacher uses an area-preserving calculation to turn a mixer rectangle into a square; the grid may guide placement, with slight rounding allowed for alignment. 3762 · U4 · L13 · 08:08–10:30
- In the creative phase, move data shapes without distorting them; overlap them so their relationship reads in the video. 3762 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–02:56; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 04:02–06:50
- The cocktail gradients are applied to liqueurs to retain the apparent strength and consistency of distilled spirits. 3762 · U4 · L14 · 02:57–04:01
- Garnishes should not overpower the cocktail; the teacher reduces their size from the initial plan. 3762 · U4 · L14 · 10:38–12:40
- Text supports interpretation of the data and subject; keep it restrained and inside the artwork. 3762 · U4 · L14 · 12:41–14:28
- Use a null object for the main path through the sequence and animate the linked shapes' own properties for secondary movement. 3762 · U5 · L15 · 08:33–10:05; 3762 · U5 · L16 · 16:09–19:45
- A Hold temporal keyframe prevents a color transition from blending into intermediate colors. 3762 · U5 · L15 · 17:13–20:29
- Linear spatial interpolation makes selected position paths straight rather than curved. 3762 · U5 · L16 · 07:48–09:24
- The teacher sets speed influence to 85% for position, rotation, size and roundness in an example, then previews and applies the approach to other elements; color is excluded from that easing change. 3762 · U5 · L16 · 12:15–14:49
- Repeating the opening position and rotation at the final keyframe makes the secondary motion loop. 3762 · U5 · L16 · 16:09–19:45
- Separate compositions allow parts of the animation to be isolated and adapted for different devices, platforms and output formats. 3762 · U5 · L20 · 02:51–03:24

## 3. Checks the teacher uses

- **Source and data validation.** Check that links and data are accurate against the sources. This cannot be established from the rendered animation alone; compare its claims with the referenced source. 3762 · U2 · L03 · 04:58–05:50
- **Chart fit and data correspondence.** Compare which values should be larger and whether the chart encoding shows the right ordering and proportions. Artifact-measurable: visible bar/area size ratios and category order can be measured; whether the encoding is the clearest choice still needs judgement. 3762 · U2 · L04 · 00:52–01:34; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 03:06–03:34; 3762 · U2 · L04 · 03:34–04:20
- **Grid and proportion alignment.** Use the grid to check shape, anchor-point and handle alignment; align the data bars and artboards, with consistent margins and distances. Artifact-measurable: visible bounding boxes, spacing, and ratios can be measured against the grid. 3762 · U4 · L10 · 01:31–02:25; 3762 · U4 · L10 · 02:25–03:37; 3762 · U4 · L13 · 02:50–04:34
- **Data preservation.** Check that modifications retain the represented data proportions and that the calculated mixer square is area-equivalent to its source rectangle. Artifact-measurable: compare component dimensions/areas and their ratios; correctness of source values still requires the editable data or source. 3762 · U4 · L13 · 01:12–02:49; 3762 · U4 · L13 · 08:08–10:30
- **Shape interaction and composition.** Preview overlaps and adjust them until their relation is clear and harmonious; keep garnish small enough not to dominate and text unobtrusive. Needs judgement by eye. 3762 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–02:56; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 10:38–12:40; 3762 · U4 · L14 · 12:41–14:28
- **Transition placement and continuity.** Preview at the cocktail references, check elements reach their intended positions and rotations, and check that transitions remain consistent. Timing and screen positions are artifact-measurable; whether the motion feels continuous and harmonious needs judgement by eye. 3762 · U5 · L15 · 10:05–13:21; 3762 · U5 · L16 · 04:19–07:48; 3762 · U5 · L16 · 12:15–14:49
- **Color changes.** Check that a color switches at the intended point without intermediate colors; frame-by-frame color values and switch timing can be measured from a render, while the intended color match also needs visual comparison. 3762 · U5 · L15 · 17:13–20:29; 3762 · U5 · L17 · 05:10–07:43
- **Secondary motion and loop.** Preview the floating motion and check that the final position and rotation return to the opening values. Position/rotation and loop duration are measurable; the floating quality requires judgement by eye. 3762 · U5 · L16 · 16:09–19:45
- **Decoration choreography.** Check that the elements appear in a consistent order, stagger across the diagonal, and morph at the intended cocktail transitions. Appearance order, timing, spacing and dimensions can be measured from rendered frames; the visual effect needs judgement by eye. 3762 · U5 · L18 · 03:27–05:50; 3762 · U5 · L18 · 05:50–07:25; 3762 · U5 · L18 · 07:25–09:27; 3762 · U5 · L19 · 00:00–01:29
- **Export.** Review output format, duration, encoding details and estimated file size in Media Encoder, then render. Format, duration and file size are metadata-measurable; the course gives no separate rendered-quality metric. 3762 · U5 · L20 · 00:24–01:26
