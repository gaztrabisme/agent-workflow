# Deal Shapes

Choose the response and commercial instrument from the deal’s solicitation, acceptance, and signature form. A proposal that becomes signed work changes owners and artifacts at the boundary.

## Choose the deal shape

- Read the deal-shape reference before choosing a workbook or pricing multiplier.
- Declare the deal shape in Phase 0 before choosing the spine, carrier, pricing method, and definition of done.
- Choose an enterprise bid when there is a scored document, client-authored acceptance requirements, or a submission receipt.
- Otherwise select fixed-price delivery.
- Treat that choice as the response and contract route; choose pricing instruments to fit the commercial arrangement within it. A direct SME pilot is a distinct pattern for a bounded first slice: state the one-time fee, itemize recurring client-paid costs, list implementer-fronted reimbursables, any approval cap, and handover settlement timing, and define the warranty term. Keep scale-out separate. (derived: dogfood 2026-10-01)

## Sizing and estimate basis

- Use client requirement references as the enterprise-bid spine and architect-authored acceptance-criteria IDs for fixed-price delivery.
- Use a blueprint workbook for enterprise bids and an acceptance annex plus obligation register for fixed-price delivery.
- For consulting fees where the client allows discretion, price against buyer-valued outcomes and results instead of using effort or materials as the basis [book:value-based-fees], 2. Basing Fees on Time or Materials and Not on Value Is Simply Crazy (and Unethical).
- Declare the artifact recipient next to the deal shape.
- Keep or append material according to whether it reinforces the audience’s story; internal status alone is not the deletion test [book:storytelling-with-data], Vertical logic.
- For a delivery planner, provide a WBS instrument with task rows and an empty effort column, not scope defence or commercial framing.
- For engineers, provide implementation specifications, contracts, data models, and success criteria; omit pricing and commercial positioning.
- For the signing counterparty, provide the acceptance annex and obligation register, not architecture detail or options analysis.
- Do not skip Phase 0 intake, fact/inference separation, or negative-space analysis on small deals.
- Pair every load-bearing question with the position to proceed on if unanswered.
- The book compares pros and cons by factor and option but leaves the recommended count of viable choices open [book:fundamentals-enterprise-architecture], Sample Template.
- Prioritize the solution qualities as design constraints; the book does not prescribe this fixed NFR subset across both deal shapes [book:fundamentals-enterprise-architecture], Summarizing NFRs.
- Use one ID spine across the artifact set, with every artifact as a view over it.
- Ask a less familiar reader to check clarity and flow; the book does not prescribe a mechanical integrity checklist [book:storytelling-with-data], A fresh perspective.
- When a won enterprise bid becomes fixed-price delivery, state the shape change and migrate requirement refs to acceptance criteria and traceability to the acceptance annex.
- Run artifact-integrity checks across the deal-shape migration boundary.
- Tailor methods and artifacts to project context and scale while preserving mandatory contract obligations [book:pmbok-guide-7e], 3.7. Tailor Based on Context.
- Communicate which processes and artifacts were tailored to the stakeholders affected by the chosen approach [book:pmbok-guide-7e], 3.7. Tailor Based on Context.
