# Story and structure

## Build the sequence before slide styling

Write a short spoken version and a one-sentence main point before laying out slides; the first checks that the story can be told without visual scaffolding, and the second makes the intended point explicit. [book:storytelling-with-data], “The 3‐minute story & Big Idea”.

Make a low-cost outline of the planned content, then arrange and remove pieces until each section supports the main point. A storyboard gives the communication a changeable structure before slide production creates attachment to individual pages. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Storyboarding”.

For each planned slide, note its job, claim, source, supporting evidence, transition to the next slide, and any caveat the audience needs at that point. This gives a reviewer a way to test whether the sequence advances the message rather than merely collecting material. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Storyboarding”; [book:articulating-design-decisions], “Chapter 7. Choose a Message”. (derived: attach the evidence and transition to each slide's stated job.)

## Set the order for the audience

Decide whether the conclusion should lead or arrive after context; the story source presents both as choices to consider for the audience, not one universal order. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Storyboarding”.

Put background, definitions, limitations, and appendix material where the audience can reach them without interrupting the main explanation; keep material that changes the claim near that claim. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Who, what, and how”; [book:articulating-design-decisions], “Limitations”. (derived: keep essential qualifiers adjacent to the statement they qualify.)

For a narrated piece, give motion an established message and sequence. Let the required reading or listening time shape holds, and use movement to direct attention or explain a transition. [motion/SKILL.md](../../../motion/SKILL.md#principles--anchoring-rules). (derived: a video shares the deck's message and adds time as a design constraint.)

## Return when

Return to the brief if reviewers cannot state the main point, a slide has no clear job, or a caveat is detached from the fact it limits. Return to data-slides if the evidence changes the argument. [book:storytelling-with-data], “The 3‐minute story & Big Idea”; [book:storytelling-with-data], “Storyboarding”. (derived: a structural defect should be fixed before polishing.)
