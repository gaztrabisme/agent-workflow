# Brief and audience

## Capture before building

Record who will see or use the work, what they should know or do, their relationship to the presenter, the setting, time available, available evidence, material risks, and what a successful communication changes. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Who, what, and how”; [book:storytelling-with-data], “Consulting for context: questions to ask”; [book:articulating-design-decisions], “Chapter 7. Choose a Message”.

Separate a live talk, self-serve deck, printable handout, editable PowerPoint, narrated explainer, and recorded app demo when their readers, timing, or reuse differ; the output pipeline study treats these as distinct use cases with different constraints. [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 1”; [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 2”; [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 3”. (derived: medium and audience can change what information must be present without a presenter.)

Keep confirmed facts, supplied opinions, estimates, assumptions, and open questions distinguishable; the source review of generated media records material confidence limits where a check was structural or a test stand-in was used. [book:articulating-design-decisions], “Limitations”; [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Risks and unknowns”.

## Fix acceptance before production

Write the one-sentence main point, desired action, evidence needed to support it, and requested file formats. Add practical requirements such as slide count, locale, playback device, editing needs, captions, speaker notes, or screen-reader access only when they follow from the brief. [book:storytelling-with-data], “The 3‐minute story & Big Idea”; [book:storytelling-with-data], “Who, what, and how”. (derived: these fields make the brief testable against each requested output medium.)

Choose checks that can answer whether the message is accurate, readable, and usable in the intended setting. A data exploration is not itself an audience explanation, and the audience's task should guide which findings remain in the deck. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Exploratory vs. explanatory analysis”; [book:storytelling-with-data], “Who, what, and how”.

## Return when

Return here when the audience or intended decision changes, when a source cannot support a claim, or when the chosen medium cannot meet the brief. Explain the consequence and suggest a revised outcome or delivery contract. [book:articulating-design-decisions], “Limitations”; [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 1”. (derived: audience, evidence, and medium jointly constrain the deliverable.)
