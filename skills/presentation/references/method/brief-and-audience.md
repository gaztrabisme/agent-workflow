# Brief and audience

## Capture before building

Record the audience, what they should know or do, your relationship to them, and how available data can support the point. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Who, what, and how”. Use the requester’s context questions to identify relevant background, the audience or decision maker and possible biases, available data, risks, a successful outcome, and a one-sentence message. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Consulting for context: questions to ask”.

Separate a live talk, self-serve deck, printable handout, editable PowerPoint, narrated explainer, and recorded app demo when their readers, timing, or reuse differ; the output pipeline study treats these as distinct use cases with different constraints. [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 1”; [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 2”; [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 3”. (derived: medium and audience can change what information must be present without a presenter.)

The source review of generated media records material confidence limits where a check was structural or a test stand-in was used. [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Risks and unknowns”.

## Fix acceptance before production

Write a one-sentence Big Idea. [book:storytelling-with-data], “The 3‐minute story & Big Idea”. Specify what the audience should know or do and what data will support the point. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Who, what, and how”. Record requested file formats. (derived from the output-pipeline study’s separate medium use cases: [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 1”; [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 2”; [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 3”.)

Add practical requirements such as slide count, locale, playback device, editing needs, captions, speaker notes, or screen-reader access only when they follow from the brief. (derived: these fields make the brief testable against each requested output medium.)

Choose checks that can answer whether the message is accurate, readable, and usable in the intended setting. (derived: this operationalizes the course’s audience, outcome, risk, and communication guidance into review criteria: [book:storytelling-with-data], “Who, what, and how”; [book:storytelling-with-data], “Consulting for context: questions to ask”.) A data exploration is not itself an audience explanation; present the information the audience needs rather than all exploratory analysis. [book:storytelling-with-data], “Exploratory vs. explanatory analysis”; [book:storytelling-with-data], “Who, what, and how”.

## Return when

Revisit the brief when the medium or editability requirement changes. The research switches to filling a supplied PowerPoint template or shipping PDF when a deck will never be edited. [src:output-pipelines/report.md], “Use case 1”. (derived: those switch rules show that the delivery path depends on project requirements.)
