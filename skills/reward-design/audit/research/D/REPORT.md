### D10 Duolingo: lower the streak barrier and protect recovery
- Claim: Duolingo reports that allowing one lesson to extend a streak, independently of a daily goal, increased Day-14 retention by 3.3% relative. The company also documents repairing streaks after service outages.
- Mechanism: Habit consistency, visible progress and anticipated loss of accumulated progress; the specific causal mechanism was not tested.
- Evidence tier: T3 — A reported product A/B experiment; sample size and uncertainty are not disclosed in the opened post.
- Sources: Duolingo product team (2020). “Improving the streak.” Duolingo Blog. https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/ ; Duolingo engineering team (2021). “Protecting streaks from site issues.” Duolingo Blog. https://blog.duolingo.com/protecting-streaks-from-site-issues/ (corporate authors used because bylines were not exposed in the opened pages).
- Applies to: UI
- How to apply: Separate a small consistency action from a stretch goal. Display both honestly. Add breaks and outage recovery, and test meaningful learning alongside return rates. A missed day should not erase the record of skills already acquired.
- Caveats: The 3.3% is a relative increase, not 3.3 percentage points. Fewer learners reached their daily goals in the experiment. Return rates do not establish better learning or wellbeing; longer-term comparisons are not randomized evidence.
- Dark pattern risk: high — Streak anxiety, guilt notifications and pressure to pay to protect progress can displace the user's actual goal.

### D25 Loot boxes: paid uncertainty is a harm boundary
- Claim: Paid random rewards are associated with problem-gambling symptoms. A 2021 meta-analysis reports r = .26 across 15 studies; this is a harm association, not evidence that loot boxes produce pleasure or a measured dopamine hit.
- Mechanism: Anticipation under uncertainty and gambling-like reinforcement; dopamine was not measured by this synthesis.
- Evidence tier: T1 — Meta-analysis of associations, not a causal or neural experiment.
- Sources: Garea, Shaun Stephen; Drummond, Aaron; Sauer, James D.; Hall, Lauren C.; Williams, Matthew Neil (2021). “Meta-analysis of the relationship between problem gambling, excessive gaming and loot box spending.” International Gambling Studies, 21(3), 460–479. DOI: 10.1080/14459795.2021.1914705. Opened abstract and citation record: https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/intgms/v21y2021i3p460-479.html ; opened authors' laboratory publication list: https://grailgamingresearch.wordpress.com/published-research/
- Applies to: UI
- How to apply: Give core benefits deterministically. If offering playful surprises, make them free and optional, with no pressure to repeat a purchase. Audit paid randomness, escalating purchase invitations and obscured real-money costs as high-risk design choices.
- Caveats: Correlation does not establish causal direction. The abstract also reports r = .25 for excessive gaming across seven studies and r = .37 for gambling after a Trim-and-Fill adjustment. The full paper's bias analysis was not reviewed here; these estimates do not describe one product or a cosmetic celebration.
- Dark pattern risk: high — Paid repeated chances can exploit uncertainty and expose vulnerable users to financial harm.
