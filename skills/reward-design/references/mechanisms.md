# Mechanisms

Reader: a designer choosing what a beat should promise or deliver. Evidence IDs resolve in [evidence-map.md](evidence-map.md); V overrides original findings. Tiers describe methods as defined in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). All recipes here are design inferences, not measured audience neurochemistry.

## Vocabulary and decisions

- **Expectation and prediction error:** reward prediction error is received value minus expected value. Fulfill the promised useful answer first; exceeding expectation is optional. If excess is an objective, measure expectation before the answer and report that measure's limits. A surprising disappointment is not a reward. Animal dopamine recordings support the mechanism, not a presentation recipe or subjective verdict from inspection. (T1; A1)
- **Wanting and liking:** wanting is motivation to obtain something; liking is enjoyment of the outcome. Evaluate continuation, satisfaction and usefulness separately. Rat taste reactions and motivation are distinct measures; the older paper's broad argument against dopamine learning is not adopted. (T1; A2)
- **Anticipation and uncertainty:** anticipation concerns a forthcoming outcome; uncertainty concerns what will happen. Leave a bounded explanatory question while making the benefit clear. Monkey delay activity has competing learning-model interpretations; no optimal media probability or suspense delay follows. (T1; A3)
- **Information gap:** show what the audience already knows and specify what is missing. Ask why the answer matters before using a teaser. This is a theoretical account, not a proven treatment for every audience. (T3; A4)
- **Curiosity and memory:** pilot relevant questions and test understanding after resolution. Curiosity ratings, resource expenditure and later trivia recall are associated; curiosity was not independently randomized and brain activity did not measure dopamine. (T1; A5)
- **Relevant evidence during anticipation:** place explanatory evidence near its question, with enough time to understand it. The incidental-face result does not license unrelated material: a 2024 study found interference for unrelated scholastic facts. Test the intended content and stated recall delay. (T1; A6)
- **Novelty:** introduce a new example or perspective where it changes understanding; preserve familiar labels and navigation. Human novelty-memory outcomes conflict, and the proposed memory–midbrain loop is a model. Novelty, rarity, surprise and pleasure are different. (T1; A7)
- **Ending:** deliver the promised answer, a useful action or real completion. Some remembered evaluations weight peaks and endings, but cold-water trials do not establish deck behavior; the meta-analysis has a coding correction and editorial dispute. Review the whole sequence. (T1; A8, A25)
- **Musical reward:** audition the lead-in and peak with intended listeners. Positron emission tomography (PET) used a dopamine-sensitive tracer during selected emotional listening; functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), a scan of blood-oxygenation changes, supplies finer anticipation/peak associations. Neither gives the dopamine response of a new edit. (T1; A9)
- **Music choice:** compare enjoyment and desire to hear the cue again. The drug experiment supports dopamine's causal participation, but measures behavior and skin conductance, so its corrected tier is T3: 29 completed, 2 excluded, 27 analysed. It tested no editing treatment. (T3; A10)
- **Contextual surprise:** after a predictable musical setup, try a relevant deviation; after ambiguity, try clear resolution. Pleasure depends jointly on uncertainty and surprise. Transfer from Western pop chords to narrative is analogy; the interaction was not in the measured reward-related brain regions. (T1; A11)
- **Personal relevance:** test visual directions with the intended audience and ask what matters to them. In the artwork study, 16 were recruited and 15 contributed to contrasts requiring the highest rating. Personal meaning and neural associations establish no universal style. (T1; A12)
- **Processing fluency:** fluency means ease of perceiving or interpreting something. Improve basic legibility and test ease separately from liking and truth; expertise and deliberate difficulty can also matter. (T2; A13)
- **Faces:** use a person's image for message relevance, then test trust and comprehension. Restricted samples separated attractiveness ratings from viewing effort; an attractive face is no universal reward or conversion tool. (T1; A14)
- **Direct address:** compare camera-facing address and object-directed gaze for comfort and comprehension. The original face study's correction excludes nucleus accumbens proper; static unfamiliar faces do not establish live eye-contact efficacy or duration. (T1; A15)
- **Social acknowledgement:** recognize a real contribution specifically and make public credit optional. The reputation study used predetermined feedback, not earned real-world recognition; overlapping brain activity does not validate badges or rankings. (T1; A16)
- **Insight:** give a solvable puzzle and reveal the relationship that explains it. Ask whether the answer is understandable and supported as well as surprising. The small fMRI study associates reported insight with reward-related activity; an aha feeling does not establish truth. (T1; A17)
- **Individual insight response:** supply an explanation even when a person does not solve the puzzle. EEG measures electrical brain activity, so V assigns T1, while its single-study evidence and indirect source reconstruction remain uncertain. The reported neural separation is no editing prescription. (T1; A18)
- **Habituation:** reserve prominent cues for meaningful events and test repeated use. Habituation is learned response reduction, distinct from fatigue and sensory adaptation; it does not mean every repeated reward loses pleasure. (T2; A19)
- **Familiarity:** repeat a recognizable motif while changing what it communicates. The exposure meta-analysis found a rise-then-fall liking pattern for visual, not auditory, stimuli; fluency is not an established complete explanation. No universal repeat threshold follows. (T1; A20)
- **Measurement language:** report the actual outcome: curiosity rating, task success, enjoyment, recall or behavior. BOLD is a blood-oxygenation signal linked to neural activity, not a dopamine assay. Reverse inference means guessing a mental state from an active brain region; regions serve multiple functions. (T3; A24)

## Check before handoff

State what the audience expects, what they receive and how you will check its value. Keep promise, surprise, enjoyment and memory as separate observations. (T1; A1, A2) Use [myths.md](myths.md) when a proposed rationale depends on a fixed attention clock or retention percentage. (T3; A21, A22, A23)
