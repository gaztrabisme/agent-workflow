# Industry playbook by medium

Reader: a designer adapting public practice to a specific deliverable. Tiers follow [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); IDs resolve in [evidence-map.md](evidence-map.md). Cases show methods or creator intent, not guaranteed engagement effects. Each recipe below is an adaptation to test; reported platform timings are not biological limits.

## Slides and keynote

- Start with a concrete audience problem, then reveal a visible working solution. Use a selective encore for a substantial extra benefit; selected Jobs keynotes supply craft, not controlled audience effects or a strict three-part memory law. Hand the located beat plan to presentation. (T4; D11)
- Alternate the present situation with an evidenced possible improvement when the argument benefits from contrast. End with a feasible action, costs and tradeoffs available. Duarte's sparkline supplies no optimal alternation count or controlled persuasion advantage. (T4; D13)
- Center a consequential idea, give evidence and show its implications. TEDx's under-18-minute constraint is editorial; reception comments differ by platform and do not establish a viral formula. (T4; D14)
- Offer an optional prediction before data, keep axes/scale stable and explain the discrepancy. The learning recipe is task-dependent with mixed recall findings; a live vote is a further transfer. (T3; B12)

## Explainer video

- Outline a normal situation, interruption, decision consequences, resolution and changed situation when causality helps the explanation. Pixar teaches a story spine, but its specific video credit was not retrieved. Attribute the origin to Kenn Adams's own 2013 account of his 1991 invention, linked in V; never present story order as causal proof. (T4; D12)
- Build from premise through complications to a meaningful payoff, with quieter explanation between accents. Lieu's trailer arc is a scaffold; an explainer must deliver its answer, while a trailer promises a later work. Hand choreography to motion and the video deliverable to presentation. (T4; D17)
- Keep the musical lead-in, compare candidate reveals and review synchronization after export. Those alignments are editing hypotheses; selected emotional music evidence supplies no universally effective commercial cue. (T1; C2) (T2; C7)
- Protect dense reading and unfamiliar narration from competing sound, comparing silence with the proposed bed. Reading meta-analysis does not directly establish every chart/narration result. (T1; C22) (T3; C23)

## Short video

- Match the title/thumbnail promise early and test the introduction against comparable pieces. YouTube's intro metric is the proportion remaining at 30 seconds, not an optimum hook length or attention capacity. Use audience explanation alongside platform data. (T4; D1)
- Treat TikTok's early hook, body/value and close as platform craft. Its first-three-second guidance and reported six-second ad-recall analysis have insufficient public methods for universal timing advice; do not generalize the percentages. (T4; D4)
- Make the opening subject and essential speech understandable muted. Meta's 2016 Facebook guidance is historical commissioned/internal reporting, not verified current Reels performance or a guaranteed caption lift. Captions and sound need separate delivered-format review. (T4; D5)
- For a narrative advertisement, test a person/goal, difficulty, turning point, consequence and resolution. Fuller dramatic arcs were associated with higher Super Bowl ratings; the observational analysis establishes neither sales nor a causal story recipe. (T3; D16)
- Inspect replay spikes with viewers. A spike can indicate valuable material, sharing or unclear content; clarify the segment before copying the editing pattern. (T4; D2)
- Use meaningful progress rather than an endless tease. The publicly circulated MrBeast memo has unverified attribution and operation-specific re-hook times; do not copy them into unrelated formats. (T4; D3)

## UI and interactive demonstrations

- Specify trigger, rules, feedback and behavior on repetition, then implement dependable progress/success/error states. A microinteraction is a small action-and-feedback unit; the framework is practitioner organization, not a measured dopamine or retention treatment. UX owns the flow/words and frontend the build. (T4; D18)
- Start with agency and clear consequences before adding game-style juice, meaning amplified audiovisual action feedback. Compare a plain version with one congruent layer. A later preregistered game experiment found amplification could reduce motives while success-dependent feedback helped; this reinforces conditional use and is not an ordinary-UI effect estimate. (T4; D6)
- Define the desired experience, then the interaction and rules that could create it. MDA means mechanics, dynamics and aesthetics; it is a conceptual game framework with contested categories. Discovery in an interactive chart is an adaptation to test. (T4; D7)
- For an actual challenge, pair achievable tasks with goals, control and hints. Flow means absorbed engagement in an activity; challenge–skill balance is associated with it but is not sufficient, and no universal success percentage follows. Transfer to passive media is especially uncertain. (T1; D8)
- Keep completion feedback reliable and any cosmetic surprise optional, skippable and after meaningful completion. Asana's occasional creatures are a product example without published retention/productivity evidence or appearance probability. Free cosmetic variability is not evidence for paid randomness. (T4; D19)
- Separate submission, waiting and actual completion before assigning a celebration. Mailchimp's launch/high-five account documents design intent; it establishes neither audience benefit nor an optimal cue duration. (T4; D20)
- Review habitual return and streak incentives only through the recognition checks in [dark-patterns.md](dark-patterns.md). Dependable utility, recorded learning and breaks take precedence over return metrics; the Hook cycle has no complete-model validation, and Duolingo's disclosed relative retention gain came with fewer daily goals reached. (T4; D9) (T3; D10)

## Infographic and data story

- Establish the baseline and key, advance a meaningful change with stable mappings, then allow inspection and a static comparison. Rosling's Gapminder 2009 example starts in 1809; the separate BBC 2010 example starts in 1810. Neither is a controlled learning experiment or proof all groups improved equally. (T4; D21)
- Offer a private prediction with an immediate skip, overlay the observations on the same scale and explain the gap. NYT's drawing interaction and The Pudding's scroll stages are separate craft cases; neither proves memory gains. Support reverse/rapid scrolling and a readable static route. (T4; D22) (T3; B12)
- Reveal one meaningful relationship at a time when complexity calls for it, with nearby labels and an integrated overview. Learning evidence supports signaling, segmentation and spatial integration; transfer to a specific chart must be checked by information-design. (T1; B16, B17, B18)
- Use a recognizable subject cue and supported message title, then test message recall separately from picture recognition. Memorability associations do not establish causal uplift from extra artwork. (T3; B14, B15)

## Commercial outcomes and shared boundaries

- Name whether the asset serves immediate action or longer-term brand memory and choose the observation period accordingly. IPA campaign evidence is selected, retrospective and observational despite the deck's meta-analysis wording; no universal emotional advantage or 60:40 allocation follows. (T3; D15)
- Use sound signatures and brand-fit briefs as candidates, not measured recognition formulas. Intel and Netflix establish deployment/intent; musical brand composition remains a small-sample proof of concept. (T4; C13, C14) (T3; C20)
- Require truthful terms, direct completion and a real stopping choice across media. Infinite continuation and invented urgency are patterns to recognize; paid random-reward harm evidence is correlational. Humane replacements are ethical design recommendations, not guarantees of harmlessness. (T4; D24) (T1; D25)
- Evaluate the chosen task, not a goldfish attention clock, arbitrary cut schedule or applause alone. Record comprehension, delivered usefulness and regret alongside continuation. (T3; D23) (T4; D2, D14)
