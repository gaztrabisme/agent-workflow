# Visual reward beats

Reader: a designer specifying what a viewer should see before, during and after a reveal. Composition goes to visual-design, palette to color-theory and chart semantics to information-design. Tiers follow [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); IDs resolve in [evidence-map.md](evidence-map.md). The decisions below are design inferences to test in the delivered medium.

## Entry and expectation

- Give the next important object a distinctive accent against a quieter field. Feature-search evidence supports relative salience, not a universal eye path; retain a non-colour cue and equal visibility for consequential alternatives. (T2; B1)
- Start relevant motion when the explanation reaches that object, then let it settle. Motion onset differs from ongoing motion and can lose capture against a competing new object; there is no tested universal animation duration. (T2; B2)
- Include faces or text when their semantic role matters. Inspect actual thumbnail or projection size; natural-image fixation evidence does not establish thumbnail clicks or message recall. (T3; B3)
- If a pictured person points with their gaze, place the relevant object there and label it. Gaze-cueing response speed has meta-analytic support, while transfer to a slide or UI is an extrapolation. (T1; B4)
- Establish a pattern before showing a meaningful exception, then explain it. Bayesian surprise describes changed expectations in a sensory model; the gaze-model result does not measure reward prediction error or an engagement lift. (T3; B5)
- Review the whole frame's grouping and density before details. Rapid screenshot appeal can be judged at a 50 ms study exposure; that is neither a comprehension deadline nor a recommended display time. Follow appeal review with an unrestricted task test. (T2; B6)
- Keep recognizable navigation and chart conventions while reducing competing decoration. Screenshot appeal varies with complexity, familiarity and demographics; a universally minimal design does not follow. (T3; B7)
- Separate figure from ground and keep labels consistent. Experiments on simple shapes support positive aesthetic judgments with perceptual ease, not guaranteed understanding or truth. This narrower source remains T3 even though A13 includes wider convergence at T2. (T3; B8)
- Check actual task success alongside perceived ease. Attractive interfaces can seem more usable and have sometimes improved performance; appearance never substitutes for testing the task. (T2; B9)
- Treat an intense colour accent as a context-dependent arousal hypothesis; arousal means activation, not liking. Saturation and brightness affected skin conductance in the isolated-colour study; hue affected ratings but not that amplitude measure. Send palette decisions to color-theory. (T3; B10)
- Name the intended entry point and inspect where users actually begin. Position, distinctiveness and task can outweigh size; the tested hierarchy model does not supply a universal first fixation. (T3; B23)

## Reveal and memory

- Offer a private, optional graphical prediction, reveal the observations on the same scale and invite an explanation. Treat this as promising and task-dependent: the 2018 follow-up found no clear true-distribution recall benefit, although a transfer judgment benefited. Live votes and video pause prompts are further adaptations. (T3; B12)
- Put essential evidence and common options on the initial view; reveal optional method/detail on demand. Progressive disclosure is craft guidance, and a timed slide reveal differs from a user-operated control. Costs, consequences and data qualifications remain available at the decision. (T4; B13)
- Use a recognizable, topic-relevant object as a candidate memory cue without distorting quantitative marks. Chart-feature associations measured picture recognition; adding imagery was not randomized and does not guarantee message comprehension. Test both. (T3; B14)
- State a supported takeaway in the title and annotate its evidence. Title/message recall associations are not a causal guarantee; avoid implying causes from correlations or duplicating long narration in dense text. (T3; B15)
- Highlight the element being explained and show its relationship to the label; retire obsolete highlights. Multimedia signaling improves learning on average, with different moderator findings across meta-analyses; the outcome is learning, not pleasure. (T1; B16)
- Break a complex explanation at meaningful state changes and finish with an integrated view. Segmenting can improve learning while increasing learning time; extra clicks or artificial delays do not inherit that benefit. (T1; B17)
- Place short explanatory labels near the relevant marks without covering the evidence. Spatial integration supports learning; direct chart labels are a medium transfer, and long labels can become clutter. (T1; B18)
- In instructional media, give striking details a role in the explanation or demonstrated output requirement. In expressive media, identify their brief-defined experiential role. Check implied claims and distraction separately; a visually rich relevant example is not automatically removable decoration. Seductive details are interesting but irrelevant elements that can impair learning; their instructional evidence does not prohibit expressive work. (T1; B19) (T3; B14)
- Preserve object identity and readable scales through chart changes, and provide the final static view. The transition experiments shared 24 participants and task-specific durations; they do not establish an interface timing default. (T3; B20)
- Animate a meaningful transformation only when it helps the task, with pause/replay or a stable counterpart. Representational and procedural content can benefit; decorative motion has no automatic learning advantage. (T1; B21)
- Map trigger, progress, success and failure before adding a flourish. Microinteraction feedback is a practitioner framework; display status text and a reduced-motion equivalent, and celebrate only actual success. (T4; B22)

## Check before handoff

Ask viewers to explain the message and locate its evidence at delivery size. Test the intended reading task instead of an assumed F/Z path; reject red/blue performance prescriptions without task-specific evidence. (T4; B24) (T2; B11) Send timing intent, rather than a claimed universal shot length, to motion. (T3; B25)
