# Timing and structure

Reader: a designer locating anticipation and payoff within a piece. For playback use timecodes; for a deck use slides and builds; for UI use states; for a static graphic use reading order. This workflow is a derived synthesis, not a tested complete method. Tiers follow [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); IDs resolve in [evidence-map.md](evidence-map.md).

## Reader, mandate and delivery mode

Before mapping, record `artifact_reader` (the person using this piece), `subject/sample_audience` (people depicted or addressed in an embedded example), `reader_decision`, `delivery_context`, and each assumption's source. Use the current review mandate; earlier production briefs are context unless the current task asks you to execute them. If the reader is missing, name a provisional reader and condition recommendations on that assumption. Do not automatically adopt an embedded prompt's audience. (T4; D7)

| Mode | Map and inspect | Evidence needed for processing time |
|---|---|---|
| Reader-paced document | Page/slide order, navigation, delivered-size legibility | Observed task reading; no invented advance deadline |
| Live slides | Slide/build order and speaker cues | Actual advance sequence or rehearsal |
| Narrated deck/video | Frame timestamps, speech, captions, marks and cues | Actual export playback and audience task |
| Interactive states | Triggers, progress, success, error and exit | Working state trace and task observation |

Keep a visible density or legibility finding when delivery is unknown, but leave processing-before-advance not judged. A slide PDF has no implied live timing. Review video with [measure-playback.md](measure-playback.md), and event pairs with [sound.md](sound.md). (T1; B17) (T3; B25) (T4; D18)

## Source provenance

Record provenance per claim, not once for the whole piece:

| State | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| Reported by artifact | The piece says or depicts this; record its location |
| Internally checked | Visible numbers, mappings or arithmetic agree; name the check |
| Verified against supplied source | Independent source of record supports the claim; cite that source |
| Source unavailable | The required ledger, log, data or other source is missing |

A claim can be reported and internally checked while its underlying source remains unavailable. Internal consistency does not verify the ledger, historical reconstruction or causal account. Continue located inspection, request the exact missing source in the handoff, and withhold source fidelity and any source-checked comprehension certification that depends on it. Do not infer a cause from an animated trace. (T4; D12, D21) (T3; A24)

## Map, promise, progress, payoff

1. Map the audience's valued outcome and each current beat before choosing effects. Work backwards from the desired experience to the interaction and its rules; MDA means mechanics, dynamics and aesthetics, a practitioner framework whose transfer beyond games is an adaptation. (T4; D7)
2. Write the opening promise as a specific question, consequence or useful result. Give enough shared context to identify the missing answer. A hook must agree with its packaging and its eventual delivery. (T3; A4) (T4; D1)
3. Pair every open loop, an unresolved question, with its resolution location. The circulated MrBeast memo provides progress and re-hook examples, but its attribution is unverified and its schedule belongs to that operation. Use a meaningful new obstacle or demonstration, not a copied interval. (T4; D3)
4. Build anticipation through relevant evidence and visible progress. Keep the benefit clear and uncertainty bounded; the monkey probability task establishes no media reward schedule. Essential UI outcomes stay reliable. (T1; A3)
5. Give each change enough processing space to explain its relationship. Complex instruction can benefit from meaningful segments and learner pace control; more learning time is part of the evidence, not a benefit to conceal. (T1; B17)
6. Let the reveal answer the opening question and explain why the answer follows. First test promise fulfillment and support for the reader's decision. Exceeding expectation is optional; if it is an objective, record a pre-answer expectation measure and its limits. Optional prediction can expose expectations, but follow-up recall results are mixed. Do not infer subjective surprise from an inspection. (T1; A1, A17) (T3; B12)

## Energy and variation

- Alternate explanation with relevant accents and quieter space. A trailer's rising action includes smaller peaks rather than constant acceleration; its promised future experience differs from an explainer's answer due within the piece. (T4; D17)
- Change shots at a shift in meaning and hold unfamiliar scenes or charts for inspection. Film-rhythm analyses are descriptive; temporal correlation in successful films supplies no causal attention or seconds-per-cut law. (T3; B25)
- Preserve the musical preparation before a meaningful peak, and test the alignment with the visual answer. PET evidence concerns selected listening, not the success of the new audiovisual edit. (T1; A9, C2)
- Put space before a still-unanswered expectation when suspense serves the message, and after resolution when absorption does. Musical closure supports a context effect; narrative transfer and pause duration are prototype decisions. (T3; C17)
- Vary examples or informational content at a conceptual transition while retaining familiar terms and visual motifs. Novelty-memory outcomes are mixed; habituation and familiarity can coexist. Avoid defeating response decline with louder or more intrusive cues. (T1; A7, A20) (T2; A19)
- Use a substantial optional encore only when it adds value. Jobs's selective encores are craft examples; routine surprise can become expected and no optimal encore frequency follows. (T4; D11)

## Peak and ending

- Locate the high point where the audience obtains the most useful insight, visible result or expressive resolution. Keep the middle worthwhile: peak/end prediction can be comparable to the overall mean, with framing and timescale boundaries and a disputed coding correction. (T1; A8, A25)
- Close with the answer, a supported implication or a feasible next action. TEDx's focused-idea structure and Duarte's current/possible-world contrast are craft options, not universal durations or guaranteed persuasion. (T4; D13, D14)
- In UI, distinguish submitted, processing, approved and completed. Celebrate the milestone actually reached and keep pending work explicit; a friendly ending must not imply delivery or approval. (T4; D20)
- Preserve a visible stopping state. If the piece offers more, give a choice; never turn an ending into an automatic chain that overrides the user's intent to stop. This is an agency recommendation, not a proven universal intervention. (T4; D24)

## Beat-plan fields

Use the following fields as a derived planning record, not a source-validated formula. They combine expectation mapping, cue planning and truthful feedback. (T1; A1) (T3; C1) (T4; D18)

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Location / mode | Slide/build, reading step or UI state; delivery mode |
| `observed_time` | Export event timestamp or sampled interval; separate from planned timing |
| `measurement_method/precision` | Frame PTS (presentation timestamp), crop, sample interval or listening method; resolution and limits |
| `reported_transcript_time` | Supplied caption/transcript window, not an independently heard event |
| `proposed_time` | Candidate timing; label as a prototype choice |
| Expected outcome | What the intended audience already expects; assumption or tested response |
| Promise / question | The valued outcome or missing answer |
| Sight | Visible evidence, change and retained context |
| Sound / silence | Cue purpose, onset, lead-in, ending and mute equivalent |
| Payoff | Delivered answer, explanation, result or expressive value |
| Resolution | Where the promise closes, including any unfinished state |
| Evidence | Artifact observation, provenance, corrected tier, rationale IDs/roles and source-task limit |
| Event pair | Impact/contact, visible speech, narrated reference-to-mark or musical transition-to-reveal |
| Test | Outcome, baseline, proposed prompt, participant plan and observation period; recall delay only for a memory objective |

For fades or builds distinguish first detectable pixels, a reviewer-judged readable state and audience-perceived onset. They are different observations. Report native frame timing or a sampling interval honestly; do not give eyeballed or caption-derived events the precision of a frame measurement. A proposed cue time does not replace the observed timestamp. The extraction record in [measure-playback.md](measure-playback.md) supplies the measurement method.

## Evaluation and return stages

- Inspect every promise-to-resolution pair and factual reveal before audience review. Review the whole sequence and fix unsupported causal stories, not just the flourish. (T1; A1, A25) (T4; D12)
- Compare baseline and candidate with the same content, task and delivery conditions. Record curiosity, enjoyment, usefulness and task success separately; continuation and replay do not diagnose delight. Derive provisional task questions from the reader's decision when the brief lacks them. Keep effect acceptance pending until thresholds and a participant plan exist; set a relevant recall delay only for a memory objective. Complete the inspection while those tests remain pending. (T1; A2) (T3; A23) (T4; D2)
- Use the checklist in [review-checklist.md](review-checklist.md); rerun affected questions after a timing or cue change. Set durations through rehearsal and task testing, never eight-second or ten-minute attention clocks. (T3; A21, A22, D23)
