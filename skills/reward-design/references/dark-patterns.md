# Dark patterns: recognition and replacement

Reader: a reviewer of reward beats in media or products. These patterns are taught so they can be recognized, never recommended. Tiers follow [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); IDs resolve in [evidence-map.md](evidence-map.md). Evidence ranges from documented patterns to behavioral experiments and harm associations. Replacement advice is an ethical design inference, not demonstrated protection from all harm.

Variable ratio describes a reward arriving after an unpredictable number of actions; it differs from merely changing a cosmetic motif. The audit documents habitual-return frameworks and uncertainty mechanisms, not a validated compulsion recipe or a diagnosis of every variable interaction. (T4; D9, D19) (T1; A3)

## Recognition checks

| Pattern | Recognition question | Humane replacement | Evidence / boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variable-ratio compulsion loop | Does an unpredictable action count keep people checking for a valued benefit? | Dependable core benefit; optional variation after completion | (T4; D9) (T1; A3); the monkey probability task validates no product schedule |
| Infinite scroll / autoplay | Does default continuation erase the user's opportunity to stop? | Visible end, explicit next choice, pause and exit | (T4; D24); not every infinite feed is deceptive |
| Fake scarcity / countdown | Is urgency based on an invented or resettable limit? | Truthful availability and genuine deadline | (T4; D24); documentation establishes patterns, not a universal effect |
| Streak anxiety | Does missing a day threaten status or erase perceived achievement? | Preserved learning record, breaks and recovery | (T3; D10); return-rate evidence is not learning or wellbeing evidence |
| Losses disguised as wins | Does celebration imply net success when the user loses value? | Explicit net outcome and distinct partial/loss feedback | (T2; C12); gambling simulators do not diagnose ordinary-UI harm |
| Loot boxes / paid randomness | Is payment buying another uncertain chance rather than a clear benefit? | Deterministic benefit; avoid paid random-reward loops | (T1; D25); harm association has unresolved direction/confounding |
| Endless curiosity tease | Is the answer repeatedly moved beyond its promise? | Specific question with an accessible resolution | (T3; A4) (T4; D3); open-loop recipes are not established causal laws |
| Hidden consequences | Is essential price, consent, evidence or exit information behind a reveal? | Consequences visible at the decision | (T4; B13, D24); optional-detail disclosure differs from concealment |
| False completion | Does a sound or animation celebrate before the actual milestone? | Accurate state first; optional acknowledgement second | (T4; B22, D18, D20); submitted is not approved or delivered |
| Approval pressure | Does recognition create a costly social obligation to return? | Specific optional credit without forced comparison | (T1; A16) (T4; D9); scanner feedback validates no leaderboard recipe |
| Reward-cue escalation | Are effects intensified to defeat declining response? | Quiet reliable feedback; vary meaningful information | (T2; A19) (T4; D6); reduced response is not necessarily reduced liking |
| Memorable finish hiding friction | Does a pleasant ending mask an otherwise poor outcome? | Repair the experience and its ending | (T1; A8, A25); peaks/ends do not erase the average experience |

## Evidence and metric limits

- Compare continued seeking with enjoyment, usefulness, regret and the user's chosen goal. A higher return count cannot establish benefit, liking or dopamine. (T1; A2) (T4; D2)
- Treat the Duolingo result as a first-party experiment with missing sample/uncertainty reporting: a 3.3% relative Day-14 retention increase accompanied fewer daily goals reached. It is not a percentage-point gain or proof of improved learning. (T3; D10)
- If quoting slot-sound estimates, use the erratum: 28 actual wins; estimates averaged 33 without sound and 36 with sound, stated as 18% and 29% overestimation. The later study recruited 157 and analysed 148. These are source results, not an audience effect prediction. (T2; C12)
- Paid loot-box spending is associated with problem gambling in a meta-analysis; causation and direction remain unresolved. Free, optional surprise is a recommended alternative, not empirically proven harmlessness. (T1; D25)
- Check cosmetic celebrations for distraction, repeated-action chasing and emotional appropriateness. Asana's opt-out example establishes practice without productivity or retention effectiveness. (T4; D19)
- Do not turn regulatory pattern examples into a jurisdiction-wide legal opinion or the research prototype into an outcome study. The cited associated paper was unavailable in the research run; its results are not used. (T4; D24)

## Review disposition

Flag the exact state or beat, the implied benefit and what actually happens. Remove deception, restore direct completion/exit and rerun the relevant [review checks](review-checklist.md). Diagnose the design behavior without claiming a clinical condition or audience brain measurement. (T4; D18, D20, D24) (T3; A24)
