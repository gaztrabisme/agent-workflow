# Ranked reading and buying list

Reader: the owner buying books for slides, explainers, UI and infographics. This preserves E's ranked purchasing judgment, with V's corrections. Rank is an editorial relevance decision, not a measured comparison of books. Buy-first means the first five priorities; prices, stock and current distribution have not been checked.

## Rigour legend

Here T1–T4 labels describe the **kind of source**, not a blanket empirical grade for a book. Under V's corrections, researcher-written science and science synthesis are T3; evidence-citing practitioner reports can be T3, while applied frameworks, practitioner craft and film theory are T4 even when they cite studies. T1 is reserved for brain-measurement reports or formal meta-analyses and T2 for replicated controlled behavioral evidence; no whole book below receives those labels. Grade individual research claims separately in [evidence-map.md](evidence-map.md). A supported E verdict verifies bibliography, not every claim or complete method.

The book cells link the verified publisher/edition records. The Rigour cells carry the book IDs; full citations and verification limits live in [E](../audit/research/E/findings.md) and [V](../audit/research/V/claims-verified.md).

## Buying and application notes

E4's separately titled successor, *Visual Thinking for Information Design*, second edition 2021, is a candidate replacement for the original when choosing an edition. Its publisher record is in E, but V's row checks the 2008 book; this is not a fresh full-book audit of the successor. (T3; E4)

Read E20 before using personalized surprise, and E23 alongside E26 before choosing a retention objective. This ordering is an editorial agency recommendation; case studies and frameworks do not demonstrate protection from all harm. (T4; E20, E26) (T3; E23)

Source descriptions and edition records were checked, not every page of all books. E3's principles can have individually stronger evidence than its whole-book synthesis label; E5's academic publisher does not turn film theory into an experiment. (T3; E3) (T4; E5)
