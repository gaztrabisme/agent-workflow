# Reward Design

Produces a reward-beat plan or critique for a designer shaping a deck, video, live talk, UI or infographic. A reward beat is a moment that promises, develops or delivers something the audience values: an answer, insight, useful result or expressive experience.

Dopamine contributes to learning from reward prediction error, the difference between expected and received value, and to wanting, the motivation to seek an outcome. It is not pleasure itself. This media-planning workflow does not measure audience dopamine. Gaze, clicks, retention, chills and ordinary brain imaging cannot establish its release. Specialized dopamine-sensitive research does not provide a ready estimate for a new design. Evaluate anticipation, understanding, delivered value and reported experience here. (T1; A1, A2, A9) (T3; A24)

> Inherits [core](../core/SKILL.md); follow its canonical gates. [V's verdicts](audit/research/V/claims-verified.md) override A–E. Design transfer remains unproven; the skill's overall verdict is PENDING. Use [EVOLUTION.md](EVOLUTION.md) for real-use history.

## Quick start

Use these moves as design choices to test. Tiers describe the source method, not a guaranteed effect; see Evidence tiers below. Make the promised answer useful before adding surprise.

- **Slides / live talk**
  - Open with a specific question the talk will answer. (T3; A4) (T4; D14)
  - Show progress toward a visible working result; make its evidence part of the reveal. (T4; D11) (T1; B16)
  - End on the answer and a feasible next action; keep the middle worthwhile. (T4; D14) (T1; A25)
- **Explainer video**
  - Open with the question and show the context needed to understand it. (T3; A4) (T4; D17)
  - Show each label when its mark is explained; sync any sound accent to the visual change. (T1; B16, B18) (T2; C7)
  - Break complex changes into meaningful steps, then hold the complete answer for inspection. (T1; B17) (T4; D17)
- **Short video**
  - Make the opening deliver on the title or thumbnail promise. (T4; D1)
  - Make the subject and essential words understandable with sound off. (T4; D5)
  - Show useful progress, then close with the promised answer. (T4; D3, D4)
- **UI**
  - Show real progress; distinguish submitted, waiting and completed. (T4; D18, D20)
  - Make celebrations optional and use them only after actual success. (T4; D19, D20)
  - Keep essential terms visible and give the person a clear stopping choice. (T4; B13, D24)
- **Infographic**
  - Put the supported takeaway in the title. (T3; B15)
  - Reveal one meaningful relationship at a time and label its evidence nearby. (T1; B16, B18)
  - Keep scales consistent and leave a complete static view people can inspect. (T3; B20) (T4; D21)

## When it fires — and when a sibling owns it

- **This skill:** plan or critique an opening hook, curiosity gap, anticipation, reveal, payoff, novelty, sound cue, pacing contrast or ending across channels. A hook gives a reason to attend; a curiosity gap is an identifiable missing answer.
- **[Presentation](../presentation/SKILL.md):** owns deck and video production, delivery format and rendered verification.
- **[Motion](../motion/SKILL.md):** owns storyboards, animatics, movement curves and choreography; use this skill to specify the intended anticipation and payoff.
- **[Visual design](../visual-design/SKILL.md) / [color theory](../color-theory/SKILL.md):** own composition, type and colour decisions.
- **[Information design](../information-design/SKILL.md):** owns chart choice, data encoding and annotations; this skill proposes how the discovery unfolds.
- **[UX](../ux/SKILL.md) / [frontend](../frontend/SKILL.md):** own product flows, microcopy and production web code; this skill reviews reward cues against actual user benefit.

> **Disambiguation:** choose by artifact and audience. This skill gives a media designer a beat plan or critique; siblings turn those decisions into their deliverables. A static graphic has a reading order, not an assumed playback duration.

## Evidence tiers

T1 means brain measurement or formal meta-analysis (a quantitative synthesis across studies), not a strong, replicated or proven design effect; T2 means replicated controlled behavioural evidence, T3 means a single study, observational evidence or scholarly synthesis/theory, and T4 means practitioner craft or documented practice.

The tier describes the cited evidence method. Every design translation remains an inference to test unless the source tested that exact treatment in that medium. A supported V verdict accepts the source claim and its caveats, not every proposed recipe. Rationale IDs can serve as direct task evidence, mechanistic analogy, craft precedent or ethical constraint; [evidence-map.md](references/evidence-map.md) defines these roles and source-task limits. Book labels describe source kinds in [reading-list.md](references/reading-list.md); grade their empirical claims separately.

## Principles / anchoring rules

1. **Promise something worth delivering.** Fulfill the promise and support the audience's decision first. Exceeding expectation is optional; if required, measure expectation before the answer and state that measure's limits. Expected outcomes can still be enjoyable. (T1; A1, A3) (T4; D1)
2. **Make curiosity answerable.** Give enough context to name the missing information and close the question within the promised piece or segment. Unrelated scholastic facts have also suffered interference during curiosity; keep evidence relevant. (T3; A4) (T1; A5, A6)
3. **Make the reveal understandable.** Signal the relevant relationship, integrate labels and segment a complex explanation. Prediction before a data reveal is a task-dependent option with mixed recall findings. (T1; B16, B17, B18) (T3; B12)
4. **Use sight and sound for the same meaning.** Test music with its lead-in, align decisive events and protect speech and reading. Ordinary brain imaging, chills and arousal cannot certify audience dopamine. (T1; A9, C2, C22) (T2; C7) (T3; A24, C23)
5. **Vary the information, preserve the language.** Habituation means a response decreases after repetition; familiarity can also increase liking. Use meaningful variation without escalating stimulus intensity or assuming a repeat limit. (T2; A19) (T1; A7, A20)
6. **Earn the peak and the ending throughout.** Improve important moments and completion while reviewing the whole experience; peak-end evidence has boundaries and a disputed meta-analysis correction. (T1; A8, A25)
7. **Measure delivered value and preserve agency.** Wanting to continue differs from liking the outcome. Give dependable completion, optional celebrations and clear stopping points; never disguise costs or losses as wins. (T1; A2, D25) (T2; C12) (T4; D18, D19, D24)

**Scale:** a quick question uses the relevant stage plus its evidence and agency check. A new piece uses all stages. For an existing artifact, use Critique mode below; the stages are diagnostic destinations. Rerun affected checks after a candidate exists.

## Critique mode — existing artifacts

1. Read the current mandate, brief and [checklist](references/review-checklist.md). Declare the actual reader, decision, delivery mode, coordinator/owner, applicable mechanisms and available evidence; treat earlier production briefs as context.
2. Probe/render/map the supplied artifact without rebuilding it. Use [Review existing playback](references/measure-playback.md) for video; map slide or reading order when there is no advance schedule. Record source provenance with [timing-and-structure.md](references/timing-and-structure.md).
3. Inspect promise, cues, payoff, variation, ending and agency. Score artifact and audience subchecks separately. Return observed defects to the owning stage/skill and mark unavailable evidence not judged.
4. Consolidate overlapping findings into a ranked change list with one accountable owner, dependencies and a test per change. Propose missing audience tests and name exact missing sources in the handoff.
5. Review a candidate only after one exists: obtain the production owner's render/code checks, inspect actual delivery and rerun affected questions. Independent cold review and audience validation remain separate gates when required.

The requested critique/report may serve as the allowed project record. An internal inspection deliverable is complete when its evidence, limitations and handoff exist. Identify the coordinator who owns acceptance; hold production, audience or validated-judgment claims until their required gates run. Apply core's release restrictions without expanding a fixed-output inspection into an unrequested rebuild or wiki.

## Execution and output

Write an editable Markdown beat plan or critique in the project, or in the reply for a small question. Keep project media and audience responses outside the reusable skill package. This is a planning and critique workflow; it supplies renderer decisions and does not render media itself.

For each planned beat record: location, audience's expected outcome, question/promise, visible information, sound or silence, payoff, resolution location, tier/IDs, design inference and test. Keep `observed_time`, `measurement_method/precision`, `reported_transcript_time` and `proposed_time` separate. Proposed times are prototype choices; measured export times are observations. Cue sheets specify event pairs, onset, lead-in, end and silent/reduced-motion equivalents. Fields and delivery modes: [timing-and-structure.md](references/timing-and-structure.md). (T3; C1) (T4; D18)

For each critique subcheck record: check ID, applicability/reason, location, observation, provenance, inspection verdict (meets, partly, misses, not applicable), evidence status (measured, reviewer-judged, not judged, skipped), observed-defect severity, priority-to-test/release implication, owning stage/skill and concrete change or test. Leave an unavailable verdict unset. Use the checklist's scoring, severity and convergence procedure; neither an absent study nor a rendered caption establishes an audience outcome. (T1; A2) (T4; D2)

Hand the plan to the named production owner. Compare baseline and candidate under the same task and delivery conditions; record actual participants, prompts and observation period. Derive provisional task questions from the reader's decision when the brief lacks them. Leave effect acceptance pending until thresholds and a participant plan exist; define a relevant recall delay only for a memory objective. Continue an inspection while those tests are pending. (T2; C7) (T3; A21, A22, A23)

## Gates (declared, inherited from core)

- **Grounding gate:** read the brief, piece, source facts and relevant digest; check corrected tiers and evidence roles, and open V for uncertain claims. Separate artifact reports, internal checks, supplied-source verification and unavailable sources. Continue located inspection when truth sources are missing; withhold source-dependent certification and request those sources in the handoff. See [grounding-gate.md](../core/references/grounding-gate.md).
- **Output Contract:** deliver the located beat plan or critique, tier/ID trail, outcome/agency checks and explicit production handoff. Report unavailable playback, sound or audience evidence as not judged. Record use and open questions in the project's allowed record or the skill evolution log. See [output-contract.md](../core/references/output-contract.md) and [wiki-protocol.md](../core/references/wiki-protocol.md).
- **Pushback and teach:** replace unsupported dopamine, fixed-attention or coercive retention requests with an honest promise, useful payoff and observable test. Explain the relevant finding and consequence. See [pushback-and-teach.md](../core/references/pushback-and-teach.md).

## Dark patterns — recognition only

Use [dark-patterns.md](references/dark-patterns.md) to recognize variable-ratio compulsion loops, infinite continuation, fake scarcity, streak anxiety, disguised losses, paid random rewards and deceptive disclosure. They are never recommended. Optional cosmetic variation still needs an agency check; free surprise is not demonstrated harmlessness. (T4; D9, D19, D24) (T3; D10) (T2; C12) (T1; D25)

## Anti-patterns (hard no-list)

- Claiming dopamine release from gaze, BOLD, clicks, chills, applause or retention. BOLD is fMRI's blood-oxygenation signal. (T3; A24)
- Hiding essential terms, task results or evidence to manufacture a curiosity gap. (T4; B13, D24)
- Treating every surprise as positive, or an aha feeling as proof. (T1; A11, A17, A18, C25)
- Adding irrelevant spectacle, constant motion or music beneath dense reading without checking understanding. (T1; B19, B21, C22)
- Applying a universal hook duration, cutting interval, scanpath, pitch, tempo or repetition count. (T3; A21, A22, B25, C5, C16) (T4; B24)
- Optimizing continuation while usefulness, comfort, satisfaction or user control deteriorates. (T1; A2, D25) (T4; D2, D24)
- Replacing the content with a flourish at the end, or using a celebration before actual success. (T1; A25) (T4; D20)
