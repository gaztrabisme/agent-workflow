## What the Value Proposition Canvas compares

Anchor: 1490 · U5 · L18 · 00:00–01:21

The Value Proposition Canvas is an exercise that helps us redefine our value proposition. It improves the proposition by setting customer needs alongside what we offer. We identify the pains a user might have when using the product, as well as what they expect from it, so that we can strengthen our proposal.

We should build products that create value for companies and cover the needs, desires, and expectations of the market. For a product to succeed, or at least begin to be used, it has to be relevant, credible and different from its competitors (captions: *Increíble*). A value proposition is valuable to the customer when there is a positive relationship between what they will get from the product and the sacrifice involved in getting it, whether that is the price, a subscription, or something else. For example, you can use Spotify for free, but many of us prefer to pay so we do not have ads. We do not mind making that sacrifice to avoid the ads. The Value Proposition Canvas (captions: *Value Processing Canvas*) is meant to prevent us from creating a value proposition without taking users into account.

## Complete the customer side for Mila

Anchor: 1490 · U5 · L18 · 01:21–02:55

Let us complete the Value Proposition Canvas for our reading-app project. First, we choose our user persona: Mila, a reader in her free time. We start on the right and work left. First we fill in Customer Jobs (*las tareas del cliente*), the things the user will do with our app. She will be able to save her book collection, write and share reviews of her reading, read and rate reviews, find books she might like, and talk about books with other people who are interested.

Next we add Pains (*los dolores*) or Gains (*los beneficios*). I will start with the pains: having to download another app; having to learn how to use a new app; and manually entering all the books you have read over your life, which I think is the worst one. We can also add pains related to reading: people may not return books they borrow, the user may not have time to read, or may spend time in our app instead of reading a favorite novel.

Now we add the gains she hopes to get from the app. She will stop wasting time searching the internet for her next book. She will meet people with the same interests. She may be able to manage her reading time, perhaps setting aside hours, or the app may remind her when to read. Her reading library will be organized in the cloud. She will gain a reputation from the reviews and comments she posts on our platform, and she will get good recommendations. Once we have completed the circle, which is everything about our user, we can complete the square.

## Begin matching the profile to solutions

Anchor: 1490 · U5 · L18 · 02:55–03:52

The square contains Pain Relievers (*calmantes de los dolores*) and Gain Creators (*potenciadores de beneficios*). We now add functions that make the pains easier to bear. A minimalist design can reduce the effort of learning a new app. A web version means the user does not have to download one more app to her phone. Scanning the books' barcodes and loading the information automatically eases the pain of entering data manually. For that same pain, the user could take a photo of a book and have the app find its information in our database. Since entering data is one of the biggest pains, I add Kindle import, which automatically takes details about the books she is reading on Kindle and imports them into our app.

## Finish the pain relievers

Anchor: 1490 · U5 · L18 · 03:52–04:20

For the pain of people not returning the books Mila lends, we can add loan reminders and mark which books are on loan. For the pain of losing reading time, we can add reminders about when to read, perhaps organized into time blocks. We can also include statistics by number of pages read, and, finally, reading challenges. This completes the pain-relief side of the square.

## Add gain creators

Anchor: 1490 · U5 · L18 · 04:20–04:52

After defining the pain relievers for the user's pains, we move to the Gain Creators. For example, we can add a reading coach, recommendations from similar users to help her meet people with the same interests, virtual reading clubs, and a visually rich profile page showing Mila's collection and perhaps excerpts she has highlighted from each text. We can let users rate recommendations and comments, and add a points system like Reddit.

## Choose products and services for the minimum viable product

Anchor: 1490 · U5 · L18 · 04:52–06:00

Once the pain relievers and gain creators are defined, we identify the minimum products and services our business needs. These could be a Progressive Web App (PWA; *aplicación web progresiva*), a web app, a desktop version of the platform, an Android app, an iOS app (captions: *app para ellos* [app for them]), and a landing page: a very simple web page where people can download the app.

If we think in terms of a minimum viable product, we could drop the desktop version and the Android and iOS versions because of their cost, and keep a landing page and a Progressive Web App. It is very important to do this exercise with the business team, because they bring more of the company's perspective and what it needs to make a profit. In the end, the exercise helps us reach agreement and define a product with the most relevant characteristics compared with competitors. If we are starting from scratch and there is no comparable product, we should include only functions that serve users.

You have learned to use the Value Proposition Canvas. In the next lesson, we will learn the differences between Jobs To Be Done and user stories.
