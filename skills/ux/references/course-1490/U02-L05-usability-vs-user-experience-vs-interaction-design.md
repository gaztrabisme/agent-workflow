## User experience: actions, outcomes and perception

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L05 · 00:09–01:34

*1490 · U02 · L05 · 00:09–01:34*

This lesson explains the differences among user experience (UX; *experiencia de usuario*), usability (*usabilidad*) and interaction design. The teacher will share a glossary of commonly used UX terms at the end.

UX is the set of factors and elements connected with a user's interaction with a particular environment or device, resulting in a positive or negative perception of that service, product or device. It is a complicated definition, so the teacher simplifies it: think of user experience as the set of factors that makes us think well or badly of a product or brand.

![1490 · U2 · L05 · 00:41](frames/U02L05-F0041.00.jpg)

On screen: "UX" / "Es el conjunto de factores y elementos relativos a la interacción del usuario con un entorno o dispositivo concretos, dando como resultado una percepción positiva o negativa de dicho servicio, producto o dispositivo." [It is the set of factors and elements related to the user's interaction with a particular environment or device, resulting in a positive or negative perception of that service, product or device.]  
Shows: A written definition of user experience.

The teacher asks us to keep this formula in mind: users take actions to get results; we are always looking for an outcome. For example, if I want to buy a cinema ticket, I go to the website to find and buy it. Each action produces a result, and that gives us a perception. The experience may be good if I can buy a ticket to the film I want to see, or bad if there are no tickets left. Users use products to get something in return.

## Usability, ease and effectiveness

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L05 · 01:34–02:31

*1490 · U02 · L05 · 01:34–02:31*

Usability is the measure of how well a product can be used by specific users to achieve specific goals with effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction in a specified context of use. The definition is complicated, so the teacher simplifies it: usability is the ease and effectiveness with which someone uses something. The teacher asks us to keep “ease” and “effectiveness” in mind.

![1490 · U2 · L05 · 01:48](frames/U02L05-F0148.00.jpg)

On screen: "USABILIDAD" [Usability] / "Es la medida en la cual un producto puede ser usado por usuarios específicos para conseguir objetivos específicos con efectividad, eficiencia y satisfacción en un contexto de uso especificado." [It is the measure of how a product can be used by specific users to achieve specific goals with effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction in a specified context of use.]  
Shows: The longer definition of usability.

The visual diagram adds simplicity. For something to be usable, it has to be simple and easy to use; it should not feel difficult. Using it without effort is efficiency. Effectiveness means achieving the maximum result with the minimum effort.

![1490 · U2 · L05 · 01:56](frames/U02L05-F0156.00.jpg)

On screen: "USABILIDAD" [Usability] / "es la facilidad y eficacia con que un usuario utiliza algo." [is the ease and effectiveness with which a user uses something.]  
Shows: A short definition of usability.

Good usability does not necessarily mean a good user experience. Returning to the cinema example, even if the website is easy to use, I will not have a good UX if I cannot get a ticket.

## Interaction design and interface design

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L05 · 02:31–02:52

*1490 · U02 · L05 · 02:31–02:52*

Interaction design (*diseño de interacción*) is the study of how interactive computer systems are designed, implemented and used, and how computers influence individuals, organizations and society. UI, or interface design, concerns what the user sees on screen. Interaction design concerns how the user interacts with the system through processes.

![1490 · U2 · L05 · 02:41](frames/U02L05-F0241.00.jpg)

On screen: "DISEÑO DE INTERACCIÓN (IxD)" [Interaction design (IxD)] / "Es el estudio sobre cómo se diseñan, implementan y usan los sistemas informáticos interactivos y la manera que influyen los ordenadores en los individuos, las organizaciones y la sociedad." [It is the study of how interactive computer systems are designed, implemented and used, and how computers influence individuals, organizations and society.]  
Shows: The full definition of interaction design.

![1490 · U2 · L05 · 02:52](frames/U02L05-F0252.00.jpg)

On screen: "UI es el diseño de lo que ve el usuario en pantalla, IxD es el diseño de cómo interactúa el usuario con el sistema." [UI is the design of what the user sees on screen; IxD is the design of how the user interacts with the system.]  
Shows: A short comparison of UI and interaction design.

## Five dimensions of interaction design

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L05 · 02:52–04:18

*1490 · U02 · L05 · 02:52–04:18*

The teacher presents five dimensions of interaction design. Words should be easy to understand and have a clear function, such as the descriptive text inside buttons. Visual representation matters too: each interface element in a product needs a purpose; design should not be added only as decoration.

Physical objects and space are another dimension. We need to consider how users interact with the real world and what devices they will use. Operating a design program with a trackpad is different from using a mouse. Visiting a website on a phone in a crowded metro is different from visiting it at home on a 27-inch monitor.

Time matters: interactions between the user and the system should take an optimal amount of time. The final dimension is behavior, including the user's emotions and reactions while interacting with the product.
