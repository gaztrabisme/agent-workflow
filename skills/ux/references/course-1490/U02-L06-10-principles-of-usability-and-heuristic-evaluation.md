## Ten principles for usable digital products

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L06 · 00:09–00:36

*1490 · U02 · L06 · 00:09–00:36*

This lesson introduces Jakob Nielsen's ten principles of usability (*usabilidad*) and a tool for measuring a website's usability: heuristic evaluation (*evaluación heurística*). Nielsen is one of the most important figures in user experience and usability; he formulated ten rules for applying usability to a website or digital product.

## Heuristics one through four: status, familiar models, control and consistency

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L06 · 00:36–01:45

*1490 · U02 · L06 · 00:36–01:45*

The first principle is visibility of the system status. The interface should always show users what is happening and where they are in the navigation. Progress bars, for example, can show the status of uploading a file to the cloud or installing a program.

The third principle is user control and freedom. Users should be able to return easily to a previous state. The examples show undoing the sending of an email and discarding a social media post.

## Heuristics five and six: prevent errors and support recognition

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L06 · 01:45–02:31

*1490 · U02 · L06 · 01:45–02:31*

The fifth principle is error prevention: prevent errors before they happen rather than showing messages after the fact. For instance, when a user enters a password and moves to the next field, the interface might tell them that it requires a special character or a capital letter. In product design, it is important to anticipate the mistakes users might make and warn them before they make one.

![1490 · U2 · L06 · 02:09](frames/U02L06-F0209.00.jpg)

On screen: "5." / "PREVENCIÓN DE ERRORES" [Error prevention] / "Es mejor prevenir errores que generar mensajes una vez se produzcan." [It is better to prevent errors than to generate messages after they occur.] / "olyimpic" / "olympics" / "olympics 2016" / "olympic trials" / "olympics schedule" / "Password strength: Too short" / "Use at least 8 characters. Don't use a password from another site, or something too obvious like your pet's name. Why?" / "Your password must have: 8 or more characters / Upper & lowercase letters / At least one number" / "Strength: strong" / "Avoid passwords that are easy to guess or used with other websites." [rest omitted]  
Shows: Search suggestions appear while a query is typed, and password forms give guidance before submission.

The sixth is recognition rather than recall. It is better for a user to recognize how to do something than to have to look up how to use it. In the smartphone example, once users have used the button, they remember how it works. They do not need to open Settings, find Sound and silence the phone; they can do it directly with the button.

![1490 · U2 · L06 · 02:31](frames/U02L06-F0231.00.jpg)

On screen: "6." / "MEJOR RECONOCER QUE RECORDAR" [Recognition rather than recall] / "Siempre es mejor sugerir un conjunto de acciones u objetos que obligar al usuario a memorizar para que pueda cumplir su objetivo." [It is always better to suggest a set of actions or objects than to make the user memorize them to achieve their goal.] / "https://www.quora.com" / "good books on" / "What are some good books on marketing?" / "Search: good books on" [rest omitted]  
Shows: A dedicated mute switch on a phone and search suggestions on Quora make available actions recognizable.

## Heuristics seven through nine: flexibility, simplicity and error recovery

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L06 · 02:31–03:56

*1490 · U02 · L06 · 02:31–03:56*

The seventh principle is flexibility and efficiency of use. A digital product should work for all kinds of users, from beginners to experienced users. The example on the left shows shortcuts for common actions in the Mac Finder. Design programs offer the same choice: use a menu or keyboard shortcuts. The example on the right shows a basic configuration for newer users and an option for advanced users.

![1490 · U2 · L06 · 03:02](frames/U02L06-F0302.00.jpg)

On screen: "7." / "FLEXIBILIDAD Y EFICIENCIA DE USO" [Flexibility and efficiency of use] / "El producto digital debe estar preparado para todo tipo de usuario, desde los más novatos hasta los más experimentados." [The digital product should be ready for all kinds of users, from beginners to the most experienced.] / "Navigation" / "System Folders" / "Account Setup" / "Enter Your Credentials" / "Exchange" / "Your Email Address" / "Password" / "Description" / "Sign in" / "Advanced Settings..." [rest omitted]  
Shows: Finder shortcuts appear beside an Exchange login form whose advanced-settings option is highlighted.

The eighth is minimalist aesthetics and design. We should simplify as much as possible and remove irrelevant content so the user focuses on what matters. Google's page is the example: it is almost empty, with only the logo and a search field. Good designers remove elements rather than adding more.

![1490 · U2 · L06 · 03:24](frames/U02L06-F0324.00.jpg)

On screen: "8." / "ESTÉTICA Y DISEÑO MINIMALISTA" [Minimalist aesthetics and design] / "Intentar simplificar, eliminar el contenido irrelevante para que el usuario solo se fije en lo realmente importante." [Try to simplify and remove irrelevant content so the user focuses only on what is truly important.] / "Google" / "Buscar con Google" [Search with Google] / "Voy a tener suerte" [I'm Feeling Lucky] / "Ofrecido por Google en: català galego euskara" [Offered by Google in: Catalan, Galician, Basque] [rest omitted]  
Shows: Google's largely empty search page contains its logo and the search field.

The ninth principle is helping users recognize, diagnose and solve errors. Error messages should use clear, simple language, state the problem precisely and suggest a constructive solution. In the MailChimp example, if the username or password is wrong, a notification explains how to recover it and links to the recovery page for both the username and password. This way the user is not stuck on the page.

## Heuristic ten, Nielsen and heuristic evaluation

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L06 · 03:56–05:24

*1490 · U02 · L06 · 03:56–05:24*

The tenth principle is help and documentation. For complex or technical products, users may need a help area with instructions. It should be easy to find and useful without being too long; it may take the form of frequently asked questions, a chatbot or a section with product instructions.

## The evaluation template and categories

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L06 · 05:24–06:48

The teacher opens the template for evaluating Renfe.com. It is adapted and translated from an English version by Jules Ford at UX for the Masses (the captions read *Masers*), and the studio has updated it. It is simpler than other templates. The evaluator fills in each field, indicating whether the website meets each rule related to user experience and usability.

The categories cover features and functionality; the home page or first screen; navigation; search; feedback, meaning how the website responds to user interactions; forms, which are very important on a website; errors and how the page returns them; content and text; help; and website performance, such as whether it loads quickly or gets stuck. ## Results and recommendation

Anchor: 1490 · U2 · L06 · 08:52–09:56

*1490 · U02 · L06 · 08:52–09:56*

After scoring the fields, the template gives a usability result. It also has a comments field for explaining each rating. The teacher leaves it blank in this example, but recommends using it if the results will be shared with a client or another department. This evaluation gives the site 61 out of 100, which counts as moderate usability. A graph makes the result easier to see by showing the percentages for the different areas. At a glance, help is the weakest area, while navigation and how errors are indicated are among the strongest. The teacher recommends carrying out a heuristic evaluation before redesigning a website, then showing the result to the client to identify which parts need improvement.
