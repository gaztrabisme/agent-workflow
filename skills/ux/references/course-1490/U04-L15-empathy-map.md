## An empathy map makes a shared view of the user

Anchor: 1490 · U4 · L15 · 00:00–01:22

In this lesson we will learn what an empathy map (*mapa de empatía*) is and what it is for. It is a Design Thinking tool that helps the design team create a shared view of users’ thoughts, needs, and influences. The teacher shows a canvas, then what it looks like once filled in.

It is similar to a proto-persona, but here we enter the user’s mind to see how they think and what influences them. Do not confuse an empathy map with a data-collection method. It is a tool for understanding, capturing, and visualizing what we believe users think, so we can empathize with them. It helps people from other departments, such as programming and technology, empathize with this person, even if the person is fictional.

An empathy map helps us understand users’ situations and the things that influence them. It shows the underlying “why” behind each action: why people do something or need one thing rather than another. Finally, it lets us internalize users’ experiences, put ourselves in their heads, and feel as they do.

## Keep the exercise focused on one person

Anchor: 1490 · U4 · L15 · 01:22–01:51

Whenever possible, make the map with the design team. If you have to do it alone, that is fine. Set a time limit so the exercise does not drag on. A client or someone from another department may start talking for too long; spending an entire morning on one empathy map makes no sense. Always use a timer. And always base the map on a user persona: it cannot be about a general audience. It must be about one specific person.

## Fill in the map for Mila, the reader in her free time

Anchor: 1490 · U4 · L15 · 01:51–04:29

We will fill in an empathy map for our reading-app project. First, say whose map it is: ours is for Mila, the reader in her free time. Next, ask what we need to know. We get that from the needs and goals in the proto-persona.

![1490 · U4 · L15 · 02:10](frames/U04L15-F0210.00.jpg)

On screen: "MILA" / "COMPORTAMIENTO" [Behavior] / "LEE A DIARIO CUANDO SE METE EN LA CAMA" [Reads daily when she gets into bed] / "LEE EN PAPEL O GRATIS EN SU KINDLE" [Reads on paper or for free on her Kindle] / "LE GUSTA LEER RECOMENDACIONES" [Likes reading recommendations] / "SE FÍA MUCHO DE LA OPINIÓN DE LOS DEMÁS" [Trusts other people’s opinions a lot] / "SÚPER LECTORA EN SU TIEMPO LIBRE" [Super reader in her free time] / "DATOS DEMOGRÁFICOS" [Demographic data] / "OFICINISTA" [Office worker] / "52 AÑOS" [52 years old] / "VIVE EN LA CIUDAD" [Lives in the city] / "SEPARADA, CON 2 HIJOS" [Separated, with 2 children] / "INGRESOS MEDIOS" [Middle income] / "NECESIDADES Y OBJETIVOS" [Needs and goals] / "QUIERE PODER ESCRIBIR RECOMENDACIONES Y VALORAR LAS DE LOS DEMÁS" [Wants to be able to write recommendations and rate other people’s] / "QUE LE ACONSEJEN NUEVAS NOVELAS" [Have people recommend new novels to her] / "LEER MÁS LIBROS" [Read more books] / "PODER LLEVAR UN DIARIO DE LECTURA" [Keep a reading journal]  
Shows: Mila’s proto-persona in four quadrants, with the needs-and-goals quadrant circled.

Mila needs to write recommendations and rate other people’s, receive recommendations for new novels, read more books, and keep a reading journal.

Then ask what she sees in her environment. She sees a lot of books available but not much quality. She looks at Amazon reviews and blogs with recommendation lists.

Next imagine what she might say. “If I had more time, I could read more”—we tell ourselves that quite often. “I can’t remember whom I lent *Anna Karenina* to” (captions: *Nekarenina*). It is typical to lend someone a book and then forget whom you gave it to. Mila might say, “Don’t read this book; it is really, really boring.” And a classic: “The *Game of Thrones* novels are better than the series.”

Then ask what she does. When she finishes a book, she usually leaves a review on Amazon. She reads reviews before buying a Kindle book (captions: *kinder*), and she also looks for free books on somewhat illegal websites. She highlights (*subraya*) passages in her Kindle books (captions: *Su braille*), but later she does not review those highlights. She also leaves many books halfway through because she does not like them or gets bored after starting a book and finding it tedious.

What does Mila hear? People might say, “What a lovely house you have, with so many books everywhere.” Or, “Next time we meet, I’ll return your *Anna Karenina*” (captions: *Ana Karenina*). “You have to read *Sapiens*; it is great.” Or, “I loved the last book you recommended.” These might come from relatives, friends, or work colleagues.

Finally, get inside Mila’s head and list the pains and gains. Pains (*dolores*) are the obstacles she would face when using our product; gains (*beneficios*) are what she hopes to get from it. One pain is having to learn to use a new app. Another serious one is having to add every book she has read one by one. Another is taking the time to write a recommendation that nobody reads. What does she hope for from our product? A library containing all her comments on the books she has read; finding the best books so she does not start them and have to leave them halfway through; and feeling that her opinions about literature are valued. With those sections filled, the empathy map for this persona is complete.
