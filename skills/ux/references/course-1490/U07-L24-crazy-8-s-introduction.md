## The Crazy 8s exercise

Anchor: 1490 · U7 · L24 · 00:00–01:04

We are going to sketch our digital product very quickly with Crazy 8s. I’ll show you how. The idea-generation technique called Crazy 8s is very simple: design eight ideas in five minutes. Fold a sheet of paper over itself three times until it has eight sections, then set a five-minute timer. Whether you work alone or as a team, start drawing during those five minutes and try not to get stuck. When the timer ends, stop drawing and present your ideas to the team very quickly.

## Moving beyond the first idea

Anchor: 1490 · U7 · L24 · 01:04–01:25

The five-minute limit helps us avoid getting blocked by thinking that an idea is bad. The goal is to go beyond the first idea, which is usually the least innovative and the one we feel attached to. We need to generate a wide variety of solutions to address the design challenge (*reto de diseño*). For example, if we have to design a product page, we should try to innovate. There will be time later to modify ideas and discard some of them.

## Recommendations for running it

Anchor: 1490 · U7 · L24 · 01:25–02:34

Here are my recommendations for the exercise. First, always use a marker with a medium-thick tip. If we use ballpoint pens, we draw with too much detail and may not finish within five minutes. Second, when doing this collectively, work in silence. At the beginning, define what you are going to design—for example, a product page—and answer participants’ questions. Third, do not keep checking the time. Focus on finding solutions and start the timer.

You may find versions of this exercise that use eight minutes or one minute per section. We are doing the original version: eight ideas in five minutes. Every idea is valid. When you begin designing, an inner voice may tell you, “This makes no sense; this is silly.” Ignore it, focus on getting the solutions out, and decide later which one to keep. Finally, and this is more of my own preference as a teacher, do not draw the borders of each section. You would lose seconds that may be vital to finishing the exercise, and the folds in the paper already mark the borders.

## The Design Studio process

Anchor: 1490 · U7 · L24 · 02:34–03:42

The Design Studio, which is based on Crazy 8s (captions: *Crazy Edge*), comes from the book *Lean UX* (captions: *Link UX*). It follows a similar process, but ends with a more finished sketch. First, review all the research notes, the poster, the affinity diagram and so on. Define the problem or design challenge with the team. Do a Crazy 8s exercise on that challenge, then vote using three dot stickers or a marker; you do not need to have stickers. Take the best ideas and do another Crazy 8s. Vote again, then develop the winning idea in more depth.

Because you have 25 minutes, you can look at references or discuss the idea with your teammates. Remember that at this point we are diverging (*divergir*): we need to generate many ideas, then choose the best one.
