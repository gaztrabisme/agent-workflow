# Unit 5 · The scope of the project

Anchor: 1490 · U5

After generating a lot of ideas and solutions, it's time to stick with only the features that add the most value.

## Tasks

- ### Create a Value Proposition Canvas 
Practice exercise 5\.1

Follow the steps below to complete the VPC in order.

 1\. **Customer Jobs** or activities that the user will carry out with our product.

 2\. **Pains** (pains): unwanted situations that you will experience when using our product or service: what makes you feel bad ?: frustrations, annoyances, etc.

 3\. **Gains** (benefits): Benefits you expect to obtain from using our product: what results and expectations does your potential user expect?

 4\. **Pain relievers** (analgesics): How can we solve the “Pains” that we have previously defined?

 Gain Creators (Vitamins): How are you providing benefits to your user based on the aforementioned expectations.

 5\. **Products \& Services** : The minimum products and services that we have to offer to satisfy users.

- ### Create five user stories 
Practice exercise 5\.2

Agile methodologies use "user stories" as the main tool to identify user requirements and needs.

 Stories typically follow a simple template: “As \[Rol/Persona], I want \[Descripción de la funcionalidad], to get \[Descripción de un beneficio o consecuencia].

 We have added the user context from the Job Story, so our story would be as follows:

 “As \[Rol/Persona], when \[Situación/Necesidad]I want \[Descripción de la funcionalidad], to get \[Descripción de un beneficio o consecuencia].

 Make five "user stories" based on your project information.

- ### Prioritize tasks with MoSCoW

Prioritize the functionalities of your project with the MoSCoW method. Remember that in a real context this exercise is recommended to be done with members of the business and technology team.

 1\. **Write in separate notes each of the functionalities** that we have proposed for our project. Many of them will have come from the reference document, the proto journeys, etc.

 2\. Rearrange your notes into the following categories:

 to. **Must\-have** These are the minimum requirements that we have to meet. Without them the project cannot be successful.

 b. **Should\-have** These are those functionalities that are highly desirable, they are functionalities that do not have such a high priority at the moment, but that will be necessary in a relatively short period of time.

 c. **Could\-have** They are also known as "nice\-to\-have". These are features that you would like to add if it falls into both budget and time scope. However, when they involve extra effort, there is no point in contemplating them.

 d. **Won't have / Would\-have** They are rather utopian or unrealistic wishes that are not yet worth investing in.
