# Unit 3 · Investigate: Agile Research Methods

Anchor: 1490 · U3

In this lesson you will learn about the most useful techniques when researching for our project without forgetting to be practical and very effective.

## Tasks

- ### Document yourself doing Desk Research

Search the internet for articles, technical papers, presentations, and studies on the subject of your project that can help you research your project.
- ### Prepare your reference document 
Practice exercise 3\.1

It is very important to learn by seeing how other projects have come up with solutions that could work for our digital product.

 1\. **Create** a document to paste the screenshots of other projects or products from which we can get ideas (they can be direct competitors or products that have nothing to do with our activity).

 2\. **Analyze** the digital products commenting on the solutions of each of the references. Try not to focus only on the visual design part and focus on functionality.

 3\. **Include** at the end of the presentation some conclusions and recommendations that will serve as the basis for our project.

 I recommend that before doing this exercise you take a look 👀 at the sample documents that I share with you in the "Additional Resources" section of this unit.

- ### Prepare and launch a survey 
Practice exercise 3\.2
- ### Conduct three interviews with potential users

**1\.** Create a short script about the topic to be investigated

 **2\.** Ask permission to record the interview with your phone.

 **3\.** After the interview, listen calmly to the recording and take note of the things that seem most important to your project.
- ### Make your affinity diagram

To correctly perform an affinity diagram we must follow the following steps:

 1\. **Collect** . Study the results of the *brief* , interviews, surveys and studies. Each, quote, comment and discovery should be written on separate sticky notes. These notes should be self\-explanatory and as summarized as possible. We can use different colors for each data source.

 2\. **Show data** . Place all the notes on a blackboard, large table (You can also do it in Figma, Mural or Miro) in a random way, without ordering.

 3\. **Organize the ideas in related groups** . You have to reposition the cards by affinity forming groups (and in case of doing it in a group, do it in silence so as not to condition the other participants). First we look for two cards that have something to do and they are grouped, then we look for more cards that can be related to the first.

 4\. **Create headlines for each group** . Once the groups are made, we will create headlines that represent each group.

 The ultimate goal is to organize the data in a way that makes it easier for us to spot repeating patterns, hot topics, and critical points that can become design opportunities.

 It is a simple, relatively fast and low\-cost technique that is of great help to us in projects with poorly structured or scattered information, and also when there is a large amount of information or it is necessary to put order in order to draw conclusions.
