## Creative influences and working off-screen

Anchor: 1490 · U1 · L02 · 00:00–01:06

In this lesson I want to talk about some of the influences that have inspired my work most. We'll start with a few books. The first is *Steal Like an Artist* by Austin Kleon. It isn't a user-experience (*experiencia de usuario*) book; it's about creativity, but it teaches you that you have to copy a lot in order to learn. It's very inspiring and has quotes like this one: “Start copying what you love. Copy, copy, copy. When you finish copying, you'll find yourself.”

It tells you to use your hands and to have projects that have nothing to do with your work, because they contribute a lot. Above all, it tells you to step away from the screen. Although we work with screens all day, making interfaces and working on user experience, it's very important to do things off-screen.

## Lean, Agile, and Design Thinking

Anchor: 1490 · U1 · L02 · 01:06–01:59

Next, in order of difficulty, comes *Lean vs. Agile vs. Design Thinking*. It's a very small book in which Jeff Gothelf (captions: *Jeff Godelf*) explains very clearly what Lean is for, what Agile is, and what the agile methodologies are for. It has really great illustrations that I'm going to show you right now. Here you can see the illustrations. They're quite funny because they remind me of American evangelical church pamphlets.

In this case, I think the man represents a UX designer (*diseñador UX*); the woman could be the developer; and the person holding the weight underneath represents the business side. That's the balance we have to achieve there, doing a bit of juggling so that the projects we design turn out well.

## Don Norman and everyday objects

Anchor: 1490 · U1 · L02 · 01:59–02:37

Now let's look at the next book. This is a great classic, *The Design of Everyday Things* by Don Norman. Don Norman is an engineer, psychologist, and designer; to me, he's like the Gandalf of user experience. In this book, with its many examples, we look at badly designed products. When we use them, we often feel stupid, when in fact the product itself was designed badly.

It also has a chapter focused on user-centered design (*diseño centrado en el usuario*), something that, when I read it, had a big influence on the way I work.

## The laws of simplicity

Anchor: 1490 · U1 · L02 · 02:37–03:10

This is *The Laws of Simplicity* by John Maeda, a professor at MIT. It presents ten universal laws for designing products, businesses, or making your life easier. In addition to the ten laws, it has three keys that helped me a lot when I was designing. The first is that things look much simpler from far away. The second is that opening up and sharing makes things simpler. The last one is about energy. By using less, we have to achieve more.

## Lean UX and Lean Startup

Anchor: 1490 · U1 · L02 · 03:10–03:45

This is my favorite book, *Lean UX* by Jeff Gothelf (captions: *Link UX*). I recommend it wholeheartedly, although it's a little difficult to read. The first time I tried, I left it halfway through and picked it up again about a year later. Honestly, it's helped me a lot in my work.

The book is inspired by another one called *The Lean Startup*, which is about testing a business with the fewest possible resources. Throughout this Domestika course, we're going to use many of the exercises from the book, because at our studio they've helped us a lot in our day-to-day work. Thanks to this book, you'll work much less while getting the maximum possible result.

## Books, product discovery, and design news

Anchor: 1490 · U1 · L02 · 03:45–04:29

I always recommend reading books rather than internet articles, but I'm going to recommend two websites. The first is Product Hunt (captions: *Produzhan*), a site where different products and services are posted and people can vote for them. There we can discover lots of new products and ideas that are coming to market. It's perfect for getting ideas for our projects, and also for finding inspiration in the design being used right now.

The second website is Sidebar, which collects the five best design news stories of the day. Instead of wasting time browsing and reading everything you find on the internet, here you can make sure you find quality content about design. I recommend checking it from time to time, and if you need to look for a design topic, use its search engine. You don't have to go there every day.

## Stoicism and user research

Anchor: 1490 · U1 · L02 · 04:29–05:11

I'll also mention Stoic philosophers like Marcus Aurelius, who have helped us take a lot of drama out of life, especially out of creative work. In the end, work is for having a good time, not for suffering all day. He has a quote that I really like when it comes to researching users (*investigar a los usuarios*): “Everything we hear is an opinion, not a fact, and everything we see is a perspective, not the truth.”

It seems Marcus Aurelius already knew how to do user research when designing an empire in the second century AD. Those are some of my influences. I'll leave you some more in the additional resources, and in the next lesson we'll talk about everything we're going to cover in the course.

### Key points (derived)

- Creative influences can come from outside user experience design; practice, copying, and off-screen work can support it.
- Lean, Agile, and Design Thinking require a balance among design, development, and business roles.
- User research treats what people say as opinion and what we observe as perspective, rather than as unquestionable fact or truth.
