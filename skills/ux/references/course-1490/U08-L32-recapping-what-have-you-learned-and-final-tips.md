You will not know whether the exercises will help, but as the project goes on, the way forward will become clearer until we reach our minimum viable product (MVP; *producto mínimo viable*). At first, we may not know what kind of digital product to make, whether it should be a website or an app. That will emerge as we research and move forward with the project.

## Adapt the process, research, and find a mentor

Anchor: 1490 · U8 · L32 · 01:26–02:23

There is no single path for UX (*experiencia de usuario*) design. I have shown you one process; you can vary it, change it, and probably improve it. We have changed our process in the studio over the years and will keep changing it. Today it works for 99.9% of our projects.

![1490 · U8 · L32 · 01:45.30](frames/U08L32-F0145.30.jpg)

On screen: "No hay un solo camino para el diseño UX." [There is no single path for UX design.]  
Shows: A slide stating that UX design has no single path.

I recommend doing a lot of research. Our clients or bosses will often want us to start drawing screens immediately, but research is very important. There is no point drawing screens if the functionality will not help our users. Please do the research. I also recommend finding a mentor or a buddy with a little more experience who can help you make certain decisions. This person does not need to be a teacher or someone with extensive experience; they could even be another student or someone who has only recently started working in this area. In the end, two brains will always think more than one.

## Unlearn, then learn by practicing

Anchor: 1490 · U8 · L32 · 02:23–03:17

It is very important to learn the art of unlearning (*desaprender*), especially if, like me, you come from visual design. I was taught to make websites by designing the final UI from top to bottom, and it took me a while to unlearn that. It also took me a while to stop starting the wireframes (captions: *webframes*) on a computer. It took almost a year before I started doing them by hand. When I finally began doing that by hand on every project, I realized how much time and energy I had wasted. It is very important to learn and try new methods.

![1490 · U8 · L32 · 02:47.60](frames/U08L32-F0247.60.jpg)

On screen: "Aprende el arte de desaprender..." [Learn the art of unlearning...] / "ESPECIALMENTE SI VIENES DE DISEÑO VISUAL" [ESPECIALLY IF YOU COME FROM VISUAL DESIGN]  
Shows: A slide advising visual designers to unlearn old habits.

Another tip is that the best way to learn is by practicing. You have just taken a Domestika course, perhaps you have taken others or are reading a manual; I have read many. But the fastest way is to do projects. They can be for clients or at work, but you can also do projects for yourself, where making mistakes has no consequences (captions: *no hay ningún tipo de pertenencia*).

## Make room for imposter feelings

Anchor: 1490 · U8 · L32 · 03:17–03:59

Embrace imposter syndrome. We will always be trying new techniques, exercises, or programs, so it is normal to feel a little insecure at first. Practice will help with that. Do not worry if you feel as though you do not know enough; it happens to all of us. If you do not have imposter syndrome, perhaps you are not learning anything, so that may be when you should worry.

![1490 · U8 · L32 · 03:17.60](frames/U08L32-F0317.60.jpg)

On screen: "Abraza al síndrome del impostor." [Embrace imposter syndrome.]

People often ask how we are doing, and we see ourselves in this cartoon by Pablo Stanley. We may say that everything is going well, but inside, our mind is telling us to stop, that we are good for nothing, that we are imposters, and so on. This is very normal; it happens to us almost every day or in almost every project.

## Choose exercises critically and learn from experienced designers

Anchor: 1490 · U8 · L32 · 03:59–05:06

Use critical thinking when deciding which exercises to do. Of the exercises we have seen in the course, some will give you more value than others. You are free to do them or not, but give them a chance. In each project, think critically about which exercises can help and which will only waste time and energy. Learn to recognize which ones work for you.

![1490 · U8 · L32 · 04:17.60](frames/U08L32-F0417.60.jpg)

On screen: "Ten pensamiento crítico a la hora de elegir qué ejercicios hacer y cuáles no." [Think critically when choosing which exercises to do and which not to do.]

One of the fastest ways to learn is from the best. On the internet and social media, you can find articles and posts by people such as Don Norman, Jakob Nielsen, and Jared Spool. They share their experience and show us new techniques, exercises, and programs. ## Build breadth, depth, empathy, and simplicity

Anchor: 1490 · U8 · L32 · 05:06–06:34

Think about the T-shaped knowledge model (*modelo del conocimiento en forma de T*). As UX designers, we need a basic knowledge of many disciplines. As we have seen in the course, we need to know a little about project management, product design, business, information architecture (*arquitectura de información*), and visual design. We will also have deep knowledge of one discipline. In my case, it is graphic design, but UX experts may come from marketing, business, programming, or any other discipline.

In the industrial era, people specialized in a single job for years and became experts at tightening a screw or making a clay pot. But the demands of current projects mean we need a basic knowledge of many disciplines. To me, the main quality of a good product UX designer is empathy (*empatía*): the ability to keep learning how to empathize, not only with users but also with the business team, clients, and others.

Something else I have learned over the years is to simplify. At first I used every methodology and every program as well as I could, but the results were not better; they were much worse than when we simplified. We need to simplify project management, product design, and every process we carry out. Most of all, we should enjoy our work, the results of our projects, and what we learn from each mistake. This is a very fun job that helps businesses and end users.

## Keep learning through resources and practice

Anchor: 1490 · U8 · L32 · 06:34–09:11

Keep learning. There are thousands, if not millions, of pages and resources about UX. I will leave you with the ones that have helped me and the ones I usually recommend to my students.

I recommend Panda, a news aggregator focused on design or programming. It gives you the leading stories from important blogs and websites in the field. Rather than reading all the stories at once, I filter them and save them in Pocket, a kind of platform or social network where I can keep articles and read them when I have time.

![1490 · U8 · L32 · 07:08.30](frames/U08L32-F0708.30.jpg)

On screen: "PANDA" / "USEPANDA.COM" / "POCKET" / "GETPOCKET.COM/"  
Shows: The Panda and Pocket logos and their websites.

To avoid fear of missing out, or FOMO, instead of reading a huge number of stories, I recommend Sidebar. It is the only site where I look at design news today. A team selects five links a day by searching Twitter and other sites, reading those stories, and keeping only the most important and relevant ones. For example, I discovered how to make prototypes (captions: *protoyornis*) through Sidebar.

![1490 · U8 · L32 · 07:30.70](frames/U08L32-F0730.70.jpg)

On screen: "SIDEBAR" / "SIDEBAR.IO"
