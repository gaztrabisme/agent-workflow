# Unit 7 · Wireframes Sketching and Rapid Prototyping

Anchor: 1490 · U7

I'll show you the different types of *wireframes* and when to use each one. I'll tell you about design patterns and why we always have to make them by hand rather than with a design program.

## Tasks

- ### Get ideas with Crazy 8's

Use this technique to brainstorm ideas for your project. You can do it alone, but I recommend that you try to do it in a group, these are the instructions:

 1\. Define the theme, screen or functionality on which you are going to do the exercise.

 2\. Fold your paper into eight sections.

 3\. Set the timer for 5 minutes.

 4\. Individually, each team member draws an idea in each rectangle, doing their best to fill in all eight sections.

 5\. When the timer goes off, it stops drawing.

 6\. We present our ideas to the rest of the team (maximum 2 min per person).
- ### Draw your screen 
Practice exercise 7\.1

*Sketching* of a screen. Based on the best idea you have obtained from Crazy 8's, draw the screen by HAND in detail (they can be one or more). For this task you must see references that you can inspire when designing. Maximum time for the sketch: 25 minutes.

 If you are brave or you handle a design or presentation program (Keynote, Powerpoint), I invite you to turn the *wireframe* into high\-fidelity *wireframes* .

- ### Make your prototype by hand 
Practice exercise 7\.2

Draw the different screens to make a prototype that we can later test with users. You can do it by hand or use your favorite design program.

 When creating the screens for your prototype, be inspired by known design patterns, take photos or scan your designed screens and create the prototype with the simple POP / Marvel tool or any other that you already know: Invision, Figma, etc.

- ### Test with users 
Practice exercise 7\.3

The time has come to test the prototype with potential users of our digital product:

 **Create** a short script with the questions and tasks for the test.

 **Test** always the prototype yourself before doing the test with users.

 **Informs** to *tester* that it is not him, but the design of our product that will be evaluated.

 **Tell him** to think aloud (Think ALoud).

 If he gets stuck tell him to do the next task.

 **Remember** not to give your opinion.

 Once we obtain the results of the tests we can iterate the design of our prototype to improve it.
