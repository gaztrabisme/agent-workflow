## What the course will cover

Anchor: 798 · U1 · L03 · 00:00–00:58

In this lesson, I want to explain what we will do during the course. First, we will begin with the basic principles of UX writing. And keep in mind: it goes much further than writing words for websites and apps. I also want you to understand the whole design process behind them, which happens before you write the first word. I will explain the science of reading. Every time you interact with an app or a website, you interact with both the visual part and the text. It is important to understand the rules, or how we read, because people say you have to understand those rules before you can break them. The good thing is that you will discover the reading patterns (*patrones de lectura*) you can use to design your content so it is easier to understand and, above all, remember. I will also give you some guidelines for writing words.

## Context shapes what the user needs

Anchor: 798 · U1 · L03 · 00:58–01:18

It is especially important to see how this changes depending on which part of the experience your user is in. Understanding the context is very important, as is understanding where they are in the experience, because that way you can make sure the content is as useful as possible for each step.

## Resources for getting started

Anchor: 798 · U1 · L03 · 01:18–02:03

## Principles to apply in the project

Anchor: 798 · U1 · L03 · 02:03–02:58

I will also share some tips and tricks I have learned over the years. This will help you make sure that everything you write is centered on the person who will use your app or website. And that all your microcopy is clear, concise, useful and, above all, enjoyable (captions: *Omar*).

For my final project, we will use all these basic principles that I will explain, and I will apply them to something I really like: tacos. So my idea is to create different screens for a reservation app for a taqueria. That is what I like; I invite you to choose what works best for you, whether it is an existing client, your personal brand, or something you want to invent from scratch. The idea is to put all these concepts into practice. Now you know what we will do in this course. In the next lesson, we will get down to the subject, and I will explain what UX writing is.

### Key points (derived)

- UX writing begins before the first word, with the design process and an understanding of how people read.
- Match content to the user’s context and stage in the experience; make microcopy clear, concise, useful, and enjoyable.
- Practice the principles by designing screens for a project of your choice.
