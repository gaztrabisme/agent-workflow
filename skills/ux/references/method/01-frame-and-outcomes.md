# Stage 1: Frame the outcome and the brief

## Reader and scope

**Reader:** a product team deciding what problem to solve, for whom, and what evidence would show progress. **Scope:** align the user outcome, business need, development constraint, and project boundary before committing to a detailed interface.

## First idea, stated as the rule

- Start by asking what the project is for. In the teacher's comparison, Sue learns the client and business needs, revises the brief with the team, and validates information structure and wireframes before making detailed comps; Ed's early detailed design leads to repeated revisions. (1490 · U2 · L04 · 00:08–02:24)
- Keep three concerns visible together: value to users, value to the business, and development cost or difficulty. (1490 · U2 · L04 · 02:24–03:22)
- Treat the brief as a hypothesis that can change as the team learns. The course describes Agile work in small blocks where testing can change specifications and send the team backward or forward. (1490 · U2 · L04 · 03:22–04:41)
- The course recap presents the route through research, empathy, scope, information architecture, ideation, prototype/testing, and UI design as iterative; teams can move between stages as they learn. (1490 · U8 · L32 · 00:00–01:26; 1490 · U8 · L32 · 01:26–02:23)
- Bring words into experience design early. The course's sports-news example shows that real content changes how much space the wireframe needs, while placeholder text hides that constraint. (798 · U2 · L05 · 00:07–01:55; 798 · U2 · L05 · 01:55–03:03)

## Put the decision in a short brief

Record the user's task, the problem to solve, the expected user benefit, the business outcome, known constraints, and the key assumption to test. Keep the requested solution separate from the problem statement so evidence can change the proposed answer. This brief format is **derived** from the course's user/business/development balance and its account of revising a requested brief after research. (derived from 1490 · U2 · L04 · 02:24–03:22; 1490 · U2 · L04 · 05:12–06:48)

## Failures

**Problem: designing the requested solution before agreeing on the problem.**
- **The failure:** detailed work is made before the client and team validate the need, then comes back for revisions. (1490 · U2 · L04 · 00:08–02:24)
- **The fix:** clarify the user's need and business outcome, note development constraints, agree what to learn next, and validate a small piece before expanding it. (1490 · U2 · L04 · 02:24–03:22; 1490 · U2 · L04 · 03:22–04:41)

**Problem: postponing content until boxes are fixed.**
- **The failure:** real wording or a longer localized string no longer fits the placeholder layout. (798 · U2 · L05 · 01:55–03:03)
- **The fix:** include draft content and realistic text lengths while defining the brief and initial screen structure. (798 · U2 · L05 · 01:55–03:03)

## Check before moving on

**T1 — Outcome and constraints:** the brief names the user and task, the problem, the user outcome, the business outcome, the main assumption, and known development constraints. Do not treat an unvalidated requested solution as the problem statement.

**Practice the teacher sets:** explain a project idea and the need it solves; write a short product introduction that says what it does, who benefits, what problem it solves, and what makes it different.
