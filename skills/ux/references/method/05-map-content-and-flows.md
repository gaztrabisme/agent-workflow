# Stage 5: Map content and flows

## Reader and scope

**Reader:** a team deciding how people find information and complete important tasks. **Scope:** organize content, label routes, and map screens and branches before building a prototype.

## First idea, stated as the rule

- Organize and label content so people can find and manage it; consider context, content, and users together. (1490 · U6 · L21 · 00:00–01:33; 1490 · U6 · L21 · 02:45–03:21; 1490 · U6 · L21 · 03:21–04:20)
- Use a sitemap to describe the content hierarchy and navigation. Make it with the people involved and check whether users can find items in it. (1490 · U6 · L22 · 00:00–00:50; 1490 · U6 · L22 · 00:50–01:12; 1490 · U6 · L22 · 03:48–04:14; 1490 · U6 · L22 · 04:14–04:45)
- Map a user flow for a task: list entry points, draw the happy path, add relevant branches, and show only the key screens. A user journey instead follows a person's actions and feelings over time. (1490 · U6 · L23 · 00:00–01:12; 1490 · U6 · L23 · 06:28–07:19; 1490 · U6 · L23 · 07:19–08:22; 1490 · U4 · L16 · 00:00–01:17)
- Consider the whole route. UX writing needs to account for where someone came from and where the product sends them next, rather than polishing one isolated screen. (798 · U2 · L05 · 09:05–11:31)

## A rule you derived (derived)

Start from journeys when a new product still needs opportunity discovery; use flows when a chosen task needs its screens, branches, and outcomes made explicit. The courses distinguish feelings over time from task routes, and the journey lesson recommends using it to discover opportunities before choosing features. (derived from 1490 · U4 · L16 · 00:00–01:17; 1490 · U4 · L16 · 12:51–13:57; 1490 · U6 · L23 · 06:28–07:19; 1490 · U6 · L23 · 07:19–08:22)

## Failures

**Problem: writing copy for an isolated screen.**
- **The failure:** a message may not make sense because its entry point, prior action, or next step is missing. (798 · U2 · L05 · 09:05–11:31)
- **The fix:** trace the task from entry through completion and include the important branches and relevant screen states. (1490 · U6 · L23 · 00:00–01:12; 1490 · U6 · L23 · 06:28–07:19; 1490 · U6 · L23 · 07:19–08:22)

## Check before moving on

**T5 — Information and route:** content has understandable labels and structure; the important task has a named entry point, happy path, relevant decisions or errors, and a completion state; the journey and flow are not being used interchangeably.

**Practice the teacher sets:** make a sitemap and define one main user flow with steps, actions, and screens. See [1490 · U6 unit](../course-1490/U06-unit.md).
