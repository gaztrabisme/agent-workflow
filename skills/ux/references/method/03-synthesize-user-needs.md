# Stage 3: Synthesize user needs

## Reader and scope

**Reader:** a team turning research into a useful working picture of people and opportunities. **Scope:** organize findings without turning assumptions into evidence, then identify the person and task that should guide the next decisions.

## First idea, stated as the rule

- Synthesize research with one concept per note, group similar observations, then name the themes together. Retain source cues when useful and keep repeated findings traceable to their sources. (1490 · U3 · L12 · 01:10–03:41; 1490 · U3 · L12 · 05:37–07:53)
- Select the most important findings for a dated research summary that shows categories, user statements, data sources, and sample sizes; put the findings before decoration. (1490 · U3 · L12 · 08:54–10:04; 1490 · U3 · L12 · 10:04–11:01)
- A persona is an archetype based on researched behavior patterns. The UX writing course also demonstrates a proto-persona made before research; treat that as a provisional hypothesis, then update it when evidence arrives. (1490 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–01:04; 798 · U2 · L06 · 05:52–08:19)
- An empathy map records what the team thinks one person sees, hears, says, does, thinks, and feels; the course distinguishes it from a way to collect data. (1490 · U4 · L15 · 00:00–01:22)

## Keep evidence and hypotheses visibly separate

| Statement type | Record it as | Use it for | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observation or research finding | Finding with source and context | Explain what people did, said, or experienced | 1490 · U3 · L12 · 01:10–03:41 |
| Early user picture without research | Provisional persona or assumption | Identify questions for research, not proof of a segment | 798 · U2 · L06 · 05:52–08:19 |
| Research insight | Discovery or insight found by asking users or researching | Use the evidence to redefine the real problem, then prototype ideas with real users | 1490 · U2 · L04 · 05:12–06:48 |

## A rule you derived (derived)

Label provisional user profiles and emotional interpretations explicitly. This preserves the usefulness of an early proto-persona while respecting the research-grounded persona definition and the empathy map's role as a team belief tool. (derived from 798 · U2 · L06 · 05:52–08:19; 1490 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–01:04; 1490 · U4 · L15 · 00:00–01:22)

## Failures

**Problem: presenting an imagined user as a research result.**
- **The failure (derived):** treating an invented proto-persona as evidence about actual users. The UX writing course calls its example fictional; the product-design course allows invented details before research and says they can be updated when research is available. (derived from 798 · U2 · L06 · 05:52–08:19; 1490 · U4 · L14 · 02:34–04:44)
- **The fix:** mark the profile as provisional, add sources as findings arrive, and revise the goals and behavior from observed patterns. (1490 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–01:04)

## Check before moving on

**T3 — Research synthesis and user model:** the synthesis retains source context; persona goals relate to a real task; assumptions and team interpretations are marked separately from findings.

**Practice the teacher sets:** make proto-personas, an empathy map, and a five-to-eight-step proto-journey with feelings and opportunities. See [1490 · U4 unit](../course-1490/U04-unit.md).
