# Stage 2: Research and evidence

## Reader and scope

**Reader:** a team deciding what it knows about people, their context, and the problem. **Scope:** choose a research method for the question and use evidence to guide language and product decisions.

## First idea, stated as the rule

- Learn what users understand, want, and struggle with by talking to users and people who support them, and by reviewing relevant product data and past attempts. Team assumptions alone do not establish what users need. (798 · U2 · L05 · 06:04–08:15; 798 · U2 · L06 · 01:30–03:08; 798 · U2 · L06 · 03:08–04:11; 798 · U2 · L06 · 04:11–05:52)
- Continue research during feature definition, ideation, prototyping, and testing; research is not only a kickoff activity. (1490 · U3 · L07 · 01:00–01:39)
- Choose the method for the question. Surveys help quantify patterns; interviews and contextual observation help investigate needs, motivations, and what people do in context. Counts can point to a problem without explaining its cause. (1490 · U3 · L09 · 00:00–01:25; 1490 · U3 · L09 · 01:25–02:20; 1490 · U3 · L10 · 01:15–02:11; 1490 · U3 · L10 · 02:11–03:11; 1490 · U3 · L10 · 03:11–03:32; 1490 · U3 · L11 · 00:00–01:57; 1490 · U3 · L11 · 01:57–03:08)
- Use secondary research to learn what already exists, while treating it as a complement to direct user research. (1490 · U3 · L08 · 00:33–01:45)

## Match the method to the question

| Need to learn | Method | Guardrail | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| How often a behavior or preference occurs | Focused survey or existing product measures | Show the response count; a proportion does not explain why people chose it. | 1490 · U3 · L09 · 00:00–01:25; 1490 · U3 · L09 · 01:25–02:20 |
| What a person wants, expects, or finds confusing | Short, neutral interview guide with follow-up questions | Avoid leading language; probe for context and reasons. | 1490 · U3 · L10 · 01:15–02:11; 1490 · U3 · L10 · 03:32–04:35; 1490 · U3 · L10 · 04:35–05:18 |
| What people do in a real setting | Contextual observation of a planned task | Record actions and context before interpreting them. | 1490 · U3 · L11 · 00:00–01:57 |
| What existing products teach about language or patterns | Desk research and annotated references | Summarize useful findings; do not substitute competitor screenshots for user evidence. | 1490 · U3 · L08 · 00:33–01:45; 1490 · U3 · L08 · 01:45–03:14 |

## A rule you derived (derived)

Keep each decision-changing statement linked to its evidence or label it as an assumption to test. This combines the UX writing course's instruction to use user conversations and data with the Lean UX course's repeated cycle of research, experiment, analysis, and revision. (derived from 798 · U2 · L05 · 06:04–08:15; 1490 · U3 · L07 · 01:00–01:39)

## Failures

**Problem: mistaking a count for an explanation.**
- **The failure:** survey percentages show which response was common, but not what caused the preference. (1490 · U3 · L09 · 01:25–02:20)
- **The fix:** use the quantitative result to locate a question, then use interviews or observation to investigate reasons and context. (1490 · U3 · L09 · 00:00–01:25; 1490 · U3 · L09 · 01:25–02:20; 1490 · U3 · L10 · 01:15–02:11; 1490 · U3 · L10 · 02:11–03:11; 1490 · U3 · L10 · 03:11–03:32)

## Check before moving on

**T2 — Evidence and assumptions:** every decision-changing user claim is tied to a source or marked as an assumption; the chosen method can answer the stated research question; survey counts are not presented as explanations.

**Practice the teacher sets:** create a reference document, prepare a survey, conduct interviews, organize findings, and make a research poster. See [1490 · U3 unit](../course-1490/U03-unit.md).
