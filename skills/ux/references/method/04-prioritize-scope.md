# Stage 4: Prioritize scope

## Reader and scope

**Reader:** a team choosing which user need and product idea to address first. **Scope:** connect user value, business value, effort, and a testable learning goal to the smallest useful scope.

## First idea, stated as the rule

- Turn a journey opportunity into one or more “How Might We?” questions, then generate possible features before filtering them. (1490 · U4 · L16 · 05:46–07:24; 1490 · U4 · L16 · 12:51–13:57)
- Connect customer jobs, pains, and gains to pain relievers, gain creators, and the product or service being considered. (1490 · U5 · L18 · 01:21–02:55; 1490 · U5 · L18 · 02:55–03:52; 1490 · U5 · L18 · 03:52–04:20; 1490 · U5 · L18 · 04:20–04:52; 1490 · U5 · L18 · 04:52–06:00)
- Express a need as a user story or a situation-led Job Story so the feature remains tied to a person and a goal. (1490 · U5 · L19 · 00:00–01:27; 1490 · U5 · L19 · 01:27–02:18; 1490 · U5 · L19 · 02:18–02:55)
- Treat an MVP as the smallest version that tests whether a hypothesis is valid and maximizes learning with minimal effort. Keep the core functions well designed; “minimum” does not mean mediocre. (1490 · U5 · L17 · 02:00–02:48; 1490 · U5 · L17 · 03:34–04:59)
- Prioritize candidate functions with MoSCoW or a user/business benefit and complexity matrix. Their scores support discussion; the teacher describes the result as indicative and negotiable. (1490 · U5 · L20 · 00:40–01:57; 1490 · U5 · L20 · 01:57–03:01; 1490 · U5 · L20 · 03:01–03:59)

## A rule you derived (derived)

For every item in the first testable scope, record the user need it supports and the assumption the team expects to learn about. This connects the course's value canvas and MVP definition to Lean UX's outcome and learning focus. ## Failures

**Problem: treating a prioritization score as a decision made by arithmetic.**
- **The failure:** a matrix can make estimates look exact even though its inputs and negotiated priority are judgments. (1490 · U5 · L20 · 01:57–03:01; 1490 · U5 · L20 · 03:01–03:59)
- **The fix:** show the criteria and assumptions beside the score, then let the team confirm the tradeoff and the learning objective. (1490 · U2 · L04 · 02:24–03:22; 1490 · U5 · L20 · 03:01–03:59)

## Check before moving on

**T4 — Scope and learning:** each selected feature supports a stated user need; the MVP tests a named assumption; business value and effort are visible; priorities are treated as team decisions rather than objective scores.

**Practice the teacher sets:** complete the Value Proposition Canvas, write user stories or Job Stories, and prioritize functions with MoSCoW. See [1490 · U5 unit](../course-1490/U05-unit.md).
