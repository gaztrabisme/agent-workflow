# Course 3069 — working notes

Source read: every available lesson transcript and unit task sheet in `references/course-3069/`. The course frames the work as a data-driven poster made from a book; it uses *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz* as its running project.

## Method steps

1. **Find a subject that matters to you.** Start from a strong interest, emotion, memory, or something you want to understand; the subject should keep you engaged. Use books, films, and other personal sources as possibilities. (3069 · U2 · L03 · 00:00–01:12)
2. **Choose a manageable source and learn its context.** Check whether its data is usable, look for background and original imagery, save useful references, and keep track of the source so readers can trace it. The teacher recommends a short book or film as a manageable start. (3069 · U2 · L04 · 00:00–01:20; 3069 · U2 · L04 · 01:20–04:00; 3069 · U2 · L04 · 04:00–05:22; 3069 · U2 · L04 · 05:22–07:10)
3. **Harvest and tabulate the data.** Mark selected characters, places, colors, objects, or other elements in the source; make repeated passes and include indirect references; enter counts by chapter or other chosen grouping into tables. (3069 · U2 · L05 · 00:00–03:31; 3069 · U2 · L05 · 03:31–06:35; 3069 · U2 · L05 · 06:35–08:07)
4. **Review the data and find possible stories.** Calculate totals and other simple summaries, compare relative sizes, look for relationships and outliers, and decide whether one dataset or a combination of layers offers a useful direction. (3069 · U2 · L06 · 00:40–01:28; 3069 · U2 · L06 · 01:28–02:44; 3069 · U2 · L06 · 02:44–03:25)
5. **Sketch a storyline and visual marks.** List important characters, places, and objects; identify features that could stand for them; test marks and how they work together. Decide what the visualization should show and begin considering the canvas and layout. Look again at references if the style is unclear. (3069 · U3 · L07 · 00:00–03:36; 3069 · U3 · L07 · 03:36–05:02; 3069 · U3 · L07 · 05:02–06:55; 3069 · U3 · L07 · 07:43–10:08)
6. **Prototype the data structure and choose a visual style.** Try a basic graph to see how the data might fit; sample a palette from relevant source imagery; test abstract symbols, patterns, sizes, and alternatives. Keep the useful variations for later. (3069 · U3 · L08 · 00:19–01:07; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 01:07–03:16; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 04:38–06:51; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 06:51–08:00; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 08:00–09:18; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 09:18–10:49)
7. **Build the main layout from the selected measures.** In the course example, chapter circles vary with word count, character marks inside them show the chapter contents, and the character symbols are also sized to their total mentions. Add other data layers, such as locations, where they add information to the story. (3069 · U3 · L07 · 05:02–06:55; 3069 · U3 · L10 · 03:41–06:15; 3069 · U3 · L10 · 16:11–18:44; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 11:35–13:29)
8. **Iterate while integrating labels and context.** Rearrange as new information changes the page; give readers chapter order, titles, counts, an introduction, and a legend. Place explanations close to the elements they explain. Finish with a title that suits the angle and subject. (3069 · U3 · L09 · 00:00–01:37; 3069 · U3 · L10 · 06:15–09:11; 3069 · U3 · L10 · 14:18–16:11; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 13:29–14:33; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 14:33–16:28; 3069 · U3 · L12 · 00:00–01:14; 3069 · U3 · L12 · 01:14–02:38)
9. **Get feedback, check the artifact, and edit.** Ask someone to interpret it without explanation; review text, placement, contrast, symbol decoding, and print appearance; make corrections and proofread through the chapters. (3069 · U4 · L13 · 00:00–01:30; 3069 · U4 · L13 · 01:30–02:38; 3069 · U4 · L13 · 02:38–03:42; 3069 · U4 · L13 · 03:42–04:16)
10. **Present the work in its intended medium.** Print and inspect the physical result, adjust if screen and print differ, prepare the print file for the chosen service, credit sources, and share progress and finished work through a suitable channel. (3069 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–01:46; 3069 · U4 · L14 · 01:46–02:24; 3069 · U4 · L14 · 02:24–03:07; 3069 · U4 · L14 · 03:07–03:59; 3069 · U4 · L14 · 03:59–04:25)

## Principles and claims

- A personally meaningful subject can help the maker stay connected to the work. (3069 · U2 · L03 · 00:00–01:12)
- Start with a manageable source; check whether its data is free to use, and name the source so readers can trace it. (3069 · U2 · L04 · 00:00–01:20; 3069 · U2 · L04 · 01:20–04:00)
- Repeated reading and marking can catch names, indirect references, and other elements that a first pass misses. (3069 · U2 · L05 · 00:00–03:31)
- Combining datasets can give viewers more to explore, while a single dataset can also be a valid starting point. (3069 · U2 · L06 · 00:40–01:28)
- Relative size, relationships, and outliers in the counts can suggest different stories or visual directions. (3069 · U2 · L06 · 01:28–02:44; 3069 · U2 · L06 · 02:44–03:25)
- A character mark can be figurative or abstract and can take a cue from a distinctive trait, material, or appearance. (3069 · U3 · L07 · 00:00–03:36; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 08:00–09:18)
- The selected shapes and marks should relate to the data or story and to the way the page is arranged. (3069 · U3 · L08 · 10:49–12:02)
- The workflow moves between source, spreadsheet, search, sketch, and design; mistakes can lead to new ideas, and the teacher recommends treating the process as exploration. (3069 · U3 · L09 · 00:00–01:37)
- The course example encodes chapter length with the chapter circle and character appearances with repeated marks inside it. (3069 · U3 · L10 · 03:41–06:15)
- Long chapter names should not force readers to turn their heads or read upside down or sideways; the teacher splits some titles across two parts. (3069 · U3 · L10 · 09:11–12:46)
- Keep a reference size when scaling total-mention symbols so the size encoding remains interpretable and mistakes are less likely. (3069 · U3 · L10 · 16:11–18:44)
- Nearby explanations reduce the need to look back and forth between a legend and the marks. (3069 · U3 · L11 · 14:33–16:28)
- A title can either create curiosity about the project or plainly state its subject. (3069 · U3 · L12 · 00:00–01:14)
- Another person should be able to understand the insight and what the visualization shows; feedback can reveal unclear text or explanation. (3069 · U4 · L13 · 00:00–01:30; 3069 · U4 · L13 · 01:30–02:38)
- Screen and print colors can differ; inspect the printed piece and adjust it if it does not match the intended result. (3069 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–01:46)
- The finished poster combines chapter circles, character marks, location illustrations, labels, title, and explanatory copy. (frame) (3069 · U3 · L11 · 14:33–16:28)

## Checks the teacher uses

- **Check data totals and summaries.** The Unit 2 task asks for sums, totals, averages, or graphs after tabulation. Rendered artifact: compare calculated totals and averages with the source table. (Unit 2 task: “Review the Data”; 3069 · U2)
- **Look for outliers.** The teacher points out that Emerald City has a much larger total than the other listed locations. Rendered artifact: compare the listed counts and identify values separated from the rest. (3069 · U2 · L06 · 02:44–03:25)
- **Check that chapter marks represent the counted characters.** The teacher repeats symbols until the chapter circles contain the characters listed in the data. Rendered artifact: count each mark and compare it with the chapter table. (3069 · U3 · L10 · 03:41–06:15)
- **Check sizes against the count graph and keep circles circular.** Rendered artifact: compare each symbol's width and height and its size with the graph's corresponding value. (3069 · U3 · L11 · 00:00–01:30)
- **Check proportions and fit on the canvas.** The teacher looks for circles that fit and are not awkwardly large; this is judged by eye in the layout. (3069 · U3 · L10 · 00:00–01:14)
- **Check long curved labels for comfortable reading direction and fit.** This is judged by eye while adjusting text on the path. (3069 · U3 · L10 · 09:11–12:46)
- **Check that the legend explains the marks and sits near them.** Readers should not need to keep looking back and forth; inspect the rendered page and judge proximity and clarity. (3069 · U3 · L11 · 14:33–16:28)
- **Ask an unbriefed viewer what the title, explanation, information, and data show.** This needs a viewer's interpretation; the course gives no numeric pass threshold. (3069 · U4 · L13 · 00:00–01:30; 3069 · U4 · L13 · 01:30–02:38; Unit 4 task: “Get Feedback”; 3069 · U4)
- **Print a draft and inspect contrast, element size, and accidental movement.** These are checked by eye on the printed artifact. (3069 · U4 · L13 · 01:30–02:38)
- **Preview color-vision proof modes and check whether marks remain distinct and decodable.** This is a visual proof check; the course gives no numeric contrast threshold. (3069 · U4 · L13 · 02:38–03:42)
- **Proofread spelling and placement, going chapter by chapter.** Rendered artifact: compare visible text with the intended copy and inspect the position of each element. (3069 · U4 · L13 · 03:42–04:16)
- **Zoom out or look through eyelashes to judge balance and emphasis.** This is a visual judgment suggested in the Unit 4 task sheet. (Unit 4 task: “Test Readability”; 3069 · U4)

## Tasks

The following are the practice tasks and activity prompts set in the unit sheets.

- **Unit 1:** introduce your background and methods; share influences from data visualization, personal experience, shapes, colors, techniques, and nature. (Unit 1 tasks: “Share Your Background” 1.1; “Share Your Influences” 1.2; 3069 · U1)
- **Unit 2:** find an emotionally meaningful subject; research it; mark data in a source; enter data in tables; review the data using small calculations and, if useful, graphs. (Unit 2 tasks: “Find a Subject,” “Search for Information on the Subject,” “Start Harvesting the Data,” “Put the Data into Tables” 2.1, “Review the Data”; 3069 · U2)
- **Unit 3:** sketch ideas; make a basic graph; collect resources in Illustrator artboards; create chapter circles and character marks; test colors and shapes; practice symbols and type on a path; add word-count, character, and location layers; finish with context, a legend, keys, and a title. (Unit 3 tasks: “Sketch Your Ideas” 3.1, “Create Basic Graphs” 3.2, “Collect Your Resources,” “Create the First Shapes” 3.3, “Create Colors and Shapes” 3.4, “Practice Creating Symbols and Using the Type Tool,” “Add in Layers,” “Add the Final Details”; 3069 · U3)
- **Unit 4:** get feedback without explaining the project; test readability and color-vision proof; print the poster; present work in progress and the final result online. (Unit 4 tasks: “Get Feedback” 4.1, “Test Readability,” “Print Your Poster,” “Present Your Work Online”; 3069 · U4)

## Tool-specific steps

- **Excel:** enter chapter-by-character, color, object, location, and word-count data in tables; use totals and calculations; make a basic bubble chart where available. (3069 · U2 · L05 · 03:31–06:35; 3069 · U2 · L05 · 06:35–08:07; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 00:19–01:07)
- **RAWGraphs:** paste or upload tabular data; choose a bubble chart; assign data to axes, size, color, and label; adjust dimensions, margins, and color range; export SVG and open it in Illustrator. (3069 · U3 · L08 · 01:07–03:16)
- **Illustrator — project file:** keep multiple artboards, source tables, graphs, sketches, and intermediate versions together, with layers for data and elements. (3069 · U3 · L08 · 03:16–04:38)
- **Illustrator — palette and symbols:** sample colors with the eyedropper; create and save pattern swatches; use circles as clipping masks; retain small and large symbol variants; use the Symbols panel for reusable instances and edit the shared definition when all instances need a change. (3069 · U3 · L08 · 04:38–06:51; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 06:51–08:00; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 08:00–09:18; 3069 · U3 · L08 · 09:18–10:49; 3069 · U3 · L10 · 01:14–03:41)
- **Illustrator — layout and text:** ungroup imported graphs and release clipping masks to extract circles; resize and align symbols; place chapter titles on paths; split long titles; create labels, word-count markers, location marks, and legends; use clipping masks for illustrations. (3069 · U3 · L10 · 09:11–12:46; 3069 · U3 · L10 · 12:46–14:18; 3069 · U3 · L10 · 16:11–18:44; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 01:30–06:46; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 06:46–07:34; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 07:34–08:50; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 08:50–11:35; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 11:35–13:29)
- **Illustrator — accessibility and final review:** use Proof Setup for Protanopia-type and Deuteranopia-type previews before editing. (3069 · U4 · L13 · 02:38–03:42)
- **Photoshop:** prepare the final print file to the format required by the selected print service; check that service's specifications because they vary. (3069 · U4 · L14 · 01:46–02:24)
