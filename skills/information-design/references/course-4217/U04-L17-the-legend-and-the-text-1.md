## Making room for a title and explanatory text

Anchor: 4217 · U4 · L17 · 00:09–01:31

I am going to work on two elements that help readers use the piece: the legend and the explanatory text. I begin by creating blocks to mark where the legend, text, and other items can go. I move the visualization down to make room. There is already a placeholder for a title, but I want a larger title for the storytelling poster. I also want explanatory text that tells readers they are looking at the top 40 countries. I leave space for the legend, and keep the title simple: “Renewable Energy.” I am not very good at finding titles, so I am often lucky to have journalists or other collaborators help me.

## Writing a short explanation in reading order

Anchor: 4217 · U4 · L17 · 01:31–05:16

When I write the explanatory text, I think about the order in which readers will explore the parts of the visualization. That order matters to their experience, and I use the same order to describe the information. I use simple sentences and simple words to explain what readers are looking at.

I state the selection and explain how I chose the countries: the top 40 according to their share of primary energy from renewable sources in 2020, grouped by region. I make sure to include the year. Then I list what is visualized for each country in the order readers will see it: region, population, share of primary energy from renewable sources, and per-capita energy consumption for renewables. I remove a sentence that repeats what has already been said and adjust the text. The point is to tell readers what they are looking at without making the explanation complicated.

## Building the legend and labeling its measurements

Anchor: 4217 · U4 · L17 · 05:16–10:59

I make the legend by copying one country and explaining each of its elements. I choose a version that fits the available space and use the same arrow that is already in the chart. The arrow can be resized. I set the explanatory text in italics and keep each note simple: identify the arrow and say what the mark represents.

The area of the circle represents population in 2020. The length of the green line represents the share of primary energy from renewable sources. The dot marks countries that are also in the top 20 for fossil-fuel consumption in 2020. I include the year in this explanation too. The leaves represent per-capita energy consumption from renewables (the caption reads “Uninomobiles”). I copy the written “in 2020” fragment into the other legend notes too, then adjust the alignment and add units of measurement.

## Showing how to read the circle and leaves

Anchor: 4217 · U4 · L17 · 10:59–12:15

I want to explain more clearly how to read the circle area and the leaves. I add an asterisk to the relevant notes and use the remaining space to explain these parts with a couple of examples, including how to read a value such as 100.
