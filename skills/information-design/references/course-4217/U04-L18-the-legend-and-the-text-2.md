## Calibrating the population circles

Anchor: 4217 · U4 · L18 · 00:00–02:30

To show how I would visualize the population data, I use Argentina as a reference. I find Argentina in the data table and copy its population, 45,376,763. In PyGraph, I create several circles using Argentina as the reference so that the first circle has the correct proportion. The circle key uses 10 million, 50 million and 100 million as reference sizes. If the Argentina circle were too small or too large, I would resize all the groups until its size matched Argentina's actual population. The circles may not correspond perfectly at first, so I scale them slightly to make them precise. Once the reference is right, I delete the Argentina circle and keep the size examples.

## Aligning the legend and refining the composition

Anchor: 4217 · U4 · L18 · 05:55–08:45

I put everything into the legend. I cut and paste with Command-X and Command-F so the items are in the layer I want, then group each part so I can align their tops. I make the gap between them consistent. I would like to align these two parts. This could be bigger. To align them, I use the key tool I showed you before. I test different layouts, moving the elements upward into the available space and making them larger where there is room. One leaf is not the same color as C, so I apply the Multiply effect there too. I keep making small adjustments until the key and graphic sit together clearly.

## Crediting sources and checking the work

Anchor: 4217 · U4 · L18 · 08:45–10:39

I add the sources; it is always essential to declare the sources of the work. I zoom in and out often to check everything. For example, I notice that there is space here. I also check the work in progress on my smartphone. Looking at it from another point of view helps me find elements in the overall composition that I can improve.

Then, very importantly, I send images of the project to my parents. They are not data-visualization experts, so they represent most of my potential readers. I need to check with them that everything is clear and works. You can ask friends or relatives who are outside your field whether your work is clear to them too. Feedback is an extremely important step for me. Now everything is ready; in the next lesson, I will finalize the project.
