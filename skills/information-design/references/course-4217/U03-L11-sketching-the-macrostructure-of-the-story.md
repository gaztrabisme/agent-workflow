## Moving from a table to a variable list

Anchor: 4217 · U3 · L11 · 00:08–01:14

In this lesson, I make the first shift from a table to a storytelling piece. I recap the data and start sketching the microstructure of the piece. Shifting from a table to something visual can be tricky. To make that shift, I find it helpful to write the variables in my notebook. The data covers 40 countries. For each country, it includes the share of primary energy from renewable sources, per-capita energy consumption, population (captions: “depopulation”), and world region.

## Ordering the narrative overview

Anchor: 4217 · U3 · L11 · 01:14–02:26

I begin thinking about the narrative and the order in which readers will see the data. First, I want to show the regional data so readers can explore where the top 40 countries are located. I also want contextual information to be among the first visual elements. From a narrative perspective, I start with data that gives an overview: macro region and population. Then I move to the main specific focus of the piece. For population, I plan to use the area of a circle (captions: “arrow of a circle”).

## Adding per-capita data and reviewing the sketch

Anchor: 4217 · U3 · L11 · 02:54–03:47

Finally, I'm going to add the per-capita data at the end of each bar. I want an organic, soft shape that recalls nature, so I'll experiment in Illustrator with curved elements. The sketch is very ugly, but it is also very helpful: it lets me see the macrostructure and the order in which readers will see the different parts of the visualization. That's why the sketch is useful. Now I can design it with my two usual tools for static projects: Raw Graphs and Adobe Illustrator. We'll do that in the next lesson.
