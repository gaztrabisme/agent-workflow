## Visual expression and emotional literacy

Anchor: 4217 · U1 · L02 · 00:00–01:44

In this lesson, I talk about the worlds (captions: *words*) that have had, and still have, an impact on my approach to design. As a child, I especially loved playing, drawing, reading, and singing. Singing was the one interest I never fully explored, apart from a few events. I still love reading, and I still draw in a certain way. I have always been attracted to illustrations, color palettes, and visual art, and I keep a small collection of vintage illustrated books. I love certain colors, shapes, and textures.

I also love the expressiveness of shapes and colors. Visiting the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam was an amazing, very emotional experience for me. I am not an artist, but I work on the expressiveness of visual elements in some of my projects when it makes sense. Sometimes the context means a project needs to be very minimal and analytical. In other cases, there is room for expressiveness and an emotional layer.

I love this quote by László Moholy-Nagy (captions: *Laszlo Molinaggi*), an artist and professor at the Bauhaus School, about what he calls “emotional prejudice.” The remedy, he says, is to add emotional literacy and an education of the senses to intellectual literacy, and to develop the ability to articulate feelings through expression. Without a balanced performance of intellect and feeling, people become crippled and one-sided.

## Reading, theatre, and the audience

Anchor: 4217 · U1 · L02 · 01:44–03:17

As for reading, I love books and being completely immersed in them. I do not have a favorite author. Different authors have affected me at different times and for different reasons: Dostoevsky, Toni Morrison, Elena Ferrante, Jane Austen, Truman Capote, Agatha Christie, and Gabriel García Márquez (captions: *Gabriel Garcia Market*), to name a few. I have also done some writing, in a way. I love the feeling of reading something and becoming immersed in it. I know this is not quite the same, but I hope that readers of my projects can immerse themselves in them temporarily. I hope the worlds I design, made from shapes, colors, and text, can engage readers. These elements matter in data visualization too.

Finally, I love theatre: acting and writing for theatre. In recent years, I wrote two plays and brought them to the stage. It was a beautiful experience. In theatre and in data visualization, I combine a story and information, and the narrative is important in both. During the course, I will talk about design, image, and narratives. Theatre has also taught me how important the connection with the audience is. That is why I act on stage: to convey something to them. For me, the same principle applies to the audience of my projects, the readers. I work for them, not for myself, so I always have to keep them in mind. I will return to this aspect during the course.

## Communication and the role of design

Anchor: 4217 · U1 · L02 · 03:17–05:07

Communication connects my influences from visual art, literature, and theatre. Communicating is my main goal, and I like to use the tools I have—shapes, colors, and words—to communicate stories.

I would like to conclude with some quotes from authors and designers who particularly inspire me or feel close to my approach to design. The first is from Italian designer Bruno Munari: “Art shall not be separated from life: things that are good to look at, and bad to be used, should not exist” (captions: *where to be used*).

The second quote comes from the book *Data Feminism* and concerns the role of aesthetics in data visualization. It says that what is excluded is emotion and affect, embodiment and expression, embellishment and decoration. These are aspects of human experience associated with women and therefore devalued by the logic of our master stereotype. I have thought a lot about this quote in recent years.

Finally, I love this quote by Italian designer, architect, and artist Alessandro Mendini: “My poetry is that of developing images of all kinds, whether architectural or design or pictorial or sculptural, which interact with people in a very poetic way, that is, they find friends. If an object of mine finds its friend, it has fulfilled its role.” In the meantime, I hope this course will find its friends too. Now that you know about my influences, in the next lesson we are going to talk about visually translated contents and the role of perception.
