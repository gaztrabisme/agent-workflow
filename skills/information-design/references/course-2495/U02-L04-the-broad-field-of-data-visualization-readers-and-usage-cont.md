## A broad field with different levels of complexity

Anchor: 2495 · U2 · L04 · 00:00–00:59

2495 · U02 · L04 · 00:00–00:59

Data visualization spans many kinds of projects. In recent years, I have designed reports, visualizations for magazines and websites, a children’s book, and an experiment mixing data visualization with theater. My projects also vary widely in complexity, depending on the needs, the readers, and the usage context.

One misconception is that data visualizations always need to be read very quickly and deliver information fast. I do not think that is always true; it depends on those factors. The field has room for both very simple infographics and complex visualizations that let readers explore complex topics at different depths of analysis.

## Match the visualization to the goal and context

Anchor: 2495 · U2 · L04 · 00:59–01:44

2495 · U02 · L04 · 00:59–01:44

It is like comparing a Twitter thread with an essay: both can be valid sources of information, but their value depends on the communicative goals, the context, and the kind of analysis. Across my projects, I focus on the connection a data visualization can create with readers. I am especially interested in studying that connection and exploring how the visual translations I work with can affect it.

## La Lettura and the reading context

Anchor: 2495 · U2 · L04 · 01:44–03:32

2495 · U02 · L04 · 01:44–03:32

I want to show an example of usage context: my collaboration with La Lettura, the cultural supplement of the Italian newspaper Corriere della Sera. I make visualizations for its data visualization column, analyzing and communicating cultural, social, and environmental topics.

Two aspects of La Lettura matter. First, it is a printed cultural supplement published on weekends. I often see readers sharing photographs of it on social media. Typical pictures show La Lettura beside a cup of tea or a brioche, in a setting that suggests reading is part of a relaxed Sunday morning. Reading La Lettura is a slow process, and knowing this affects my design approach. I like to think of my visualizations as the visual equivalents of long articles: a way to create an interesting connection with readers through slow reading.

Second, I am asked to experiment visually while providing meaningful, interesting content. Together, these requests let me make work with an informative purpose that also sits near the data-art field, so it can visually engage readers. This gives you an idea of how broad data visualization can be.
