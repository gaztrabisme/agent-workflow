## A first collaboration across fields

Anchor: 2495 · U2 · L06 · 00:00–01:10

In this lesson, I’m talking about the role collaboration has played in my work and the lessons I’ve learned. For my master’s thesis, I knew I wanted to analyze textual data by extracting information from the annual reports of the Direzione Nazionale Antimafia. I also needed a tool that could extract information automatically. While researching online, I found the Italia NLP Lab at the Istituto di Linguistica Computazionale Antonio Zampolli. It is a research laboratory that develops resources for understanding and processing human language.

## Turning letters into a data visualization

Anchor: 2495 · U2 · L06 · 01:10–02:01

After my thesis, we stayed in contact. It is a project I’m very fond of, and it was also my first project as a freelancer. Any kind of information, even letters, can be transformed into a visual project and a data visualization. ## Research broadens a menstrual-cycle visualization

Anchor: 2495 · U2 · L06 · 02:01–03:01

Some time ago, Jen and I worked on a visual representation of the menstrual cycle, a project I really loved designing. Along with the data I needed to visualize, she sent me an interesting paper about cultural practices around menstruation and the taboos that still surround the subject. Reading it meaningfully influenced my design approach.

I wanted to make a beautiful, informative piece. In this case, I focused particularly on beauty and aesthetics in contrast with the stigma still associated with menstruation. This kind of collaboration is inspiring: it brings me new perspectives and new angles from which to look at the subjects I discuss.

## Designing a visual language for children

Anchor: 2495 · U2 · L06 · 03:01–04:41

I’m also a co-author of two books, and collaboration was key to both. The first is a children’s book that depicts our planet using infographics mixed with illustrations. I co-authored and designed it with Chiara Piroddi, a psychologist who specializes in developmental neuropsychology. Combining our experience was very interesting and helpful because I had never designed data visualizations for children before. I needed to understand whether such a young audience would understand my visuals.

We decided on the project’s main structure together. Chiara focused on the research, while I worked on the overall style and look and feel. We chose the data to visualize together, looking for examples that were simple and curious. I then worked on the infographics, aiming for elements that were simple but also evocative.

![2495 · U2 · L06 · 03:35](frames/U02L06-F0335.00.jpg)

I spent a lot of time on the style and visuals, and I also spent a lot of time talking with Chiara about the content and its accessibility. We knew we were asking children to make an effort to read the book, so we focused on creating a consistent, clear visual alphabet. We made a specific index for the visualizations—a page where readers could see all the visual elements together—and I designed small, clear legends for every page. Collaborating with Chiara was not only interesting but incredibly helpful, and it pushed the boundaries of my approach to data visualization.

## Setting expectations and giving honest feedback

Anchor: 2495 · U2 · L06 · 06:43–07:45

Over the years, I’ve learned some lessons about collaboration and mistakes to avoid. First, do not be afraid to state your expectations before starting a collaboration, and to explain what you can and cannot do. The interesting part is bringing different competencies together.

![2495 · U2 · L06 · 06:57](frames/U02L06-F0657.00.jpg)

On screen: "What are you expecting from collaborating" [rest omitted]  
Shows: A text card introducing the importance of setting expectations for collaboration.

![2495 · U2 · L06 · 07:03](frames/U02L06-F0703.00.jpg)

On screen: "What you can / can’t do" [rest omitted]  
Shows: A text card about being clear on what you can and cannot do.

If something is not going well or is not working during the collaboration, do not be afraid to say so. You are not there to offend anyone. Giving feedback is completely normal, as is saying when something is moving in a direction you did not expect. One project did not go well for me because the other collaborator was not giving me actual feedback or telling me whether things were going in the direction they wanted. Be honest and clear with the people you collaborate with. Share your expectations and goals; doing that is very important when you start working together.

## Collaboration as an ongoing source of perspective

Anchor: 2495 · U2 · L06 · 07:45–08:31

I encourage you to look for collaborations with people who have different stories, backgrounds, and competencies. One aspect I love about this field is the possibilities it offers: projects to work on, outputs, solutions, and ways to integrate different competencies. I cannot be an expert in everything I’m going to discuss through my projects, which is another reason collaboration is key.
