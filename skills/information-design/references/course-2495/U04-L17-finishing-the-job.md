## Step back and review the whole composition

Anchor: 2495 · U4 · L17 · 00:00–01:15

I will visually refine the project and work on the last details. This is a phase I particularly love. I often work at 1,000% zoom and at 50% at the same time, while keeping 100% in mind. I like to look at the overall picture as well as inspect the visual refinements closely.

Along with printing tests at the correct size, I find it helpful to step away from work in progress. I need to detach from the device I am working on, so I look at my visualizations on my smartphone. A new point of view helps me find elements in the overall composition that are not working and that I should fix. Let's do this with our project.

## Adjust the labels, circles, and grid

Anchor: 2495 · U4 · L17 · 01:15–03:11

At 50% zoom, I look at the overall composition and decide what to work on. I want the country names to be more visible, so I increase their font size by one point. Then I make the circles larger because some points are hard to see. I use Select Same > Fill Color and Transform > Transform Each to adjust their size slightly smaller.

I also apply a Multiply effect so I can see overlaps; it works for me. I adjust one overlap, including the shade around the Turkmenistan shape, and move the relevant lines down. I make the grid elements thinner and adjust them so their spacing is consistent. I add an arrow because it is clearer, using the same approach for population and GDP; for GDP, I choose a different style. Back at 50% zoom, I move one element up a little. The overall composition now works for me.

## Tune the colors, lines, and overlap effects

Anchor: 2495 · U4 · L17 · 04:23–06:49

I want to look at the visualization's details: the colors, textures, and look and feel. I think the care that emerges from this focus can be understood by readers, more or less consciously, and can have a role in their engagement. First, I select the per-capita shapes and the matching shape in the legend, slightly adjust their color so they match, then slightly change another color. I increase the thickness of the lines for 2005 and the other years by selecting objects with the same stroke width (the captions say “2025”). I try the change, and you can experiment to see what works for you. I like this result, then increase the stroke weight of another line too. I also add a very small effect that may only be noticed at high zoom; these details contribute to the finish. I set the shapes to Multiply so overlaps have a shaded effect.

Next I select the circles by fill color and change their color. Sometimes I use a very light gradient to give the visualization more depth. This gradient is too strong, so I refine its two colors. I keep one color as it is and make the other a similar, slightly pinker version. I try different options and consider adding grain, then reverse that choice. I take time to find a color I am satisfied with. I enjoy focusing carefully on the details; even if I am the only person who notices them, they matter to me.

I export the visualization as a PNG or JPEG at the highest resolution possible. After the project is published, I use details from it for social media. I use social media a lot to share projects, including Twitter and Instagram. Even when a project is not intended to be viewed on Instagram, sharing details of it, including the legend, is a good way to share my work and my approach to data visualization, build a community of people who are curious about what I do, and reach clients.
