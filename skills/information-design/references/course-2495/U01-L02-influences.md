## Theater and the audience

Anchor: 2495 · U1 · L02 · 00:00–01:00

In this lesson, I’m sharing some of the influences that shaped my approach to design. One may not seem directly related to design: I love theater. I have practiced as an actress since high school. It isn’t something I do professionally, but in recent years I’ve had the chance to write and direct plays and bring them to the stage at events in Italy and abroad.

In theater, building a flow between you and the audience is essential. You always have to remember that you’re working for them. This constant focus on the audience has had a meaningful impact on my approach to data visualizations. I keep the readers in mind and remember that I’m working for them.

## What theater and data visualization share

Anchor: 2495 · U1 · L02 · 01:00–02:05

I have also worked at the intersection between data visualization and theater. I think these two worlds have some things in common. First, they convey stories and information, and theater also conveys emotions. They both visualize: data visualization does this, obviously, but theater does it too. Bringing a play to the stage means visualizing the scenes and giving shape to words through gestures, scenography [captions: sonography], and actors.

Each element is the result of choices. Every element in a visualization comes from a choice; so do an actor’s position on stage, gestures, and words. Keeping these elements in mind, I experimented with the intersection between data visualization and theater. I wrote a play and brought it on stage, visualizing what was happening in real time with shapes and visual elements.

## Van Gogh and expressive visual elements

Anchor: 2495 · U1 · L02 · 02:05–03:00

Another influence of mine is Vincent van Gogh’s paintings. I still remember the first time I saw one of his paintings in Paris; I simply couldn’t take my eyes off it. The incredible expressivity of his lines and brushstrokes, and his capacity to enrich visual elements with a strong emotional component, inspire me most.

I think visual elements in a visualization can also be expressive, and that expressivity can help communicate the stories they tell. My first data visualization as a freelancer was a visual exploration of the letters Van Gogh wrote to his brother, Theodorus. One of the plays I acted in was also based on Van Gogh’s life and letters.

## Nature, beauty, and reader engagement

Anchor: 2495 · U1 · L02 · 03:00–03:53

Finally, I know it’s a cliché, but I’m strongly inspired by nature. My house is full of plants. I love being surrounded by natural elements, and I love organic shapes. I think designing organic shapes can help me enrich the sense of engagement I hope to create when I work on data visualization pieces.

The pursuit of beauty isn’t only aimed at reaching a high aesthetic value; it is also meant to engage readers and encourage them to read my pieces, and therefore the content and stories I communicate through them. In the additional resources section, you’ll find other artists, books, and resources that inspire me. Now that you know where my inspiration comes from, let’s move to the next lessons, where I’ll talk about designing visual alphabets.
