## Reading patterns in the counts

Anchor: 3069 · U2 · L06 · 01:28–02:44

Some patterns already appear in the numbers, including differences in the totals. Dorothy has many more mentions than Uncle Henry. In the visualization, you might show such differences with objects or shapes of different sizes. Keep proportions in mind, and look for possible relationships between characters and colors, or identify which character is mentioned most in the book.

You could also visualize the color counts themselves, for example by putting yellow squares in the visualization and fewer black squares. The data you collected gives you several possible directions. Look for your own story in the numbers and at their relative sizes. You can count by chapter, character, or color and see what you can do with those results.

## Checking for outliers and moving to sketches

Anchor: 3069 · U2 · L06 · 02:44–03:25

It is important to check the data for outliers. The teacher says, “It’s not a big one,” but the Emerald City count is 72 (the captions render the name as “ML City”); the other values are much smaller. If you visualize the counts as circles, that value would make a much bigger circle than the others, so you can already imagine how it might look in the visualization.
