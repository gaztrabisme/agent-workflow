## Choose a manageable source

I’ll introduce the source I’m using for this course: The Wonderful Wizard of Oz. I have the book here. I hope you know the story, since there have been films, other books, and musicals based on it. You can choose your own book, movie, or anything else that interests you.

I chose this book because it is available through Project Gutenberg, an online collection of books whose copyrights have expired. Check whether you can use data you find online for free. Large datasets are available, but I recommend starting with a short book or movie so the project stays fun rather than stressful. I’ll use a printed copy so we can harvest the data by hand; I’ll show that process in the next lesson. You can also search online for information about the author or the time the book was written and take notes.

## Search for background and traceable sources

Anchor: 3069 · U2 · L04 · 01:20–04:00

Effective Data Visualization: Transform Information into Art · U02 · L04 · 01:20–04:00

Now is a good time to search for additional information about the book. It helps to choose a subject you feel connected to: when you feel angry, sad, or irritated about something, those feelings can help you stay connected to it. I’m looking for something that makes me happy and feels nostalgic. Many people may know The Wizard of Oz from a film, including the older movie or the version with Michael Jackson and Diana Ross, The Wiz. This subject makes me happy, and I want to show what data we can draw from it.

Project Gutenberg is an online collection of books whose copyrights have expired. I search there for The Wonderful Wizard of Oz. For any project, check whether you can use the data freely; otherwise, you may have to deal with rights and pay to use it.

It would be even better to donate to them, because they do the hard work of putting books online and making the information available to the public.

## Look at the original illustrations

As a designer, I’m curious about the visual aspects of a book and other designs, so I start by looking at images. A search for Wizard of Oz illustrations brings up many pictures and book covers. I’m looking for illustrations from the original 1900 book. I found a link to the Library of Congress, where the whole original book has been uploaded. It includes images specially made for the book by its illustrator, W. W. Denslow; the book was written by L. Frank Baum. I like the way the colors and style are handled. You can save these images and use them as a source for a project.
