## Rebuild patterned symbols without losing density

Anchor: 3069 · U3 · L11 · 01:30–06:46

Simply resizing the outer circle makes the pattern too sparse. I want the new symbol to keep the original pattern density. When I double-click the element, I can see its separate pieces; the ellipse is the clipping mask. I leave that selection, choose the pattern I need, copy it, open the character element, and place it inside. I also bring in the blue circle, move it into place, and make sure it has no fill. I align all the pieces so they are centered. Illustrator's Align controls are available in Properties or in the Align panel; I choose alignment to the selection and align both vertically and horizontally.

![3069 · U3 · L11 · 02:54.00](frames/U03L11-F0254.00.jpg)

On screen: “Align” / “Align Objects” / “Distribute Objects” / “Align To:” [rest omitted]  
Shows: The Align panel with object alignment and distribution controls, beside the selected patterned circle.

Next I add an outline. It is hidden by the smaller clipping mask, so I choose a color (captions: *girl*), give the circle a stroke, remove the ellipse that is masking the rest of the pattern, and use the larger circle as the new clipping mask. I adjust the lines around the border and copy lines into gaps so the pattern reaches across the whole circle. I remove doubled or unwanted lines, including ones that will not show inside the final mask, and lock layers I do not want to select repeatedly. The larger circle must sit on top. I select the layers and choose Object > Clipping Mask > Make. The result is a patterned Dorothy symbol that has the same visual density as the other symbols.

## Draw the palace and add a small sparkle effect

Anchor: 3069 · U3 · L11 · 08:50–11:35

I trace the palace (captions: *Trish. The bellage.*), keeping only some parts rather than every detail, and draw lines over it to bring out the structure. I will change the red color. This is not finished yet; I will add more layers later. Since we have used circles elsewhere, I put the palace illustration in a circle too. I place the circle above the drawing and use it as a clipping mask. If the layers are in the wrong order, I move the illustration above the blue layer and manually reposition the lines. That gives me the beginning of the palace illustration.

I add a background and then make small twinkles for the tops of the towers. To make one, I draw a circle and choose Effect > Distort & Transform > Zig Zag. With Preview on, I can resize it and adjust how many points it has until it looks right. If I later want to change the effect, I can reopen Zig Zag in Properties and adjust it. A few small details help the illustration feel finished.

## Place a legend beside the elements it explains

Anchor: 3069 · U3 · L11 · 14:33–16:28

Background information is important, but readers also need guides for how to read the visualization. That is what the legend does. I could place it between the elements or at the top, but then readers would have to keep looking back and forth to remember what each mark means. I put the explanations as close as possible to the relevant elements instead.

I use a different font for this layer so it does not become the main emphasis. The reader's eye should go first to the visualization; when something is unclear, they can zoom in to read the explanation. The legend also shows the layer with the characters' names. It explains the dotted line as the chapter order and shows where to find a chapter title. These guides make the structure of the poster easier to understand.

The project now has the elements it needs, except for its title. I have put some text on the page, but in the next lesson I will choose and style the title so it fits the whole project.
