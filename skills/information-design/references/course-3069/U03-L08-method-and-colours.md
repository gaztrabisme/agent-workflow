## Use RAWGraphs to map and export the data

Anchor: 3069 · U3 · L08 · 01:07–03:16

3069 · U03 · L08 · 01:07–03:16
RAWGraphs is a handy online tool, especially if you are a designer without a coding background. There are many data-visualization tools and the choices can feel overwhelming, but RAWGraphs lets you paste or upload data and try different graph types. For this project, I paste the Excel data, which includes chapter numbers and word counts, and choose a bubble chart. I assign word count to the axis and to bubble size, and use the chapter number as the label so I can work faster in Illustrator.

## Sample colours from the original illustrations

Anchor: 3069 · U3 · L08 · 04:38–06:51

3069 · U03 · L08 · 04:38–06:51
Next I decide which colours to use. The original illustrations in the book are beautiful. They were created at the beginning of the 19th century, and are so beautifully made; this style works perfectly for the project. I copy an illustration from the book and paste it into Illustrator. After zooming in, I use the eyedropper to select a colour, such as the white-yellow, and add it to the swatches. Repeat this for every colour you want. I name the swatch folder and remove the default folders that I do not need, so they do not get mixed up with the project colours. I also place small squares on the artboard to make my own palette. Once the colours have been selected, they appear both on the artboard and in the swatches library.

## Clip a pattern into a character circle

Anchor: 3069 · U3 · L08 · 08:00–09:18

3069 · U03 · L08 · 08:00–09:18
Once you have drawn a pattern, decide its size. Copy it and draw a circle over it with the ellipse tool. The circle does not need an outline. Move it towards the middle, select everything, and use Object > Clipping Mask > Make. This creates Dorothy's circle. Repeat the process at the same size for the other symbols. I tried several Scarecrow patterns because I was not yet sure which one was right; I also considered putting the patterns in a row, but decided that was not the best option. Choose symbols by considering the character's features: clothing for Dorothy, fur for Toto, and iron as the material for the Iron Woodman. The symbols can suggest those things in an abstract way.

## Make symbols for the other characters too

Anchor: 3069 · U3 · L08 · 10:49–12:02

3069 · U03 · L08 · 10:49–12:02
The main characters are only part of the story, so I also make symbols for the others. I repeat the circles and consider ways to represent groups such as the Munchkins, Winkies [captions: “wingies”], and Quadlings. I want the Wizard of Oz to look like a magic trick. The cyclone has a whirling movement, which is another reason I chose circles: it looks circular from above. I like linking what the data or story says to the elements and to the way the project is laid out. Now that the style is chosen, the next lesson is about workflow.
