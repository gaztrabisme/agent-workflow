## Printing and presenting the finished poster

Anchor: 3069 · U4 · L14 · 00:00–01:46

We are going to talk about presenting the project, because we want to show all the hard work we put into it. The poster was meant to be printed, so I have put my printed version in front of me. It is always nice to have it there, see the colors, and look at it. It is a special moment when I have the final printed version in front of me, because then I can see the actual colors and how I created everything. There is always a moment when I think something is not right. I can edit it in my program and print it again to see whether that is correct.

Where you want to present the poster matters. You might want a smaller version, such as A4 (captions: “a four”), perhaps printed in a book or another medium. I chose 50 centimeters by 70 centimeters because I like to put things on the wall. You also have the opportunity to sell it online. Keep in mind that colors appear differently on screen than in print, so you might be disappointed when you print something. You can alter the colors on screen and print it again to see whether it fits what you had in mind.

You can also share the project online, in your portfolio or on social media. In between, I share work in progress and sneak peeks to make my audience curious about what is going to happen and what the final result will be.

## Showing the project on a website and crediting sources

Anchor: 3069 · U4 · L14 · 02:24–03:07

You also want to share the results of your hard work with the world. If you have a website, be sure to put the project there. My professional website is Studio Terp, at studioterp.nl, where I put up different projects to show my work.

As I have mentioned before, it is important to mention the sources you used. For example, I did this with my Brat Pack project (captions: “breadbuck”). As you scroll through the page, you can see the project and statements about the data I collected.

## Sharing progress and the final project on social media

Anchor: 3069 · U4 · L14 · 03:07–03:59

Another thing I mentioned is using social media to show sneak peeks of work in progress and make your followers curious. I am showing you my Instagram account, where I share sneak peeks, details, sketches, and sometimes mistakes I made during the process.

If you are familiar with social media, you know that you should use hashtags, as I did with one of the images from this project. You could use tags such as data viz, data art, data visualization, and graphic design, along with anything else you think is worth mentioning when you post the project. There are all sorts of images on my account, and you should post your final project there as well.
