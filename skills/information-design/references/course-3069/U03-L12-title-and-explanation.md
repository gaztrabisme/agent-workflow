## Choose and style the project title

Anchor: 3069 · U3 · L12 · 00:00–01:14

The title is one of the most important parts of a visualization. I may already know what I want to call it, but the angle I want to communicate can help me decide whether the title should make people curious about the project or plainly state its subject. Here I state the subject: the book's title, *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz*.

I use the same font as the chapter titles, in another color. I add a circle and another layer in the same way I made the chapter titles. That layer says “A data visualization per chapter,” so the audience knows what the visualization is about.

## Finish the title and prepare for feedback

Anchor: 3069 · U3 · L12 · 01:14–02:38

I split the title into parts and move them around, including “Oz.” I think it will look more attractive if I use the emerald green from the book instead of blue for Oz (captions: *us*). The title extends beyond the edge of the circle, so I add a small shape to cover the extra bit and give the lettering one more visual detail. The design now looks finished.

I have not mentioned that I mainly work with keyboard shortcuts in Illustrator and use the mouse to draw. You can work in whatever way suits you: use a tablet, or work in an analog way by drawing or painting with a skill you already know. With the final design in place, the next lesson is about getting feedback and making the final edit.
