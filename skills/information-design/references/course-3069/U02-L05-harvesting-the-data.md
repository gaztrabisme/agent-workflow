## Mark the book to build a dataset

Anchor: 3069 · U2 · L05 · 00:00–03:31

Effective Data Visualization: Transform Information into Art · U02 · L05 · 00:00–03:31

I’ll show what I mean by harvesting data. I recommend buying a very cheap copy of the book because we’ll draw and take notes in it. This book has 24 chapters. I’ll use pencils to mark characters and other subjects, and start in Chapter 2 to see what data is there.

For example, I mark Dorothy in red. I check each place she is mentioned and look back to see whether I missed any. I also mark where she is referred to in the third person; a phrase such as “the little girl” can also mean Dorothy. I highlight Toto, the dog, as well. We’ll look back over the pages to catch anything missed.

## Enter the marked counts in tables

Anchor: 3069 · U2 · L05 · 03:31–06:35

Effective Data Visualization: Transform Information into Art · U02 · L05 · 03:31–06:35

Once the marking and notes are finished, move the data into tables on a computer. You can use Excel or another program, or write the table by hand. Write the chapter titles and characters into a table. You can fill in one character across all chapters, or start with a chapter and count all the characters there.

For example, I include the Wicked Witch of the West and the Wicked Witch of the East; there are four witches in the book. Count by page or by chapter, then enter the totals for each chapter. Dorothy appears 16 times in Chapter 1, for example. Add the other data you found. Toto is another important character. The Scarecrow, Woodman, and Lion do not appear in the first chapters, so those entries are empty there. Continue with the characters you have chosen to track.
