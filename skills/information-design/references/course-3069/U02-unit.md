# Unit 2 · Data and Emotional Subject

Anchor: 3069 · U2

In this unit, you’ll look at how to search for and find a subject that you’d want to visualize as a data illustration. Then, I’ll brief you on the goal, the issue, and how you will collect and list your data.

## Tasks

- ### Find a Subject

Write down different subjects. Then ask yourself: What subject are you touched by emotionally―what makes you angry, happy, or sad? What subject are you passionate about―your job, a hobby? Do you have nostalgic feelings on a matter―for example, a series you watched as a child? What part of your life would you want to improve―your weight, your sleep?
  

![](https://assets8.domestika.org/course-images/000/028/618/28618-big.jpg)
- ### Search for Information on the Subject

While we already have the source for our data, it's also essential to gain some background information. Use a search engine like Google to gather fun facts, images, history, and related information on the subject.
- ### Start Harvesting the Data

Take the book, preferably the analogue version, and highlight specific elements you want to collect with colour pencils or any other materials. These elements could be anything from characters to locations.

## Additional resources

- ### The Tools

[Microsoft Excel](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel)
  
This program is probably familiar to most: Many scientific, economic, and research fields use it to create graphs and tables for their data. Data visualization is often used as well: Many clients collect and deliver their data in Excel tables and sheets. Open data is often downloadable in CSV format to be opened and edited in Excel.

[Google Sheets](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/63824?hl=en-GB&ref_topic=9055207)
  
If you don’t have a Microsoft license, you can use a limited online version. Google Sheets is an excellent alternative. When you have a Google account, you have more graphs options than the Microsoft Excel online version.
