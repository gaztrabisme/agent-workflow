## A shared approach to data art

Anchor: 3069 · U1 · L02 · 00:00–00:36

In this lesson, I want to share my influences and where I get inspiration. I’ll show you people whose work inspires me. They all take a similar approach to data visualization or data art. What attracts me in their work is their methods, their passion, and their emotional attachment to a subject.

## Stefanie Posavec and Dear Data

Anchor: 3069 · U1 · L02 · 00:36–01:23

The first artist I want to share is Stefanie Posavec (captions: Stephanie Posavec). Her work is intriguing because you can follow her steps from the first sketch to the finished project. In one project, she manually gathered all the data from the grammar inside a book, coded it, and created delicate structures of lines. The work shown is titled *Sentence Drawings* and identifies its source as Jack Kerouac’s *On the Road*.

Stefanie is also one of the artists behind *Dear Data*, a project in which two people gather personal data and send postcards to each other. You can find more information about it and additional resources.

## Mushrooms and natural inspiration

Anchor: 3069 · U1 · L02 · 02:34–03:12

There are other worlds to look into, such as nature. My favorite part is mushrooms. I don’t only look at them online; I also go into the woods and photograph them myself. It’s a wondrous miniature world where you can see all kinds of colors and textures. Other fields are inspired by them too, such as architecture or painters. I have a dedicated Instagram account, which you can find in additional resources. Now that I’ve talked about influences, in the next lesson we’re going to find a subject for our project.
