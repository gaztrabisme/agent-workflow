- ### My Influences

Inspiration is undoubtedly more accessible than ever before, and for good reason. But don't forget that inspiration is more than a pretty or colourful feed of posts on social media. Our work is inspired, informed, and influenced by the conversations we have. The people we engage with, the things we read, the experience we live, and the places we visit all weave the threads of who we become. To this extent, I encourage you to take advantage of the Domestika forum by sharing your influences with fellow creatives and feeding into our shared passions.

If my influences have caught your interest, here's a list of the main things that inspire me so you can delve further. You can also download a summary in the attached documents below.

[Maps](https://medium.com/nightingale/sketching-the-world-an-icebreaker-to-working-with-data-de8df5e0c21a)
  
As a designer tasked with mapping information through data design, I have become fascinated by the way in which we represent the world's geography in its simplest form: the hand\-drawn map. 

Over the past decade, I've collected hundreds of hand\-drawn maps from people all over the world. With almost no exception and no matter where the creators were from – the Philippines or Pakistan, Cuba or the US, New Zealand or Indonesia – people's geographical representations were shaped by their personal experiences, from their academic studies to travels and other influences. 

Capturing individual perspectives through this form of 'mind\-mapping' has become a powerful tool in my practice as both a designer and educator. To me, it perfectly encapsulates the principles behind effective visual storytelling. 

You can learn more about my passion in this article – you might get inspired to draw a map of the world and sketch your own data story!

**Bruno Munari**
  
Munari (1907–1998\) was an Italian artist, designer, and inventor who contributed to many fields of visual arts (painting, sculpture, film, industrial design, graphic design) within the movements of modernism, futurism, and concrete art. He also engaged in non\-visual arts (literature and poetry) with his research on games, the didactic method, movement, tactile learning, kinesthetic learning, and creativity. If you don't know his work, I have linked his biography and an interesting article below: 

[MoMA](https://www.moma.org/artists/4163)
  
['Drawing a Tree: Uncommon Vintage Italian Meditation on the Existential Poetics of Diversity and Resilience Through the Art and Science of Trees'](https://www.themarginalian.org/2021/11/05/drawing-a-tree-bruno-munari/)

[*drawing a tree* \| Bruno Munari](https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/2/41305/files/2017/01/Munari-drawing-a-tree-2eyl8fo.pdf)
  
As children, a tree is often one of the first things we draw. We tend to gravitate to them because they are so human\-like, with branches serving as arms and trunks as bodies. Munari recognised how perfect and universal the tree is as a subject to engage people in making art. For all his intended playfulness in the book, you still learn about maths, science, and architecture. But the best takeaway is that Munari asks us to draw not just the way he suggests, but in any way we want to and without any constraints. 

**Otto and Marie Neurath**
  
A visual program for displaying facts and quantitative information, the ISOTYPE system was born from the research and theories of Otto Neurath (1882–1945\), a Viennese philosopher, economist, and social scientist. As a child, he was fascinated by the function of Egyptian hieroglyphics – their forms and ability to communicate a story. This early influence was integrated into his life's work: the development of a system to pictorially organise statistics. After Otto died in 1945, Marie carried on their work until the 1960s mostly through the production of educational books for children. Her publications clearly explained very complex subject matter about the physical world, scientific discoveries, and the people of non\-Western cultures. Here are a few links to read more about their work:

[Website](https://www.marieneurath.org/)
  
[*Isotype Revisited*](https://isotyperevisited.org/)
  
['Before emojis: the utopian graphic language of Marie and Otto Neurath'](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2019/aug/27/pictures-unite-graphic-design-vision-marie-otto-neurath)

**Manuel Lima**
  
Lima authored the site Visual Complexity. This intends to be a unified space for anyone interested in the visualisation of complex networks and offers an extensive library of resources. Since the launch of this website, Lima has authored three books about different types of frameworks for visualising data – from systems to trees and circles. All his books are packed with great inspirations:

[Visual Complexity](http://www.visualcomplexity.com/vc/project.cfm?id=23)
  
[*Visual Complexity: Mapping Patterns of Information*](https://papress.com/products/visual-complexity-mapping-patterns-of-information)
  
[*The Book of Circles: Visualizing Spheres of Knowledge*](https://papress.com/products/the-book-of-circles-visualizing-spheres-of-knowledge)
  
[*The Book of Trees: Visualizing Branches of Knowledge*](https://papress.com/products/the-book-of-trees-visualizing-branches-of-knowledge)

**Natural History and More**
  
I find inspiration across many fields, but if I had to select one I would say that I am most attracted to the aesthetic of Natural History art. I am fascinated by the way artists have assisted scientists in their work to identify, describe, and classify nature through history, producing gorgeous visual works. Here are some examples:

[Ernst Haeckel](https://www.themarginalian.org/2020/03/26/ernst-haeckel-medusae/)
  
A German zoologist, naturalist, eugenicist, philosopher, physician, professor, marine biologist, and amazing artist/illustrator. His work is a feast for your eyes and an incredible source of inspiration. 

[Johann Georg Heck](https://www.c82.net/iconography/)
  
I love the work of this lithographer, cartographer, geographer, and author, and this curational project by data designer Nicholas Rougeux is incredible.
