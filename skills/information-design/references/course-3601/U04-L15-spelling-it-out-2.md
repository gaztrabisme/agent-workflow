## Softening the pattern and marking the global trend

Anchor: 3601 · U4 · L15 · 03:09–05:34

I change the pattern and colour it white to make it more subtle, because it is almost interfering with the global trend. I add a line from the base towards this trend to indicate that the trend is global (captions: “From base that”). I turn the guides on again (captions: “the glasses”) and add the axis labels. I select them, rotate them, and place them around the chart, checking that they are rotated correctly and parallel to the first line.

I think all the text is now in place. I could add a note about Nepal here, but the layout already feels quite full (captions: “quite filmed”), so I would first test whether people understand the map and refine it after that.

## Evaluating the finished layout

Anchor: 3601 · U4 · L15 · 05:34–06:58

Now that all the elements are in place, the best way to know whether we have completed our job is to evaluate it. The first step in the evaluation is done by you. I like to print the infographic and review it on paper, because seeing my design in a different format reveals a different kind of interaction with the work. I go through every element and write down what could be refined or improved.

The second part of the evaluation will probably come from other people. For commercial work, a big part of the evaluation will probably come as feedback from the client. I take that feedback and implement it. If you do not have a client, perhaps because it is a self-initiated project, I highly recommend testing the effectiveness of your design with potential users, or even just a friend who is not that familiar with the subject matter. I try to see whether they understand it. Our job is storytelling: it is not only about beautifying data, but about communicating insight. Testing with others is the best way to find out whether they understand what I have designed. In the next lesson, I will export the final artwork and consider how to share it for the best impact across different platforms.
