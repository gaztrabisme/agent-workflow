# Unit 4 · Designing with Data

Anchor: 3601 · U4

After the investigative and ideation stages, the next step is to use information design to craft the display of information. By the end of this unit, you'll have learnt how to transform a sketch into pixels using digital tools and craft the encoded system for maximum impact.

## Tasks

- ### The Design In Action: Let's Get Started! 
Practice exercise 4\.1

Once you have an idea formalised on paper and you have validated it with a prototype of the data, you can start working on the design using Adobe Illustrator. Refer to the steps I laid out in this unit to import the prototype from Data Illustrator to Adobe Illustrator or use the tools you prefer.

Explore the listed resources below to inspire your own approach. Pick and choose the elements of your design according to the best fit for your data and curiosity. The final design can be rendered digitally or even by hand. I also suggest you to avoid being overly ambitious: rather a project finished with pen and paper than a great idea never made!

If an illustrative element (like the taps and glass) fit your story/audience/idea, explore how you can execute those.

- ### Spell Out the Story Behind the Data 
Practice exercise 4\.2

Once all the design elements are in place, spell out the story by using text. A reader's understanding of the data can be vastly improved by directly labelling insights and points of interest with clear and engaging writing. Make sure that the key illustrates your encoded system. Add labels and scale to the axes and all the text needed for maximum comprehension. 

As always, if you have any questions, pose them in the forum. Share pictures of your progress.
- ### Evaluate the Design 
Practice exercise 4\.3

It's time to evaluate the work and see if it works. To do this, I personally like to print the infographic and evaluate it on paper. Once I have approached it with critical eyes in the format the audience will read it, I'll implement some changes and then test it on others. 

If you're unsure about any elements (like the choropleth in my design), iterate it and create options to test with people. Since the key objective in this job is to communicate, we need to ensure that the design we have produced is truly effective in communicating the insights. And there is no better way to test the effectiveness of the visualisation than sharing it with others to see if they understand it. 

You can also get the feedback of family and friends or potential users. If you conduct face\-to\-face user testing, the key rule is to listen. Don't explain the infographic. The design must be interpreted without your voice\-over.
