## Additional resources

- ### File Formats

When working in Adobe Illustrator, everything can be scaled and adjusted with few clicks. But how do you export your artwork without losing quality and data?

[AI format](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/file-types/image/vector/eps-file.html)

The native Illustrator file format. With this one, you can scale your graphics as much as you want. If you exchange an AI file, remember to also export it in the older version available since the yearly program updates can contain new features not displayed on previous versions.

[EPS format](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/file-types/image/vector/ai-file.html)

EPS is a vector file format often required for professional and high\-quality image printing (billboards, large marketing advertisements, etc.). But it can be also opened by other vector programs like Figma, Corel Draw, Affinity, etc.

[PDF format](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/about-adobe-pdf.html)

PDF is the one of the most common file formats to export vectorial artworks. You can scale and rearrange a PDF file by importing it into your program, embedding fonts, or transforming letters into outlines to maintain the style.

[SVG format](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/file-types/image/vector/svg-file.html)

This format is used if your outcome is digital. SVG files maintain crisp quality and scalability across all devices. You can transform your typography into outlines as well.

Showcasing your artwork can be just as important as creating it. Mock\-ups add a professional finish and show the audience what your work would resemble in the real world.

[Mockup World](https://www.mockupworld.co/)

[Graphic Pear](https://www.graphicpear.com/)

[Freepik](https://www.freepik.com/)

- ### Where to Share Your Work

Promoting your projects is the last but not least important phase of your work, especially if you establish yourself as an independent designer. Present it in the best and most clear way possible, and spread it across different platforms for maximum visibility.

[Domestika Featured Projects](https://www.domestika.org/en/projects/featured)

Before going anywhere external, I encourage you to take advantage of the creative community we have here! Not only is Domestika the largest e\-learning platform, but it's an amazing community of talented, diverse people – students and professionals! So I highly recommend you to publish your project on the Domestika platform. They often spotlight projects which are seen by many. Check out the featured gallery!

[Behance](https://www.behance.net/)

Behance is the largest creative network for showcasing and discovering creative projects. It features materials from several disciplines, including Information Design and Data Visualisation. Create a consistent portfolio: if your project is picked on the main gallery you will get tons of views and comments.

[Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/)

Instagram isn't only about food and fashion. Every design studio or independent has its own profile: it's the fastest way to share your work. Present appealing images and extensive descriptions, pay attention to the choice of hashtags, and tag accounts who repost design works.

[Medium](https://medium.com/)

This is the place to share longer thoughts and images in the form of a post. It's a great platform to discover insights about processes and tools. If you want to publish on Medium, consider your audience and prepare an accurate report of methodologies for the research and development of your work. 

[LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/)

A platform to find jobs and connect with colleagues, clients, and designers. It's also a place to join communities and spread your work in the form of an insightful post (shorter than Medium). 

[Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/)

A repository containing mostly UX/UI designs and vectorial graphics/illustrations. However, it's a useful source of inspiration for dashboards or digital charts, especially if developed for mobile devices.
- ### Community\-Based Projects

Deepen your knowledge and connect with mentors and peers with these two projects that aim to provide learning resources as well as fostering a community for data visualisation professionals.

[Data Visualization Society](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/)

A great community founded to serve as a professional home for those working across the discipline, while leading others into this growing, dynamic field. It aims to increase the visibility and the value of data visualisation to the general public. Their mission is to connect data visualisers across tech stacks, subject areas, and experience. They share useful resources in their magazine and Slack channel, in which you can also share your work.

[Elevate](https://blog.elevatedataviz.com/)

This site showcases articles and videos focused on practical data visualisation techniques; as well as freelance and career advice, and live group\-coaching. It's a great place to level up your skills, get feedback on your work, and learn to handle tricky client situations.
- ### Hackathons

Participating in hackathons and workshops is a great way to experiment with people from different backgrounds, and to learn something new hands\-on.

[Data4Change](https://www.data4chan.ge/events)

This network runs data\-driven design sprints, training sessions, and contributes to events and conferences all over the world. They organise annual events. Take a look at their website!

[Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions)

Kaggle features several competitions for data science and analysis, from beginners to advanced. 

[The Center for Global Data Visualization](https://www.centerforglobaldata.org/)

The CGD organises data challenges involving participants from all over the world.
- ### Conferences

Conferences are an important reference to stay up\-to\-date with the current data visualisation landscape, and they're becoming more and more accessible. Many take place digitally!

[Outlier](https://outlierconf.com/)

This conference provides talks and facilitates the ability to meet and connect with the global visualisation community. 

[Eyeo](https://eyeofestival.com/)

Eyeo Festival brings together in Minneapolis all kinds of creators working at the intersection of data, art, and technology. If you can't attend the conference, follow their content on Vimeo. Their archive of recorded talks from previous editions is truly outstanding.

[On Data and Design](https://www.youtube.com/c/ondataanddesign)

An evening lecture series organised by the Basel based studio for information design, Superdot – visualising complexity. Follow the link to access their YouTube channel.

[EU DataViz](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P5LJGdmRzD0&list=PLT5rARDev_rnycvW6qNol1_uGyR5FAd-5)

Online from Luxembourg to the world, EU DataViz is an international conference on open data and data visualisation addressing the needs of the public sector across the EU. Here are their YouTube webinars.

[S\-H\-O\-W](https://graphichuntersshow.nl/) 

A two\-day single\-track event which consists of talks, discussions, music, and other activities. The conference is available online.

- ### Podcasts

Podcasts are a great way to integrate and stay fresh on what's happening in the dataviz community. They've gained enormous success as a format in the past few years and are a great way to hear the voice of experienced practitioners, who share not only their work but also their design philosophy. 

[Data Stories](https://datastori.es/)

A podcast on data visualisation run by two practitioners, Enrico Bertini and Moritz Stefaner. In each episode, they invite people from the field and start great conversations on data visualisation and beyond. It's my favourite!

[Data Viz Today](https://dataviztoday.com/)

This is a podcast run by Alli Torban, a data designer that shares the latest tools and methods discovered on the job and also interviews top designers.

[Visualising Data](https://www.visualisingdata.com/podcast/)

This podcast by Andy Kirk (check out his book!) comes along with a video series and gives you great insights on the process of creating infographics. 

[PolicyViz](https://policyviz.com/podcast/)

This podcast by economist Jonathan Schwabis shares interviews ranging from data visualisation and presentation skills, to open data and the use of technology. I also recommend checking out his book about charts, *Better Data Visualizations: A Guide for Scholars, Researchers, and Wonks.*

[Loud Numbers](https://www.loudnumbers.net/)

Data sonification is the process of turning data into sound. Loud Numbers is a data sonification podcast, created by Duncan Geere and Miriam Quick, which takes sonification a step further by turning data not only into sounds but into music.
