# Unit 2 · Learning the Basics

Anchor: 3601 · U2

In this unit, I'll introduce the broad field of Data Design, starting with a definition of data. You'll familiarise yourself with data as creative material. Then, I'll progress to the different types of data and where to find them. 

After, I'll cover why and how to visualise numbers, including a walk through the process of transforming raw numbers into a final visual output. Finally, I'll wrap up the unit with some considerations about what makes a good data design project to ensure a successful outcome.

- ### Organise Your Desk 
Practice exercise 2\.2

While data may be infinite, the ways of structuring it are not. Information can be organised by the 'L.A.T.C.H.' system: **L**ocation, **A**lphabet, **T**ime, **C**ategory, or **H**ierarchy. 

These modes are applicable to almost any endeavour, from your personal file cabinets to multinational corporations. They are the framework upon which annual reports, books, conversations, exhibitions, directories, conventions, and even warehouses are arranged.

![](https://assets8.domestika.org/course-images/000/037/688/37688-big.png)
- ### Translate Numbers Into Visuals 
Practice exercise 2\.3

I hope you've enjoyed learning about why we visualise data and how to take full advantage of visual literacy and perception in order to communicate data. 

It's now time to familiarise yourself with the process of translating data into visuals. Take this simple list of numbers and give them a visual form: 10, 20, 40, 50, 5\. Use the visual attributes below to be reminded of the many possibilities: 

· Form Attributes: Length, width, curvature, shape/pattern, added mark, and blur.

· Spatial Attributes: Orientation, proximity, enclosure, and motion.

· Colour Attributes: Hue, saturation, lightness, and transparency.

You don't need to confine yourself to using only lines (if you don't want to!). Repeat the encoding system at least 4 times and every time use a different visual attribute to encode the data. You can sketch the data by hand or use Adobe Illustrator. 

If you have any doubts, ask me in the forum! Don't forget to show us your results.

- ### About 'L.A.T.C.H.'

A handy framework to organise data and information was proposed by Saul Wurman. He coined the term 'L.A.T.C.H.' in his book *Information Anxiety*. He claims that there are five ways to organise information and this acronym will help you remember them: **L**ocation, **A**lphabet, **T**ime, **C**ategory, and **H**ierarchy. Below I have linked his book and an informative blog post to learn more:

[*Information Anxiety*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/952902.Information_Anxiety)
  
['LATCH – Methods of Organization'](https://parsonsdesign4.wordpress.com/resources/latch-methods-of-organization/)
- ### Visual Perception and Pre\-attentive Processing

Pre\-attentive processing is the subconscious 'consumption' of the visual environment. Understanding what this entails is important for data designers: the fundamentals of this process can be harnessed to make information easier to understand. For this reason, I encourage you to explore the following resources to enhance your understanding of visual perception: how we're all programmed to process the world around us.

[*Thinking, Fast and Slow* \| Daniel Kahneman](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780374533557/thinking-fast-and-slow)
  
This book explains in great detail how the brain works and its two primary systems. System one is fast, intuitive, and emotional; system two is slower, more deliberative, and more logical. Kahneman exposes the extraordinary capabilities – and also the faults and biases – of fast thinking, and reveals the pervasive influence of intuitive impressions on our thoughts and behaviour.

['Perception in Visualization'](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/healey/PP/)
  
A detailed article to learn more about pre\-attentive visual attributes.

['Gestalt Principles for Data Visualization'](https://medium.com/@Elijah_Meeks/gestalt-principles-for-data-visualization-59f18f20bd40)
  
Here you can learn about how to align graphical elements in the most effective way.
- ### A Historical Perspective

[*History of Information Graphics* \| Sandra Rendgen](https://sandrarendgen.wordpress.com/2021/07/23/new-book-the-history-of-information-graphics-june-2019/)
  
Contrary to general belief, data visualisation is not a modern development. Since prehistory, information such as the location of stars and the passing of time were visualised on the walls of caves. I recommend this beautiful, large\-format book to dive deep into the history of visualising data.
