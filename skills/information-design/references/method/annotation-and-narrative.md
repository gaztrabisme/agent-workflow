# Annotation and narrative: explain the encoding to the reader

## Reader and scope

**Reader:** a designer turning a tested chart into a self-explanatory graphic. **Scope:** title, contextual copy, direct labels, legends, units, dates, selection rules, sources and exceptions. The wider product flow and general UX writing belong to `ux`.

**Source lessons:** [course 2495 · U4 L15](../course-2495/U04-L15-always-keeping-in-mind-the-readers-legends-and-feedback-1.md) and [L16](../course-2495/U04-L16-always-keeping-in-mind-the-readers-legends-and-feedback-2.md); [course 4217 · U2 L05](../course-4217/U02-L05-visual-and-written-words.md), [U4 L17](../course-4217/U04-L17-the-legend-and-the-text-1.md) and [L18](../course-4217/U04-L18-the-legend-and-the-text-2.md); [course 3069 · U3 L11](../course-3069/U03-L11-incorporating-the-elements-into-your-design-2.md), [L12](../course-3069/U03-L12-title-and-explanation.md); [course 3601 · U4 L14](../course-3601/U04-L14-spelling-it-out-1.md) and [U5 L17](../course-3601/U05-L17-refining-exporting-and-sharing.md).

## Explain marks in reader order

- Write a title and short explanation that orient the reader to the subject and point of view. A title can state the subject plainly or invite curiosity. (4217 · U4 · L17 · 01:31–05:16; 3069 · U3 · L12 · 00:00–01:14)
- Introduce encodings in the order readers encounter them. Explain what position, size, shape, colour or repeated marks represent; use sample marks or concrete values for an unfamiliar scale. (4217 · U4 · L17 · 05:16–10:59; 4217 · U4 · L17 · 10:59–12:15; 4217 · U4 · L18 · 00:00–02:30; 4217 · U4 · L18 · 02:30–05:55)
- Include units, period, filters, selection rules and source names. The CO₂ example explains its scope, measures, years, selection rule and source; in the following lesson, the designer notes Turkmenistan's 2018 GDP exception. (2495 · U4 · L15 · 08:18–10:30; 2495 · U4 · L15 · 10:30–13:22; 2495 · U4 · L16 · 05:56–07:42)
- Put explanation near the marks when the reader would otherwise need to look back and forth to a distant key. (3069 · U3 · L11 · 14:33–16:28)
- Add the view's titles, axis names and scales, direct labels, annotations and keys; each should describe the data actually shown. (3601 · U4 · L14 · 09:24–12:46; 3601 · U4 · L14 · 12:46–15:43)
- Use simple, direct wording, but do not assume an infographic needs no words. Words and legends bridge the visual system to the reader. (2495 · U4 · L15 · 00:00–01:26; 4217 · U2 · L05 · 00:00–00:57)
- Declare the data sources in the graphic. (4217 · U4 · L18 · 08:45–10:39)

## Preserve a readable order

Course 4217 places title and explanation before its legend and regional chart; course 3069 integrates chapter sequence, headings, counts, introduction and nearby key; course 2495 uses a legend to show sample visual forms and explain the data. These examples support varying-depth reading, not a single mandatory layout. (4217 · U4 · L17 · 01:31–05:16; 4217 · U4 · L17 · 05:16–10:59; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 13:29–14:33; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 14:33–16:28; 2495 · U4 · L15 · 01:26–04:03; 2495 · U4 · L15 · 04:03–08:18)

**Rule synthesized (derived):** each visual encoding needs a corresponding explanation, and every explanatory claim needs a matching value or source note. Check the key against the rendered marks rather than checking its words in isolation. Derived from the courses' legends, sample keys and explanations of actual encodings. (derived from 4217 · U4 · L17 · 05:16–10:59; 4217 · U4 · L17 · 10:59–12:15; 2495 · U4 · L15 · 04:03–08:18; 2495 · U4 · L15 · 08:18–10:30; 3069 · U3 · L11 · 14:33–16:28)

## Failures

**Problem 1: an unexplained visual alphabet.** A reader cannot be expected to infer a novel mark's meaning. Add a short key and examples. (2495 · U4 · L15 · 00:00–01:26; 4217 · U4 · L18 · 02:30–05:55)

**Problem 2: context separated from the evidence.** A title or key that describes a different scope or mark creates a false reading. Compare each text item with the data mapping and the displayed selection. (3601 · U5 · L17 · 00:09–01:30; 3601 · U5 · L17 · 02:24–03:13; 2495 · U4 · L16 · 03:34–05:56)

## Check before moving on

Pass T7 when every visible mark and scale has an explanation; title, period, units, selection and source details are present where relevant; and text agrees with the actual chart. Return to annotation for unclear wording and to chart/data for mismatched claims.

**Practice the teachers set:** write the legend and explanatory text, test it with a non-expert, and revise the work from feedback. See [course 2495, Unit 4](../course-2495/U04-unit.md), [course 4217, Unit 4](../course-4217/U04-unit.md) and [course 3069, Unit 3](../course-3069/U03-unit.md).
