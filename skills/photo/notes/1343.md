# Course 1343 — working notes

Source: *Adobe Photoshop: Beginner's Guide for Photographers* reading copy. Anchors point to lesson sections. “(frame)” marks a claim read from a figure. The reading copy is translated from captions; do not rely on figure captions for U28 L44, U48 L71, or U56 L86.

## Method steps

- Inspect the histogram before adjusting; use the RGB composite to read tonal range, and inspect the full-image histogram rather than the cached thumbnail. Decide what tonal range the photograph actually contains. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 00:00–01:55; 02:59–03:57]
- If a small bright or dark subject is overwhelmed in the whole-image histogram, select that area and read its histogram separately. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 05:16–06:44]
- Set a tonal target before editing: place shadows close to black when appropriate and leave highlight detail short of clipping unless a light source/reflection should bleach out. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 06:44–08:10]
- Make a working copy, adjust brightness/contrast while watching the histogram, then compare the adjustment on/off. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 08:10–09:23; 1343 · U13 · L16 · 09:23–10:30; 1343 · U13 · L16 · 10:30–12:43]
- Correct tonal range before judging color casts; remove an unwanted cast until neutral colors look neutral, then do any creative coloring as a separate adjustment and compare before/after. [1343 · U14 · L17 · 00:00–01:05; 02:33–04:57]
- For a crop, decide whether it removes a distraction, levels an edge, fits a chosen format, corrects perspective, or trims a tiny border; select the crop mode and preserve or delete out-of-crop pixels accordingly. [1343 · U15 · L18 · 00:00–02:36; 02:36–04:39; 04:39–07:20; 10:07–14:35]
- For a series, maintain common dimensions by using the original image dimensions when straightening/cropping. [1343 · U15 · L18 · 07:20–10:07]

## Principles and claims

- A middle-heavy histogram is not a universal sign of correct exposure; it is expected only for an image dominated by mid-tones. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 01:55–02:59]
- A bright or dark photograph can be correctly exposed even when its histogram clusters toward the right or left. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 02:59–03:57; 1343 · U13 · L16 · 03:57–05:16]
- A small bright or dark region may barely register in the whole-image histogram, making that histogram misleading for that region. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 05:16–06:44]
- A histogram gap at the highlight edge indicates captured highlight tones; check that edge while shooting as well as while editing. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 03:57–05:16]
- A light source, its reflection, or the sun may bleach out; other bright tones need not reach pure white, and this example's highlights should stop short of the edge. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 06:44–08:10; 10:30–12:43]
- Brightness shifts the histogram left/right; contrast expands or compresses its tonal spread. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 09:23–10:30; 1343 · U13 · L16 · 10:30–12:43]
- Correcting tonal range first makes a color cast easier to see. [1343 · U14 · L17 · 00:00–01:05]
- Complementary pairs neutralize casts: yellow/blue, red/cyan, green/magenta. A negative channel value denotes removing that color. [1343 · U14 · L17 · 01:05–02:33; 1343 · U14 · L17 · 02:33–03:55]
- Color correction and creative coloring are distinct steps; keep the neutralized version as the comparison point for creative coloring. [1343 · U14 · L17 · 03:55–04:57]
- There are no fixed rules for creative coloring; the warm-tone values shown are the teacher's recipe, not a universal rule. [1343 · U14 · L17 · 04:57–06:35]
- Looking only at sliders can lead to over-adjustment; judge the image before and after. [1343 · U14 · L17 · 06:35–07:45]
- Crop guides can help recompose or straighten; deleting cropped pixels commits the crop, while keeping them permits later recovery and makes a larger file. [1343 · U15 · L18 · 00:00–02:36]
- A ratio defines crop shape, not physical output size. For a print size, set width, height, resolution, and pixels per inch. [1343 · U15 · L18 · 04:39–07:20]
- A normal Background layer cannot retain hidden pixels; after a reversible crop Photoshop may rename it Layer 0. [1343 · U15 · L18 · 04:39–07:20]
- The Perspective Crop tool straightens an object photographed at an angle by fitting a four-corner plane. [1343 · U15 · L18 · 10:07–11:45]

## Checks the teacher uses

- Read the RGB histogram's distribution and its highlight edge; check whether tones are clipped or leave a gap. Rendered artifact: histogram bins and edge contact can be measured, but whether that distribution suits the depicted scene needs judgement. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 00:00–01:55; 02:59–05:16; 06:44–08:10]
- Select the face/subject and inspect its local histogram when its tones occupy little of the frame. Rendered artifact: compare selected-region histogram bins; choose the region by eye. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 05:16–06:44]
- Toggle the adjustment layer and compare tonal range before/after. Rendered artifact: histogram spread/edge clearance; suitability of the result needs judgement. [1343 · U13 · L16 · 10:30–12:43]
- For cast correction, check that neutral colors and white look neutral; for creative warmth, use Preview to compare before/after and judge whether the result is appealing. Rendered artifact: channel/color values can be sampled, but neutrality and appeal need judgement by eye. [1343 · U14 · L17 · 02:33–03:55; 06:35–07:45]
- For a straightening/crop, align to a real edge such as a waterline and inspect the angle readout; press Enter/Return to commit. Rendered artifact: angle and edge alignment can be measured, with final framing judged by eye. [1343 · U15 · L18 · 02:36–04:39]
- For print output, check crop width, height, resolution, and unit; for a series, confirm original pixel dimensions. Rendered artifact: dimensions and resolution are directly measurable. [1343 · U15 · L18 · 04:39–07:20; 1343 · U15 · L18 · 07:20–10:07]
- After a perspective crop, inspect all edges zoomed in; use the pixel grid and Navigator to spot thin leftover lines, then compare history before/after. Rendered artifact: edge pixels and border width can be measured; whether the remnant matters is judged by eye. [1343 · U15 · L18 · 11:45–14:35]

## Continued notes — Levels, Curves, and black and white (U16–U18)

### Method steps

- In Levels, set black/white points from the histogram; use gamma to reassign midtones when correcting an exposure, then leave gamma at 1 if no midtone change is needed. [1343 · U16 · L19 · 00:11–01:59; 1343 · U16 · L19 · 01:59–05:33]
- For a color edit, read the overall brightness first, then adjust color, then contrast; revisit brightness if the later edits change its appearance. [1343 · U17 · L20 · 05:42–07:43; 12:05–13:04; 1343 · U17 · L21 · 07:13–09:50]
- Read Curves as a tonal map: points above the diagonal brighten their tone range, points below darken it; build contrast with a raised highlight point and lowered shadow point. [1343 · U17 · L20 · 01:39–02:54; 1343 · U17 · L20 · 02:55–04:41; 10:07–12:04]
- When removing color by individual channels, compensate the lost light in RGB; use layer opacity if the complete effect is too strong. [1343 · U17 · L21 · 03:46–06:03; 1343 · U17 · L21 · 06:04–07:12]
- For contrast in a specific subtle tonal region, sample the tones with the Curves hand tool and raise the lighter point/lower the darker point. [1343 · U17 · L21 · 13:55–17:58]
- For black and white, use a Black & White adjustment to separate color tones first; use Curves afterward for overall contrast. [1343 · U18 · L22 · 03:11–05:40; 09:16–11:09]
- Compare Hue/Saturation desaturation with the Black & White conversion; adjust color-specific sliders to retain separation or reduce skin blotchiness. [1343 · U18 · L22 · 01:08–03:11; 05:40–09:16]

### Principles and claims

- Levels sets black and white points and tonal range; its input controls are black 0, gamma 1.0, and white 255 by default. [1343 · U16 · L19 · 00:11–01:59]
- Gamma reassigns the midtone relationship between black and white; moving the gamma control left assigns more highlights in the shown example. [1343 · U16 · L19 · 01:59–05:33]
- The teacher advises against black/white eyedroppers because they are destructive and often poor; clicking a snow point with the white eyedropper makes it pure white and clips the histogram. [1343 · U16 · L19 · 05:33–08:02]
- Output levels constrain the range used by a printer; lowering output white can prevent paper-white blank areas in a light image. [1343 · U16 · L19 · 05:33–08:02; 1343 · U16 · L19 · 08:02–10:28]
- Output black lifts/fades black; the input black point can restore true black by moving to where the histogram begins. [1343 · U16 · L19 · 10:28–11:48]
- For product photography, white should be true white. [1343 · U16 · L19 · 11:48–14:20]
- A Curves graph can be read as a visual language; the position of a curve relative to the diagonal indicates how tones change. [1343 · U17 · L20 · 00:00–01:38; 1343 · U17 · L20 · 01:39–02:54]
- Curves provides independent highlight and shadow control; it is preferred for specific contrast, while Brightness/Contrast is a simple option. [1343 · U17 · L20 · 02:55–04:41]
- Raising a color-channel curve adds that color and light; lowering it removes that color and darkens. [1343 · U17 · L20 · 04:42–05:41]
- Stock images are already finished and their brightness/color choices are subjective. [1343 · U17 · L20 · 05:42–07:43]
- Removing color also removes light; the teacher's example balances 20 units blue + 10 green removed with 10 units added in RGB. [1343 · U17 · L21 · 03:46–06:03]
- A Curves point can cause clipping in the darkest tones; read the histogram while strengthening shadows. [1343 · U17 · L21 · 07:13–09:50]
- The Point Sample mode is intended for graphics; for photography use at least 3 by 3 Average (nine pixels) to sample a tone. [1343 · U17 · L21 · 13:55–17:58]
- Raising/lowering a Curves point affects the clicked tone and the brighter/darker range beyond it, respectively. [1343 · U17 · L21 · 13:55–17:58]
- Desaturation removes color information but retains the brightness of each original color; the result can become similar gray tones rather than distinct black-and-white tones. [1343 · U18 · L22 · 01:08–03:11]
- Black & White maps original colors to tones; its main purpose in the workflow shown is tonal separation, with Curves used for overall contrast. [1343 · U18 · L22 · 03:11–05:40]
- Photoshop may treat visually green foliage as yellow because foliage can contain more yellow than green. [1343 · U18 · L22 · 05:40–08:27]
- Strong contrast can work especially well in black and white, though not always in color. [1343 · U18 · L22 · 09:16–11:09]
- A Black & White adjustment blended at reduced opacity or Soft Light can alter a color palette while retaining color; Soft Light adds contrast and slightly increases saturation in the demonstrated seascape. [1343 · U18 · L22 · 11:09–13:17; 1343 · U18 · L22 · 13:17–14:57]

### Checks the teacher uses

- Check whether the shadow point reaches the histogram's lower-left and whether the image's brightest tone is below the desired white; leave a highlight gap for the snowy example. - Inspect whether black-and-white conversion preserves pattern/separates colors and whether newborn skin blotchiness is reduced; this needs judgement by eye. [1343 · U18 · L22 · 05:40–08:27; 1343 · U18 · L22 · 08:27–09:16; 1343 · U18 · L22 · 09:16–11:09]
- Compare the color seascape before/after the adjustment and lower opacity if the effect is too strong; this needs judgement by eye. [1343 · U18 · L22 · 11:09–13:17; 1343 · U18 · L22 · 13:17–14:57]

## Continued notes — selections (U19)

### Method steps

- Choose selection method for the job: rough Lasso for quick area edits, object/quick selection for subjects with imperfect areas to refine, channel selection for difficult hair, and Pen paths for clean geometric/product edges. [1343 · U19 · L23 · 00:00–01:01; 1343 · U19 · L23 · 01:01–03:10; 1343 · U19 · L24 · 00:00–02:02; 1343 · U19 · L25 · 00:00–02:31; 1343 · U19 · L26 · 00:11–02:48]
- Refine selections in Select and Mask with a view suited to the background and desired edge: Overlay for context, On Black/On White for cutout contrast, Black & White for mask values; use Smooth for jagged edges and Feather for softness. [1343 · U19 · L23 · 03:10–05:54; 1343 · U19 · L23 · 05:54–09:29]
- For a channel cutout, pick the color channel with most subject/background edge separation, copy it, adjust Levels while preserving fine subject texture, load it as selection, add/invert a layer mask, then paint/fill mask corrections. [1343 · U19 · L25 · 00:00–02:31; 1343 · U19 · L25 · 02:31–04:36; 1343 · U19 · L25 · 04:36–08:17; 1343 · U19 · L25 · 08:17–10:58]
- For a smooth product edge, trace a path, refine anchor points/handles, convert it to a selection or vector mask, and save/reload the selection if it will be reused. [1343 · U19 · L26 · 00:11–02:48; 1343 · U19 · L26 · 02:48–04:51; 1343 · U19 · L26 · 04:51–08:05; 1343 · U19 · L26 · 08:05–12:13; 1343 · U19 · L26 · 12:13–13:16; 1343 · U19 · L26 · 13:16–14:36]
- Use a selection to constrain an adjustment; feather broad areas before local tonal changes, exclude the subject when darkening background, and invert a vignette selection when the outside should be affected. [1343 · U19 · L24 · 05:48–08:01; 1343 · U19 · L24 · 08:01–12:01; 1343 · U19 · L24 · 12:01–13:03; 1343 · U19 · L24 · 13:03–15:56]

### Principles and claims

- Selection tools serve different accuracy needs; there is no single best method for all selections. [1343 · U19 · L23 · 00:00–01:01]
- Selection modes mean replace, add, subtract, and intersect; feather softens a selection edge while Smooth reduces jaggedness. [1343 · U19 · L23 · 01:01–03:10; 1343 · U19 · L23 · 03:10–05:54; 05:54–09:29]
- Marching Ants does not show soft feathering clearly; Overlay gives a better preview. [1343 · U19 · L23 · 03:10–05:54; 1343 · U19 · L24 · 05:48–08:01]
- A mask uses white for selected/visible, black for unselected/hidden, and gray for partial selection. [1343 · U19 · L23 · 05:54–09:29; 1343 · U19 · L25 · 04:36–08:17]
- Magnetic Lasso is slow and poor; Object Selection and Quick Selection can start a cutout but still need refinement. [1343 · U19 · L24 · 00:00–02:02; 1343 · U19 · L24 · 02:02–05:48]
- A luminosity selection uses brightness values; choose the channel with the strongest edge contrast. [1343 · U19 · L25 · 00:00–02:31]
- A channel cutout may require sacrificing some background texture or correcting the mask; pushing Levels too far makes hair strands clump. [1343 · U19 · L25 · 02:31–04:36]
- A vector path remains smooth when scaled, unlike the underlying pixels; vector selection is useful for clean, straight product outlines, where shaving a little extra object can look better than a bump. [1343 · U19 · L26 · 00:11–02:48; 13:16–14:36]
- Pen paths are stored in a Paths panel; Work Path is temporary unless saved. [1343 · U19 · L26 · 02:48–04:51]
