# Brief and session: decide what the photograph must do

**Reader:** the photographer planning a portrait, product-led social image, or small commissioned set. **Scope:** translate an audience and use into a subject, visual direction, session plan, and permitted edit scope. Composition-system questions belong with `visual-design`.

**Source lessons:** [669 U1 L03](../course-669/U01-L03-what-will-we-do-in-the-course.md), [669 U2 L04](../course-669/U02-L04-the-conversation.md), [669 U2 L05](../course-669/U02-L05-the-location.md), [669 U2 L06](../course-669/U02-L06-the-equipment.md), [227 U1 L02](../course-227/U01-L02-great-instagrammers.md), [227 U3 L06](../course-227/U03-L06-the-brand.md).

## Set the direction before choosing the treatment

- Decide what the photograph is for, who will see it, what it should communicate, where it will appear, and what format or crop it needs. The Instagram course starts from the account's character and values; the portrait course starts from a desired image and space. (227 · U1 · L02 · 00:00–00:56; 227 · U3 · L06 · 00:00–01:19; 227 · U3 · L06 · 01:19–03:19; 669 · U1 · L03 · 00:00–00:37)
- Study work you admire and name the qualities you want to borrow, rather than treating a reference as a recipe. (227 · U1 · L02 · 00:00–00:56)
- For a portrait session, choose a comfortable conversation routine. Listen, ask genuine questions, observe the person's gestures, and let them decide how much to share. The teacher describes her own manner as personal, not universal. (669 · U2 · L04 · 00:00–01:36; 669 · U2 · L04 · 01:36–03:24; 669 · U2 · L04 · 03:24–06:21; 669 · U2 · L04 · 06:21–08:07)
- Plan location, time, light, equipment, and props around the intended image. A modest room can work when background and light support the picture; the portrait course uses 35 mm when place or several people matter and usually uses 50 mm for a closer single-person portrait. (669 · U2 · L05 · 00:00–01:39; 669 · U2 · L05 · 05:58–07:18; 669 · U2 · L06 · 00:00–01:22; 669 · U2 · L06 · 01:22–03:00)
- For a brand or social account, let its existing visual identity guide colors and props; use neutral supports when product packaging varies. (227 · U3 · L06 · 01:19–03:19)

## Record the decisions

| Decision | Record |
|---|---|
| Audience and use | Who will see this, and where? |
| Subject and message | Who or what is pictured; what should be noticed first? |
| Direction | Reference images, light, setting, gesture, and color intent |
| Delivery | Required crop, orientation, dimensions, file type, and profile if supplied |
| Edit scope | Target image, requested change, protected details, references, and any structural change needing approval |

The edit-scope row is a **derived** control: the courses ask the photographer to choose an image direction and one course asks for client approval of structural retouching; the field rules require a precise target/change map before edits. (derived from 669 · U1 · L03 · 00:00–00:37; 1343 · U46 · L69 · 01:27–02:40; 1343 · U46 · L69 · 02:40–03:45; 1343 · U46 · L69 · 03:45–04:42; field rules FR-01, FR-04)

## Check before moving on

T1 passes when audience, purpose, subject, output, and permitted changes are recorded clearly enough to map each target image. Resolve material ambiguity before editing. See [review-checklist.md](review-checklist.md).
