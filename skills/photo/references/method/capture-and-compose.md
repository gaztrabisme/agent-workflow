# Capture and compose: make the frame serve the subject

**Reader:** the photographer capturing portraits and social images. **Scope:** choose light, framing, camera position, and coverage. Graphic layout systems and campaign composition belong with `visual-design`.

**Source lessons:** [669 U2 L05–L06](../course-669/U02-L05-the-location.md), [669 U3 L07–L09](../course-669/U03-L07-working-with-natural-light-in-interior-windows.md), [227 U2 L03–L05](../course-227/U02-L03-materials.md), [227 U3 L07–L09](../course-227/U03-L07-create-an-environment.md), [227 U5 L15](../course-227/U05-L15-taking-photos.md).

## Find light and framing together

- A room does not need to be impressive by itself. Find the useful background and light, move furniture if it helps, and frame out distractions that do not serve the picture. (669 · U2 · L05 · 00:00–01:39; 669 · U2 · L05 · 01:39–03:59; 669 · U2 · L05 · 05:58–07:18)
- Check the time and direction of natural light. The portrait course compares warm, low evening sun with stronger afternoon contrast; the phone course recommends soft light for manageable highlights and shadows, while showing hard light as a deliberate compositional choice. (669 · U2 · L05 · 03:59–05:58; 227 · U2 · L04 · 05:17–06:50; 227 · U2 · L04 · 06:50–08:07; 227 · U2 · L04 · 08:07–09:31)
- For a phone, use its largest image setting and RAW if supported (227 · U2 · L04 · 00:00–01:27); avoid digital zoom (227 · U2 · L04 · 01:27–03:07); clean the lens, hold the phone firmly, and tap the shutter gently (227 · U2 · L04 · 03:07–05:17). Tap the screen to focus and expose (227 · U3 · L07 · 05:41–06:28). A thin white curtain can soften strong window light (227 · U2 · L04 · 09:31–11:20); a white reflector can soften dark shadows (227 · U3 · L07 · 06:28–08:32).
- Choose lens and distance for the picture: the portrait course gives a wider lens more room for setting or several people and uses a closer view when the face should dominate. Phone wide-angle distortion makes stepping back useful for portraits. (669 · U2 · L06 · 00:00–01:22; 669 · U2 · L06 · 01:22–03:00; 227 · U2 · L03 · 00:00–01:55)
- For product-led social images, arrange larger props first, check their shadows, then place smaller props inside the phone frame; an overhead flat lay benefits from a more direct viewpoint and space around the grouping. (227 · U3 · L07 · 02:25–04:33; 227 · U3 · L08 · 00:00–01:16; 227 · U3 · L08 · 01:16–02:25; 227 · U3 · L08 · 02:25–03:32; 227 · U3 · L08 · 04:07–05:19)
- Set the final format before building a still life. Frame through the phone first, then arrange only what appears inside it; use thirds, intentional symmetry, or negative space when each fits the subject. (227 · U2 · L05 · 00:00–01:15; 227 · U2 · L05 · 01:15–02:27; 227 · U2 · L05 · 02:27–04:10; 227 · U2 · L05 · 04:10–05:15; 227 · U2 · L05 · 05:15–06:59; 227 · U3 · L07 · 01:25–02:25; 227 · U3 · L07 · 02:25–04:33)
- In an interior, start with the broad room view, then photograph areas and details; keep architectural verticals as straight as the camera position permits. (227 · U3 · L09 · 00:57–01:51; 227 · U3 · L09 · 01:51–03:29)
- Explore pose, activity, angle, and light response. The portrait teacher moves the subject until the eye sits between shutter-light bands and abandons smoke when wind prevents the intended effect. (669 · U3 · L07 · 00:00–02:45)
- Test a room or found object as an optical effect only after the plain-light frame works; change distance and framing, then remove the effect if it weakens the image. (669 · U3 · L08 · 00:56–02:51; 669 · U3 · L09 · 00:00–01:43; 669 · U3 · L09 · 03:15–04:31; 669 · U3 · L09 · 04:31–07:05)
- Sharpening can emphasize detail already present, but it cannot reliably restore missed focus. (1343 · U43 · L66 · 00:11–03:15; 1343 · U43 · L66 · 05:01–07:21)

## Capture checks

On the phone, tap the screen to focus and expose; on an iPhone, raise exposure a little without overexposing the image, which the lesson identifies by part of the screen turning white. (227 · U3 · L07 · 05:41–06:28) For highlights, the Photoshop course says a gap at the histogram's right edge means the image captured highlight tones, and recommends checking the right side for highlight detail. (1343 · U13 · L16 · 03:57–05:16) Highlights need not reach the far right; without a light source, the histogram should typically end just before the edge. (1343 · U13 · L16 · 06:44–08:10)

**Derived capture rule:** keep distinctive expressions and useful alternate views in the set, even when one frame is the initial favorite; later selection can compare what each image contributes. This combines the portrait teacher's developing gestures and viewpoint comparisons with the Instagram teacher's multiple angles. (derived from 669 · U4 · L10 · 05:57–08:53; 669 · U4 · L11 · 00:00–03:14; 669 · U4 · L11 · 03:14–06:44; 227 · U3 · L07 · 06:28–08:32)

## Check before moving on

T2 passes when the requested subject and intended light are captured, the intended focus is usable, and no important highlight or edge is unintentionally lost. See [review-checklist.md](review-checklist.md).
