# RAW and base correction: establish clean geometry, tone, and color

**Reader:** the editor preparing selected photographs for local retouch or grading. **Scope:** develop a sound base image before detailed work; composition intent remains with the brief and `visual-design`.

**Source lessons:** [1343 U13 L16](../course-1343/U13-L16-histograms-image-quality-brightness-contrast.md), [1343 U14 L17](../course-1343/U14-L17-colour-balance-and-creative-colouring.md), [1343 U15 L18](../course-1343/U15-L18-crop-straighten.md), [1343 U23 L32–L33](../course-1343/U23-L32-power-of-raw-1.md), [1343 U24 L34–L36](../course-1343/U24-L34-local-edits-in-the-raw-converter-1.md), [669 U4 L10](../course-669/U04-L10-editing-with-lightroom.md), [227 U4 L11](../course-227/U04-L11-editing-photos-with-snapseed.md).

## Develop in a reversible order

1. Preserve an untouched original and make a working copy. Where the editor supports it, use nondestructive adjustments or a linked RAW smart object so tone and color decisions can be revisited. (1343 · U23 · L32 · 00:00–02:45; 1343 · U23 · L32 · 02:45–09:20; 1343 · U25 · L37 · 00:00–02:44; 1343 · U25 · L37 · 02:44–05:51)
2. Read the full-image histogram, then inspect the face or another small subject separately if it occupies little of the frame. A middle-heavy histogram is not a universal exposure target. (1343 · U13 · L16 · 00:00–01:55; 1343 · U13 · L16 · 05:16–06:44)
3. Set tone for the actual scene. Keep useful highlight information below clipping; light sources and reflections may intentionally bleach out. Compare the adjustment on and off. (1343 · U13 · L16 · 06:44–08:10; 1343 · U13 · L16 · 10:30–12:43)
4. Use Levels or Curves to set the needed black/white range and midtone or contrast changes; watch the histogram when a point moves toward clipping. For monochrome, compare a Black & White conversion with plain desaturation when tonal separation matters. (1343 · U16 · L19 · 01:59–05:33; 1343 · U16 · L19 · 05:33–08:02; 1343 · U17 · L20 · 01:39–02:54; 1343 · U17 · L20 · 02:55–04:41; 1343 · U17 · L21 · 07:13–09:50; 1343 · U18 · L22 · 01:08–03:11; 1343 · U18 · L22 · 03:11–05:40)
5. Correct geometry and crop for the intended output. Straighten a real horizon or vertical; crop after perspective corrections when the editor needs to fill edge gaps. Keep source pixels recoverable when later reframing matters. (1343 · U15 · L18 · 00:00–02:36; 1343 · U15 · L18 · 02:36–04:39; 1343 · U15 · L18 · 10:07–11:45; 1343 · U15 · L18 · 11:45–14:35; 227 · U4 · L11 · 01:38–04:12)
6. Correct unwanted white-balance or color casts after tone is stable; compare neutral references if present. Treat creative color as a separate, reversible choice. (1343 · U14 · L17 · 00:00–01:05; 1343 · U14 · L17 · 02:33–03:55; 1343 · U14 · L17 · 03:55–04:57; 227 · U4 · L11 · 10:04–11:36)
7. Use local RAW adjustments for local exposure, highlights, shadows, color, or lens corrections before committing to pixel retouch when they can solve the problem cleanly. (1343 · U24 · L34 · 00:10–05:03; 1343 · U24 · L34 · 13:06–15:13; 1343 · U24 · L35 · 05:45–09:05; 1343 · U24 · L35 · 12:08–17:54; 1343 · U24 · L36 · 04:34–05:48; 1343 · U24 · L36 · 05:48–08:47)

The course demonstrations use Lightroom, Camera Raw, and Photoshop; the order above is **derived** by combining the RAW, histogram, tonal-control, color-correction, and crop lessons. It describes decisions, not required software. ## Course differences

The portrait course establishes scene color with the first photograph and copies the look to other photographs from that space, then adapts each image. The phone course copies edits only when light, background, and conditions match. The Photoshop course tests a recorded action on a photograph before batch processing. Apply a copied base only to a genuinely matched scene, then inspect and adapt each frame.
