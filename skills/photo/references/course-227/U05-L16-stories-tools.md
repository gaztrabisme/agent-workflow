## Choose a Stories capture mode

Anchor: 227 · U5 · L16 · 00:00–02:15

In this lesson we will learn to use the tools in Instagram Stories. They may be useful because they are practically the same as the ones in Reels. We open the app and go into Stories. At the top of the interface are the controls for leaving Stories, turning the flash on or off for photos, and opening the Stories settings. On the right are the modes for making a text-only story, a Boomerang, a layout, hands-free video and Dual.

I do not recommend the text-only mode. It gives you these gradients, which rarely suit your project and look quite random, so I almost never use it. There is also Boomerang, a short clip that records and repeats over and over.

The grid collage mode (*layout*) gives you a grid where you can put different photos or take them one by one. For example, you can take photos until all six spaces are filled, or swipe up and pick different photos from your phone. You can then move them around and reposition them, zoom in on them, or delete one and choose another until you have made the collage.

If you need both hands, perhaps to film yourself, or want to leave the phone still while it records, choose hands-free (*manos libres*). Tap once and it records without you holding the phone, as it would normally do. The final option is Dual. It uses the front and rear cameras together, inspired by an app called BeReal. When you take a photo you can see yourself in the selfie camera and what you are looking at through the other camera at the same time. I would not upload the example I am showing because there is nothing in it, but that is the idea.

## Explore and adjust filters

Anchor: 227 · U5 · L16 · 02:15–04:58

At the bottom there are many little circles like the Story circles, but these are filters. Instagram calls them “effects,” although everyone calls them filters (*filtros*). A filter is an effect applied to your Story. It might add a 3D object, change the colors, change your face, or do something else.

I will show you examples that are very different from each other; there are millions. You can explore many of them at the far right, under Explore Effects.

## Add text and design it

Anchor: 227 · U5 · L16 · 06:29–10:11

The first tool at the upper left is text, one of the most important Story tools. We want to communicate something. Even when I film myself talking, I add context to what I am saying because people often watch without sound. It is very important.

For example, I have typed “new candles.” We can align a paragraph to the left, right or center. With only two words, alignment does not matter much because we can place them wherever we want, but it matters with a longer paragraph because it changes its shape.

We can also change the color using one of the choices shown, or press and hold a color to open the whole rainbow and choose any color. We can decide whether the letters have a highlight behind them or appear on their own. Each tap toggles the foreground and background colors. If I choose white, it first gives me white letters with a black highlight. Tap again and it reverses them; tap once more and the highlight disappears. We can also slide through the font (*tipografía*) choices and pick the typeface we like.

Here is a useful trick: the color tool changes whatever is selected. If I select everything and change it to green, all of it changes. If I select another part and tap yellow, only that part changes. This lets us make a color gradient (*degradado*) in the lettering. I would not use every rainbow color on this photo; perhaps I would use a warmer area of the palette. In fact, with this photo and this brand I might not use that either, but I am sharing the trick because it can be useful on many other photos.

To make the gradient, choose a color and hold it so the palette expands. Then deselect the word while moving across it. When you finish, tap Done and you have a gradient.

You do not always have to place the text the same way. I tend to put it in the center and stop there, but we can do something more creative. Imagine writing “sales” and changing the size of each letter, then placing the letters in different spots. That may seem like a small change, but moving the letters around, or making one start in one place and finish in another, creates many different graphic designs and changes the feel of a Story. I recommend using Pinterest to look for graphic-design inspiration. You may be surprised how many ideas you can make with just the text tool. When the screen is ready, move to the next story.

## Draw over a Story and use stickers

Anchor: 227 · U5 · L16 · 10:11–15:47

The next tool I like is Draw, behind the three dots. It lets you draw over a photo and also turn a Story into a solid-colored background, which I use often. You can start with any photo, even an absurd one (captions: *a surda*) or just the black image that appears when the phone is resting on a table. Pick a color and hold your finger down to fill the whole background. Then you can write on it, add a sticker or image, or put a whole paragraph on the colored background.

If you color the whole background and then use the eraser (*goma*) to remove part of it, you can reveal things from the photo underneath. That makes a teaser of what is in the photo or what is coming. There are millions of ideas. On this photo I would not use green, because I do not think it matches the picture. I would pick the marker again and use the eyedropper (*cuentagotas*) to choose a color from the image: the red, for example, or the blue on the box. Then I would fill the background with that color. It looks much better. You can add text, stickers or anything else; the eyedropper often finds a color that harmonizes with the photo.

The Draw tool has more options. The standard marker has a side bar to change its size. We can also draw arrows. The highlighter (*subrayador*) covers the image only partly, with low opacity, so we can add more layers. If I use it across the background, it makes a blue veil over the photo. Text then reads well against the blue while the image remains visible. There is also a neon option, which I do not usually use because it does not fit with anything for me. We can also draw decorations to give the Story a different look. If a person appears, for instance, you can trace around their silhouette.

Stickers are the tool I have left until last because there are so many. I recommend looking at them one by one and thinking of several ways to use each one. Asking “How else could I use this sticker?” is a very useful creativity exercise for getting more out of Stories.

Some basic stickers include Location. It can show where you are, or any place you choose: even at home you can type a place into the search and find it. You can mention someone. There is also the Add Yours sticker (*Ahora tú*; captions: *el aura tú*): you choose a theme, such as a photo of your cat, and other people can accept the challenge and post one too.

![227 · U5 · L16 · 14:09](frames/U05L16-F1409.00.jpg)

The question sticker is useful for asking your community for opinions or ideas. You can create a question box with whatever question you want, or open a question-and-answer round. People tap “Write something,” send their answer and give you a set of responses from your community.

You can add a GIF or music, or use interactive stickers. In one kind, you choose an emoji and people respond by moving it along the sticker. Other stickers let the audience choose among answers, such as polls and quizzes. I recommend exploring them all and trying them, because new ones appear all the time. The ones I see now may not be the ones on your phone: the options change every week. One I find useful is the image sticker, which lets us make a colored background and place an image over it.

### Key points (derived)

- Use Stories' camera modes and filters to build varied vertical sequences.
- Make text readable and intentional; color selections can vary within a word.
- Combine Draw tools and interactive stickers to add context and invite responses.
