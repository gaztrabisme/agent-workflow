## Start with the project's values

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L06 · 00:00–01:19

In this lesson, we will look at how to create this photographic style, or how we created it for the client we will work with during this course, Singular Olivia. Before taking photographs, define the photographic style for the project. First, stop and think about the project you are working on, whether it is your own or someone else's. Consider especially which values you want to express, because those values will guide every decision you make when taking a photograph.

The brand we will work with throughout the course is The Singular Olivia. It is a natural cosmetics shop with a beautiful little store in Segovia and one of the most important online shops in the country. It sells all kinds of natural cosmetics, as well as food and lifestyle things such as books and bags. Its most important products are ones the owner created herself, such as soaps and candles. Their quality is exceptional, so people who try them tend to become devoted to them. Another thing I would single out about the brand is the story behind it. ## Align photography with the brand identity

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L06 · 01:19–03:19

First, check whether the brand already has defined branding (*branding*), meaning a graphic design system. If it does not, it is useful to start by deciding its colors, typefaces and overall style. If it does, its branding should be closely connected to its photographic style.

For Olivia, the brand had recently been rebranded. The references used included Paris, Mary Poppins, Wes Anderson, Tiffany's and Tim Burton. It is an explosive mixture, but it represents the style the brand had always carried and brings those references together well. Other important ideas are natural cosmetics—everything is organic, eco and even vegan—and the laboratory process behind the products. The laboratory concept has been part of the brand throughout its history, and we wanted it to appear in the photography.

We defined a color palette (*paleta de colores*) that respects the light blue of the previous brand, adjusted slightly, and uses vanilla as the main color. The two colors work with greens, desaturated grays, rice tones and black for more contrast. We considered that the products' packaging varies widely and includes many colors. We therefore needed both identifying brand colors and gray shades that would coordinate with all, or at least many, of the products that do not match the branding as closely. The palette helps us choose product combinations for photographs and colors for backgrounds.

## Choose props that fit the photographic style

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L06 · 03:19–05:01

The prop style (*atrezo*) we chose for Olivia is very natural. We use typical bathroom elements and natural materials such as rattan and wood. We also bring in laboratory pieces and put them into everyday scenes—for example, using one as a vase or putting a toothbrush in it—so that they feel part of daily life. We add plant elements too.

I usually buy props at the most familiar stores in my city, such as IKEA and Zara Home. Clothing stores sometimes have things that can work for lifestyle photographs. El Corte Inglés is another example: its home-decor section surprisingly has many neutral items, such as glass cups, that suit many types of photographs and are useful to have in a prop collection. Wherever you go, keep your eyes open for the kinds of things you need. Once you have defined the style of your brand or project, make a list—for Olivia, natural materials such as wood, bathroom items, laboratory items and vintage objects. Keep that list in mind when you travel or go out; even a beach stall might have an old clock that could come in handy.

## Compare three distinct brand styles

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L06 · 05:01–06:31

To make the choices clearer, here are three brands I have worked with, each with a different style. Oléptic is another cosmetics brand that also uses natural elements, but we approached it differently. The idea is a minimalist style with lots of white and food. We consistently included vegetation because the plain-background product photographs on its online shop have plant frames around them. We also photographed plants in the street, which the brand uses on Instagram and its website. Its palette is white and black, with a little green from the vegetation. The props are ceramics, healthy food, plants, bathroom accessories, fabrics and towels.

## Set the style for the next shoot

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L06 · 06:31–07:48

Charuca is very different from the previous brands. It is a stationery brand that could be called the queen of stationery in Spain. It conveys femininity and elegance, with gold elements. The scenes are carefully studied but retain a natural touch. There is also a creative side in which we show new designs or campaigns, such as the always-€2 shipping offer. Those images sit in the brand's Instagram gallery alongside more everyday scenes.

For its props, we look for desk items such as pencils and pens, as well as all kinds of gold objects because gold appears often in its designs. We also pay attention to backgrounds: its palette is light blue, pink and dark gray, with some of the coral we saw earlier. Once you know what your project is, which values you want to convey and how you will communicate them through a color palette and the props you need, you are ready to start taking photographs. ### Key points (derived)

- Define the values a project should express before making photographs.
- Use an existing brand identity to guide the photographic palette and props.
- Make deliberate choices about color, materials and objects so each image fits the brand's style.
