## Photographing places beyond home

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L09 · 00:00–00:57

In this lesson I’ll explain how to photograph places other than your home, studio, shop, and other spaces you already know. These photographs work well alongside the ones we have been taking, where we show products or services directly or speak clearly about our project. They are also useful for recommending places we have visited and especially like, such as shops, bookstores, cafés, and similar spaces. They are great to pair with a story about the place.

When you arrive somewhere you especially like and want to photograph, I recommend starting by looking at the light again. See where the artificial light is. We probably will not be able to turn it off, so we will avoid it. As we said in the lesson about light, get as close as possible to a window or another source of natural light, and take the photographs there.

## Photograph from the whole space to its details

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L09 · 00:57–01:51

When I go in, I first look for the parts I like most to photograph. I also try to take a few shots that show the entire space, a general view of everything. I take others that show a wall I especially like or broad areas of the place, without going straight to details. I also take some details.

One important thing to keep in mind is the lines in the image. Make sure all the vertical lines of walls, doors, and other features are perfectly straight, or as straight as possible, so editing will not be too difficult later. If you want to add an object—for example, a vase on the next table that you especially like and want to add to your photograph—move it to your table without worry. In our case, we suddenly had hammocks that we did not especially like the color of for the rest of the composition, so we took them out. People generally do not mind when you move things like that.

## Expose for the interior and make a varied set

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L09 · 01:51–03:29

When you take a photograph of a window, or a window appears in the picture, and it looks out onto an exterior you do not especially like, expose for the interior (*exponer dentro*) so the photograph gets bright enough that the outside is not visible. When you take the photo, set the exposure on something inside the space—a chair or a wall—and that will brighten it enough that the outside does not show. Later we will edit a photograph like this so you can see exactly how it looks.

Remember to work from the largest spaces down to the smallest details so you do not forget anything, and take lots of photographs. Take the same thing you want to photograph from different viewpoints. For example, photograph the same table from the front, from above, or from a high angle (*plano picado*), or in another way like that. It will give you many more options when you edit.

Above all, when you visit places you like to eat, an exhibition, or anything you personally enjoy and think is related to your project—to the values you want your project to express—pay attention to the photographs you could take and keep your phone ready. Then the photographs are ready; we only need to move on to editing. Before that, I’ll give you some creative ideas that are widely used on Instagram and are fun to start trying with your phone.
