# Lesson 08 · Compose a Flatlay

## Choose the background and frame

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L08 · 00:00–01:16

Compared with the previous, more realistic photograph, in which we tried to find or create an environment that could really exist, this one is more creative. We will make a slightly surreal flat lay (*fotografía cenital*) with a flat-colored background. Ours is a large sheet of vanilla-colored card. We buy these sheets at art-supply stores, usually in a size of 100 x 70; if we find smaller ones, we overlap two. We will build everything around the background. Vanilla is a warm shade, so we chose products and props in blues, sky blues and related shades.

I set this photograph up as I did the previous one. First, choose the frame. This time it will be horizontal, because I plan to crop it to a square later. I recommend not using the camera's square-photo setting, because then we lose the ability to reframe if we need to. Set the frame horizontally and arrange the composition within it.

## Arrange the composition and secure loose items

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L08 · 01:16–02:25

I chose an orderly composition (*composición ordenada*), like the type we discussed in the composition lesson, but I left some negative space (*espacio negativo*) so the photograph can breathe. The objects lie horizontally or vertically along an imaginary grid. The composition has three large groups: the tote bag with its products and envelope; the map with the perfumes and a leaf; and another group.

Here are some tricks we used. The bag raised the envelope a little, leaving it bent and looking unattractive because its perspective (*perspectiva*) was off. We put washi tape (*washi*) underneath to lift it. The perfumes also rolled a lot and ran out of the photograph, so we placed a little Blu Tack (captions: *blue tag*) underneath to hold them in place.

## Match the props to the photographic style

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L08 · 02:25–03:32

The products we will photograph are these cosmetic bottles, the brand's tote bag and two perfumes, as well as the soap from the brand that we already know. The props I chose match the style of The Singular Olivia. When you choose props, make sure they fit your photographic style. I recommend looking at lots of photographs on Pinterest, Instagram and elsewhere, and noticing which props people use; you may get ideas you would not otherwise think of. For example, the stamps I often use for The Singular Olivia give the photographs a retro touch. I first saw them at a stationery brand called Presegan.

Whenever you arrange a composition, check the framing again and make sure the composition works within it. If it does, take the photograph. We will turn off the set lights and see how it looks under the real light.

## Reflect window light with a foam board

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L08 · 03:32–04:07

We have turned off the set lights so you can see how the window light affects the photograph. The shadows cast by the objects are quite dark, so we will use the reflector again: the foam board we already bought. As before, use a stool, chair or anything you have on hand to support it. That is our improvised support.

## Use the phone and photograph from overhead

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L08 · 04:07–05:19

Once the reflector is set up, take the photograph. This time I am using an Android phone to show that the process works in the same way. The difference is that when you focus, the phone also exposes the image. A line appears; I will put something dark there so you can see it. Moving the control toward the plus makes the image lighter, and moving it toward the minus makes it darker.

## Adapt the idea to services and familiar places

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L08 · 05:19–06:18

Both this overhead photograph and the previous one can work for a project centered on services, or another kind of project that does not have products. You can plan the photograph to express a message. For example, if you want to say that you are working on a particular assignment, you can make a behind-the-scenes photograph with the same background, some sketches you have developed, your usual tools and a laptop. These kinds of objects tend to work well and make your work feel more human.

We made both photographs in a place we know well—our home, studio or shop, somewhere we are familiar with. Now we will take photographs in unfamiliar interiors, such as a café, restaurant, art gallery or museum, or any other place.

### Key points (derived)

- Build the composition around a warm, single-color background and leave some space for the image to breathe.
- Use a grid to place objects and secure anything that might roll or bend.
- Overhead photographs can communicate a service or process as well as show products.
