## Why Stories work for everyday communication

Anchor: 227 · U5 · L15 · 00:00–02:07

In this lesson, we will introduce Instagram Stories so you can start using it.

Instagram Stories is a feature for sharing content that lasts for 24 hours. After those 24 hours, it disappears. When we open Instagram, circles appear automatically across the top. They show the profile photos and usernames of people we follow who have posted Stories. To watch one, we tap its circle and move through the stories.

Stories is one of Instagram's most viewed features. One of its advantages is that you can share things from day to day without making them look especially polished. Because they disappear after 24 hours, you do not have to work them up as much. That gives us a lot of freedom: we can make content wherever we are, as things happen, and do it more quickly.

In Stories, I focus especially on what I want to communicate: the message I want to give. Because the other parts matter less, the message needs to be very clear and should always offer something useful. You can use Stories for any kind of content. I often share pictures of my everyday life, and of places I go to or visit that I love and want to share. I also share many reflections: things I learn and discover. When I get the impulse to say, “Oh, I want to tell people this,” I do it there, either by recording myself, writing, or using another format.

The stickers make Stories a direct way to interact with a community. You can start a direct conversation and let people respond to your stories. It is the feature that makes it easiest to build a strong relationship.

## Fit the image to the 9:16 frame

Anchor: 227 · U5 · L15 · 02:07–03:20

The format (*formato*) for Stories is 9:16. It is narrower than what a camera may capture by default. This depends a little on the phone, since some phones take photos or videos directly in 9:16. Check how yours behaves. Take a photo and crop it to 9:16 to see whether the original already has that format or whether the crop changes it.

Here is my check: I see how much my phone crops from my photos. I take a picture of the table, go to the gallery, tap Edit, and crop it to 9:16 (captions: *916*). You can see that it leaves two very narrow strips at the sides. So, if I take a photo or video for Stories with my phone's camera rather than in Instagram, I remember that those edges will be cut off. I cannot make a photo where an important object reaches all the way to the edge and expect it to stay there. Important things should never sit in the part that gets cropped.

If you are unsure, you can take photos in the Instagram app. The downside is that the app does not take them at quite the same quality as your phone.

## Keep the composition vertical and simple

Anchor: 227 · U5 · L15 · 03:20–04:52

For Stories, try to make all your content vertical. Almost nobody will turn their phone just to watch your story. Usually people will skip it because they did not come only to see your content; they are moving through stories continuously.

When I take a photo or video, I remember that the strips on the right and left will be cropped. I estimate where the crop falls in my phone's grid and keep it in mind. Nothing important should stick out at either side or sit too close to the edge; the subject needs to be more centered.

Stories do not require photo editing, especially not a lot of editing. If you want to make a small adjustment, you can use the VSCO copy-and-paste function we saw earlier, or use filters inside Instagram. Many creators make filters that adjust the tones, including filters that imitate VSCO. They are useful for making attractive content quickly.

To open Stories, tap the plus sign and switch to Story. Another quick way is to swipe left, which takes you straight to the camera. At the bottom you can choose between Story, making a Reel, and the other modes; here, choose Story.

## Vary the screens in a Story

Anchor: 227 · U5 · L15 · 04:52–05:59

My recommendation is not to make every Story the same kind of content. If you are building a complete story, do not use similar photos the whole time, and do not make every screen text over a background. Vary it: one screen can be a photo, the next a video, and the next a background with text. This makes the sequence more dynamic and less boring. We want the content to be attractive enough that people keep watching instead of skipping it.

We take the photos as before. If I am making a Story with these products and props to promote the candles and this Olivia soap, for example, I use the same approach as before: set up side lighting and do everything the same way. The difference is that I can work more quickly and do not need to labor over it as much. I can make simpler compositions, or take an everyday photo of a candle on the desk where I am working. I do not have to build a set just to make Stories. In this example, though, I am going to build one.

## Build a set, then change the framing

Anchor: 227 · U5 · L15 · 05:59–08:35

One thing I often do for Stories is build a larger set, take several photos and even videos from it, and use them in one story. For example, I can make a desk area with magazines, papers and other things. I overlap several objects, add something attractive, and put the candles there so I can take an overhead photo (*foto cenital*).

I have arranged everything in a bit of a pile, with some things on top of others and nothing straight—the opposite of the flat lay we made earlier. Now we can see how it looks through the camera and perhaps move something. We could take the photos directly in the app if we want to upload them immediately, or use our camera. I would not compose it this way, though. I like it better when one side of the image is cut off and there is space on the other side. If I put the objects here, the crop will look like this, so I need to move them farther away.

Then I hold the phone firmly, hold my breath, and take the photo. From this set I can keep changing the content: for example, take a closer picture of one corner (captions: *ha sido una esquina*), or move the objects so there is more room to write something. I avoid showing the bottoms of the candles because they have all the instructions on them, and I do not think those look as nice in the photo. I move the candles a little so the instructions show less. I can also alternate the still photos with a video; I switch to video and hold my breath again.

Once the photos are ready, we can edit them in VSCO with the copy-and-paste method if we want to add an extra touch quickly, or upload them as they are. In the next lesson, we will look at the tools inside the app that we can use to level up our Stories.

### Key points (derived)

- Stories last 24 hours, so they suit quick, everyday updates and direct interaction.
- Compose vertically for 9:16 and keep important details away from the side crop.
- Vary each Story sequence with photos, video and text, and reuse one set for several framings.
