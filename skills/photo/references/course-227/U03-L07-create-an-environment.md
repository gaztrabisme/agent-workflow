## Choose a place, light and format

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L07 · 00:00–01:25

Let us start taking photographs with the phone. I am sitting on the floor because I have been looking around my house, as you will need to do, for the places, areas, furniture and shelves I like most. They should also be near a source of light. There is a window beside me that will let the light in; you cannot see it right now because the filming lights are on, but later you will see exactly where the light comes from and where the shadows fall. I am making the photograph here on this bench, which is why I am lying down.

We will start by choosing the format (*formato*). For this photograph, I will use a vertical format. Try to work in the brightest room, taking into account which room is brightest at the time you plan to take the photograph. If you have no natural light and have to use artificial light, you can still take some photographs for Stories.

The products are an Olivia soap (captions: *jamón*), a hair product and a ceramic piece. The props (*atrezo*) we chose fit the color range and materials we defined for the brand: a ceramic plate, a small marble bowl, a wooden hand and towels. We have also added a leaf for the natural touch we want.

## Frame first, then arrange

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L07 · 01:25–02:25

In this kind of photograph, I place and move the elements without moving the framing. First, find the framing (*encuadre*) you want. I will use a vertical frame and the rule of thirds (*regla de los tercios*) to place the objects in this area of the photograph, leaving a lot of negative wall space (*espacio negativo*) to add interest.

When the framing is set and the camera is configured, the only thing left before decorating is to clean the lens. Why do this now instead of framing after decorating the bench? If you decorate the whole bench with all your props and only then frame the shot, you may find that you will use just one corner. You would have done extra work decorating parts of the bench that the photograph will not show. I will keep checking the framing and place the elements where they work in the composition.

## Build the arrangement and control the shadows

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L07 · 02:25–04:33

I will start with the ceramic piece because it is large and will make a strong shadow. The light will come from this direction. If I put the piece here, its shadow will extend this way and enter the photograph, so I will put it on the other side to keep the shadow outside the frame. Next I will place the plate, the other large element, and move the ceramic piece a little farther to the left to adjust the composition.

Now I will fill in with the remaining objects. I will roll the towels and put them inside the ceramic piece to suggest a bathroom where clean towels are neatly arranged. When rolling them, first fold them lengthwise; then roll them and put the wrinkled side where it cannot be seen. I will place one next to the other.

On the plate, I will make a pyramid composition. The wooden hand is the tallest object, with the hair product and soap below it. The soap and hair product are at almost the same height, so I will adjust them: I will put the small marble jar where the hair product was and leave the hair product at the back, almost as a prop for the photograph. Finally, I will add some greenery so the image feels less cold and closer to the photographs we have been making for Olivia. Keep checking through the phone's framing to make sure the arrangement is exactly as you want it.

## Check natural light before taking the picture

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L07 · 04:33–05:41

I recommend checking the framing often. The arrangement looks good to me now, so we will see how it looks in natural light and correct that light with our reflector. We have turned off all the set lights so you can see the light coming from the window. As you can see on the radiator and backpack, the shadow from the ceramic piece moves to the left. If I had put the piece here, the shadow would have covered a large part of the photograph.

The set and framing are ready. It is still a good idea to make sure the camera lens is clean and that the screen brightness is at maximum.

## Focus, set exposure and take the photograph

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L07 · 05:41–06:28

Now we can take the photograph. Set the frame as planned and hold the phone firmly in the left hand. Focus on the products by tapping the screen once; phones generally focus and expose for that area. On an iPhone, after tapping, move up or down to adjust the exposure (*exposición*). Ideally, raise it a little without overexposing the image—without a part of the screen turning white. Once we have the exposure set, we take the photograph.

## Soften the shadows and compare several shots

Anchor: 227 · U3 · L07 · 06:28–08:32

The shadows are quite dark, so use the foam board (*cartón pluma*) we discussed. Place it on the side opposite the light. If the window is on your right, put the board on your left, following the direction in which the shadow falls. To hold it up, put something behind it: a stool, a chair if you are on the floor, an encyclopedia, a book or any box you have handy. Any support that holds the white board up will do. I will stand it up and use this stool.

With the reflector in place, take the photograph again. Reset the framing and focus on the products. The shadows around the hand and below the plate are now much softer, and the image feels more harmonious. Hold the phone firmly, focus gently, raise the exposure a little, hold your breath and take the photograph. The picture is ready; we will edit it later, after we finish this unit.

When you make this kind of photograph, the set is already in place, so take some extra shots from different angles: a little farther to the right or left, higher or lower. Take several photographs so you can compare them and choose the one you like best. I took only one because I knew exactly what I wanted. In the next lesson we will make another kind of photograph, an overhead photograph (*cenital*), using a colored background.

### Key points (derived)

- Choose the place and frame before arranging the props so you only prepare what the photograph will show.
- Place the largest objects with their shadows in mind, then balance the light with a reflector.
- Compare photographs from several angles before choosing a final shot.
