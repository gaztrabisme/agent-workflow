## Batch-prepare posts and choose a scheduling app

Anchor: 227 · U4 · L14 · 00:08–01:47

![227 · U4 · L14 · 00:08](frames/U04L14-F0008.00.jpg)

On screen: "CÓMO MANEJARTE POR INSTAGRAM Y PROGRAMAR PUBLICACIONES" [How to manage yourself on Instagram and schedule posts]  
Shows: Lesson title over a typewriter shot.

In this lesson, I’ll show you how to schedule posts to save time. If you use Instagram to promote your project or professionally, it will probably be very useful to work more efficiently.

The most efficient way I’ve found to create content is a kind of batch cooking: you know how you cook everything for the next week over one weekend? It is the same idea, but with posts. I usually set aside an entire afternoon, for example, to take lots of photos: photos, Stories, anything that comes to mind. I might go out and photograph plenty of fruit and flowers, then take photos at home or of other things like that. I edit all those photographs together, so I have a lot of content ready.

The ideal way to finish this process is to schedule everything and write the text for all the posts. Then, during the month, we would not really have to worry about publishing. With the automatic publishing features available today (*autopublicarse*), I could be here telling you about this and teaching you this course while something was also being published on my Instagram. That would mean not having to worry about it at all.

How do I do it? I normally use an app called Later. It lets you schedule posts and preview (*previsualizar*) how your gallery is looking, which is also very useful. There are many apps for this (captions: *publicaciones*); another very well-known one is Planoli, among others. I use Later because I have used it for years and like it best. Still, I recommend trying others if you feel like it and using whichever ones you like most. Let’s open the app and I’ll show you how.

## Add images to the media library

Anchor: 227 · U4 · L14 · 01:47–02:23

When we open the app, it goes straight to the calendar. If we tap Media, though, we can add images directly. We tap the plus sign, then Photo Library, or whatever the equivalent is, and add images from our camera roll or whichever album we want. We select the images and tap Add.

As you can see, I have added a lot of images because I take many of them in advance. I organize them all together to see how I like them to look. To start scheduling, you go into each image and create a post for that photo.

## Add caption text, hashtag sets, location, and people

Anchor: 227 · U4 · L14 · 03:16–04:36

One thing I like about Later is that it can save text. I do not use this for captions themselves, but I use it to save packs of hashtags (*packs de hashtags*). To open them, we tap the icon on the right that looks like a page with a hashtag. To add a new set, we tap New. To add one we already have, we select it directly. Let’s imagine it is this one, and then all the hashtags appear below.

Another thing I like is that the app tells you how many hashtags you have used. Here it is four, and the maximum is 30, in case we wanted to use a million hashtags. When we have everything ready—in this case it is obviously not ready, but let’s imagine it is—we tap Done.

Then we get to the other options we can add for free: a location and tags for the people who appear in the photo. To tag someone, we keep tapping. We need to be completely sure that we have the right user. The app does not autocomplete the name here, so we have to make sure we enter it correctly. Sometimes you tag someone and it turns out to be another person on the other side of the world.

## Choose automatic or notification publishing

Anchor: 227 · U4 · L14 · 04:36–06:32

When everything is ready, we choose whether we want the post to publish itself, using Auto Publish, or whether we want to publish it manually. With Auto Publish, we choose the date and time when we want the post to go up. With manual publishing, we also choose a date and time, because the app sends us a notification reminding us to upload it. Even when we publish manually, it is very convenient: the caption is already saved and the app reminds us when to post. We open the app and simply tap to publish. It copies the text automatically; we upload the post, paste the text, and everything is ready.

To set it up, tap No Time Selected, tap Change, and choose when we want it to publish—tomorrow at some time, for example. When it is ready, tap the button on the right, which means Schedule Post, and it will be scheduled.

What do I usually do? Since I schedule a lot of photos one after another, I do not choose Auto Publish. I choose the notification option so the photos will not publish automatically. I plan several posts and then rearrange them, so I do not set their times yet. I do that later, once they are in order. I repeat this process for each of my photos and then arrange them.

## Crop a photo that is too tall

Anchor: 227 · U4 · L14 · 06:32–07:09

If this warning appears while you are scheduling a post, it means the photo is too tall for Instagram. Tap the photo and you will get the option to crop it as a square or to a four-fifths ratio (*cuatro quintos*), which is the Instagram Portrait format. Crop it the way you like, tap Save, and it will be ready.

## Preview and arrange the gallery

Anchor: 227 · U4 · L14 · 07:09–09:44

Once we have scheduled everything, we can tap Calendar and then Preview on the right. The preview shows how the posts look in the order we scheduled them. We can see whether we like the arrangement or want to change something. To change it, tap at the top right and drag and drop.

One thing I usually do is avoid putting two very similar posts close together. Here, two selfies are close to each other. These photos are also darker than the rest of the gallery, and in this case they are both quite warm in tone; they are right beside each other. I would move them farther apart. I would do the same if there were several photos of the sky. I also space out photos with people and photos without people.

Another thing that works very well is to put a photo with lots of elements beside one that has almost no elements. I would not put another busy photo right beside it; I would leave a little space between them. Here I would take this warm-toned photo and move it a good distance from the other warm one. I would move the selfie too. I might use this dark photo that is not very colorful here, so that these two colorful pictures are not so close together.

What I recommend is trying things, moving the posts around, and seeing what you like best. There are many creative ways to arrange the gallery. You could put three very similar photos in the same row and organize the gallery by rows. Or you could keep making three different columns, with every third photo being the same kind. You might end up with a gallery where the whole right-hand side is portraits and the center is recipes and things like that. You can arrange it in a thousand ways.

I really like the gallery as it is now, so I would leave it. Although perhaps the longer you look at it, the more you want to change it. For example, the last post here is a picture of my dog, and beside it is another one with my dog. Maybe I would swap the two warm photos so they are farther apart and the gallery looks like this. It is simply a matter of playing around so it does not look too repetitive.

## Set the final schedule and stay present

Anchor: 227 · U4 · L14 · 09:44–11:26

When we are happy with the order, we can now choose Auto Publish. We write the captions properly and set suitable dates. This post will not be at the end of the week, and this one will not be tomorrow; those dates will be the other way around. If you want to find out what time might be best for you to publish, you can look at your statistics. Then we can schedule everything and leave it ready.

There are several things to keep in mind about Later specifically. The conditions for its free account keep changing. I am telling you what they are as I record this, but you can go to Later’s website and tap Plans to see the free conditions that apply at the time. One thing that will always be required, unless Instagram changes it, is a business account if you want to Auto Publish. Another condition is that the free account lets you publish ten posts a month. I never publish more than ten in a month; it seems impossible to me. I also prefer to put a lot of work into each post so it has high quality, rather than publishing constantly without adding more richness. The app lets us publish Instagram photos and videos, including automatically. What it does not let us Auto Publish is Reels (captions: *rígidas*).

With this app, managing social media will be much easier and the whole process will be more efficient. You still need to pay attention on the day you publish: answer comments, answer messages, and always be present.
