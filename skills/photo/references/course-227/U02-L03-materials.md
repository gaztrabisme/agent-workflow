## Adapting the phone camera

Anchor: 227 · U2 · L03 · 00:00–01:55

Throughout this course, we will always use a phone to take photographs. That means changing gears a little, especially if you normally take pictures with a camera. The phone has several advantages: we always have it with us, we can take pictures quickly, and we can edit them and upload them to social media almost immediately after taking them.

A professional camera has other strengths. It makes larger, higher-quality files that we can print. It offers more creative possibilities because we can configure it manually, and it can be specialized with different equipment. For example, you can use filters to remove reflections from a glass product.

So what should we keep in mind when taking pictures with a phone? Everything will stay in focus. The familiar camera effect in which the foreground (*primer plano*) is sharp while the background is blurred will not happen. That means we need to take as much care with the background as with the foreground.

A phone camera usually has a wide-angle lens (*objetivo angular*). If I photograph you from the end of this table, a professional camera might frame only part of the table, while the phone may show all the way to its far end. A wide-angle lens also distorts objects that are very close to it, so we should step back a little. This matters especially for portraits: if we stand too close, a person’s face can look distorted or an arm can look enormous.

## Use abundant natural light

Anchor: 227 · U2 · L03 · 01:55–02:18

The best way to get good results from a phone is to have plenty of light, especially natural light. More natural light gives us a higher-quality photograph. In very low light, even with the living-room light on, image quality can be poor. You may know the very grainy, yellow, strange-looking photos you get at night.

## A simple reflector and colored backdrops

Anchor: 227 · U2 · L03 · 02:18–03:01

Alongside the phone, we will use a few other materials. One item I use often at home or in the studio is a reflector. We do not need a professional reflector or anything like that; a white foam board like this will work (captions: *No serviría*). It does not need to be very thick; it simply needs to be white. This one is 100 by 70 centimeters and 5 millimeters thick.

## Securing and raising objects

Anchor: 227 · U2 · L03 · 03:01–04:18

I use a sheet of card to put objects on and make a background. If we join two of the large 100-by-70-centimeter sheets, we can also put a person in front of them and use them as a backdrop. We could do that for a dog, too, or anything else.

To keep products from falling or moving on the background, we will use Blu Tack. It is important to get the white kind, because the blue kind stains and leaves a greasy mark. It is a moldable putty that sticks things in place.

![227 · U2 · L03 · 03:24.50](frames/U02L03-F0324.50.jpg)

On screen: [none]

We will also use washi tape. Ordinary adhesive tape can tear and damage paper when you pull it off. Washi tape works more like painter’s masking tape, but it is better for this purpose and can be reused many times without ruining the paper.

We also use the roll itself to raise things in the scene. For example, if we photograph a photograph on a white background and its edge blends into the floor, we can raise it so that it casts a shadow. The shadow makes the edge visible. That is one example, but the roll is useful for many things because of its size.

## Choosing props for the brand

Anchor: 227 · U2 · L03 · 04:18–05:35

And, of course, we will use the phone. Finally, we will use all kinds of props that fit our brand or particular project. Here we have a book, a natural sponge, photographs, and a wooden comb. They suit the brand we will work with in this course.

When I start working with a brand like this cosmetics brand, I try to think of objects that fit its natural, ecological values and the way its products are used. If the brand uses a lot of shampoos and other bath products, I look for things we might find in a bathroom. If your brand sells stationery, you will need plenty of desk items: writing pens, scissors, pencils, ballpoint pens, rulers, and whatever else could be on a desk. If you have a children’s clothing shop, neutral wooden toys, pretty stuffed animals, fabrics that go with the prints you use, and perhaps small chairs or similar furniture will be useful.

Above all, remember that these materials should reinforce the values you want your brand to communicate. In the next lesson, we will see how to get the best quality from our phones.

### Key points (derived)

- Step back from nearby subjects to reduce wide-angle distortion, especially in portraits.
- Use abundant natural light and take care with the background as well as the subject.
- A white foam board, colored card, white Blu Tack, and reusable washi tape can support a simple setup.
- Choose props that fit the project’s uses and reinforce its values.
