# Lesson 12 · Get moving!

Anchor: 669 · U4 · L12 · 4:18 · https://www.domestika.org/en/courses/669-intimate-photography-portraits/units/3864-editing#course_lesson_12214

## Put finished photographs into the world

Anchor: 669 · U4 · L12 · 00:00–00:41

In this lesson we’ll look at what to do once our photographs are finished. Taking good photos and feeling proud of your work is very important. But even if you do that very well, if you stay at home on the sofa waiting for someone to knock on your door, it’s very unlikely that anything will happen.

I’ll show you a few ideas and ways to move your work so it can bring you the results you hope for, or would love to see.

## Choose a route to your audience

Anchor: 669 · U4 · L12 · 00:41–02:21

First, if you want to work for brands or take photographs of particular people, don’t wait for them to call one day and somehow guess what you want. Put together a portfolio (*portafolio*) that you think they’ll like. Write to them; if that doesn’t work, call; if it still doesn’t, go to their offices.

I think it’s important to have a little boldness: to be sure of yourself and convince the other person, the brand, or the company that they need you—that you’re the right person for them and that your work will help them, just as working for them will help you.

If what you’d like, on the other hand, is to give your photographs a more artistic direction and, for example, put on an exhibition, build a solid project that you like and feel proud of. Send the project to exhibition spaces in your town or city, and to bars that also put on small exhibitions. Find a place where you can show publicly what you’ve made.

If you want to publish with a publisher, send them your work. Write up the book project you’d like to make and send it to them too. In some way, all your projects can exist and happen, as long as you know which doors to knock on.

It’s important that when we get a “no,” we don’t get sad, because it doesn’t mean that what you do is bad. Maybe your work hasn’t matured enough yet and you need to keep practicing. Or maybe they aren’t your client, and you need to look for another part of the market that fits what you have to say better.

## Combine persistence with luck

Anchor: 669 · U4 · L12 · 02:21–03:35

My favorite band was a group I loved. One day I went to one of their concerts and thought I’d love to take photos of them. I didn’t stay home waiting for them to write to me. I wrote to them, spoke with them, and offered to take photos for free because I truly wanted to photograph them. What was happening on stage moved me, even if I wasn’t getting paid.

After I photographed them for free a couple of times, they hired me, and I was their photographer for a few months. I say this because it would never have happened if I’d stayed at home thinking, “Wow, I’d love to take photos of them!” Instead, I wrote to them and pursued the opportunity, believing I had something to say.

I think this is very important. For me, success is a mixture of consistency and a little luck. We can’t control when we’ll get a stroke of luck and land the job of our lives, which might lead many other brands or people to trust us. Since that part isn’t in our hands, we can manage consistency. We can be there, working, so that when some luck comes along, it finds us in the middle of the work.

## Get up, practice, and pursue the work

Anchor: 669 · U4 · L12 · 03:35–04:18

So from here I invite you to get up off the sofa, think about what you want and what you’d like to do, and keep practicing so you can improve and become the kind of photographer you would hire. Then go after the brands, people, festivals—whatever you’re passionate about—and use every means you have to try to make it work. If you don’t believe in yourself, nobody else will.

That’s all the course content. Even so, I invite you to watch a video that summarizes everything we’ve done so far, so the whole process is clear.

### Key points (derived)

- Create a project suited to the people or spaces you want to reach, then contact them directly.
- Treat rejection as information about timing, readiness, or fit, and keep practicing and approaching new opportunities.
- You can’t schedule luck, but you can keep working so you’re ready when an opportunity arrives.
