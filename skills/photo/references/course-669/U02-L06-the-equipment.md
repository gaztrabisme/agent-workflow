## Choose a lens for the space and portrait

Anchor: 669 · U2 · L06 · 00:00–01:22

I will explain the equipment I use and what we will use in the practical session that follows. I use a Canon 6D camera body (*cuerpo de cámara*) with a Sigma 35mm f/1.4 lens (*objetivo*) attached.

This lens works very well when I want the space to have more prominence. It gives me a much wider framing, so I can include all the elements I want in the photograph without having to move very far back. I also use it a lot when I photograph more than one person. They take up more room, and the wider frame lets me move around the models without having to stand too far away. This is the lens I use most for those two situations.

For standard portraits, where there is one person and the space is almost secondary, I use the 50mm f/1.4. It is the lens I use most. In portraits I tend to concentrate on the person’s face, rather than giving the setting as much strength or prominence.

## Other lenses and choosing what works for you

Anchor: 669 · U2 · L06 · 01:22–03:00

Apart from these two lenses, there are two others I use a lot, though we will not use them in this practical session. The first is Sigma’s 24–70mm. It works well for event photographs, for example, when I need to zoom, and for concert photographs. I also use a second lens for concerts: the 14–24mm, which lets me make very wide-angle photographs where the audience can be seen clearly. We will set concert photography aside and focus on these two lenses for the next lesson (captions: *de helado*).

If you do not have this equipment, do not worry. Each of us finds the lenses that work best for our kind of photography. These lenses work incredibly well for me, but I think you can get a good result with any lens. For me, the essential thing is to get a lens whose aperture (*diafragma*) opens as wide as possible. That gives us a very shallow depth of field (she says *distancia focal*, “focal length”), which I consider an indispensable portrait tool because it directs all the attention to the face, where I connect most. Still, you can find the lens that is right for you; this equipment is not a requirement for making this kind of portrait. These are simply the lenses that work for me.
