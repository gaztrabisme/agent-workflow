## Choosing a natural way to strengthen color

Anchor: 1343 · U26 · L38 · 00:10–01:23

I’m going to show how to make colors vivid while keeping the result natural. We will use Hue/Saturation as well as Vibrance, compare the two, and look at where each works best. Photoshop offers many ways to work with color, and people often enhance or saturate specific colors in a picture. Here I’ll show ways to change or enhance color that is already present, as well as ways to add color where it has been lost.

I’ll start with a color checker chart. It shows how colors have been recorded in an image. Color checker charts can also be used to create custom color profiles for raw converters, so that images can be edited with very precise color. This can be useful in product photography.

## Master hue and saturation affect the whole image

Anchor: 1343 · U26 · L38 · 01:23–03:14

I click the black-and-white adjustment circle at the bottom of the Layers panel to add a Hue/Saturation adjustment layer.

Hue/Saturation lets us change the hue, or color, of a color. By default, the dialog edits Master, which means all colors.

You might change the Master hue for a special effect or to play with color combinations in a logo.

The Master controls also let us change overall saturation. I often see people use this slider to increase saturation. That is not wrong, but this generic saturation slider is not designed for photography. When I raise it, even neutral grays become tinted because they contain a hint of blue. Noise becomes much more noticeable, as does the image’s chromatic aberration. Some colors also become noisy.

## Target a particular color with Hue/Saturation

Anchor: 1343 · U26 · L38 · 03:14–04:52

Hue/Saturation is especially useful when we target colors. I’ll reset the adjustment, open the list that currently says Master, and choose a specific color. The red in this chart looks a little orange, so I’ll choose Reds and use the eyedropper to sample the shade I want Photoshop to work on. After I click the red, the small range bar shifts to center around that sampled shade.

We can then change the hue of that red. When making this adjustment, look at the lower range slider to see which colors are being changed, rather than relying on the hue slider above it. The interface can be confusing, and it may change in a future version. I can also increase the saturation of the targeted red. Toggling the preview shows the before and after. Keep in mind that related red colors elsewhere in the image will also be edited.

## Compare Saturation and Vibrance on the chart

Anchor: 1343 · U26 · L38 · 04:52–06:45

To limit an effect to one area, make a selection and use a layer mask. I’ll add a Vibrance adjustment layer from the bottom of the Layers panel to compare it with Hue/Saturation. Raising Saturation produces a similar result, but the algorithm is slightly different: the grays do not become as blue, the colors are less noisy, and the chromatic aberration is less obvious, though it is still visible in parts of the image.

Some colors change strongly. The three squares at the chart’s upper left partly represent skin tones. The Saturation control does not treat them differently because they are skin tones. With Vibrance, saturation still changes, but not as strongly as it did before. Hue/Saturation is useful for specific colors and effects. For a general boost to colors, I recommend a Vibrance adjustment layer. Sometimes combining Vibrance and Saturation can work well.

## Keep skin tones natural in a portrait

Anchor: 1343 · U26 · L38 · 06:45–07:52

Now I’ll try the difference on a photograph with greenery, skin tones, and some flat or weakly saturated colors. I’ll add a Vibrance adjustment layer, then start by raising Saturation all the way. The person’s skin turns very orange, although parts of the image look quite nice. If the person were not in the foreground, Saturation might work well, perhaps with the setting reduced a little. With Vibrance, even at its highest setting, the skin tone still looks good. The colors are much richer while the skin remains natural.

### Key points (derived)

- Hue/Saturation is suited to targeted color changes and special effects; Vibrance is the suggested general boost for photographs.
- Strong global Saturation can exaggerate gray casts, noise, chromatic aberration, and skin color. A combination of Vibrance and Saturation may sometimes work well.
