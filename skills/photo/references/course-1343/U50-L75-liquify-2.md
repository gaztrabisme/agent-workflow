## Set up an editable Liquify pass

Anchor: 1343 · U50 · L75 · 00:00–01:23

Start by creating a new layer: duplicate the Background by dragging it onto the new-layer icon. Then right-click and choose Convert to Smart Object so the Liquify edit can be changed later.

Choose when to do this in the workflow. Do it either first or last. Even though a Smart Object remains editable, cloning on top of it will not fit once the Liquify changes are moved back. For client work, the same timing applies: either Liquify at the beginning and get the result signed off—for example, slimming arms or legs—or do it at the end. He personally tends to Liquify as the final step.

## Shape the belly gradually and review

Anchor: 1343 · U50 · L75 · 01:23–04:17

Open Filter and choose Liquify. At the top of the menu, Photoshop offers the last-used filter; choosing it would apply the same settings used on the previous image. Do not click that top command. Choose Liquify from the menu instead.

To slim the belly or make its outline smoother and straighter, start with Forward Warp. Set pressure low, around 5, and use a fairly large brush with density around 50. Pull the contour out very slowly and gradually. Push some parts in and others out to create a straighter line; that looks more natural than pushing everything in or everything out.

Avoid creating bumps. Zoom in to inspect a slightly straight edge and use a smaller brush to round it off, then increase the brush size again if needed. Even pressure 5 feels a little high to him; he prefers to work more gradually and review often. Press P to compare before and after. Keep some of the image's original shape: retain the way the belly goes in and then comes out instead of making it perfectly straight. That remains true to reality, just less pronounced. Zoom out and press P again; he is happy with the result.

## Smooth and slim the legs

Anchor: 1343 · U50 · L75 · 04:17–08:02

There are small bumps on the legs, so first make the line a little straighter. Push out and then push in. The difference is subtle: the lower leg is a little straighter. Make the brush larger when needed. On the other side, push a curve out, push the next part out, then push another part in. Repeat this on another bump to make a smoother line. Once the small bumps are removed, switch to the Push Left tool, which he thinks of as a “push pixel” tool.

To push a line right, paint from the top down. Reduce pressure a lot, to 3, and use a fairly large brush. Start slightly outside the contour and work inward so there is no small bump where the stroke begins. Make several passes, then press P to review. The result looks good; he adds a little more.

For the other leg, protect the back-leg line with Freeze Mask so it will not move. Use Push Left again, painting from the bottom up to push left. Because the curve bends, start a little higher rather than at the very bottom. The line is now a little straighter. Push this area left just a little as well. Working at low pressure should produce a good result.

## Adjust facial features with Face-Aware Liquify

Anchor: 1343 · U50 · L75 · 08:02–10:18

Open Filter and choose Liquify again. The last Liquify feature here is Face-Aware Liquify. Photoshop detects the face; this portrait has one face. Controls change eye size. In retouching, eyes are often made slightly bigger, as in cartoons. Adjust the eyes separately or click the link to change both at once. This can look unnatural, so use care. There is also a control for the distance between the eyes.

The nose controls change its height and width. The tool can make someone smile or frown a little. It also controls the heights of the upper and lower lip, the width and height of the mouth, and the face shape, including making the forehead lower or taller. The top edge of this image is close to the hair. Keep in mind that pushing from an image edge means some cloning work will be needed afterward. The chin height and jawline controls are also available. Photoshop shows them for this portrait because the face is very square, and they can slim it a little.

## Combine facial controls with manual tools

Anchor: 1343 · U50 · L75 · 10:18–12:42

Face-Aware Liquify does not change the top of the image when adjusting face width, so combine it with another tool if more change is needed.

For example, use Push Pixel or Push Left. To move this side of the image left, paint from the bottom up. The width around the middle of the ear is already good, but he wants to push it in a little more, so he starts there and paints upward. On the other side, move it right by painting from the top down. Use Forward Warp to even out the line: density around 50 is fine, reduce pressure to about 3, and start with a fairly large brush, gradually making it smaller. The aim is to straighten the lines of the face a little. Use Freeze Mask along the neckline to avoid disturbing it.

Press P to compare the original and edited image. If doing this kind of edit, make sure the client or the person in the photograph is happy with it. Do not assume that a person with a large or small nose should have it changed; get approval first.

## Use Liquify sparingly

Anchor: 1343 · U50 · L75 · 12:42–13:05

Liquify can be destructive, so use it sparingly. When possible, use other methods, such as dodging and burning, to slim an area a little. The next lesson will cover frequency separation.
