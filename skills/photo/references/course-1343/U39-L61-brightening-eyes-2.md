## Bring out the iris color and outline

Anchor: 1343 · U39 · L61 · 00:37–01:19

The last thing I want to show is how to enhance an eye. This woman has quite interesting, brightly colored eyes with a marbling effect. There is also a dark line that I can continue to frame the iris more clearly. I’ll work with the outline already present, but you can add this effect to an eye without an outline too, because it can add quite an interesting effect.

The color is already strong, but I’ll enhance it with a Curves adjustment layer called “Iris.” Hue/Saturation or Vibrance can often look artificial. Instead, I’ll show how changing a blend mode can create an interesting effect.

## Use Soft Light without enlarging the pupils

Anchor: 1343 · U39 · L61 · 01:19–02:32

I change the Curves layer from Normal to Soft Light. The first result is very strong and damages the skin tone, but looking just at the eye shows how the color and contrast come through. The blacks bleed a little and seem to get larger. If that is the effect you want, you can keep it. To avoid it, lift the black point. That reduces the bleeding; the pupil still gets a little darker, so I brighten it slightly as well. That feels like a good starting point.

## Paint and feather the iris mask

Anchor: 1343 · U39 · L61 · 02:32–04:53

I invert the layer mask with Command-I or Control-I, then select the regular Brush tool with B, at full opacity and flow. Choose a hard brush that fits the iris as closely as possible. Use the bracket keys to set its size, make sure white is the foreground color, and click once. Because the photograph is square-on, the same brush size fits the other eye; in another image, you may need a different size. Make another click and adjust its position if needed.

The first click has gone over the eyelid. I swap to black, make the brush smaller and paint over the lid on both sides. A useful habit is to inspect the mask by holding Alt or Option and clicking it. Two small bits are missing. They are difficult to see in the image, though once I know where they are I can spot a bright streak. Looking at the mask makes mistakes easy to see. Alt- or Option-click again to return to the image.

Add a tiny amount of feathering so the edge is not razor-sharp. A slightly soft brush, perhaps 95% hardness, could work, but setting feather here is more precise and adjustable later. I want only one or two pixels; more would let the effect bleed into the rest of the eye. Compare before and after: the effect is very strong, so I reduce the layer opacity. I may revisit it, but I’ll leave it at 50% for now. It still looks strong, though I like it.

## Reveal the marbling without losing its brush strokes

Anchor: 1343 · U39 · L61 · 04:53–07:23

For the marbling effect, I add another Curves adjustment and use another blend mode. Lighten will not make a difference; Screen, just below it, brightens the image and works in the opposite way from Soft Light’s effect on the blacks. The whites begin to bleed, so I pull them down a little and darken the adjustment to avoid too much brightening.

I invert the mask, set white as the foreground color and zoom right in. A brush around four pixels is close to the size of the little lines in the iris. I paint over those lines. It looks a little odd at this point, and I’m doing it quickly; normally I would use a tablet, which makes this brushwork faster and more precise.

I intentionally used a hard brush so I can control the mask. I inspect it first, then check the image. I soften the mask enough to blend it without losing the brush strokes. Two and a half pixels is too much; around one and a half to two pixels works well. I Alt- or Option-click to return to the image, zoom out and compare. The marbling now comes through, but the result is very strong. I lower the opacity; even 50% may be too strong, and the saturation may also be too strong. I rename this layer “Marble,” put it into a group with Command-G or Control-G, then compare before and after.

## Continue the dark outline with a masked color layer

Anchor: 1343 · U39 · L61 · 07:23–11:12

The last change is to continue the dark outline around the eye. I put color over the whole eye first, then work with the mask. I add a blank layer inside the eye-effect group and call it “outline.” Zooming in, I hold Alt or Option to sample a color. This is a little like working with the Clone Stamp. It looks black, but it is actually a dark blue that matches the eye. I cover each eye completely with a brush click. It looks strange for now. I set the blend mode to Multiply; details start to show through, and lowering opacity blends the two together.

I soften the edges with Filter > Blur > Gaussian Blur (captions: *Caution blur*), using about 1.5 pixels. Then I add a layer mask and choose Hide All. Alternatively, Alt- or Option-click the Add Layer Mask button to create a black mask. With a small, soft brush, white as the foreground color and opacity around 30%, I paint on the mask. The circle I placed matches the eye, so I do not need to worry about crossing into the white of the eye; I just avoid painting in the wrong places inside it. I make the outline slightly wider at the top and reproduce its little irregularities. Then I swap to black and remove some of those irregularities, and repeat on the other eye. A tablet would make this easier. I swap back to white and reduce opacity to 20% with the 2 key.

Zoom out and compare. The result is a little strong, so I lower the layer opacity again. It looks fairly natural, perhaps still slightly strong, but the idea is clear. The final eye has added saturation, contrast, the marbling effect and the enhanced outline. This technique can be used to brighten many other things in an image, not just eyes. In the next lesson, I’ll show how to reduce wrinkles; it is important to reduce them rather than remove them.
