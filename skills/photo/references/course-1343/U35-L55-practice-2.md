## Set the overall contrast and autumn colours

Anchor: 1343 · U35 · L55 · 04:05–07:08

Next I work on the overall colour and feel. I add a Curves adjustment for contrast. The brightness is all right, although I would normally begin there; this time I go straight to contrast. The bright tones are already fairly bright, so I darken the shadows and perhaps the very darkest blacks. This changes the look of the image quite a lot. I try the Luminosity blend mode, which looks a little more natural.

Then I shift the colours slightly because it is not fully autumn yet. I name the first adjustment “Contrast” and the next one “Autumn colours.” I sample the reds and move their hue a little toward red. I sample the foliage that looks green; Photoshop identifies it as yellows, which is typical because those foliage colours are actually closer to yellow. I shift them further toward yellow-orange and reduce their saturation a little. I do not want them to turn grey, just to lose some of the colour. The autumn-colour effect is a little strong, so I lower the layer opacity.

I also try a Black & White adjustment in Soft Light. It gives the photograph a dramatic impact and adds contrast, but it is much too strong, so I lower its opacity. The left side is now rather dark; I will brighten it next.

## Brighten the left side and the path

Anchor: 1343 · U35 · L55 · 07:08–08:46

The left-hand side has become quite dark, so I add an adjustment to brighten it and call it “Colour effect,” so I remember what it does. I add a Curves adjustment that brightens the right and left areas, name it “Right and Left,” then use a gradient from white to black so the curve affects the intended areas. Another section is also quite dark, so I pick up a very large brush; the mask does not have to be exact. I paint over it and increase the opacity a little. I would also like the small path to be brighter, so I paint there, and add the effect to a few other small areas as well.

## Shape the light across the mountains

Anchor: 1343 · U35 · L55 · 08:46–10:09

I like this section of the image, so I make a targeted Curves adjustment for it. I call it “Mountain top,” lift the highlights and darken the shadows. The effect is too strong overall, but I like the result on the top area. I invert the mask, lower the brush flow and paint the adjustment in selectively. It creates a pleasing diagonal across the image. I use Shift to temporarily turn the mask off and look around for other areas that might benefit. The mountains in the background could use some work too, but I want to treat them separately.

Anchor: 1343 · U35 · L55 · 10:09–12:17
