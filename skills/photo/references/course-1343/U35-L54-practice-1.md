## Set up the practice image

Anchor: 1343 · U35 · L54 · 00:00–03:02

We have reached the end of the third course, so we will do another practical exercise together. You can use some of my sample images, but it will be more interesting to use your own. Retouch an environmental image with the techniques from this course. It can be any kind of environmental image; ideally choose one that can be changed dramatically, for example by replacing its sky or making a multiple-exposure action shot. Work from a RAW file if possible, for the best results. If you cannot find a suitable photograph, you can use my sample (the captions misread this as “MySomp”).

I start in the RAW converter. The exposure looks fine, though I will make a small brightness adjustment guided by the histogram. I lower the highlights a little, open the shadows, and make sure the blacks stay properly black. I add a little clarity and make the colours slightly more vibrant. With a radial filter, I bring out detail and texture in one part of the image by adding some clarity there.

Next I inspect the optics. I suspect there may be chromatic aberration, so I zoom in closely. There is a tiny amount along the edge. Since I plan to replace the sky, I want to avoid carrying too much of that colour fringing into the composite. I leave the profile corrections as they are and open the photograph as a smart object, so I can return to the RAW converter and change anything later.

## Choose and prepare a replacement sky

Anchor: 1343 · U35 · L54 · 03:02–04:30

Looking at the photograph in Photoshop, I identify the small bus, some cars and the people as distractions. The light in the landscape is one of the reasons I took the photograph, so I want to emphasize it. The sky is rather bland, so I will replace it first. I copy a separate storm-sky photograph across and name that layer “New Sky.” When replacing a sky, check the direction of the light: in this scene it comes from the right, while it comes from the left in the sky photograph. I use Free Transform to scale the sky up slightly.

## Select the old sky and refine its edge

Anchor: 1343 · U35 · L54 · 04:30–06:26

I flip the new sky horizontally to match the light direction, then hide it temporarily while I select the old sky. I use the Quick Selection tool and paint across the old sky. The selection looks reasonable, though I missed the top corner. I adjust the edge carefully to avoid fringing or halos, first expanding the selection by one pixel. In Select and Mask, I set the preview to On Layers so I can see the new sky behind the landscape and judge the boundary. I may still move the sky, but this preview lets me inspect its edge. I soften the edge very slightly. It looks good, with only a tiny amount of fringing in places that I can fix later if necessary. I try increasing the setting slightly and shifting the edge; that improves it.

## Position and balance the composite

Anchor: 1343 · U35 · L54 · 06:26–08:46

I zoom out, add the layer mask and reveal the selected sky. To move the sky without moving its mask, I unlink the two. Then I use the Move tool to adjust the sky on its own, taking care where I place it. I test it slightly higher and decide that placement looks good. The sky looks a little dark on the left, but I can brighten that part. I add a Curves adjustment and clip it to the layer below so it affects only the sky. I want the brightening only on the left, so I use a white-to-black gradient on the mask. I add a little more where needed; the result looks acceptable.

## Remove the bus and other distractions

Anchor: 1343 · U35 · L54 · 08:46–11:57

Now I remove the distracting parts of the landscape. I create a new layer for cloning, zoom to 100% and then closer still. Removing the bus will be difficult because it covers part of a tree. I consider what can be cloned: I try copying a nearby tree, then part of the wall. This kind of repair often takes trial and error when there is no obvious source. The wall works fairly well, although the line along the road looks slightly suspicious; for now it is acceptable.

I then cover the small lorry. I begin by cloning a little wall, then use part of a tree.
