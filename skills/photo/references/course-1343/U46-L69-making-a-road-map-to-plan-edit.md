## Why plan a retouch with a roadmap

Anchor: 1343 · U46 · L69 · 00:00–00:47

In this lesson, I show how to make a roadmap for a retouch. It is extremely useful when you are working with a client or as part of a team. A roadmap can speed up editing and improve communication. When you are not working directly or face to face with a client and need to send things by email or talk on the phone, the roadmap gives you something useful to refer to.

## Planning before retouching: Richard Avedon’s print notes

Anchor: 1343 · U46 · L69 · 00:47–01:27

Before you begin retouching, it is essential to have a plan and a clear structure for what you are going to do with the picture. Retouching existed long before Photoshop. The picture on screen is Richard Avedon’s notes to a print. Avedon knew exactly what he wanted from his pictures, but he did not do his own printing, so he scribbled on the pictures to tell the printers what he wanted. We use a very similar technique in Photoshop today.

## Building the roadmap and marking the first changes

Anchor: 1343 · U46 · L69 · 01:27–02:40

If I am retouching this image for a client, I first speak with the client to find out what they want from the image and what changes are required. Then I make a small roadmap on the image. I create a group, which I could do from the Layer menu, but I prefer to use the Layers panel. I call the group “Notes,” add a blank layer inside it, and call that layer “Roadmap.”

I choose bright, contrasting colors. Bright magenta (the captions read “Brian Magenta”) works well, as does cyan or a bright red like Avedon used.

For example, I can cross out flyaway hairs that we will remove in the retouch (the captions read “flyway hairs”). There are also bits of the backdrop showing at the bottom and on this side that we will probably need to remove. I could mark structural changes such as slimming the arms, although I do not particularly think she needs that here. That is the kind of change to confirm with the client first; do not make structural changes to a person without approval.

## Using different colors for teeth, glasses, and clothing

Anchor: 1343 · U46 · L69 · 02:40–03:45

Other roadmap notes could mark fixing teeth, reducing yellowing in teeth, or dealing with reflections in the glasses. I choose a different color for those marks, a bright cyan. The roadmap can also note changes to the shape of clothing, which is especially important in fashion retouching. Here I mark an area that I intend to reshape a little. For a fashion brand, we might also try to bring out logos more clearly and enhance other features in the garment.

## Confirming permanent marks and scars

Anchor: 1343 · U46 · L69 · 03:45–04:42

There is no need to mark every spot or skin imperfection; that is understood. But if someone has a scar or a permanent mark on their face, always get the go-ahead first. For example, this woman has a little scar on her arm. Before you start working, it is useful to clarify whether it should stay or go. You can mark small things like this to give an overall idea of the edits you are planning.

When talking with the client by phone or email, you can refer to the roadmap. Using different colors for different things also makes the marks easier for the client to find than having to describe where on the image you drew each one.

## Writing notes and keeping them with the image

Anchor: 1343 · U46 · L69 · 04:42–05:34

You can also write directly on the image. For example, I could write “Flyaways” by hand, or pick up the text tool and type on the image. Use a bright, contrasting color for these notes. Make sure every note, drawing, or text layer you add is inside the Notes group. Then it is easy to keep the notes above everything else and switch them off. You can refer back to the roadmap as you work on the image.

## Working alone and knowing when the edit is finished

Anchor: 1343 · U46 · L69 · 05:34–06:00

A roadmap is most beneficial when you are working with a client or in a team, but it can also help you plan edits when working on your own. That can help you spend less time and find a finishing point for the project. In the next lesson, I will teach you how to write an action so Photoshop performs tasks for you automatically.
