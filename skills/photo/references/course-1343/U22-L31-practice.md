## Set up the composite

Anchor: 1343 · U22 · L31 · 00:00–02:36

We have reached the end of the second course, so it is time for a practical exercise. You can use some of the sample images or your own images; seeing your own results would be more interesting. We are going to blend images, and there are many ways to do it, so choose your favorite method.

In this practical exercise, I blend two images. You can also work with a black-and-white image. The main thing is to demonstrate what you have learned: levels, curves, color corrections, converting to black and white, selections, and layer masks.

I have a portrait and a mountain landscape to combine.

## Match the color and tone

Anchor: 1343 · U22 · L31 · 02:36–04:05

The background has more reds and oranges, and it is darker, so I brighten it a little with a Curves adjustment layer. I also want to darken the portrait slightly and change its colors. I add another adjustment layer through the menu so I can clip it to the previous layer, creating a clipping mask, and darken the portrait. That helps the colors.

I add one more Curves layer and create a clipping mask again. I add a little red, a hint of magenta, and a little more yellow. The colors match better. Here is the before and after.

## Select and soften the blend

Anchor: 1343 · U22 · L31 · 04:05–05:59

There are many ways to blend images. I could use blend modes, but when two images have a very sharp edge splitting the picture in two, blend modes will leave that edge. I use a mask instead: I select the portrait layer and add a mask, then make a selection with the Quick Selection tool. I make the brush a little bigger. I like this tool because it quickly gives me a fairly accurate selection.

I am not aiming for a perfect cutout; I want the blend to follow the subject rather than a straight line.

![1343 · U22 · L31 · 04:57](frames/U22L31-F0457.00.jpg)

On screen: “Select and Mask...” [rest omitted]

I choose Select > Select and Mask and apply fairly heavy feathering, around 200 pixels. I do not want the selection to cover the edge of the subject, so I adjust Shift Edge.

## Refine the mask by hand

Anchor: 1343 · U22 · L31 · 05:59–08:15

I can see part of the mountain range at the top of the trees coming into the portrait. It is quite nice in a way, so I could blend it a little further, but I would rather hide it a little. I pick up the brush at fairly low opacity and paint to bring the portrait back a little.

## Brighten the background with a gradient

Anchor: 1343 · U22 · L31 · 08:15–08:58

To finish the blend, I want to brighten the right-hand side of the background a little. I add another Curves adjustment layer called “Brightness match” and brighten it a little more. Then I use the Gradient tool, going from white to black. I think that works better.

## Apply a final global curve

Anchor: 1343 · U22 · L31 · 08:58–09:59

Finally, to tie everything together in a composite like this, it is good to add a global adjustment layer on top of everything. I add another Curves layer called “Coloring,” spelled “Colouring” on screen, add a little contrast, and warm the colors a bit. That is my result.
