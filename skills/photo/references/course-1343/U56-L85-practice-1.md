## Plan the retouch on a roadmap

Anchor: 1343 · U56 · L85 · 01:16–03:01

I will start by making a roadmap to plan what I want to do. I am choosing a bright pink that contrasts with the image. I will clean up the skin and some flyaway hairs. A little light is missing here, and I will try to emphasize the small red light. I will reduce the bags under the eyes a little. The ellipse here is slightly faded. This arm looks a little thin, so I will make it more like the other one; I will slim this arm a bit and open this one out. There is a little dirt on the backdrop, or on the camera sensor, which I am cleaning up. I want to add contrast in the hair to give it more volume. I will brighten the eyes, clean up a small area, and address a couple of patchy areas in the eyebrow and a little bit sticking out. This gives me a good idea of what I want to do. I will switch the roadmap off and may refer to it later to check that I have done everything I planned.

## Reduce skin redness while preserving the red light

Anchor: 1343 · U56 · L85 · 05:24–07:04

Now I will adjust the skin to remove some redness. I want to keep the red light because it is part of the image’s styling, so I will not change it. I am targeting the blotchiness in the skin. I add a Hue/Saturation adjustment layer, sample the redness, and subtract it to get a better skin tone. That looks like a good target, so I shift its hue very slightly. Once it looks good, I invert the mask and brush the adjustment in, reducing the brush flow and opacity. I like the redness in the cheeks, so I do not brush over it.

## Build the frequency separation layers

Anchor: 1343 · U56 · L85 · 07:04–08:56

Now I will make a composite layer—or two, to be precise. The first one is the skin tone. I soften it a little, using around five pixels. You can use the action you created for this if you do not remember how; it is good to do the process a few times before using an action so you understand it. The second layer is the texture. I go to Image, choose Apply Image, and subtract the skin tone from the texture. The blend mode should be Linear Light. I put the layers in a group and call it fs, for frequency separation. Especially when you are beginning, toggle this group on and off. If you can see a change in the picture beyond a tiny one, you may have done something wrong or used incorrect values.

## Even the skin tone and preserve texture

Anchor: 1343 · U56 · L85 · 10:12–11:44

Next I move to the skin tone. I make selections and use Gaussian Blur. As you can see, I choose the top option to apply the last-used setting. I undo that because it did not work very well. It is starting to look good. Equalizing and evening out the skin tone helps create a smooth look while retaining the original skin texture, which is important.

## Paint back color and reduce shine

Anchor: 1343 · U56 · L85 · 11:44–14:30

I also use my brush and simply paint to reduce some shine. I am not sure whether one shiny area is part of the makeup or just shine, so I reduce it a little. I return to the last setting and use the shortcut again, which applies that setting. We can still see a hint of redness in the skin, but fixing or improving it earlier helped. I continue with the brush and paint over it. Frequency separation is a little like hand-coloring a picture, so be careful and sample continually. For example, where the color has faded a little in the lip, this technique makes it easy to paint some of the color back. Compared with cloning, this is much easier, though it is not necessarily an easy exercise.
